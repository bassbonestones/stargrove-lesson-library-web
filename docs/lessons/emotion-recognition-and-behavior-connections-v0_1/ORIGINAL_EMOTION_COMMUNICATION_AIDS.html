01 · Feelings are personal; choice is yours

Child-facing optional choice material, with educator instruction. Do not demand a child reveal a private feeling or enact an unpleasant one.

I'm interested

I might continue looking at a favorite book. Someone else might ask a question.

I'd like a quiet pause

I may ask for space or move to a safe accessible calm area.

I'm pleased

I may show my work, enjoy it privately, or move to another activity.

I'm unsure

I may ask a trusted adult to help. I can take my time.

Child agency: These are examples, not a test of a child's own feelings. A learner may say something different, communicate with sign/AAC/home language, decline, or choose not to describe how they feel. No expression is required to receive help. Never decide a person's emotions from their face.

PK3 real source action

Across available meaningful examples, recognize and express a range of emotions. Personal disclosure is optional; describe fictional expression honestly. One icon or adult echo does not prove a range.

PK4 real source action

Give a possible explanation of how a feeling may connect to an action, without requiring multiple alternatives as an extra test.

02 · Two original stories — different actions can fit

Nia's garden paper

Nia finishes a big paper garden and says, “I feel pleased.” Nia can invite a friend to see it. Nia could also enjoy looking at it quietly alone.

What feeling did Nia actually say?
What might Nia choose? Could another choice also make sense?

Sol's reading place

Sol says, “This area is busy, and I want some quiet.” Sol moves to an available reading table. Sol could also ask a trusted adult to help find a quiet seat.

What did Sol actually report, rather than what we guess?
How might Sol's wish for quiet relate to the action?
Do not teach “pleased means invite” or “quiet means sad” as automatic rules. Many safe behavior choices are possible. These characters are fictional, not real partners or proof of the child's private feelings. A child's own meaningful role expression can be recorded as fictional expression; merely hearing a character's words or watching an adult puppet is not child expression.

03 · Story conversation and honest uncertainty

Use for an optional EXIT. Familiar material is valid when the child contributes meaning rather than copying an adult answer. Children's answers may differ meaningfully.

Leo's large poster

Leo is not sure where a large paper poster belongs. Leo asks a trusted educator, “Can you help me find the safe place for it?”

What might Leo be feeling? What clues actually tell us?
How might that feeling influence asking for help?
Could a child feel differently and still ask for help?

Teacher-only alternate interpretations

Uncertainty is allowed. A learner may say, “We cannot know for sure; we could ask Leo.” Reward careful reasoning, not certainty or a guessed face label. An actual child's internal feeling is private.
