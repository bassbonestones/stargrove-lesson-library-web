Facilitator guide

Exploring Feelings and Actions · original teaching resource

Facilitator: genuine voluntary feeling expression

Use one adult with one child, or one teacher supervising ordinary visible art/reading. The complete operative steps are in the lesson; no truncated historical excerpt is instruction.

Prepare

One familiar book or one paper and broad age-safe crayons per child, ordinary communication aids, visible comfortable pause space. No second adult, purchased printable, digital tool or forced disclosure. Optional first-party aids teach vocabulary; confidential adult notes are optional. Review the setting's safeguarding and supervision procedures.

ENTRY, MODEL and DURING: begin and respond

Offer book, drawing, continuing their activity, or declining. Listen first. Model one truthful mild adult feeling or openly fictional character, with two possible actions spoken by the same educator. Join meaningful practice for two to five flexible minutes. A request for help receives help, never a test first. If a child corrects an adult's interpretation, accept it. If there is no emotional communication, record not observed rather than diagnosing.

PK3: notice genuine recognition AND expression across varied emotional meanings. Welcome partial evidence without pretending one feeling proves a range. Personal disclosure is optional. Story/role expression must be labeled accurately rather than falsely treated as the child's private state.

PK4: notice a beginning meaningful feeling–behavior connection through speech, sign, gesture, home language or AAC. No mandatory alternative enumeration, formal causal sentence, novel context or occasional-only-support cap. Distinguish a child's meaningful contribution from adult-supplied content regardless of support duration.

Correct without coercion

“Quiet means sad” needs a gentle alternative: quiet can accompany enjoyment, uncertainty or wanting space; ask rather than diagnose. Validate emotions while blocking harmful actions. Do not induce upset for range evidence. Natural distress means stop instruction and provide care. Follow safeguarding procedures for concerning disclosures; keep identifying notes private.

Finish and revisit

Familiar activity is fine for EXIT. Listen for the child's meaning rather than demanding an unfamiliar task. Record context, action, actual help, partial or unclear evidence and an optional next opportunity. A same-day response is not later retention. REVISIT remains NOT_OBSERVED until a later actual date; use familiar materials if appropriate. Stop or defer at refusal, fatigue, distress or supervision needs.

One teacher with a class

During ordinary art/reading in the teacher's sight, give a short fictional model without inviting public disclosures. Let children keep working or pass. Listen briefly to one or two willing children while scanning the group. Rotate over normal routines, not everyone serially while others wait. No collective product, chorus or peer answer counts for another learner. If the room needs you, put notes aside. No unsupervised quiet corner or second adult assumption.

Access and valid alternatives

Maintain the child's communication, sensory and physical supports throughout. Describe visual story content; offer tactile or spoken alternatives; allow processing time and familiar routines. Home language and culturally different expression are valid. A neutral adult-assisted communication movement can preserve child meaning, but an adult choosing the feeling cannot. Avoid compulsory eye contact, facial enactment or English vocabulary. A declined autobiographical question is neither a skill failure nor a barrier to fictional learning.

Boundaries

The detailed source quotes PK3.I.B.2.a and PK4.I.B.2.a with original publisher concordance. The outcomes concern emotions and connections, not emotional obedience. SOLO is NOT IMPLEMENTED; no field outcomes, specialist approval, physical printing or autonomous software effectiveness claimed. Full candidate review and family consistency still pending.

Four delivery routes

ADULT-LED uses the one-adult procedure above. CO-PLAY follows genuine shared activity without a test script. CLASSROOM uses supervised normal activity and individual evidence as described above. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future software cannot infer feelings and is not required for these human teaching paths.
