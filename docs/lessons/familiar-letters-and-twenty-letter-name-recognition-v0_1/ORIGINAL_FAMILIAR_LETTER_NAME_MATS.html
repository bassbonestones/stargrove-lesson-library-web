Letters that matter: adult preparation

Choose a familiar initial, book letter or label with the child. No name disclosure is required. Plain hand-lettered paper is an equally good alternative.

Use the next pages flexibly

Page 2 has every uppercase letter; page 3 has every lowercase letter. Choose the case the child knows. Cover unused letters or copy one large letter. No cutting is necessary. Keep letters upright, clear and comfortable to see.

Offer a model and meaningful play. For an uncued observation, do not say the answer first or move a pointer through alphabet order. Familiar previously taught letters and the same card are valid; immediate echoes are supported practice.

Different developmental aims

PK3: offer alphabet-song/play and notice some frequent-letter recognition; record each separately. Participation is voluntary. One initial does not establish the whole source outcome.

PK4: extend toward twenty different recognized-and-named identities, upper OR lower case. A/a count once. Twenty is not a one-session quota. A sound is useful knowledge but is not automatically a letter name.

Accept verifiable home-language, signed or AAC names. Keep access help and child agency. Stop comfortably. No worksheet, device or public score is needed.

2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines III.C.1. Written resource, not observed child results or physical print validation.
