Objective-level educator overview: familiar letters

This uncounted family overview distinguishes the source objective from three historical lesson mechanisms. PK3.III.C.1 concerns alphabet songs and recognition of some frequent letters; PK4.III.C.1 concerns recognizing and naming at least twenty letters, upper OR lower case. See DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md for exact original 2022 TEA page 36 cells and the flexible progression.

Use Lesson 01's own facilitator guide for the current personally meaningful initial/letter candidate. Its two website routes are one adult with one child and one teacher with a supervised class. Lesson 02's environmental hunt and Lesson 03's varied-letter transfer remain historically authored siblings, not qualified by DOT25. Their historical novelty language must not override the source: familiar taught letters, either case and accessible communication are legitimate; changed material is optional diagnostic transfer, not compulsory credit.

ENTRY invites and notices; MODEL teaches responsively, including the chosen letter when useful; DURING supports purposeful practice; EXIT separates a willing uncued response from immediate copied answers; REVISIT is genuinely later, same or different context, NOT_OBSERVED until real. Keep observed child response, access assistance, answer cue and uncertainty separate. Do not use conflicting historical H/U/A/X codes for decisions. PK3 components must be noted separately, and one initial does not prove every source component. PK4 count different recognized-and-named identities once, not A/a twice; partial repertoires are useful progress, not failure of a brief lesson.

ADULT-LED and CO-PLAY need one adult, meaningful print and ordinary large paper choices. CLASSROOM uses one supervised group, meaningful exploration for all and feasible focal observations, never a chorus as individual evidence. SOLO is NOT IMPLEMENTED. Printed galleries have both cases; use a few glyphs or handwriting instead of a compulsory array. The private observation sheets do not belong in a shared student-data repository. Home language, familiar AAC/sign, quiet/large print and safe tactile routes preserve access; specify the actual modality and never infer visual recognition from a spoken answer supplied by an adult. No actual child, external educator, physical print or efficacy validation is claimed.
