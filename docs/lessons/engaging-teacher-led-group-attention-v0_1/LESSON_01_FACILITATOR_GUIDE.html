Engaging Teacher-Led Group Attention · original teaching resource

Facilitator companion: shared object noticing

Read the complete lesson. PK3.I.B.3.b concerns engaging teacher-led group attention up to 10–15 minutes at a time; PK4.I.B.3.b up to 20 minutes. These are not compulsory timing quotas. This lesson develops meaningful attention, not posture, naming accuracy or novel answers.

Prepare and invite

One educator, two safe large intact objects, accessible shared space, communication/sensory supports, a supervised quiet option and private notes are sufficient. No second adult, child worksheet or unmodeled object is required. Inspect choking/allergy/edge risks. Keep every learner supervised. If care cannot safely coexist with the activity, pause it. For one adult with one child, teach joint noticing but mark the official GROUP outcome NOT_OBSERVED.

ENTRY, MODEL and DURING

Let learners choose which object to investigate. Ask “What do you notice?” They may watch, touch, point, sign or speak in their preferred language. If helpful, briefly model your own observation, then return to their interests; MODEL is optional. Do not assign your action to a child. Respond to what learners attend to, shorten talk, bring content closer, wait for processing and adapt challenge. Moving, silent listening and familiar material can all accompany genuine attention. Unexpected naming is not an attention error. Offer help and breaks without shame; nobody leaves supervision alone.

EXIT and decisions

Close naturally with an optional further-noticing invitation, using the same objects if useful. Observe a manageable number of individuals without losing oversight; rotate focal observations later. Document exact topic-linked actions, support, access and uncertainty. Record observed intervals and interruptions only if actually known; never substitute scheduled duration. No visible response does not prove inattention. Timing unknown does not erase qualitative engagement. A brief episode is partial evidence, not mastery or a diagnosis. Repeated modeled words alone do not prove sustained attention, but neither do they invalidate genuine engagement observed in context. Record adult help instead of automatically rejecting it.

REVISIT and boundaries

Return later to familiar or different interesting content. Distinguish repeated familiar engagement from transfer, and both from unobserved future learning. REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED until it occurs. Keep identified records private and out of repositories. Teacher-ready reader and classroom guides provide practical routes; neither promises observed effectiveness. SOLO NOT IMPLEMENTED; CO-PLAY and ADULT-LED dyads remain related practice, CLASSROOM offers actual group context. Source, safety and privacy rules apply across all paths.
