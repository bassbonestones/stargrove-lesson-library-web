1 · Shared object noticing: start here

Optional adult aid. One educator can lead a supervised class. One adult with one child can use the same noticing activity, but that is related practice, not real-group evidence.

Prepare

Two large intact washable safe objects, such as a soft ball and broad cloth; usual communication/sensory supports; an accessible shared view. Check allergies, edges, loose parts and mouthing hazards. No worksheet or special purchase required.

Invite

“Which shall we explore? You can watch, touch, point or tell us something.” Let child interests shape the exchange. Offer a brief adult noticing example only if useful.

Respond

“You noticed a soft part. Would you like to show us?” Wait, shorten adult talk, bring content closer or allow movement. Quiet listening does not need a public performance. A naming mistake is not an attention failure.

Written teaching aid only. No observed outcomes, physical-print usability or field effectiveness verified.

2 · Access and supervision

One teacher keeps everyone safe

Lead the whole supervised group instead of unsupervised stations. Offer an in-view quiet place and safe sitting/standing choices. Maintain oversight while helping a learner. Pause when care needs prevent safe supervision. Follow established supported transition arrangements; no preschooler leaves alone.

Let attention look different

Home language, sign/AAC, tactile access, hearing supports, processing time and meaningful movement can support attention. Do not demand eye contact, stillness, correct names or original spoken answers.

Keep it worthwhile

Follow children's interests. End before fatigue or distress. Water, toilet, food, comfort and breaks are never earned by minutes. Revisit a favorite object later; novelty is optional.

3 · Interpret observations carefully

Developmental context

PK3.I.B.3.b: engaging teacher-led group attention up to 10–15 minutes at a time. PK4.I.B.3.b: up to 20 minutes. These are not compulsory minimums or ceiling failures. A short episode is partial evidence, not mastery.

Notice individuals without losing oversight

Focus on one or two learners and rotate later. Note exact engagement, access and help. Keep known intervals separate from assumed continuous attention; include interruptions. When timing is unknown, keep qualitative evidence and say so.

Familiar is legitimate

Using the same material, repeating a word or accepting support does not erase real attention. Adult action alone, silent posture alone or a class timetable cannot prove individual sustained engagement. Return later with familiar or different content; transfer is a separate claim.
