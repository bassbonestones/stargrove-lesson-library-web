Capacity: staged learning of usable space INSIDE objects and comparable holding ability

Official 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Measurement V.D.2: printed p.55/PDF index 54; illustrative Child Behaviors continue on printed p.56/PDF index 55. Research cells omit printed punctuation, preserved verbatim: PK3.V.D.2 “Child begins to recognize capacity based on how much space exists within an object” (SHA-256 af075d156cb7f89fb2adf8dc3868c173f7db3375755781c76423b1bb16ff9f56). PK4.V.D.2 “Child recognizes and compares capacity based on how much space exists within an object” (SHA-256 3b79f798a1971f8adbe5c11113650b2f73531846df9d7662735a9dfafd0b4717). Both are PRINTED_GUIDELINE_OUTCOME. The p.56 examples show comparing space, using measuring cups while filling, and identifying which container could hold more water; they do not create a PK3 count requirement or a PK4 ml/mathematical-volume mandate.

Target and non-target. Capacity is the usable room inside an object for holding material, not the amount already there, the surface area outside, the tallest rim, the weight of the vessel, or a conspicuous decorative pattern. PK3 begins to recognize this idea by pointing to, exploring, or showing interior holding space and an appropriate amount that can fit. PK4 additionally compares usable capacity of two accessible containers with correct empirical or unambiguous internal-space reasoning. Neither grade requires exact standardized measurements, numerical formulas, conservation of volume across vessel shapes, ordering every vessel, or flawless spatial estimates. Count of identical safe units is one instructional comparison method, not a state-mandated score. A container with thick walls has less usable space than a similarly sized thin-wall one; the inner cavity, not outer silhouette, is the reference.

Safety and reliability setup. Use two or more clean, dry, stable, light, large-mouth, rounded rigid containers with no lids accessible to the child, sharp rims, loose parts, dangerous previous contents or accessible water. For a common-unit comparison choose adult-tested boxlike containers with interiors that accept identical oversized smooth blunt blocks (each reliably larger than a choking hazard; age- and local safety-screened) without forcing, stacking instability, wedging, compression, hidden voids or spilled excess. The unit must be identical in material and dimensions across vessels; at every demonstration fill to the same agreed upper INSIDE rim, in one consistent packing arrangement. Adults pre-check the specific pair gives an obvious reproducible inequality/equality in safe full units; if they do not, do not score a quantitative ranking. A supervised clear, sealed demonstration vessel may show a level without any child-accessible liquid; NEVER put loose water near unsupervised tablets or present classroom water-transfer tasks as automatic child-safe activities. Avoid dry rice, beads, beans, marbles, food fillers, aerosols or small loose counting items; avoid forced ingestion or food tasting. Clean/sanitize shared objects.

Provenance: For each child/date/grade/stage/method/source pair, record ENTRY first response before MODEL, actual materials and how they were validated, observed child action/explanation, whether adult supplied a dimension/answer, correctness, aid code U independent (unprompted idea), A child-origin with access-only motor/vision/AAC assistance, H teacher conceptual cue or demonstration on target, or X unclear. U/A can support a candidate independently demonstrated source action; H is supported practice only. No single vignette proves mastery. Repeated real-world observations and later educator judgement are pending. An adult pouring/filling and the child echoing the result does not constitute child-origin comparison. A print picture alone cannot validate a physical vessel's capacity.

Assessment across every stage: ENTRY before any teacher MODEL with unfamiliar safe objects (never an answer reveal); MODEL on DIFFERENT teacher-only items; DURING own object exploration with responsive assistance and unscored coaching; novel uncued mini EXIT using a separate new pair and no answer cue; actual REVISIT on a different calendar date with unseen real objects, recorded NOT_OBSERVED until done. If the first probe was hinted, record H and take a further genuinely uncued different-pair check rather than erasing original support. Switch to tactile, translated, AAC or child-directed proxy response without downgrading concept. One stage can span several playful sessions; do not demand whole sequence in a day.

PK3 path — emerging recognition, not a PK4 ranking exam

PK3 C3-1 — find the inside

ENTRY: hand child unfamiliar broad safe empty box and same-color solid broad block; ask “Show where something COULD go inside.” No pointing to a correct spot first. MODEL: on a different large open bowl and solid foam cube, teacher runs finger around inside and says, “This hollow room is inside. The solid piece does not have that room.” DURING: child feels/looks/points inside own box, places one child-safe large unit where it fits, or directs an access proxy; teacher asks “Where is the room?” and re-models with other items if child points to outer rim. False inference: child chooses taller item or decorated exterior; demonstrate on different plain objects and let child trace newly presented inner cavity. Fresh EXIT: unused low, wide container versus a solid display prop; learner identifies capacity-bearing inside, not merely largest-looking exterior. REVISIT: other safe dry vessels on future date; NOT_OBSERVED.

PK3 C3-2 — available room vs material already there

ENTRY: two otherwise identical safe open broad boxes, one empty, one partially filled with large safe blocks: “Which has room inside to hold things? Does the empty one have no inside?” MODEL: on another equal-size pair, remove safe adult-controlled filler and narrate, “Taking things out did not shrink the room inside.” DURING: child removes/replaces oversized units from their pair to see room remains. False inference: declares fuller box “has more room” because more objects are visible; model the unchanged cavity with a different box, then child shows room inside a new empty box. Fresh EXIT: new container left partly filled, child shows/indicates usable INSIDE space rather than reporting only current contents; do not demand conservation language. REVISIT: new differently occupied container on later actual day; NOT_OBSERVED.

PK3 C3-3 — room may differ without a height-only rule

ENTRY: new short broad open container and tall slender container with a clear view of their interiors, ask what inside might hold; do not require perfect winner. MODEL: use different pair of broad open containers with visibly differing cavities; “I cannot tell just by the tall outside. I look at the room inside.” DURING: child explores inner walls/base and places a few identical oversized safe pieces if practical. Adult notices whether learner attends to inside and gently contrasts height with cavity, without imposing PK4 numerical comparison. False inference: confidently picks tall narrow container solely due to outer height; tutor looks inside different low wide versus tall slim pair, then unprompted fresh interior identification. Fresh EXIT: original different empty low/wide versus tall/slim safe items; child independently indicates capacity is about internal space, even if does not rank correctly. REVISIT: new container pair another date; NOT_OBSERVED.

PK3 C3-4 — everyday generalization of “can hold”

ENTRY: unfamiliar safe wide shallow bin and solid decorative foam model: “Which could hold these big safe play pieces? Show how you know.” MODEL: different adult-only carton and solid block, “A container has room inside; I can put something there.” DURING: learner puts/directs large safe pieces in an open bin, removes them and traces room left; asks own question or tests a new object. Misconception: points to the most colorful object without any demonstrated inner space; teacher contrast with differently colored novel hollow/solid items then child gets own alternate pair. Complete unmodeled PK3 source EXIT: two new solid/hollow and partially filled/empty container contexts; child shows emerging recognition of available inside holding space via act, gesture, signing, home language or AAC. Correctly comparing two exact capacities is NOT required to credit the PK3 source action. Later REVISIT: genuinely new date, new safe materials and independent first response, currently NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 path — recognize AND compare total capacities

PK4 C4-1 — retrieve inner-space distinction

ENTRY: fresh empty open broad box and partly full same-size companion: child shows what room exists INSIDE each before teaching. MODEL: distinct vessel, removing contents without changing the underlying cavity. DURING: child explains/points why current contents are not total capacity; return to PK3 C3-1/C3-2 if not ready. False inference: more visible cubes means “bigger inside”; adult contrasts other same-size containers, then independent new. Fresh EXIT: new full/empty SAME-capacity container pair; child indicates internal holding space equality even if contents differ. REVISIT: later new partly full/empty pair, NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 C4-2 — fair common-unit capacity comparison

ENTRY: two new stable large boxes with contrasting interiors; ask “Which can hold more of the SAME large blocks when filled to the inside top? How could we check?” Record prediction and child's proposed check separately. MODEL: teacher uses another verified pair of regular boxes, identical oversized blocks, fixed orientation and the SAME inside-top stopping rule; says, “Same blocks, each fitted in the same way, no gaps I hide. This one took more, so its inside has room for more of these.” DURING: child selects/directs consistent block type, transfers/rechecks carefully, states/points to capacity finding; avoid interpreting a few incompletely placed blocks as total capacity. Misconception: mixes large and tiny filler pieces and compares raw counts, or stops one vessel halfway; adult explicitly repairs with DIFFERENT boxes, common unit, identical packing and full-to-rim, then new independent check. Fresh EXIT: new physically verified pair and identical units, child independently completes/directs a fair test and meaningful more/less/equal comparison. REVISIT: actual future day with unpracticed vessels, NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 C4-3 — capacity is not height or width alone

ENTRY: unused tall narrow and short broad large-mouth safe containers whose capacity ranking is preverified using the SAME units or transfer; no winner visible or adult prompt. Ask for a prediction and a verification plan. MODEL: different teacher-only deceptive pair; compare the inner room with controlled same units rather than outer rim height. DURING: child tests own pair; adult distinguishes “taller” and “holds more” with gentle error-specific feedback. Misconception: child picks tallest by appearance but later repeats corrected winner from teacher: do not credit echoed result; probe a genuinely unfamiliar counterbalanced pair with positions/colors swapped. Fresh EXIT: new short-wide versus tall-slim verified pair; child independently chooses tests and compares holding space with evidence. REVISIT: unfamiliar other deceptive pair later date; NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 C4-4 — capacity stays conceptually separate from current fill level

ENTRY: a half-filled preverified large box and a full smaller box; ask “Which is holding more right now? Which has room for more if both were empty?” Do not assume those answers are the same. MODEL: another separate identical-capacity empty/full pair, then another verified unequal-capacity pair, narrating “How much is here NOW is not the most it CAN hold.” DURING: child empties/directs emptying and re-fills to same valid top boundary with consistent large units, records two different answers as appropriate. Misconception: says the full small box has greater capacity simply because it looks fuller; demonstrate with different paired originals, independently test an unmodeled half/full counterexample. Fresh EXIT: new verified half-large versus full-small case, independently distinguishes amount-now from internal possible holding capacity and verifies comparison. REVISIT: real later date, NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 C4-5 — independently plan and verify a novel capacity comparison

ENTRY: unmodeled safe pair without teacher specifying tall/large/wide winner, child describes/acts a fair way to compare actual inside holding space and suggests more/less/same. MODEL: only after first response and on different verified containers, teacher demonstrates suitable identical-unit or consistent transfer check. DURING: child tries an alternative pair and corrects if necessary; no numeric threshold demanded, no requirement to use measuring cups rather than a valid equal-size unit or transfer. Complete PK4 unmodeled EXIT: another pair with deliberately conflicting outer height/width or current contents; learner independently identifies inside usable room and correctly compares total capacity using credible common-unit complete fill/transfer or a uniquely unambiguous controlled internal-space observation. Store first-origin evidence, materials, verification basis and confounds. One correct visual guess without demonstrated relation is not enough to confidently infer conceptual comparison. REVISIT: later real date with unpracticed vessels and a first uncued source-action probe; currently NOT_OBSERVED.

Mechanisms, materials and evidence boundaries

  1. Inside-space discovery / tactile occupancy teaches that capacity belongs to interior holding room and separates hollow from solid, empty from nonexistent.
  2. Fixed-unit transfer / fair evidence tests actual capacity with equivalent oversized physical units, common fill boundary, repeatability and honest no-result when materials do not afford a fair comparison.
  3. Prediction and counterexample detective provokes false beliefs based on height, width, color, current fullness and repairs by child-proposed controlled verification; no answer cues.

Four complete WRITTEN routes (educational ambition comparable, effectiveness UNVALIDATED)

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future prereader-operated tablet with audio and robust no-text controls. PK3 narrates a separate example of hollow interior; child sweeps/drops broad virtual pieces into empty real-scale schematic cavities, reveals cross-sectional inside/outside, then independently answers NEW uncued trials (including an empty container). PK4 retrieves PK3, then drags identical visibly sized virtual blocks into two verified grid-aligned simulated box cavities with fixed inner rim and auto-detects incomplete/mixed fillings; an optional narration asks the child which can hold more, regardless of actual current fill. Prediction detective presents unfamiliar short-wide vs tall-slim / half-big vs full-small. Adult narration does NOT disclose EXIT answer; switch scanning, gaze, AAC, sensory settings, audio and accessible navigation must be implementable. Simulation can test represented space only, not physical vessel generalization; physical transfer remains not observed. Later software must implement source-first records, offline persistence, protected child privacy, accessibility testing and real alternate-day revisit, not merely a replay button.

CO-PLAY: caregiver lays out screen-safe broad dry real containers and identical very large clean nonchoking units, asks ENTRY before a separate example, child controls which container to explore, physically fills/directs adult proxy, chooses capacity finding. PK3 focuses “inside, even when empty,” PK4 moves to same-unit full comparisons and independent deceptive pair. Caregiver may steady heavy vessels only at child's direction (A), never choose answer (H). Child and adult reflect on surprising results together, and adult returns with new vessels on another calendar day.

ADULT-LED one child: educator selects calibrated real material pairs, gets distinct ENTRY, MODELS on teacher-only containers, checks child DURING and new stage EXIT; learner can use home language, AAC, signing, gaze or tactile exploration. For PK3 avoid escalating a partially emerging idea into an unsupported numerical capacity ranking. For PK4 distinguish full vs half-full and tall vs truly higher-capacity; adult intentionally abstains from saying winner until response recorded. Schedule a real delayed revisit, currently NOT_OBSERVED.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: adult gathers whole group to watch an entirely separate teacher-only container example (no choral answers as individual evidence), then two supervised stations: large hollow/solid explorations for PK3, physically verified common-unit container test and appearance-prediction for PK4. Each child independently selects/directs a different verified new trial as observer records actual first-origin response; group discovery is teaching, not individual source EXIT. Rotation materials are sanitized and limited so crowding, choking items, spills and unsupervised liquids are avoided. Several sessions and alternate supervised stations acceptable.

Access / individualization: high-contrast and large-type originals, adult-safe tactile rim and cavity maps, translation and home-language/cultural object choices, signed/AAC button labels, audio description, child-chosen motor proxy, sensory and visual breaks; extra time not penalty. Consult actual family and IEP/IFSP/Section 504 teams and qualified specialists where appropriate. Do not infer a developmental disability from a single inaccurate judgment. Keep original object materials culture-neutral, first-party and optional; never require tasting food or water play. Log U independent, H teacher conceptual cue, A child-origin access only, X unclear. No printed picture is a substitute for verified physical comparison.

Retrospective graded-stage learning-differences teaching and probes — 2026-11-01

PK3: Child BEGINS TO RECOGNIZE capacity from how MUCH SPACE EXISTS WITHIN an object, including distinction between available inside room and currently visible contents.

Construct boundary: Correctly identifying taller outside vessel, reading volume labels, counting items ALREADY INSIDE or mandatory accurate greater-capacity ranking cannot be imposed as PK3 source evidence.

PK3 C3-1: Access and scaffold: Let child see large open bowl versus safe sealed solid block, access real interior, and choose which has actual space inside; fade adult 'look here'. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Fresh empty safe pencil cup: child finds available room INSIDE and distinguishes from solid similar-looking object. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK3 C3-2: Access and scaffold: Show identical safe container partly filled versus empty and ask about what room remains; teach whole capacity doesn't change with current fill. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Fresh two otherwise equal safe cups, one with fixed nonloose filler: child recognizes cup itself still has same total inside capacity. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK3 C3-3: Access and scaffold: Explore tall thin safe tube versus low wide bowl, talk about interior as more than outer rim height, avoid compulsory exact ordering. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: New wide short bin vs tall narrow cup: learner recognizes actual interior holding space may differ despite which reaches higher. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK3 C3-4: Access and scaffold: Move from cups to safe backpack pocket and large pencil holder, child chooses accessible real inside space without measuring marks. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Fresh classroom container: learner identifies where usable room INSIDE exists and how contents might fit; no required formal rank. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK4: Child RECOGNIZES AND COMPARES CAPACITY based on how much INSIDE holding space exists in objects, controlling for actual equal-unit evidence if filling, not confusing exterior size or current contents.

Construct boundary: Tallest rim, current liquid level, unequal units, pretending a visual drawing is actual measured capacity, or adult premeasured answer does not show valid PK4 capacity comparison.

PK4 C4-1: Access and scaffold: Retrace PK3 difference between true inside space and present contents using safe open vessels, learner inspects rather than adult decides. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Fresh otherwise identical empty and partly occupied cups: child explains same total holding space, different currently available room. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK4 C4-2: Access and scaffold: Use appropriate safe equal-sized large units or controlled transfer in two REAL vessels, child selects fair comparison procedure and keeps unit size fixed. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: New short broad and tall narrow containers with actually tested common-safe units: child compares true capacity rather than height. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK4 C4-3: Access and scaffold: Offer two containers whose external rim-height is misleading, child verifies with real equal-unit controlled method where safe. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Fresh tall skinny and shorter wider vessels: learner rejects height-only 'tall holds more' and chooses actual interior comparison with evidence. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK4 C4-4: Access and scaffold: Use one real container at empty and partially filled states, separate total capacity from currently present amount and room left. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Novel same-size cups one half full with safe fixed filler: learner recognizes equal capacity despite differing visible fill level. Adult model/echo or inaccessible input must be distinguished from not-yet-observed skill.

PK4 C4-5: Access and scaffold: Present genuinely unfamiliar safe everyday containers and child-planned comparison without adult preselecting wider/shorter answer, vary access mode. Fade: remove answer previews, not access to language/AAC/motor/vision/hearing. Novel child-owned source evidence: Fresh two safe real storage containers: learner independently verifies which can hold more using fair real interior evidence; later actual revisit NOT_OBSERVED.
