Teach Finding the Space Inside Things

Plain-language teacher and family guide. Children should investigate the usable inside of real safe objects, not guess capacity from how tall the outside looks.

What the child is learning

A container has room inside that can hold something. A solid object may look big but have no open space inside to receive a block.

For a 3-year-old, beginning to notice the interior space is important. For a 4-year-old, the child also learns to compare how much actual usable space is inside different containers. A high outside wall alone does not establish which can hold more.

Prepare two safe kinds of objects

Gather broad, blunt, stable hollow containers, a similarly sized solid piece, and large safe blocks that fit comfortably without choking or sharp edges. Have separate, unfamiliar containers ready for the child's later check. An accessible child-directed adult mover is okay if the child owns the placement choice. Dry materials are enough; you don't need to pour water or use heavy objects.

Five gentle steps

1. Ask where a block could go

Present a previously unseen hollow carton and similarly large solid object. Put no block inside yet.

Say: “Where is there space for this big block to go? Can you show me?”

Observe the first attempt without pointing into the hollow one for the child. If reaching is difficult, let the child direct your hand.

2. Demonstrate using other objects

With a different open basket and solid item, move a large block inside the basket. Then remove it and show the same cavity remains.

Say: “The basket has room between its sides, even when it's empty. This solid block doesn't have that kind of open space inside.”

Your model is separate from the child's original or later unknown pair.

3. Let the child explore an unfamiliar container

Offer a different safe hollow object.

Say: “Where is the room inside? What could fit there? Is the space still there when we take the block out?”

Invite the child to place and remove a safe block or direct an adult's hand. For a 4-year-old ready to compare, choose two actual interiors and see which holds more under a meaningful common comparison, not just from outside height.

4. Give a fresh independent opportunity

Bring out new, unfamiliar safe hollow and solid objects or two unlike real containers.

For younger learners: “Which of these has room inside for something? Show me where.”

For older learners: “Which has more room inside? How could you check?”

Allow an accessible real exploration; don't supply the answer. Record whether the child recognized the hollow space or genuinely compared interiors. Guessing from external decoration alone isn't adequate comparison evidence.

5. Revisit with new safe objects another day

Offer different usable containers and a separate opportunity. Do not invent evidence of a later visit from a planned task.

When a child is unsure

What happens Try
Child points to the tallest outside wall Show a different tall solid shape and a shorter hollow container
Child thinks an empty basket has no space Remove a large block and explore the cavity that remains
Child cannot reach or look inside Allow tactile inspection, accessible sightline or child-directed hand placement
Child notices the hollow interior but can't compare two capacities Preserve the younger skill and model a fair comparison separately
Two containers seem the same from outside Compare their actual usable interiors instead of surface appearance

Teach Finding the Space Inside Things with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

A container has room inside that can hold something. A solid object may look big but have no open space inside to receive a block.

For a 3-year-old, beginning to notice the interior space is important. For a 4-year-old, the child also learns to compare how much actual usable space is inside different containers. A high outside wall alone does not establish which can hold more.

Prepare the classroom

Provide a variety of large safe hollow and solid objects and familiar pieces that might go inside them. Check openings for hand-trap hazards; avoid choking-size fillers, sharp edges and liquids unless separately safely planned. Arrange a separate educator demonstration with different objects.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “Could something go inside this object, or would it only sit on top or beside it?” Invite a first attempt to locate an actual opening, then demonstrate on a different known container what interior space means.

Give children meaningful practice

At stations, children explore real containers and solid objects, deciding which have usable space within. Partners may compare discoveries, but each focal learner should have a real opportunity to point to or use an opening instead of repeating the class's answer. Teacher moves between a few safely prepared stations.

Observe individual learning fairly

Give an unfamiliar object and ask a focal child to indicate whether it has space inside and how they know. Observing the child actually locate or use an interior is stronger than hearing them recite 'hollow.' Record whether another adult placed the piece inside for them or only helped with access.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Offer large pieces, tactile explorations, directed adult manipulation or other access routes; never force a hand into a container. Distinguish a lid, surface depression or drawn picture from an actual usable interior. If material safety is uncertain, use a safe replacement and defer that example.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

Method 01 — Inside-Space Discovery Laboratory (hollow-space / occupancy mechanism)

Original first-party written E2.2 teacher guide, 2026-10-30. Read DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md before selecting stages. Published PK3.V.D.2: begins to recognize capacity from space inside object (SHA af075d156cb7f89fb2adf8dc3868c173f7db3375755781c76423b1bb16ff9f56). Published PK4.V.D.2: recognizes and compares capacity from space inside (SHA 3b79f798a1971f8adbe5c11113650b2f73531846df9d7662735a9dfafd0b4717). This method first constructs the referent of the word capacity: a usable hollow interior. It uses visual/tactile exploration, guided placement/removal and child explanation, not a vocabulary drill or a disguised counting lesson. PK4 must additionally make an independently fair comparison; hollow recognition alone is insufficient.

Safety + two disjoint teaching sets

Teacher brings clean open rounded stable large plastic baskets or cartons that can safely accept oversized nonchoking blunt nontoxic play blocks, plus matching-color solid foam models with no hollow center. Both must be stable without a lid, sharp edge or reachable liquid. Prepare 3 different sets: ENTRY/probe novel A pair, teacher MODEL B pair, and independent EXIT C pair, with visibly different colors, shapes and distribution so child cannot recall answer position. For PK4, the teacher prepares a real pair whose actual usable inside space has an evidenced, stable comparative relation independent of the child's answer. Identical oversized blocks are a fair total-capacity proxy only for suitable regularly shaped containers that can both be comparably and fully packed up to the same defined interior boundary without materially different unfilled gaps, blocked openings or orientation effects. If blocks fit differently because of packing inefficiency, do not call the larger count greater total capacity; that only demonstrates how many of those objects happen to fit. An equally legitimate accessible child-origin nonnumeric comparison grounded in a prevalidated real interior relation is allowed. If the source pair or check cannot meaningfully establish which actual usable interior holds more (or whether equal), mark the total-capacity comparison NOT_ASSESSABLE and choose a better verified real pair for future teaching; never supply the child a secret answer. The printed ORIGINAL_INSIDE_SPACE_MATS.html illustrates spaces but cannot itself prove volume. Do not use rice/beans/beads, loose drinking water, food or unstable stacked items. Children may communicate by point, placement, sign, words, AAC or deliberate adult-directed action.

Recording sheet: date, child code, source action, materials and actual internal capacity validation, stage, first ENTRY response before model, separately MODEL/DURING prompt, new uncued EXIT act, U independent / A child-origin access-only / H adult conceptual hint / X unclear, revisit actual future-date outcome left NOT_OBSERVED. Teacher must not award complete PK4 comparison credit from merely naming a bowl or placing one block.

First teach it — deliberate, teachable exchanges

ENTRY before MODEL: Present a hollow opaque plain broad carton next to a similarly large solid safe model. Place nothing in either. Ask, “Where is space for a big block to go? Show me.” Say nothing else until first action. Write exactly whether child points inside, touches rim only, points at tall exterior, tries placement or says unclear. If the child cannot reach, ask them to direct the adult hand, not ask “This one, right?” If child does not yet grasp hollow/solid, stay at PK3 C3-1 even when working with older child; access questions do not determine readiness.

MODEL on different objects: Take a low rounded basket and solid chunky prop (not used for ENTRY or EXIT). Teacher says, “I can put this large safe piece inside the basket. Watch my hand go down between these walls. This solid piece does not have an empty inside to hold it. This empty basket still has that space.” Remove piece slowly; keep showing the empty cavity. Do not state which independently scored item is correct.

DURING supported practice: Learner selects a novel broad container and feels or points to base and inner walls, places one large block wholly inside or directs a proxy, removes it and shows same inside cavity. Ask “Where did it fit? Where is the room now?” If child says “no room” when empty, on a different teacher-only container remove one block and ask child to point to unchanged inner walls; return learner's different pair with no hint.

Fresh independent EXIT: unseen safe plain solid model and open bowl. “Show which has room inside for this piece. How can you tell?” A child-origin purposeful placement/gesture or meaningful description of holding space, not imitation, supports PK3 emerging action. A guess based solely on largest exterior is not adequate. Independently record first answer before validation.

PK3 staged directions — specific novice teaching ladder

PK3 C3-1 — find the cavity. Show a large empty box and solid block in ENTRY. On unrelated materials teacher models touching inside boundaries. DURING child moves hand/safe big object into a separate open container and directs placement. New EXIT is a low hollow bin plus a tall solid model, child shows available space; no compare requirement. If child taps outside, teacher says on another object “This is outside; watch how this hand goes in,” then retests with truly different pair.

PK3 C3-2 — empty still has capacity. ENTRY: a previously unseen partially filled bin versus same-size empty bin. Ask whether both have inside room. MODEL uses different equal baskets and removes a large safe piece: “I took it out, but the walls did not move.” DURING child empties/replaces own oversized toy, traces the interior even empty. EXIT: another unused empty basket asks child to indicate room, not count its current contents. Do not credit verbal imitation “empty” without showing inner holding space.

PK3 C3-3 — attend to interior, not container height. ENTRY: tall narrow versus low wide safe model, ask child to explore where holding room is, not require a correct comparative capacity rank. MODEL an unrelated exaggerated pair, tracing inner walls while saying “Outside height is not the same thing as the space inside.” DURING child touches or looks into cavities, tries nonchoking oversized insert if fits. EXIT: new high outside/solid item and low/hollow item, child recognizes which could actually hold; if child still chooses tall, reteach C3-1 and C3-2 first.

PK3 C3-4 — everyday transfer. ENTRY new clean dry play basket and solid mock package. MODEL on new teacher-owned examples how a real cavity accepts large toys. DURING child finds a container use in a pretend supply station. EXIT two unfamiliar real objects, including an empty one, child independently demonstrates available inside space in varied context. REVISIT on a later real date, not the same class session; NOT_OBSERVED until actual source response recorded.

PK4 staged directions — interior knowledge into authentic comparison

PK4 C4-1 — retrieve source precursor. ENTRY equal-size empty versus partly occupied vessels. MODEL separate pair to show equal cavities despite contents, DURING independently show boundary unchanged, EXIT new identical empty/full vessel pair and the child distinguishes current contents versus possible total. Teacher can briefly return to PK3 practice when necessary; this alone does not achieve the PK4 compare verb.

PK4 C4-2 — introduce verified pair. ENTRY different large stable regular, comparably packable box cavities whose actual usable inside capacity has a defensible prechecked relation with fully filling common large units, or another real source-verifiable fair relation. Ask what would provide evidence beyond appearance. MODEL teacher uses DIFFERENT two regular boxes filling to common inside rim with identical safe giant blocks. DURING learner controls their verified pair and demonstrates one held more (or equal if actually equal); record child-origin meaning. EXIT unpracticed pair independently compared by child with a fair child-selected complete common-unit no-gap method where actually valid, or another legitimate independent interior-based comparable evidence; merely inserting more big blocks in a poorly packed unlike-shaped cavity is not enough. If only says bigger-colored box, retest after model with unseen pair; do not overcredit.

PK4 C4-3 — conflicting outer heights. ENTRY unmodeled squat broad and tall slim large safe boxes; let learner describe where usable room is. MODEL different deceptive containers, then independent child explores actual inner room and uses only a verified genuinely fair comparable filler method (not arbitrary chunky blocks leaving misleading gaps), or child-origin safe direct interior reasoning rather than guessing. DURING child can revise prediction after evidence. EXIT fresh height-conflicting pair must show correct interior-based comparison; mere noticing hollow items is insufficient for PK4.

PK4 C4-4 — misleading fill levels. ENTRY same-capacity containers, one full and one partly filled: “If both were empty, could they hold the same amount?” MODEL unrelated pair; DURING child empties own vessels then fills to same safe inside line and checks. EXIT new half-filled larger and fully filled smaller verified pair; child explains why possible total capacity may reverse how full it currently seems. Return to PK3 C3-2 if empty is interpreted as zero capacity.

PK4 C4-5 — novel source EXIT and REVISIT. After practice, select new visually different but empirically verified container pair, do not point toward wider/taller “correct” side. Ask learner to determine which can hold more and show reliable evidence; another valid independent check is acceptable. Record U/A/H/X and actual first child action, not simply correct answer. REVISIT other date with different vessels, NOT_OBSERVED.

False-positive, repair, and remaining limitations

False-positive child: Learner identifies bright tallest object, or pronounces “space” after adult points to empty bin; child never shows an interior-based response. Why false: exterior choice, adult origin or guessed speech is not the published PK3 action. Correction: different teacher-model plain hollow/solid pair, child explores walls/bottom, then uncued new bowl vs solid model where correct answer swaps side. Another: PK4 child only identifies the hollow bin, never compares two total capacities; add separate complete common-unit independent comparison, NOT automatic promotion from PK3. Limitation: visual and tactile access may depend on props; single contrived container success, even correctly recorded U/A, is a candidate not real mastery.
