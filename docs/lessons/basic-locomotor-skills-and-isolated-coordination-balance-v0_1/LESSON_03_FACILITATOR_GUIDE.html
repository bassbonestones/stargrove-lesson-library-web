Facilitator Guide 03 — Child-Generated Genuine Pedal Motion and Separate PK4 Isolation

Companion to: Lesson03 — The Gentle Garden Wheel and graded family progression. This is an educator-only companion, not a fourth lesson or observed child source.

PK3.IX.A.1: “Child masters basic skills of running, jumping, climbing, and pedaling.” Frozen original research SHA ef6c5a3d57bd1aa826cfb4555317667f5c27813b83adeeeaeb989c41f4d6bed7. Here the distinctive third method supplies actual PEDALING, not all four separate skill assessment opportunities. Safe child-generated running and ground jumping come from Method01, rated low climbing from Method02. “Masters” across all four requires genuine qualitative adequate actual skill observations across safe occasions and competent judgment; a single pedal attempt cannot establish entire PK3 published outcome.

PK4.IX.A.1: “Child demonstrates coordination and balance in isolation.” SHA 3971da87c203d5f0d34bf72a2a1e662532b01ca209be17635983baa6bfa50222. A separate real small child-initiated safe isolated body action with genuine balance/recovery (including appropriate safety support) is assessed, not presumed from riding or treated as an IX.A.2 sequence to complete a task.

Materials and preflight — inspect actual equipment, not an illustration

A responsible adult truly verifies selected mobile or stationary child-rated pedal apparatus matches learner age/size/weight and permits comfortable seat and pedal reach through real cycle without strain. Confirm frame/stationary base/wheels fully stable, moving pedal/crank mechanism sound, brakes as applicable, no exposed chain/pinch or entrapment hazards, footwear/clothing and required protective equipment per actual device and local rules. For a mobile tricycle, select enclosed clear flat dry traffic-free area with genuinely safe turn/stop distance, doors/roads/crowds absent. For a stationary adapted pedal trainer, prevent tipping/rolling and make sure hand/foot/body positioning is stable with no exposed crank hazard. Confirm child-specific mobility/vision/hearing/sensory needs, guardian proximity, staff coverage of others and safe immediate stop. No learner uses equipment before the real safety gate. If absent or ill-fitting, leave actual PEDALING NOT_OBSERVED and offer appropriate safe preparatory movement without falsely claiming a source skill.

The adult does not push a passive learner's tricycle, let wheels spin via downhill/motor or manually move feet while scoring child motor contribution. Safety spotting and qualified frame stabilization can remain A where child truly drives crank; adult creating decisive leg movement/vehicle propulsion is H. No removal of needed safety aid solely to label U.

Five practical observable learning phases

ENTRY: If actual equipment safe, child may voluntarily sit/position and try to personally rotate actual pedals. Observe what learner really does, including alternating leg drive, safe personal reaction, only one exploratory motion or declining. Watching wheel move while adult pushes is not direct pedaling. PK4 independently offers a different stable-base self-generated safe simple isolated adjustment/recovery rather than vehicle motion credit.

MODEL: Adult safely demonstrates related alternating pedal/crank motion using adult-rated stable apparatus if available, or a safe ground-level actual alternating leg-pattern demonstration on appropriately stable adult footing, showing personal force and stop. Never overload a child's frame with an adult; don't model only an unrelated stationary reach. Adult demonstration teaches pattern and doesn't prove learner source. PK4 separately models an isolated gentle body-controlled shift and recovery at a different stable safe place.

DURING: With live guardian and actual fitted stable equipment, child may choose to make crank turn slowly through actual own alternating force. Educator attends to crank movement caused by child legs, not incidental rotation while adult pushes or a powered motor runs. Provide neutral support or accessible safe orientation if necessary without moving the child's legs for them. Record authentic attempt versus age-appropriate basic adequate child-driven rotation, and allow choice/rest/stop; do not invent a required revolution count, distance, speed or racing goal. PK4 in a separate supported/secure stable position actually self-generates one mild isolated coordinated movement and recovers balance, not inferred from ride.

EXIT: Offer the same safe familiar fitted device again or another independently safe option only if appropriate; first-source validity does not require changing device or track. Ask neutrally whether learner wants to turn pedals. Record true self-generated repeated enough meaningful qualitative motor action for educator's prudent candidate judgment; a passive spin or instructor-driven rotation must not count. PK3 full four skills remain independent and not required same day; PK4 independent safe discrete child-controlled isolated motor action on different stable base where actual and qualified. If declined/unsafe, NOT_OBSERVED.

REVISIT: An educator may safely offer a later genuinely different calendar-day real pedaling opportunity with suitable equipment. At authoring, REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED; do not invent retention or implemented results.

Valid and misleading motor observations

A child who personally drives a fitted stable stationary crank via alternating coordinated force can show actual pedaling without traveling down a path, whereas a tricycle whose wheels turn because an adult pushes does not demonstrate child pedaling. A child moving legs without engaging a crank may learn a precursor pattern but has not shown actual pedaling. One genuine skill record is not all-four PK3 mastery. A child who safely begins to move pedals then rests can be assessed as emerging or a basic candidate according to actual quality, not a fixed “three turns equals mastered” rule. A child with neutral adapted pedal supports can retain personally authored motor action. Distinguish actual child leg-drive from a parent physically rotating a learner's feet with each revolution.

PK4 self-initiated isolated coordination and active balance recovery can be demonstrated with appropriately secure child-supported seated/standing stance, a small reachable body action or safe gentle step, without a moving tricycle as proof, a stopwatch or unsupported one-leg hold. The phrase in isolation separates a genuine discrete action from a compound task route, not from any gentle dynamic movement.

U genuine child-owned pedal force/body coordination with ordinary supervision; A neutral accessible stabilization or protection while child genuinely produces the target movement; H adult or machine actually moves the decisive pedal/body action; X unclear. Real observer captures actual physical provenance rather than checkbox inference, and no synthetic test automatically establishes developmental competence.

Responsive feedback, safety and options

Invite: “Would you like to help the gentle garden wheel turn, or watch?” Celebrate the child’s genuinely controlled action, not the frame’s travel distance. A child may use home-language, sign or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) to ask for help or stop. Learner can pause and rest, with privacy and no competition. If a device cannot fit a child securely or a parent must generate every turn, no pedaling source is attributed; offer safe separate leg-control preparation if suitable. Respect mobility and sensory accessibility, and qualified staff decide whether a particular supported pedaling action preserves real learner force, not universal unqualified assumptions.

CLASSROOM: actual staff supervise all other pupils while only feasible focal children use suited fitted safe stationary/mobile pedal devices in protected uncrowded zones. No cross-moving trikes, unsupervised equipment or assumption that attending class means true individual pedaling; rotate/defer, NOT_OBSERVED for missing chances.

Seven access pathways and limitations

For attention/executive, short individual invites and rest; language/communication, accessible safe start/stop instructions in home language/sign/AAC; literacy, no required written response; numeracy, no counted rotations or speeds as cutoff; sensory needs, calm predictable equipment choice and no surprise wheel motion; developmental support, related safe actual adult example with gradually fewer motor directives but full safety protection; mobility/hearing/vision, qualified fitted stationary adaptive device, stable safety support and sensory cues preserving personally generated leg drive. Alternate instruction remains valuable without granting motor equivalence to absent actual pedal rotation.

False credit: adult pushes tricycle, crank rotates because of motor, an adult guides every limb move, child touches a paper spinning wheel, completed bike drawing is scored as pedaling, or one skill is called four. False rejection: safely fitted stable stationary device with actual child-driven crank, familiar safe setup rather than new cycle, proportionate needed protective spot, or missing apparatus interpreted as learner inability.
