Teach Safe Movement and Balance

Practical guide for a trusted adult supervising genuine, age-appropriate physical movement. No speed, distance, balance-time or athletic ranking is part of this lesson.

What the child is learning

For a 3-year-old, offer a safe opportunity to practice an actual child-generated run and a separate gentle ground-level jump. These are only two parts of a broader skill area that also includes climbing and pedaling elsewhere; do not claim that one movement lesson demonstrates all four.

For a 4-year-old, the focus is a controlled, personally initiated movement and safe balance or recovery, such as a gentle shift, reach or small stable step. The child may use an appropriate seated or supported position. Neither age group must win a race, jump high or hold a pose for a fixed number of seconds.

Safety comes before assessment

Clear a dry, flat, stable, accessible area with plenty of room and simple broad floor markers. The markers are places to approach, not obstacles to jump over. Check footwear, mobility/support needs, surrounding hazards and whether a trusted adult can supervise every child who moves. Keep any individualized safety plan and appropriate spotting support. Do not physically lift a child through a jump or propel them and call it their performance. Children may watch, rest, decline or choose a safer alternative without shame.

Movement invitation

1. Ask what movement feels comfortable

Say: “Would you like to move toward that safe spot? How would you like to do it?”

For a younger learner, observe a natural comfortable run opportunity and, separately, a possible tiny ground-level jump; it is fine to see a walk or an attempt instead. For an older learner, invite a comfortable small adjustment, reach or weight shift on a stable base. Notice actual child-generated motion, not only words or enthusiastic participation.

2. Show a safe example yourself

At another open space, the adult demonstrates a gentle controlled run and modest in-place ground-level jump only when safe to demonstrate. For the older learner's goal, demonstrate a slow stable reach/shift and return instead.

Say: “I decide how to start, move and return safely. I can stop whenever I need.”

The adult's actions are teaching examples, not child evidence.

3. Invite a child-chosen practice

Let the child choose a safe marker or action.

For running: “Would you like to move across this clear space and then slow down?”

For jumping: “If it's comfortable, would you like to try a little jump straight up from the ground and land safely?”

For older children's coordination and balance: “Would you like to try a gentle reach, step or body shift, then settle comfortably?”

Do these separately rather than making a difficult obstacle course. Describe what you actually saw without saying a child “ran” if they walked or “jumped” if they simply stepped.

4. Offer another safe unprompted opportunity

After practice, see which actions the child chooses or can initiate without you physically making them happen. Record running and jumping separately for the younger child and isolated coordination/balance for the older child. Neutral necessary safety support can remain; note who supplied the actual movement.

If a child declines, is unsafe to attempt, or needs a break, stop. A movement that did not occur is not observed, not a failure of the child.

5. Return on another real day

Offer a different comfortable, safe opportunity later. Do not invent observed retention or progress from a future plan.

Respond without pressure

What you see Appropriate next step
A child walks instead of runs Accept meaningful movement; record walking, not running
A child steps over a mark instead of leaving the ground Respect the choice; don't label it a completed jump
A child moves with neutral spotting or assistive support Describe their own motor contribution and the safety assistance separately
A child uses a different comfortable seated or standing adjustment Assess actual child-directed coordination and balance at the relevant level
A child is anxious, fatigued or uncomfortable End the attempt; never push for a higher jump or longer balance

Teach Safe Movement and Balance with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

For a 3-year-old, offer a safe opportunity to practice an actual child-generated run and a separate gentle ground-level jump. These are only two parts of a broader skill area that also includes climbing and pedaling elsewhere; do not claim that one movement lesson demonstrates all four.

For a 4-year-old, the focus is a controlled, personally initiated movement and safe balance or recovery, such as a gentle shift, reach or small stable step. The child may use an appropriate seated or supported position. Neither age group must win a race, jump high or hold a pose for a fixed number of seconds.

Prepare the classroom

Inspect real age-suitable clear movement space and safe running, jumping and balancing opportunities, including any needed accessible supports. Set boundaries that separate active movement from waiting areas and avoid simultaneous hazardous stations without adequate staff. Genuine body movement—not cartoon imitation—is the target.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “Which safe way would you like to move today? Where could you stop and hold your body still?” Demonstrate a gentle unrelated adult movement and explain boundaries; children may observe or choose a comfortable start without a race.

Give children meaningful practice

With the staff actually present, children take turns at suitably supervised real-movement spaces while classmates remain genuinely safe and engaged. Do not assume a single teacher can help a jumping child and independently watch twenty other moving children. Offer short optional intervals and rotate focus learners across actual later opportunities.

Observe individual learning fairly

Observe the specific learner action witnessed—such as actual running, a safe jump or a distinct stable balance attempt—rather than scoring a whole motor-skills family from one happy dance. Grade-stage progression and particular locomotor skill claims remain separate. A teacher's physical guidance, the child's own movement and an adult lifting a child are different evidence.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Offer appropriate seated/assistive pathways for meaningful movement and safely defer unavailable components. Never rank height, speed or daring, or require a risky feat to 'pass.' If a skill isn't attempted or seen, keep it unobserved; neither substitute a video nor treat equipment absence as child deficiency.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

Lesson 01 — The Safe Movement Meadow: Real Run, Ground Jump and Isolated Balance

Learning context: a preschooler chooses whether and how to move between two nearby, clearly marked safe ground-level “meadow” spots. The marks may be broad nontoxic mats or a pair of contrasting large safe floor markers—never obstacles to leap over. The objective is genuine basic movement, not reaching a prize or completing a timed course. Children may watch, rest or choose a supported movement alternative without stigma.

Published 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Physical Development IX.A.1 (frozen source): - PK3.IX.A.1: “Child masters basic skills of running, jumping, climbing, and pedaling.” Registered exact full-cell SHA-256 ef6c5a3d57bd1aa826cfb4555317667f5c27813b83adeeeaeb989c41f4d6bed7. - PK4.IX.A.1: “Child demonstrates coordination and balance in isolation.” Registered exact full-cell SHA-256 3971da87c203d5f0d34bf72a2a1e662532b01ca209be17635983baa6bfa50222.

Source boundary: This Method 01 gives meaningful PK3 RUNNING and separately JUMPING opportunities; climbing and pedaling are distinct actual skills in the family’s Methods 02 and 03, not required as same-day Method 01 exit stations. Only actual documented basic competence across all four distinct safe activities can support a cautious whole-PK3-source judgment; neither four written plans nor four boxes marked once prove observed mastery. Missing opportunities are NOT_OBSERVED, not evidence of child inability. For PK4 a real child-generated isolated balance AND coordination action is observed separately; an errand-type multi-step movement sequence (IX.A.2) or adult positioning the child does not establish IX.A.1.

Why this method matters

Children have a bodily reason to notice their own movement: “Which safe meadow spot would you like to move toward? Would you like to feel one gentle jump from where you are?” A meaningful real short run on clear level ground and a separate child-controlled ground-level jump let the learner explore propulsion, rhythm, landing and choice without races, speed or height performance demands. PK4 can choose a single gentle “find my steady place” isolated coordinated motion—for example a small within-reach movement and return to stable base, controlled mild weight shift, or safe lateral step-and-settle—without making isolated balance into a timed unsupported one-leg stance. Participation alternatives remain worthwhile instructional engagement, even if a specific source movement is not witnessed.

Safety and accessibility preflight — educator must make the real-world decision

The trusted adult physically checks the actual space before any proposed movement: level dry nonslippery surface, no sharp corners, traffic, door crossings, obstacles, clutter or nearby children intersecting the path; footwear and clothing suitable to the specific child and activity; large flat markers securely placed so they cannot slip or trip; comfortable distance and no raised takeoff or landing. The educator chooses actual protection/proximity appropriate to the learner and setting, including qualified or specialist-established accommodations where applicable. Stop for discomfort, fatigue, loss of safe balance, unsafe surfaces, distress or a child’s request; do not retry merely to complete a receipt. Never require leaping off furniture, climbing to heights or speed trials in this activity. The adult actively safeguards the one focal mover and all other children have genuinely available supervision.

If an individualized mobility plan calls for steady spotting, mobility aids or safe hand support, retain those safety supports; document who generated the movement. A child voluntarily propelling a comfortable action with neutral safety support may produce bounded relevant physical evidence; an adult moving, lifting or propelling a passive child does not establish child-run, child-jump or isolated child-controlled balance. Adapted nonmatching movement can be educationally meaningful without being falsely relabeled “mastered jumping.” Do not penalize the learner for a task that cannot safely be offered, and never withhold safety support to manufacture an “independent” performance.

Five observable learning phases

ENTRY — before adult modeling. Offer a freely chosen safe level-ground movement invitation and separately an optional tiny ground-level jump. Watch what the child genuinely does, not what the teacher says: for running, purposeful child-generated alternating locomotor propulsion with recognizable change from walking at an age-appropriate comfortable pace; for jumping, a child-initiated takeoff/brief airborne movement and controlled age-appropriate ground return, allowing individualized specialist-informed movement variants to be described honestly rather than mechanically rejected. If only a walk or attempted lift is witnessed, record that exact evidence instead of attributing a completed skill. PK4 entry independently offers a single low-risk isolated safe coordinated adjustment and stable recovery, potentially with appropriate neutral support. Observe actual child-control and context, not test speed, height or unsupported time.

MODEL — educator-owned related physical example in a different safe spot. A trusted adult models an actual safe brief running pattern and a modest in-place ground-level jump on their own clear line where age/development allows, pointing out gentle start, slowing, safe return and choice to stop. This directly explains the relevant PK3 motor pattern instead of substituting only an unrelated weight shift. For the PK4 route the adult instead shows a gentle individually safe one-action weight shift, reachable arm movement or step-and-settle with recovery on a different secure base. Demonstrations are invitations, not a model child must copy exactly, and the educator’s body action is never evidence of the learner’s motor skill.

DURING — supported, responsive meaningful practice. Ask which available safe spot/motion the child prefers; keep each skill a separate simple episode. PK3 run: invite an actually child-generated modest run across clear short level space, then slow down; a walk or adult-propelled pass remains useful exploration but not confirmed running. PK3 jump: return to safe stable ground, invite child-owned small level-ground jump and age-appropriate landing; a step-over or simulated icon remains practice/other skill, not jumping. Avoid coercive repeat attempts or fixed athletic thresholds. Provide simple feedback anchored to observation: “You chose to make your feet go faster,” or “I saw you bend and return to the ground,” without praising a skill not actually shown. PK4: offer only one comfortably controlled isolated postural adjustment/reach/side-step and return, allowing safe individualized stance or seated base and required steadying support; observe the child’s coordination and active equilibrium recovery separately from adult holding the entire body up. This is not a multi-step errand sequence.

EXIT — genuinely observed child action, familiarity allowed. Offer a calm authentic second opportunity on the same familiar safe level area or an equally safe nearby alternative, without verbal/motor propulsion prompts dictating the movement. An actual child-generated run and actual ground-level jump can be individually recorded as Method 01 source-relevant skill evidence; other two PK3 locomotor skills stay explicitly NOT_OBSERVED for this method until safely demonstrated elsewhere, so no automatic entire PK3 “masters all four” determination. A true different safe location, partner or simple variation probes stronger generalization if performed but is not a published prerequisite. For PK4, observe a single child-initiated controlled safe isolated coordinated adjustment and balance/recovery on a stable appropriate base, with assistance type specified; changing to an unrelated multi-step task isn't necessary. If an unsafe/declined occasion prevents performance, label NOT_OBSERVED, not failed.

REVISIT — genuinely later calendar date. A trusted adult may offer the child a new comfortable safe opportunity for one or both motor actions and for PK4 isolated coordination/balance. At authoring, this actual later-day performance remains NOT_OBSERVED; written future activity is not observed retention or demonstrated classroom effectiveness.

Observable evidence and honest judgments

PK3 running: child, not a pushed prop/adult, visibly produces the running movement pattern with personal propulsion, adapted appropriately to the learner. An energetic walk, a tiny animated runner, a single lifted foot or a teacher moving the learner cannot automatically count. PK3 jumping: a genuine self-generated basic ground-level jump/takeoff and safe landing pattern, not stepping over a floor mark, an adult lifting the child's torso or a video. No minimum speed, measured distance, height, repeated jump count or race. Record authentic attempts separately from demonstrably adequate basic skill, with “INCONCLUSIVE/DEVELOPING” when the practitioner cannot yet justify competence. PK3 full IX.A.1: separate genuine adequate basic skill observations for running, jumping, climbing and pedaling, over appropriately available safe opportunities and by competent educators, not this single plan’s written narrative or an automatic checkbox. Missing skill opportunities remain NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 isolated coordination AND balance: child initiates a safe simple discrete controlled adjustment/shift/reach/step, organizes their own segments/body and stabilizes or recovers equilibrium with documented appropriate access and spotting. An isolated motion can be gently dynamic—not necessarily an absolutely still unsupported stance. Passive adult-supported placement, a solely dynamic multi-stop errand or inference from fast running are not sufficient. The child may perform seated, standing or using a warranted assistive base where meaningful personal motor control and original isolated construct are preserved; a specialist/educator evaluates honest comparability. No arbitrary one-leg hold, balancing seconds, performance race or complete independence without needed protection.

Record support provenance for each actually witnessed skill: U child-generated motor action with ordinary safe supervision, A neutral accessibility or safety support while the child genuinely directs/propels the relevant motion, H adult physically supplies the decisive target action/propulsion rather than the child, X unclear. Distinguish safe adult spotting from adult-driven movement; no diagnostic or high-stakes score can be inferred from these design descriptions. The illustrated original educator packets are not individual observed motor data.

Developmentally differentiated routes

PK3 G3-1: safe self-propelled modest run; G3-2: distinct ground-level self-generated jump; G3-3: appropriately rated low safe climb in Method 02 when available; G3-4: actual child-driven safe pedaling in Method 03 when available, with across-method cautious four-skill synthesis only on real observations. Any unoffered action remains NOT_OBSERVED, never “failed because no tricycle.”

PK4 B4-1: active stable supported or unsupported posture within appropriate base; B4-2: gentle personally controlled weight transfer/recovery; B4-3: isolated reachable arm/body adjustment and stable return; B4-4: optional another safe isolated simple movement/context preserving own motor control; B4-5: broader genuine isolated coordination/balance in a new safe context as higher-ceiling transfer, not an official first-source threshold.

Accessible learner choices: attention/executive needs one action and predictable rest; language/communication instructions may be home-language, signed or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) without a required spoken response; literacy and numeracy are not tested; autism/sensory needs predictable calm options; developmental supports can offer gradual adult-owned demonstrations with real child motor choice; mobility/vision/hearing support may include personalized safe floor orientation, reachable markers, qualified spotting, stable assistive stance or seating. Retain protections and meaningful participation, but never claim absent running/jumping merely because the learner engaged by a different accessible action.

Four delivery modes — WRITTEN, not demonstrated equal

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. Future narration or independent child choice may suggest safe adult-supervised movement, never authorize unsupervised jumping/climbing/pedaling or measure real motor ability from a cartoon. A working accessible SOLO route and real outcomes are unverified.

CO-PLAY. A trusted caregiver first inspects a real safe environment, provides appropriate physical protection, invites the child’s own short chosen movement, records only genuine personally generated run/jump or isolated balance action with support provenance, and stops on fatigue or request. A caregiver pushing a child is not a child-propelled motor result.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD. Educator checks actual child needs and physical safety, observes ENTRY, models the relevant movement on their own different line, offers supervised meaningful practice, then records a genuine child-owned EXIT with partial/unknown distinctions; no safety support is removed just to produce a score.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM. Staff provide real all-room supervision and arrange at most a feasible small number of focal individually observed movements in a clear separated zone while others remain safely engaged or supervised. No simultaneous unguarded jump/climb/pedal stations or assumption that a chorus/single peer completes every child’s movement outcome. Rotate or defer; child with no witnessed opportunity remains NOT_OBSERVED.

False credit and adversarial integrity and limits

A teacher who records all four PK3 skills from two successful run/jump episodes, a cartoon movement video, an adult holding a child aloft or an unsupported unsafe climbing challenge falsely accepts. Conversely, a teacher who rejects a genuine safe familiar motor action solely for lack of a new setting, penalizes an appropriately spotted child, demands high jump or one-leg balance, or marks a child as unable because climbing equipment is unavailable falsely rejects legitimate learning. The method separately protects specific skill evidence, across-family complete-source synthesis, access-appropriate educational alternatives, and real safety.

Facilitator Guide 01 — Safe Running, Ground-Level Jumping and Isolated Body Balance

Companion to: Lesson 01 — The Safe Movement Meadow; whole-family educator overview; age-stage progression. This guide is an educator-facing resource, not a fourth lesson and not a record of a real child.

Printed source and exact inference scope

PK3.IX.A.1: “Child masters basic skills of running, jumping, climbing, and pedaling.” Frozen publisher-source cell SHA-256 ef6c5a3d57bd1aa826cfb4555317667f5c27813b83adeeeaeb989c41f4d6bed7. Method 01 directly teaches/observes actual child-generated basic RUNNING and JUMPING; climbing and pedaling are distinct developmentally appropriate family methods. Do not make Method01 child attempt unsafe apparatus just to complete the published four-skill list. An authentic single run is bounded skill evidence, not proof of “masters all four.” Full source requires sound genuine separate motor observations and qualitative competent educational judgment for all four, not mere four booleans or one attempt.

PK4.IX.A.1: “Child demonstrates coordination and balance in isolation.” Frozen cell SHA-256 3971da87c203d5f0d34bf72a2a1e662532b01ca209be17635983baa6bfa50222. Offer a separate single safe isolated controlled body movement plus genuine child-generated balance/recovery, not a whole sequence to perform a task (IX.A.2). “Isolation” does not mean a forced unsupported static one-leg test; a gentle controlled supported-to-independent adjustment, stable seated active reach or mild lateral step-and-settle may be legitimate with specialist-informed access and actual child control.

Prepare a genuinely safe, inviting choice

An educator physically checks a dry, clear, nonslip, level space in a protected area with suitable footwear, safe marker placement, no elevated takeoff/landing, collisions, furniture/traffic, or crowd running across another learner. Decide appropriate closeness, fall-prevention and individualized mobility/sensory/hearing/vision access with competent staff and family/specialist guidance as needed. Use two broad flat stable “meadow” spots or large contrast/tactile floor indicators as choices, not as a timed race or thing to clear. Confirm the entire room is supervised and there is a workable focal safe movement zone. Offer rest, watching or alternative adaptive participation without public deficit labels. If no safe movement option, stop, leave the applicable observed skill NOT_OBSERVED and seek qualified alternative planning; don't lower the physical safety standard.

Three quick teacher checks: (1) Can the child actually initiate motion without a trusted adult generating movement for them? (2) Will the support prevent hazards without hiding who propels/rebalances? (3) Could the child or adult safely stop immediately? The level-ground run/jump are not reasons to stage climb/pedal apparatus today.

Complete five-phase educator sequence

ENTRY: Before example, ask neutrally “Would you like to move toward either safe spot?” and separately “Would you like a small jump from safe ground, or to watch?” Record precisely how the child moves, including a genuine recognizable personally generated run, age-appropriate jump takeoff/return, safe walk, emerging attempt or no current observation. On PK4 path, invite a single comfortable isolated safe posture/weight shift/reach/step and watch child-produced coordination and balance, including neutral support. Neither an offered choice nor adult-made movement counts as learner achievement.

MODEL on educator's own different clear line: For PK3, show a safe brief actual run with recognizable gentle rhythmic progressing steps and a real small in-place ground-level jump, plus safe slowing/resting. This is relevant motor modeling, not an unrelated generic still pose. Never instruct any child to copy an adult athletic height/pace. For PK4, show a single small self-directed body movement and safe recovery on educator's different stable spot. A teacher's MODEL demonstrates an idea but provides no child-source evidence.

DURING, individualized choice and feedback: Allow the child to choose a safe movement and number of comfortable invitations. For PK3 keep running and jumping separate: actual self-propelled run (not just fast walking), then rest, and later actual in-place ground jump (not stepping across a mark or being lifted by adult). Support safety, signal safe stop, and offer descriptive feedback on what was truly observed. Use familiar safe materials rather than a spatial puzzle; no speed/distance/height/time targets. For PK4 isolate one controlled action at a time, for example a comfortable small shift followed by child's balance recovery, with age/access-suitable protected base. The adult may spot or stabilize an accessible aid without moving the child through the target. If adult physically produces the decisive motion, that attempt is aided practice H, not child-generated motor demonstration. If a learner actively directs and produces motion while safety spotting remains, annotate A and the actual level of child control instead of either auto-pass or automatic exclusion.

EXIT, individual and honest: Offer the same familiar safe ground or another equally safe verified place, with a neutral invitation rather than precise step-by-step physical movement cues. Record each witnessed skill independently. PK3 RUNNING observed ≠ JUMPING observed; both observed ≠ climbing or pedaling observed; complete four-skill mastery cannot be inferred. PK4 demands one actually self-organized isolated coordination and balance action, with real child-controlled recovery, not from running fast or adult-held posture. A safe familiar source is legitimate; changed safe situation can strengthen transfer if genuinely observed and is never obligatory printed criterion. Child decline/unavailable safety ≠ learner failure.

REVISIT: On a different real calendar day a capable educator may revisit actual low-risk age-appropriate motor actions, documenting witnessed movement and supports honestly. In these authored materials REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED; never copy future date into a fabricated mastery receipt.

Seven realistic access pathways

Attention/executive: one calm motor invitation at a time, visible safe start/stop, rest chosen by learner; no performance timer. Language/communication: accessible short home-language, sign or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) instructions and stop signal; motor skill itself remains physical, no required spoken evidence. Literacy/phonology: printed cue card is optional educator reference, never assessed decoding. Numeracy: no counting of laps, seconds or distance as a publisher cutscore. Sensory/autistic needs: predictable quiet movement, no spontaneous races, forced contact or unexpected change. Intellectual/developmental: graded adult demonstration and safe practice without physically doing the learner's motion, document emerging attempts and offers. Physical/motor/hearing/vision: individualized safe floor, tactile/contrast indicators, hearing accommodations, qualified adapted stable support or positioning as appropriate. Keep actual child motor contribution identifiable; adaptations need not be magically equivalent to omitted published actions.

Four complete written delivery routes

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future child-directed narration or animated cues may invite a choice only in a real adult-cleared supervised space; a virtual avatar cannot run or jump on behalf of the learner, and a child should not use elevated/play apparatus unattended. No working application, safety testing or equal outcomes exist.

CO-PLAY: trusted caregiver checks real safe environment, offers chosen noncompetitive level-ground run/jump, documents child source candidacy separately from actual whole-PK3 four-skill claim, and observes a distinct appropriate isolated PK4 movement when relevant. No pushed tricycle or adult-supported puppet counts.

ADULT-LED: trained educator selects actual accessible risks/needs, obtains learner assent, observes entry, directly models the relevant motor pattern on another safe spot, provides practice and fades content-bearing cues while never removing safety; separate real child exit before any tentative source inference.

CLASSROOM: only feasible small-group/focal real movements under actual staff supervision of all other pupils, no unguarded stations, safety collisions or public motor ranking. Do not label an unwitnessed child's ability deficient or claim all 24 simultaneously met four locomotor sources. Defer missing safe activities honestly.

Provenance and professional boundary

Write U where genuine motor control was personally generated with ordinary supervision, A where neutral child-directed accessible/fall-prevention help preserved motor authorship, H where an adult produced the scored movement/propulsion, X if uncertain. Real educator must decide whether supported attempt preserves sufficient target motor construct, not software automatically. Do not turn individual disability or inaccessible apparatus into a stigmatizing failure. Different source outcomes are not interchangeable: PK3 four locomotor basic skills, PK4 isolated coordination and balance, and IX.A.2 movement sequence to complete tasks remain distinct.
