Safety before engaging a child

A responsible competent adult must personally verify an actual current child-rated, properly secured/assembled, low, stable climbing structure whose real age, size, weight, condition and use limitations fit the learner, manufacturer/install guidance and actual local setting; safe nonslip contact areas and suitable protective landing/approach zone; no sharp edges, trapping gaps, loose connectors, slick rungs, crowd traffic or hard fall hazard. Determine required adult direct proximity, protection/spotting, staffing of other pupils, learner-specific access plan, safe exit/down route and stop protocol. Never improvise by stacking furniture or using unapproved ladders/blocks, elevate challenge height to earn a score, or rely on a printed illustration as if it guarantees apparatus fit. If safety cannot truly be established, do not offer climbing; engage in optional safe flat-ground hand-foot coordination practice and leave actual PK3 climbing NOT_OBSERVED. No field physical setup is inspected by authoring this guide.

A safe observed motor action includes actual child-produced bodily progression using coordinated hand/foot/weight transfer on suitable approved low apparatus, with child-selected pause, safe down/return and active adult protection. A child who places a hand on apparatus, climbs because adult lifts their torso, appears in a climbing animation or steps onto unstable furniture has not demonstrated genuine safe personally performed climbing.

Five teaching phases with legitimate alternatives

ENTRY: After the actual safety gate, invite child to see a low safe “lookout” only if freely willing. Before adult demonstrations observe the learner's actual hand-foot positioning, personally generated body movement, emerging attempts or a respectful stop. A child does not need to risk unfamiliar height just to yield diagnostic evidence. If no rated structure is available, show a harmless teacher-owned ground-level movement concept but record actual climb as NOT_OBSERVED. PK4 independently choose a comfortable safe isolated adjustment and self-generated equilibrium without inferring from climb.

MODEL, relevant and safe: An adult demonstrates a climbing-relevant grip/step/weight organization using adult-suitable stable safe ground-level support or other appropriately adult-rated approved context, not an adult walking on a child-rated structure beyond its capacities. Explain stopping, placing hands/feet only where safe and returning calmly. A stationary weight shift alone doesn't fully explain climbing: the related hand-foot position must be visible. The adult's own demonstration and a printed image are not a learner skill.

DURING: Let child choose a modest low section of actually suitable climbing structure and move body using personally controlled limbs, with qualified direct guarding. Adult supports physical safety/necessary adapted stable support but does not carry the child through scored movement. Describe exactly whether the learner genuinely transfers weight, advances and coordinates a safe movement; do not require a top height, particular number of rungs or timed completion. If child seems frightened or fatigued, accept stop; never compel another attempt. PK4 action is separate and at a stable appropriately adapted safe base: one child-owned small reach/shift/step followed by active balance recovery, with qualified neutral aid and no forced one-leg unsupported pose.

EXIT: If equipment remains safe and child willingly chooses, another genuine modest child-generated CLIMB on the same familiar vetted structure can provide a truthful motor candidate; changed equipment only strengthens transfer if independently safe and actually used. No mandatory run/jump/pedal task now. Record actual action, skill adequacy versus emerging attempt, safety, and assistance U/A/H/X. All other three PK3 components remain independently sourced and unobserved here unless genuinely witnessed in separate suitable settings. A real simple PK4 isolated movement and stable return is separately recorded, not assumed from climbing.

REVISIT: On a genuinely different calendar day, if safe suitable rated equipment and actual professional supervision exist, the child may choose another safe low climbing experience or discrete PK4 balance movement. At authoring, REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED, without invented retention or four-skill mastery.

Access, safety and equity specifics

An attention-sensitive child may prefer predictable one-step voluntary movement and rest. Home language/sign/AAC must make start/stop and danger information accessible; there is no required verbal motor response or reading. Do not impose numeracy rung count or competition. For autism/sensory needs prepare calm non-surprising access/retreat. Developmental supports can break down voluntary practice with teacher-owned related model but never physically manufacture purported child outcome. Motor/vision/hearing accommodation may include professionally determined protected footing, appropriate stable hand support, trusted guarding, tactile orientation, suitable hearing cues and genuinely child-origin progression; never claim an unrelated safe alternative automatically proves literal basic climbing. Qualified caregiver, educator or relevant specialist determines child-specific safe apparatus/protection. Stop promptly for pain, fear, loss of balance, unsafe crowding or actual pupil request.
