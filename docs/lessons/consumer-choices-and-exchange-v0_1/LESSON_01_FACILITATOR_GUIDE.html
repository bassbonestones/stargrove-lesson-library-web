Facilitator guide: Consumer Choice Studio

Companion to the complete lesson, not an additional lesson. Written-design candidate; no actual child observations.

Goal and fit

Texas 2022 PK4.VII.B.2: “Child demonstrates an understanding of what it means to be a consumer.” A consumer obtains or uses goods or services. The child chooses something useful for an art purpose and demonstrates who uses it and how. Buying, counting money, saying “consumer,” speaking English and distinguishing every good/service category are not required. PK3 participation is enrichment only, not an invented PK3 social-studies outcome. Choose a starting point from the child's experience, not age alone.

Prepare

For one child: two sheets of paper, two familiar safe washable drawing tools or an accessible equivalent, one large block or an oral building story, and a private observation slip. For a class, supply each participant or use the classroom's safe established sharing arrangement without engineered scarcity. Inspect size, caps, surfaces and materials for actual learners; no small toys, money, food or sharp tools needed. Use normal language, sign, augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), tactile or neutral child-directed motor supports. Do not require touch or public performance.

Printables are optional adult practice references containing instructional answers. They are not reserved assessment cards. Keep library examples and answer labels out of first-response checks. No printer or screen is required. The blanket/sign-repair stories can be reserved for optional transfer. Immediate repetition of a supplied or overheard relationship is helped practice; prior familiarity alone does not invalidate later child-owned understanding.

ENTRY: start with an idea

“This maker wants to make a picture. What could they use? Show me what they could do.” Offer paper/tools without naming a correct choice. Folding paper, drawing marks, or a different useful idea can be valid. Wait for the child's communication rhythm. A neutral “Show or tell me more” may clarify; an adult-selected choice is conceptual help. Record a first response only if actually seen; declining or inaccessible input is not inability.

MODEL: use a different context

Show a large block or speak this story: “I want a shelter for my toy. I borrow a block and use it to build. I am a consumer of the block. Someone provides it. I don't have to buy it.” No actual child must lend anything. Demonstrate purpose, getting and use, not a label to repeat. People may provide in one event and consume in another.

DURING: follow the child's project

“What would you like to make? What could you use?” Let the child choose and change their purpose, then use supplies or direct a proxy. Ask how the item helps without interrogating every action. A favorite brand or coin count alone does not show the target; acknowledge it and model purposeful use on another object. If a surprising choice serves the child's aim, accept it. If unclear, invite a demonstration. If frustrated, simplify or stop. Optional service: child requests help holding paper while retaining control of the work. Neutral access and conceptual help may both occur; record each. Fade hints, never needed access or safety.

EXIT: notice meaning; offer transfer only if useful

If the child already clearly shows consumer meaning in their own project, record that limited evidence and help without requiring another task. If useful and welcome, privately offer an optional new-context story: “Lee borrows a blanket from Jo to make a comfortable place for a toy to rest. What is Lee doing? What is Jo doing? How does the blanket help?” No real sleep setup or blanket needed. Accept enactment, sign, home language, AAC or child-directed proxy. The relationship may be clear without answering every question or saying the word consumer. A child showing Lee obtaining/using the blanket for a purpose gives limited relevant evidence; provider identification is useful additional information, not a mandatory separate cut score.

For an optional changed-context transfer check when the blanket relationship was just supplied, another fair context is: “Sam receives help from Ren fixing a torn pretend paper sign. What does Sam receive? What does Ren do?” If both relationships were supplied, do not claim fresh transfer. Familiar taught contexts can still show later genuine child-owned understanding; record exposure and actual help. No additional task is required. If you hint or model, mark supported practice, not independent transfer. If meaning is unclear, say so; do not guess competence or incompetence. A real child's conceptual reasoning about a fictional scenario can be evidence. This document itself contains no real child data.

CLASSROOM organization

One educator introduces and models while everyone stays in the normal supervised area. Children then make their own art, share ideas, or choose a familiar quiet alternative. During familiar play/art, invite a nearby willing child to a brief individual observation only when you can safely supervise everyone. Other children continue meaningful work; no unattended station or waiting line. If you cannot do both, teach and observe later. Track unobserved children privately across days. Immediate copying of a peer answer is not independent meaning. Later genuine understanding of a familiar case is allowed; reserve a new case only for new-context transfer. A chorus, volunteer or finished display never establishes everyone's understanding. Do not preserve a test by withholding needed teaching.

Record and respond

Record date, local identifier, scenario/prior exposure, exact action or message, neutral access, conceptual hints, uncertain interpretation, and the next teaching step. No uploads, photos or household finances. Describe independent limited demonstration, supported practice, partial/unclear evidence, or not yet observed. Goods or services may yield relevant evidence; no mandatory all-parts checklist. A nonspoken child-directed action can be enough when its meaning is clear. Copied terminology alone cannot.

REVISIT and boundaries

Stop for refusal, fatigue, distress, lost access or inadequate supervision. Return to a meaningful familiar or changed context on a genuinely different actual date; REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED until it happens. Retention and generalized mastery are not established by one session. Family sharing is optional, without purchases or private financial discussion.

ADULT-LED / CO-PLAY: one adult supports child-owned decisions. CLASSROOM: use the feasible organization above. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. No actual teaching trial, learning result, physical print verification, specialist approval or mode equivalence is claimed. The family teacher guide and developmental sequence give broader context; the exact example allocation in this lesson governs its checks.
