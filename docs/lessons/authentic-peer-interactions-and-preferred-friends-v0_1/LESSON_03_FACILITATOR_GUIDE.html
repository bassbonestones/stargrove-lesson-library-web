Lesson 03 companion facilitator guide — Friendship autonomy and inclusive boundaries

Pair: Lesson 03; source PK3.I.C.7 and PK4.I.C.7. Specific companion guide, not a counted lesson. See objective guide.

Why this approach adds distinct teaching

This is a consent, autonomy and inclusive access practice, not a friend-popularity poll. Children learn that choosing a preferred companion and welcoming other peers' equal dignity are compatible; other children may refuse, and relationships change. Correctly answering a fictional kindness scenario cannot itself establish authentic peer interaction or a preferred friend, though it can prepare ethical real action. PK3 preferred friend is optional; PK4 needs genuine child-owned preference and real peer interaction, with independent consent for each shared encounter; a one-time genuine preference expression may be relevant, while recurring friendship history is stronger and optional.

Before facilitation

Choose first-party neutral story illustrations of two children sharing art and one opting for quiet work, plus large safe duplicate creative materials and a genuine voluntary group option. Do not identify real classroom peers as rejected or popular; no “best friend” badges or forced invitation exercise. Ensure everyone has activity access and a quiet alternative, privacy and an individualized communication path for real consent.

Six adult phases and decisions

ENTRY: Observe first genuine free peer engagement, not an answer to “Who is your favorite?” MODEL: Consenting adults on separate safe ADULT materials demonstrate a present-day optional invitation and a kind “not right now”, then optionally talk about a HYPOTHETICAL different future choice; not real dated pupil preference or mandated child peer response. Neither adult becomes a child's observed peer. DURING: On fictional cards practice invitations, independent alternatives, consent and no punitive exclusion. Where a natural genuine peer context exists, learner may independently enact respectful sharing; adult does not script acceptance or pressure a selected classmate. FADING: Remove moral answer boards, public preference questions, adult-assigned group members and forced eye contact while retaining neutral sensory and communication access. EXIT: If an actual natural child–peer exchange arises, an independently chosen familiar real response can be source; an unmodeled different human context probes stronger transfer only when actually witnessed. An authentic learner invite–peer real no–learner acknowledgment is a brief human exchange, not continued play. PK3.I.C.7 does not require a preferred friend. PK4.I.C.7 distinguishes real peer interaction AND genuine private child-origin preferred-peer interest from stronger optional naturally recurring relationship history. No mandatory past dates, mirrored feelings or coercion. A fictional correct moral answer can show LOCAL consent understanding, not real peer source. If not actually observable, NOT_OBSERVED. REVISIT: On a different real calendar date, authentically freely chosen peer context, not a repeated skit.

Misconception-specific feedback

Child declares a peer must play: model right to decline on unrelated adults, then offer child alternative activity; no staged refusal. Child excludes another from a shared school resource: protect equal classroom access; inclusion does not force private friendship. Child is reluctant to name a best friend: don't require disclosure or penalize; real choices may be private. Adult supplies friendship phrase in AAC: count supported practice H, not independent U. Rehearsed imaginary reply: teaching only until actual peer opportunity.

Seven profiles and all modes

Attention/executive: short predictable consent alternatives and fading visuals; language/communication: personally authored sign/AAC/home-language consent/response; literacy: no written friend ballot; numeracy: no friendship quotas; autism/sensory: no forced social proximity, parallel/quiet options; developmental: concrete adult-only model of willing/declining choices; physical/motor/hearing/vision: accessible materials and individual team-guided unbiased access. No peer identity or private friendship data in the repo. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: fictional choices teach ethical concepts without real peer-source equivalence. CO-PLAY: adult rehearses boundaries but real willing peers required. ADULT-LED: one-child adult conversation not a peer interaction. CLASSROOM: teacher uses actual available staff to supervise all children, documenting only a feasible few personally witnessed focal child-peer interactions, while the others remain in supervised quiet/safe independent alternatives; defer/rotate and mark unobserved NOT_OBSERVED. One freely responding real peer is sufficient for a peer exchange. Equal access to teacher-provided classroom art/book station is ensured through duplicates or fair alternate access, but no individual child is compelled to like, touch or join another for private play. Real learning, external approval, printed-material accessibility, genuine delayed REVISIT and operational parity remain NOT VERIFIED.

2026-11-03 focused method-diversity calibration addendum

CALIBRATION: CONSENT_BOUNDARY_AND_NONCOERCIVE_INCLUSION_PRACTICE (Method 3)

Actual learning action: Learners explore how to express preferred companionship, respect another child's refusal, keep shared materials accessible and respond to naturally arising inclusive choices; REAL willing peer interaction remains the direct source requirement. A purely fictional correct-answer conversation is supplementary social/consent teaching and cannot be credited as real-human peer interaction or an existing PK4 preferred relationship.

Child-facing invitation and preparation: Use a harmless ADULT-OWNED imagined situation such as two adults wanting the same storybook to offer optional consent language. Let children propose several kind responses, including 'not now' or quiet separate play, by sign/AAC/action. Never engineer rejection, ask who is disliked, orchestrate real distress or require friendship with any classmate.

Mechanism-specific contingent teaching: If the learner repeats adult 'everyone must play together', demonstrate separately with willing adults that a peer may decline and materials still remain shareable; ask for alternative safe choices without a compulsory answer. If a natural real peer interaction actually occurs, watch for child-authored honoring of assent and boundaries; correct harmful behavior immediately, never withhold protection to collect assessment evidence.

Source-valid exit/evidence interpretation: Record fictional rehearsal as practice of a boundary concept only. Source credit needs genuine child-origin interaction with actual freely willing peers; for PK4 add separately evidenced real child-authored preferred-peer expression, with naturally repeated voluntary encounters adding stronger stable relationship confidence when actually documented. If no authentic boundary occasion arises, boundary application remains NOT_OBSERVED, not a failed child score; no contrived EXIT confrontation. Nonverbal refusal can be meaningful and must be respected; SOLO NOT IMPLEMENTED.

Adversarial positive/negative contrast: A child may say no to personal play yet still use a teacher-provided public art station. Distinguish real adult-managed equal material access from another child's unforced friendship consent. A real peer invitation–no–acknowledgment exchange is genuine human contact without continued shared play. A fictional avatar apology does not prove a real mutual relationship.

Cross-method non-equivalence and safety: Mechanism is ethical reciprocal CHOICE/CONSENT in real life with fictional practice as optional support. It is not a friendship popularity survey, a third identical 'select a friend' activity, or a requirement to engineer social conflict.

Honesty: This is a proposed pedagogical design clarification; not a child observation, qualified external educator assessment, validated implementation, or awarded score. The source-specific original developmental sequence remains required.
