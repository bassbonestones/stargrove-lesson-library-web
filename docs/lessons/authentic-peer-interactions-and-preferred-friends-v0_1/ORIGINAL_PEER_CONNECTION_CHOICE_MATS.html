01 · I can choose safe peers; everyone has choices

For optional age-safe classroom connection teaching. Never require public favorite-friend declarations or rank children.

Enjoy real peer activity

I can select an accessible big-paper or picture book activity with an actually willing child.

Ask and respect no

Another child may play with me, choose a different activity or say “not now.” They can decline.

Choose familiar peers freely

Sometimes I enjoy spending time with a particular willing friend. Sometimes I choose a new activity or different people.

Keep everyone included

Other learners must still have safe learning choices; no bullying, social ranks or compulsory best-friend badges.

PK3: meaningful interaction with REAL peers; *may* have preferred friends, NOT mandatory. PK4: PK4: real peer interaction AND genuine child-owned preference for real peer(s); natural repeated contexts strengthen stable friendship evidence but official source does not impose two dates or matched feelings. Peers may decline any shared play and all children retain dignity.

Accessible child-selected communication

Home-language speech, sign, AAC, meaningful nonverbal pointing/offer, safe motor proxy, individual quiet and sensory comfort are all supported. Never demand hugs, eye contact, fluent spoken invitation or disclosure about whom a child likes.

02 · Original gentle stories — not friendship evidence

Story 01 · A book on two tables

In a fictional story, Sam likes the big garden picture. Another child likes a wide paper path. They each can choose related or different materials and may show one another their ideas. Nobody has to choose a “best friend” before playing.

What choices do both children have?

Story 02 · Choosing an activity again

In a fictional story, Kim enjoys making a paper garden with Jo. Another day Jo wants to read alone and Kim chooses a different activity. Both can freely choose and still care about one another. A friend is not required to always join.

How can we honor a friend's “not now”?

Story 03 · Equal access for classmates

A fictional class has an optional art activity. Two children choose to work together. A third child can still safely use available original materials at an accessible open station. No friend ranking decides who may learn.

How can a classroom welcome learners without forcing friendships?
A fictional favorite character, repeated seat assignment or a friend icon is NOT a genuine real peer preference. Actual safe willing peers and meaningful reciprocal choices are required for source evidence.

03 · PK3/PK4 real developmental ladder and evidence

PK3 four stages — real peer interaction

F3-1 Real safe willing peer exchange. F3-2 Different peers can be enjoyed without favorite label. F3-3 Preferred peers MAY emerge through genuine choice. F3-4 Transfer meaningful interactions to unlike natural real peer contexts, without required best friend.

PK4 five stages — authentic preference

F4-1 Interact with actually willing familiar peers. F4-2 Self-select recurring shared interest. F4-3 Child-owned real preferred-person interest, with optional natural recurring evidence for stable friendships. F4-4 Protect friendship boundaries and nonexclusive classroom access. F4-5 Genuine real peer exchange, respectful refusal allowed; new context probes stronger transfer if witnessed.
