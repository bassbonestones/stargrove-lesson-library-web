Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 02 Duck Bridge Dialogic Information Exchange

Pair: Duck Bridge lesson plan, source PK3.III.D.3 and PK4.III.D.3; distinct objective educator guide. This is facilitation, not a plan counted as a fourth method.

Why role-switching is instruction, not just a second story

Real information-gap exchanges give the child a reason to own a QUESTION to another person and later take responsibility for an ANSWER. The Duck Bridge book's physical problem offers meaningful what/where/how curiosity and safe toy representation; it is neither passive story hearing nor merely a new title. The child alternates genuine interviewer and responder roles, with partner withholding the answer rather than completing the child's utterance. At PK3 the published criterion remains a real ask AND answer about a book, not successful complex conversational turn-taking; at PK4 both turns must relate to text actually read aloud. Don't count a puppet’s teacher-voiced question as learner-owned.

Preflight

Prepare original complete Duck Bridge text from DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md, large safe duck image, low wide soft toy blocks on ground-level pretend BLUE CARPET river (never elevated bridge or real water), optional partner puppet for dramatic practice, and blank two-sided interviewer/listener role mat with no prewritten questions or answer choice bins. Prepare an adult MODEL on distinct Garden Seed story; DURING materials that are not the EXIT probe; fresh full After the Rain or an entirely different untouched real book for EXIT; independent new text on a later calendar date for REVISIT. For this selected workshop PK4 receives the complete short Duck story read aloud; official source needs genuinely relevant contextualized actual TEXT read aloud, not a universal whole-book-length quota. Ensure child safety, hearing/quiet, AAC and respectful opt-out.

Facilitator sequence

ENTRY: After accessible genuine book encounter, invite a learner-origin question about Duck's journey and a separately learner-owned relevant answer to that same real self-question after source inquiry or another book question; a different partner question is a useful default practice. PK3 doesn't need an imposed read-aloud prerequisite; PK4 needs genuinely relevant actual text read aloud, but not universally a whole book. Record if a puppet/adult actually asked instead of child.

MODEL: With teacher-only Garden Seed, adult takes interviewer role: “What do I want to find out?” Ask an actual question to adult partner; role-swap and demonstrate giving an independent response to a DIFFERENT question. No exact Duck Bridge answer clues in the model.

DURING: Learner can manipulate safe broad blocks as a representation but source evidence still consists of ASK and ANSWER about the BOOK. Give role marker to child, let them originate a who/where/how problem question to uninformed listener; partner responds without prompting child script. Swap roles in the supported practice: child now independently responds to their earlier own authentic book question or a separate real book-relevant question. The role swap is pedagogical scaffolding and not an official requirement. If child's wording is unclear, ask neutrally what they meant, then preserve their actual idea rather than invent words. The partner may use sign/AAC and home-language interpretation while child controls meanings.

FADING: Remove adult-supplied complete semantic utterances, interviewer answer cues and adult's “ask how it crossed” hints, and fade blocks giving away one answer, but retain child-origin independent use of familiar/preprogrammed AAC phrases or conventional question frames when the learner controls referent and inquiry intention. Teach on new practice story as needed rather than prompting exact EXIT. Adult moving a duck to communicate learner intent is neutral A only if the content and question/answer are child-authored; supplied content is H.

EXIT: With a never-shown genuine complete book, PK3.III.D.3 requires child-owned asking and answering age-appropriate book questions; role-switch performance is instructional support, NOT an additional published assessment bar. PK4.III.D.3 requires genuine relevant text read aloud first, then independent ASK and RESPONSE grounded in THAT actually read text; this workshop chooses its complete short first-party story, not a universally imposed completion quota. Two different child communicative acts are documented, not partner dialogue attributed to child. No printed answer cues.

REVISIT: Later calendar date, fresh original text and separate child-generated ask/answer without rehearsed puppet lines; PK4 new complete read-aloud, status NOT_OBSERVED until real.

Diagnostic options

Child only moves duck: Appreciate play but explain on other source that question requests information; invite new practice book question, then fresh EXIT. Puppet asks but child replies: Good receptive practice; do not credit the child ASK, explicitly shift interviewer role. Adult gives completed AAC question: H, not source origin; preserve child-independent meaningful selection of familiar, previously stored AAC question messages with child-chosen book referent and curiosity, rather than requiring new lexical composition. Teacher asks while giving the answer: Neither child action; redo without leading response. Child asks about real bridge rather than book: PK3 actual about-book relevance required; PK4 actual read-aloud-text relevance mandatory. A child-origin open interpretation about Jo's choices can still be text-relevant even if not answered in one literal sentence; document which details were genuinely read and which conclusions are reasoned rather than explicitly stated. Learner cannot tolerate a real partner: Quiet adult-assisted, solo rehearsal or low-pressure exchange; source evidence still recorded separately and actual software not claimed.

Seven access profiles

Attention/executive: predictable one interviewer/one respondent turn, fade role card; language/communication: independent semantic sign/AAC/home-language turns without adult script; literacy: never require decoding words on role cards; numeracy: no measuring bridge or exact number of turns quota; autism/sensory: quiet predictable partner, opt-in puppet/no puppet; intellectual/developmental: model clear real question-response contrast then fade narrator hints; physical/motor/hearing/vision: ground-level safe materials, adapted book/read-aloud access, child-directed symbol or gaze and qualified input for non-equivalent speech/hearing access. Do not infer disability from reluctance to role-play.

Mode-specific adult responsibilities

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: A virtual partner would need genuinely responsive dialogic listening and independent child-origin semantic evaluation; toy taps cannot substitute for real ASK/RESPONSE. CO-PLAY: Caregiver changes roles without supplying words. ADULT-LED: Educator provides supported role-switch practice and logs independent ask+answer separately. CLASSROOM: Partners rotate, but each learner's words/expressive AAC are privately observed, not credited from group chorus. External educator approval, software parity, later retention and real child learning remain NOT VERIFIED.
