Teach the Garden Seed Wonder Workshop

Plain-language guide for a real adult-and-child shared reading experience. The original book text and deeper learning-evidence details remain in the accompanying lesson materials.

What the child is learning

The child asks a genuine question about a book and responds meaningfully to a question about the book. A child repeating an adult's prewritten question does not demonstrate their own question-making.

For the 3-year-old preschool group, questions and answers should be age-appropriate and may be supported through reading, pictures and discussion. For the 4-year-old group, the actual story needs to be read aloud in full before text-relevant questions and responses are assessed. Pointing to a color in an unrelated picture is not the same as responding about the story.

Prepare the story and an inviting place

Open the complete original Garden Seed story from the lesson resources. Check that the complete text is accessible to the child, with large illustrations or accessible communication supports as appropriate. Read it yourself first so you know the details: Ari finds a seed, Grandma helps plant it, and a small green shoot appears after many days. The child may speak, sign, gesture, use a communication device, or respond in their home language.

Have a different original story, Duck Bridge, available for your model; this prevents accidentally giving away a question or answer intended for the child's Garden Seed activity.

Five comfortable moves

1. Read or share the actual Garden Seed story

For the 4-year-old group, read the entire original story aloud before asking for a relevant book question. For the 3-year-old group, full shared reading is also useful but isn't an extra mandatory assessment prerequisite.

Say: “What are you wondering about what happened in this story?”

Allow the child to ask something they genuinely want to know. Notice the first question before supplying an example of what they might say. If the child is not ready, keep reading and talking without labeling them unsuccessful.

2. Show a question using a different story

Turn to the separate Duck Bridge story.

Say: “I wonder where the little toy duck wanted to go. My question is, ‘Where did the toy duck want to go?’ I can find the answer in the story.”

Use the real Duck Bridge text to confirm the answer, rather than manufacturing book facts. The adult's example is not the child's demonstrated question.

3. Invite a child-owned question and an answer

Return to Garden Seed.

Say: “What would you like to ask about Ari or the seed?”

Let the child's own curiosity guide the question. They might ask where a seed goes or why Ari checked the soil, but don't require those exact words.

Then ask a different question that genuinely follows the text, such as: “Who helped Ari?” or “What did Ari notice after many days?” Accept authentic meaningful responses in any suitable communication mode.

4. Try with a genuinely new question

Without repeating the child's earlier answer, invite them to ask one more real story question and respond either to their own earlier genuine question after looking back in the book, or to another genuinely relevant question. Avoid supplying the exact answer or counting repetition of an adult's wording as independent child question-making.

For the older group, check that the question and answer are actually grounded in the read-aloud text. For younger children, preserve legitimate age-appropriate emerging questions and responses without requiring polished grammar.

5. Return to a new book another day

On a truly later day, offer another complete age-appropriate original story and a fresh chance for the child to ask and respond. The written plan does not mean this return has already happened.

When something is tricky

What happens Your response
The child can answer but cannot yet form a question Model curiosity using another story, then offer an open new opportunity
A response describes picture color but not the book events Revisit the relevant text and invite the child's own meaningful response
The child has a question you did not anticipate Explore it if connected to the book; don't demand your preferred phrasing
Spoken language or fine-motor pointing is difficult Accept sign, communication aids, gesture and home-language contributions
The child wants to stop End warmly and return another day

Teach the Garden Seed Wonder Workshop with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

The child asks a genuine question about a book and responds meaningfully to a question about the book. A child repeating an adult's prewritten question does not demonstrate their own question-making.

For the 3-year-old preschool group, questions and answers should be age-appropriate and may be supported through reading, pictures and discussion. For the 4-year-old group, the actual story needs to be read aloud in full before text-relevant questions and responses are assessed. Pointing to a color in an unrelated picture is not the same as responding about the story.

Prepare the classroom

Have the complete original Garden Seed story available for genuine read-aloud or accessible shared reading, with enough time for children to encounter the relevant passages before questions. Prepare a separate adult model using a different book and picture or AAC question choices where helpful.

Introduce the idea together

Read the actual story so the group understands what happened. Ask, “What are you wondering about the seed or what happened next?” Pause to take real child questions, rather than giving all the questions and answers first.

Give children meaningful practice

After whole-group reading, pairs or small circles choose a child-authored question about the story and offer text-relevant responses. The teacher models asking about a different familiar book, then visits a few individual exchanges to hear what each child genuinely asks and answers; quiet picture exploration can remain meaningful for children awaiting turns.

Observe individual learning fairly

Observe a focal learner produce or choose a relevant question and provide a response grounded in the actual text or pictures, noting which aspect was child-origin versus prompted. Repeating an adult's ready-made question is supported practice, not automatically independent question generation. A different real story later provides a fair novel check.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Allow signing, AAC, pointing to meaningful pictures, home language and flexible pauses. Avoid rejecting a plausible question simply because it wasn't on the teacher's list. If the story wasn't fully and accessibly presented, don't infer that a child failed to comprehend unseen content.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

For the exact technical learning targets, grade-specific later-range checks, safety conditions and original materials, open Full technical plan or Facilitator guide above.

Four PK3 and five PK4 distinct developmental stages

PK3 Q3-1: learner asks an own who/what book question and answers a distinct genuine age-appropriate book question. PK3 Q3-2: learner personally answers about actual book event AND raises a new own question. PK3 Q3-3: questions and answers may be authentic sign/AAC/gesture/home-language while educator holds communication aid but not answers. PK3 Q3-4: source transfers to an entirely different BOOK, child owns both ask+answer; no extra mandatory read-aloud requirement printed for PK3.

PK4 Q4-1: child poses a genuine question specifically relevant to actual TEXT READ ALOUD. PK4 Q4-2: child responds with a detail from that actual text as well as asking. PK4 Q4-3: child asks and responds about genuine cause or event sequence from read text, not a color clue. PK4 Q4-4: meaningful child-authored relevant question and response across follow-ups, with accessible support and no adult precompleted message. PK4 Q4-5: truly unfamiliar original full text read aloud and independently child-owned relevant ASK plus RESPOND; fresh not rehearsed. For both grades adult asking and child replying alone lacks child ASK.

Five fully authored grade-specific instructional phases

ENTRY: Provide accessible original Garden Seed book and optionally read fully to PK3, observe child first self-authored genuine book question and personal response to neutral adult book question. PK4 requires reading the COMPLETE actual original story text aloud FIRST, then observe learner's own relevant question 'Why did Ari check soil?' or similar and own relevant response, without teacher pre-writing question.

MODEL: Adult models a question and own answer from DIFFERENT original Duck Bridge complete story, 'Where did the toy duck want to go?' -> 'Toy pond', not child Garden Seed targets. Adult demonstration never counts as child's source for either grade.

DURING: Let child choose first-party original broad illustrations of finding seed, planting/watering, shoot after many days. If child isn't ready to form a question, model unrelated book and provide scaffolded future chance, no fabricated credit. PK4 after full oral first-party Garden Seed text asks genuine text-RELEVANT question 'How did the seed get water?' and RESPONDS to an adult's different text question 'What did Ari notice after many days?' with 'A small green shoot', or a relevant own response. Ensure response uses source details, not unrelated picture/color, or adult's prearranged completed plot/answer. Garden seed growth happens over DAYS, never instantly; don't use real small seeds or tools. Adult can help sign/AAC positioning, hearing and home-language interpretation while child chooses words/content. Avoid awarding BME retell D1 rather than D3 child asking too. Fresh EXIT different original full book question/answer, teacher model separate.

EXIT: Give actual new original After the Rain complete book content, PK3 learner independently asks one real age-appropriate book question and answers in a separate meaningful child-owned response act, either the same genuine earlier child question after book inquiry or a different age-appropriate question; PK4 after full original text read aloud asks and responds to truly text-relevant rain/puddle questions without already highlighted answer. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Future independently child-operable digital book experience must offer full original accessible human-like narrated TEXT for PK4 before source check, child-controlled page/replay/pause and optional quiet, vision, home-language and sign/AAC/motor modes. PK4 truly ASKS a new relevant question about actual text READ ALOUD and RESPONDS to a different relevant question grounded in that read text. Pressing a generic “?” icon while automated characters supply the question or answer is NOT learner-owned source; prerecorded menu options need meaningful child-directed selection with content, not decorative gestures. Nonreader control independent of adult operator intended, software NOT IMPLEMENTED and no real child/mode efficacy tested.

Adult may repeat, clarify/access, but not supply whole child messages or a guessed answer before child attempt. Fresh EXIT and actually later REVISIT only if truly observed.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: Educator provides authentic original age-safe book; for PK4 actually reads relevant full original TEXT ALOUD FIRST, and for PK3 can read aloud to support literacy but doesn't claim an unwritten prerequisite. PK3 ASKS and ANSWERS age-appropriate book questions; PK4 ASKS and RESPONDS to questions relevant to actual read-aloud text. Give vision, hearing/quiet, home language, sign/AAC/motor and privacy; no deficit label for child unfamiliar with books or speech. TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Teacher shares first-party original safe books and actual text read aloud for PK4, gives authentic unprompted questioning opportunities and neutral open-ended text questions, but records individual child-owned meaningful ASKS and ANSWERS/RESPONDS, not teacher-generated questions or chorus as each student's performance. New genuine source EXIT and later revisit not manufactured.

Source integrity and validation boundaries

Child should own the asking as well as answer; adult who asks everything then counts one child response fails source. An adult-origin question can be a stimulus for the child response but not the child's own ASK. No guessing from only animated picture unrelated to actual book, recycled rehearsed teacher exact answer, or absence of source text at PK4. Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 01 Garden Seed Wonder Workshop

Pair: complete Garden Seed lesson plan; official source outcomes PK3.III.D.3 and PK4.III.D.3; objective educator guide. This is not another counted lesson.

Specific pedagogical rationale and preparation

Children first need to discover that a question can seek unknown information rather than merely repeat an adult's comprehension quiz. Lesson 01 makes curiosity visible through a blank wondering space linked to an original accessible Garden Seed book. The adult deliberately treats the learner asking and the learner answering as TWO source actions, not a single teacher-controlled question-answer turn. Read the complete first-party passages in advance. Have large safe original seed/soil/water/shoot illustrations or printed cards, blank rather than prefilled question bubble, and separate books/texts for ENTRY (Garden Seed), adult MODEL (Duck Bridge), child guided DURING (new unfamiliar part/context or different book), and previously untouched EXIT (After the Rain). Real small seeds and forced touching are unnecessary. For this chosen workshop PK4 children receive the complete short first-party story read aloud. This is a strong lesson implementation, not an official universal full-book quota: a coherent actual read-aloud passage sufficiently contextualized for authentic relevant child ASK and RESPONSE can also be source-valid when documented; a picture-only guess is not. Book/picture access may differ per learner, but log what actual source exposure took place.

Scoring and accessibility clarification: Genuinely distinct child ASK and child ANSWER/RESPONSE evidence acts are necessary, not necessarily two different question topics. Independent child-selected stored AAC or familiar question wording is valid when semantic intent and book/text referent are learner-owned; adult-selected speech buttons are H. Full original workshop short-story reading is an instructional design choice, while the official PK4 source asks for actual relevant text read aloud rather than universal complete-book delivery.

Scripted adult moves, feedback and fading

ENTRY: Let child encounter authentic accessible Garden Seed book. “What would you like to find out about this book?” is a neutral opening; if no genuine question, don't supply “Why did Ari plant it?” as if child-authored. Independently ask a separate age-appropriate book question and record the child's first answer without correction. For this workshop the complete short Garden Seed text is read aloud before PK4 assessment. The official outcome requires relevant actual text read aloud, not an arbitrary whole-book completion threshold; record actual source passage and access.

MODEL: On different complete Duck text, adult says “I wonder WHERE Duck can go; that's MY question,” and separately demonstrates an answer to another adult question. Distinguish a statement (“Duck is here”) from a genuine unknown (“Where will Duck go?”). Never let adult model transcript count as child content.

DURING: Let learner choose a genuinely unknown character/event/sequence from a fresh practice encounter and generate their own who/where/what/why question. If child's question is a statement, ask whether it is intended to find out something, model on another adult example, then fade the “I wonder” visual frame. Repeat source audio for genuine access, but do not repeat the exact assessment answer as a clue. Misconception and access decision tree

“What?” only or presses ? with no content: treat as opening communicative intent, teach personally authored question on separate practice book; no official source pass yet. Only answers: model own curious question elsewhere and wait for learner ask; new exit. Echoes example: change the adult's demonstration text and let child pick unmodeled unknown from a fresh one. Asks about real seed instead of the text: accept social curiosity, but for PK4 explicitly practice relating to actual read-aloud content on DIFFERENT text, then fresh source probe. Uses short/generic response: verify relevance to the specific book event, not word count or preferred English syntax; document if meaning cannot be established. Access difficulty: provide sight/hearing, semantic AAC, home-language interpretation and independent child-directed selection; never fill answer for learner.

Seven learning-differences profiles

Attention/executive: single curious turn then single answering turn with predictable visual routine, faded reminders; language/communication: child-generated semantic sign/AAC/home language with neutral interpreter; literacy/phonology: actual book encounter without print decoding/phonics prerequisite; numeracy: no counting or numeral response quota; autism/sensory: voluntary quiet page access, no forced oral performance, predictable choice of book; intellectual/developmental: adult-only clear question-vs-statement contrast then gradually remove frames; physical/motor/hearing/vision: large accessible pages, actual read-aloud accessibility and child-directed switch/eye-gaze, with external specialist review pending for equivalence. Respect privacy and consent, avoid storing identifiable child content.
