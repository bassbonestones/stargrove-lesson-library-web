Objective-level Educator Guide — III.D.3 Asking and Responding about Books

Purpose: This document explains and coordinates the three standalone lessons. It is an overarching objective guide and is NOT counted as a lesson plan. The source research scope is the 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Reading III.D.3; original PDF layout concordance and independent specialist approval remain pending.

PK3.III.D.3 official source (SHA 348048d8b024078eaddcf2d95d5a15db194b25dff75cd24ee39bbe0cd5b2e48e)

“Child asks and answers age- appropriate questions about a book.” The two child-origin acts are ASK and ANSWER about a real book; an adult asking every question and a child only answering is not enough. PK3's printed cell does NOT explicitly mandate a completed read-aloud before every probe. A developmentally appropriate picture-book, narrated book or otherwise accessible book encounter can support the source action, but a game with no book is not book-question evidence. Emergent questions may be spoken, signed, personally composed in augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), in home language or conveyed through unambiguous communicative gesture with reliably recorded intent.

Progression: Shared attention to an accessible full book, notice a meaningful character/event/detail, raise genuine curiosity, then distinguish asking for information from independently responding to that same inquiry later or another genuine book-related question. At PK3 Q3-1 through Q3-4 in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md, gradually fade “I wonder…” frames, adult topic choices and verbatim stems; maintain sensory and motor access. Never require mature syntax or reading decoding as an extra official attainment threshold. Accept child-authored simple who/what/where/why questions when genuinely about the material.

Independent EXIT evidence: Child independently authors an age-appropriate question about an untouched book and independently answers their earlier own inquiry after actual book exploration or another authentic age-appropriate book question. Record both acts, the real book context and actual assistance. Adult answering a child's question does not supply the child ANSWER half of the published source.

PK4.III.D.3 official source (SHA a9f421b97fef7cbc25c3186bed915ba77425d6fc08c34c983ee40a75e92143ca)

“Child asks and responds to questions relevant to the text read aloud.” Crucial grade difference: source text must ACTUALLY be read aloud, with the learner's personally generated ASK and meaningful RESPONSE demonstrably relevant to that heard content; an arbitrary whole-book completion threshold is not an additional official condition. A static picture without accessible reading or a synthetic narrator not yet implemented cannot prove the real read-aloud event. Do not demand exact adult wording, advanced scientific explanation, conventional English grammar or a particular question-word quota. An AAC/sign/home-language question can be child-composed original language if its semantics are genuinely supplied by learner rather than a partner.

Progression: From understanding what in an actual text happened, build learner curiosity linked to a specific read detail; practice asking why/how/what only as appropriate to child, and supplying an independently relevant response to their earlier authentic question after source exploration or another question. Use PK4 Q4-1 through Q4-5 with explicit read-aloud context. Fade adult recast, rehearsed category buttons and pictorial cues; preserve repeats of the text for auditory access only if not covertly telling the answer.

Independent EXIT evidence: Following genuinely relevant actual text read aloud (this workshop chooses the complete short story), child makes a genuinely text-relevant question and an independently authored relevant response to that earlier real question or another source question; record textual details and exact child content, not merely choosing a pictured answer. A response may be short, signed or home-language; the criterion is meaningful relevance and child authorship.

Three meaningfully different instruction mechanisms, not book rethemes

  • Lesson 01 — Narrative Wonder Workshop builds question generation from child-origin curiosity and checks separately the child's ability to respond to another book question. Companion 01.
  • Lesson 02 — Role-play information exchange uses a real child-as-interviewer versus listener role switch to practice reciprocal ASK/RESPOND with authentic book facts. It is not merely a new illustrated story. Companion 02.
  • Lesson 03 — Informational evidence detective teaches locating what a factual book actually says and forming questions plus responsive explanations rooted in its text; does not teach or assess independent science expertise. Companion 03.

All three retain distinct ENTRY (uncued), MODEL (another adult-owned text), DURING (guided practice and explicit fade), EXIT (previously unseen book/text with BOTH child-origin verbs), and REVISIT (genuinely later calendar date on different material). A three-lesson inventory is documentary instructional breadth, not a guarantee of success. Rotate genuine novel books when teaching more than one plan so a prior familiar book cannot be disguised as fresh transfer.

Diagnosis and feedback

Child answers but never asks: Teach curiosity as a separate communicative purpose on adult-only text; offer child their own blank wondering space, then test a new book without choosing the question for them. Question echoes the adult: Record as supported practice, fade leading stems; do not “translate” adult content into child authorship. PK4 asks about clothes or nearby toy: Kind acknowledgment, then on a different practice text model distinguishing questions about actually read content; use a fresh independent EXIT. Child says “Rain” to everything: Ask learner to identify which event the question refers to, use text sequence evidence during guided practice, remove highlighted answer for clean fresh EXIT. Adult asks and answers itself: Document neither child-source action. Child cannot hear read-aloud: Arrange appropriate accessible signed or qualified alternative communication and professional input; do not claim source-equivalent actual read-aloud evidence without valid individualized review.

Inclusion, evidence and four written modes

For all seven individualized profiles—attention/executive, language/communication, literacy/phonological, numeracy, autism/predictability/sensory, intellectual/developmental, and physical/motor/hearing/vision—preserve the child's ownership of TWO communicative turns. Offer quiet/replay, accessible signed/home language, meaningful composed AAC, large visual and tactile book access, and neutral motor support without scripting the child question or response. A sensory-access barrier is not a reading/comprehension diagnosis. Coordinate with qualified specialists/families as appropriate, not assumed equivalence.

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: The future application must have a genuine full text/listening experience, open learner-owned question creation and relevant answer recording, with privacy-safe prereader interaction; no script here is executable. CO-PLAY: Caregiver provides the book and genuinely listens. ADULT-LED: Educator documents both independent acts separately. CLASSROOM: No choral questioning or adult puppet lines as individual learner proof. Across modes, true independent EXIT, new-calendar-date REVISIT, external educator validation, live learner achievement, accessible physical material and software parity remain NOT_OBSERVED or NOT VERIFIED.
