Method 02 — Toy Duck Carpet Bridge who/where/how book conversation

Official source: PK3.III.D.3 asks and answers age- appropriate questions about a book, SHA 348048d8b024078eaddcf2d95d5a15db194b25dff75cd24ee39bbe0cd5b2e48e. PK4.III.D.3 asks and responds to questions relevant to the text read aloud, SHA a9f421b97fef7cbc25c3186bed915ba77425d6fc08c34c983ee40a75e92143ca. Frozen full source cells SHA verified; raw original source PDF two-column textual concordance pending.

Distinct substantive child-owned question+response mechanism: Original playroom Duck's Block Bridge provides a different physical problem/solution narrative for self-authored who/where/how questioning: toy duck must cross BLUE CARPET pretend river, Jo and Sam lay broad soft floor blocks, duck reaches toy pond. PK3 poses and answers genuine age-appropriate questions ABOUT this BOOK. PK4's own question and response must clearly connect to original full TEXT actually read aloud. Safe pretend problem-based expressive conversations differ from nature book and factual rain process questioning.

Legitimate child-led exchange and source boundary: The selected Duck Bridge workshop reads the complete short original story aloud to PK4 as an engaging method, but the TEA source only requires genuinely heard text with sufficient context for a relevant child ASK and independently meaningful RESPONSE; an age-appropriate coherent real read passage may suffice when the actual words/accessible interpretation are documented. Child questioning and answering are two genuine learner-owned acts, not necessarily two different question topics or a formal swap of physical role markers. A child may ask 'How did Duck cross?', inspect/hear relevant source, and later independently answer that original question. Existing AAC message 'How did it cross?' intentionally chosen by learner for this new book context is authentic inquiry, not an adult script merely because wording is stored. Text-relevant genuine interpretive wonder can be asked without a predetermined literal answer, while claims about book facts must stay faithful to what was actually read. A child may ask a quiet educator/caregiver rather than an unfamiliar or uninformed peer. Adult choosing the utterance, supplying the answer, echoing a peer or auto-playing a puppet dialogue cannot prove either learner-owned verb.

Four PK3 and five PK4 distinct developmental stages

PK3 Q3-1: learner asks an own who/what book question and answers a distinct genuine age-appropriate book question. PK3 Q3-2: learner personally answers about actual book event AND raises a new own question. PK3 Q3-3: questions and answers may be authentic sign/AAC/gesture/home-language while educator holds communication aid but not answers. PK3 Q3-4: source transfers to an entirely different BOOK, child owns both ask+answer; no extra mandatory read-aloud requirement printed for PK3.

PK4 Q4-1: child poses a genuine question specifically relevant to actual TEXT READ ALOUD. PK4 Q4-2: child responds with a detail from that actual text as well as asking. PK4 Q4-3: child asks and responds about genuine cause or event sequence from read text, not a color clue. PK4 Q4-4: meaningful child-authored relevant question and response across follow-ups, with accessible support and no adult precompleted message. PK4 Q4-5: truly unfamiliar original full text read aloud and independently child-owned relevant ASK plus RESPOND; fresh not rehearsed. For both grades adult asking and child replying alone lacks child ASK.

First-party original book materials, honest developmental prerequisites

Original complete Duck's Block Bridge read-aloud story in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md, large original duck/paper toy pond/blue carpet illustration and broad flat soft pretend blocks (never real water or high unstable bridges), neutral adult question 'Where does toy duck want to go?' and independent learner-authored 'How did duck cross?' Adult MODEL on different Garden Seed book, fresh EXIT different Rain factual text. Complete original full book passages and four PK3/five PK4 stages live in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. Teaching begins with learner's own question as well as own response; teacher's own book-related question does not prove the child's ASK and a teacher/script supplied answer doesn't prove RESPOND. PK3 source about BOOK, without compulsory read-aloud condition; PK4 source specifically actual TEXT READ ALOUD. Respect accessible home language, sign/AAC/gesture, listening/quiet, vision/motor, and age-sensitive safe context.

Five fully authored grade-specific instructional phases

ENTRY: Show original safe Duck playroom story; PK3 may ask actual 'Why is duck here?' about book and answer a neutral actual book question without compulsory full read-aloud standard; PK4 first hears entire original Duck story READ ALOUD and then asks a genuine relevant who/where/how question before adult gives exact answer.

MODEL: Adult on UNRELATED original Garden Seed story demonstrates personal adult asking 'Who found the seed?' and responding 'Ari' using teacher-only paper mat. Do not act both Duck characters and count the puppets' adult questions as learner asking.

DURING: PK3 can actively ask a book question such as 'Where is the duck?' with sign/AAC/voice while holding broad first-party duck card, and answer a different valid question 'What did Jo and Sam build?' with 'Block bridge' or accessible gesture to bridge when genuine book-linked; no forced formal text comprehension or verbose answer. PK4 after teacher genuinely reads FULL Duck story text aloud, learner owns a text-specific question like 'What did they check before moving duck?' and personally RESPONDS to a different relevant question 'How did the toy duck get across?' with correct 'Across the low soft block bridge on carpet'. Adult should not set up a single correct colored toy pond button or pre-record actual child question. Child might choose sound/AAC talk or point to combined word content not merely a decorative “?” control. A teacher can restate a child's actual child-origin sign question to make it understandable but must not invent the question. Use only floor pretend river and low broad soft blocks, NO real river crossing and no elevated walking surfaces. Separate this from III.D.1 narration BME skill; source requires child-generated questions too. For actual new EXIT use complete different original informational rain text with relevant cause or where question, not the same practiced blocks.

EXIT: Actual novel complete first-party After the Rain informational book: PK3 truly asks/answers meaningful age-appropriate book rain questions, PK4 first hears whole factual read aloud then asks/answers specific relevant rain-to-puddle question with own language/sign/AAC rather than teacher-voiced script.

REVISIT: Genuine later date new original book in learner's meaningful language with real child-owned both asking and answer, PK4 TEXT actually read aloud; NOT_OBSERVED.

Four substantive WRITTEN delivery modes plus source limits

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Future independently child-operable digital book experience must offer full original accessible human-like narrated TEXT for PK4 before source check, child-controlled page/replay/pause and optional quiet, vision, home-language and sign/AAC/motor modes. PK3 can access a genuine book by first-party readable/picture/narrated means and individually ASK and ANSWER real age-appropriate questions with own safe voice/sign/AAC/motor choices. PK4 truly ASKS a new relevant question about actual text READ ALOUD and RESPONDS to a different relevant question grounded in that read text. Pressing a generic “?” icon while automated characters supply the question or answer is NOT learner-owned source; prerecorded menu options need meaningful child-directed selection with content, not decorative gestures. Nonreader control independent of adult operator intended, software NOT IMPLEMENTED and no real child/mode efficacy tested.

CO-PLAY: Caregiver offers the genuine original book and listens for child's own age-appropriate question or enables child-authored AAC questioning, and offers neutral age-appropriate question to which learner responds. PK3 source is 'about a book' and does NOT expressly mandate that text be read aloud every time, though caregiver can read accessible book; don't fabricate that additional criterion. PK4 must have genuinely relevant age-appropriate TEXT READ ALOUD before child ASKS and RESPONDS about what was actually read, in home language with sign/AAC gesture access if needed. Adult may repeat, clarify/access, but not supply whole child messages or a guessed answer before child attempt. Fresh EXIT and actually later REVISIT only if truly observed.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: Educator provides authentic original age-safe book; for PK4 actually reads relevant full original TEXT ALOUD FIRST, and for PK3 can read aloud to support literacy but doesn't claim an unwritten prerequisite. Observe child ENTRY question and answer before unrelated adult MODEL on DIFFERENT book, then teach distinct real who/what/why question stems and responsive listening without compelled exact wording, standard answer length, or adult-only puppet dialogue. PK3 ASKS and ANSWERS age-appropriate book questions; PK4 ASKS and RESPONDS to questions relevant to actual read-aloud text. Give vision, hearing/quiet, home language, sign/AAC/motor and privacy; no deficit label for child unfamiliar with books or speech. Fresh unlike EXIT from new book/text and true later date REVISIT only when real.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Teacher shares first-party original safe books and actual text read aloud for PK4, gives authentic unprompted questioning opportunities and neutral open-ended text questions, but records individual child-owned meaningful ASKS and ANSWERS/RESPONDS, not teacher-generated questions or chorus as each student's performance. PK3 book engagement may be multimodal and does not need adult imposition of exhaustive content memory; PK4 must actually relate both question and response to text previously READ ALOUD. Accessible native/home-language sign/AAC/quiet/hearing/vision/motor methods, refusal respected; private observations not collected in shared source. New genuine source EXIT and later revisit not manufactured.

Source integrity and validation boundaries

Neither grade objective requires child to use only spoken English, ask a fixed number of questions, answer all 5W prompts, or recall unrelated facts. Both verbs apply: PK3 ASKS AND ANSWERS actual age-appropriate questions about a BOOK; PK4 ASKS AND RESPONDS to actual text-relevant questions after real full TEXT READ ALOUD. Child should own the asking as well as answer; adult who asks everything then counts one child response fails source. An adult-origin question can be a stimulus for the child response but not the child's own ASK. No guessing from only animated picture unrelated to actual book, recycled rehearsed teacher exact answer, or absence of source text at PK4. Safe original books, no copied copyrighted works; Duck story only low pretend carpet bridge, Rain story no unsafe puddle handling, seed planting pretend no small choking pieces. No observed learner, independent qualified educator, actual later date retention, physical/tactile printed QA, functioning independently child-only SOLO or empirical four-mode effectiveness established. False credit, concrete teaching correction and candid evidence

False credit: Teacher puppets both toy Duck and Jo questions and hears class chorus 'pond', then awards every child a question-asking turn. Adult-owned puppet dialogue and group response cannot demonstrate any individual child ASK; neutral prompt then genuine child phrasing and independent relevant reply required.

Correction: A real child must personally ASK a meaningful BOOK-based question and independently ANSWER/respond in a separate meaningful act, to their own earlier genuine book question or another relevant one; a teacher can ask to elicit the response but cannot take the child asking turn. For PK4 confirm genuinely relevant actual TEXT read aloud, with enough source context for both turns; full short Duck story remains our selected teaching route, not an official universal whole-book qualification threshold. For PK3 don't impose PK4's extra mandatory text-read-aloud criterion, though reading a book to child is valid pedagogy. Future actual documented actions use U for child-own question/answer, H for guided own source, A for access-only and X for teacher/autoplay-only source; X is never achievement. Actual child learning, independent qualified educator, physical/tactile print, genuine later REVISIT, functioning child-only SOLO, measured four-mode equivalence NOT VALIDATED.

Retrospective source-specific learning access — November 1, 2026

These are instructional corrections and fresh proposed assessment checks, not real children’s demonstrated learning, operational SOLO software, or qualified special-educator approval. Preserve exact published PK3 versus PK4 verbs, honest learner agency and a source-valid book.

PK3: pretend carpet duck and genuinely narrated bridge problem

Actual adapted teaching and prompt fade: Use the original book’s real illustrated page sequence separately from safe low, broad toy bridge play. A child may play but must also make an actual question ABOUT the book, such as wondering where duck goes; scaffold by modeling a question for a DIFFERENT story character, then fade. Allow AAC/sign/home-language inquiry and wait without requiring a toy to be physically moved.

New source-bound exit: On a NEW illustrated book about an imaginary toy boat on a playroom floor, log child’s independently authored about-book question and separate age-appropriate answer. Duck movement, pointing to a blue carpet or answering adult puppet questions alone is play/response, not independently originating inquiry.

False-credit trap and repair: False credit: A puppet controlled by teacher asks the question, child answers “bridge” in group chorus and everyone receives credit. Record this as adult Q and prompted or non-attributable answer, not child ASK+ANSWER.

PK4: pretend carpet duck and genuinely narrated bridge problem

Actual adapted teaching and prompt fade: Actually read the entire Duck Bridge text aloud, ensuring safe auditory or individually planned accessible text-language route. Let child ask a genuine open information-gap question about Jo/Sam’s choice or why the duck uses broad soft blocks, not a teacher-puppet script; then separately respond to a text-relevant question, which may be their earlier genuine question after authentic inquiry or another prompt. Preview the role routine and fade adult-selected answer-coded question buttons while preserving independent use of familiar preprogrammed AAC phrases.

New source-bound exit: On freshly read original text about two friends building a pretend paper tunnel for a toy, record individually attributable learner-authored text-relevant ASK and learner-origin RESPONSE to separate question. An enacted correct duck crossing or generic “river” label cannot substitute for independent relevant text reasoning; no elevated/real river hazards.

False-credit trap and repair: False credit: Child moves duck across ready-made bridge while software narrates Q and A, or repeats a peer’s complete question. Preserve actual source read-aloud, original question provenance and novel text response; record HELPED or NOT_OBSERVED otherwise.

Across the four WRITTEN modes: SOLO (not implemented) would require independently operable accessible story presentation, child-authored questions and meaningful novel response; app voice-generated question, fixed answer-button and synthetic avatar interaction are not child inquiry. CO-PLAY distinguishes actual learner-authored question/response from caregiver paraphrase. ADULT-LED uses a different modeled story and independently attributable fresh text-book source probe. CLASSROOM records EACH learner’s actual question AND separate response; a teacher’s question followed by chorus answers is not individual complete attainment.

Specialist and prospective observation: Verify family language and actual IEP/IFSP/Section 504 and hearing/vision/assistive communication supports at real delivery. Additional method-specific dialogic teaching sequence

Role exchange, not a rethemed story: Arrange two blank role markers, “curious interviewer” and “person who listens and answers,” with no written answer prompts. On unrelated adult Garden Seed MODEL, adult and partner exchange who asks and who responds to a different book question as the selected teaching example, not a restriction on the child's independently valid self-question then self-answer. During Duck DURING, the learner chooses a genuine meaningful about-book question and conveys it to a willing listener or through child-directed AAC, then responds in a separate learner-owned turn to the same self-authored question after actually learning from the book, or another genuine duck-book question. Adult does not supply a complete question or answer via a puppet. When the child only moves the duck, teacher explains during separate practice that pretending a solution is not yet making a BOOK question. Fade markers and adult example questions; switch roles is a method for learning two source verbs, NOT an extra official skill requirement.

PK3.III.D.3 independent EXIT: With wholly new genuine book, independently age-appropriate ASK about book and separate meaningful ANSWER to their own earlier question after book inquiry or another relevant book prompt, no compulsory read-aloud constraint. PK4.III.D.3 independent EXIT: After actual relevant new text read aloud (this workshop uses its complete short story), child personally asks and responds to questions relevant to that text; preserve two child-owned content turns. REVISIT requires a distinct later calendar date and new book and remains NOT_OBSERVED.

Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 02 Duck Bridge Dialogic Information Exchange

Pair: Duck Bridge lesson plan, source PK3.III.D.3 and PK4.III.D.3; distinct objective educator guide. This is facilitation, not a plan counted as a fourth method.

Why role-switching is instruction, not just a second story

Real information-gap exchanges give the child a reason to own a QUESTION to another person and later take responsibility for an ANSWER. The Duck Bridge book's physical problem offers meaningful what/where/how curiosity and safe toy representation; it is neither passive story hearing nor merely a new title. The child alternates genuine interviewer and responder roles, with partner withholding the answer rather than completing the child's utterance. At PK3 the published criterion remains a real ask AND answer about a book, not successful complex conversational turn-taking; at PK4 both turns must relate to text actually read aloud. Don't count a puppet’s teacher-voiced question as learner-owned.

Preflight

Prepare original complete Duck Bridge text from DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md, large safe duck image, low wide soft toy blocks on ground-level pretend BLUE CARPET river (never elevated bridge or real water), optional partner puppet for dramatic practice, and blank two-sided interviewer/listener role mat with no prewritten questions or answer choice bins. Prepare an adult MODEL on distinct Garden Seed story; DURING materials that are not the EXIT probe; fresh full After the Rain or an entirely different untouched real book for EXIT; independent new text on a later calendar date for REVISIT. For this selected workshop PK4 receives the complete short Duck story read aloud; official source needs genuinely relevant contextualized actual TEXT read aloud, not a universal whole-book-length quota. Ensure child safety, hearing/quiet, AAC and respectful opt-out.

Facilitator sequence

ENTRY: After accessible genuine book encounter, invite a learner-origin question about Duck's journey and a separately learner-owned relevant answer to that same real self-question after source inquiry or another book question; a different partner question is a useful default practice. PK3 doesn't need an imposed read-aloud prerequisite; PK4 needs genuinely relevant actual text read aloud, but not universally a whole book. Record if a puppet/adult actually asked instead of child.

MODEL: With teacher-only Garden Seed, adult takes interviewer role: “What do I want to find out?” Ask an actual question to adult partner; role-swap and demonstrate giving an independent response to a DIFFERENT question. No exact Duck Bridge answer clues in the model.

DURING: Learner can manipulate safe broad blocks as a representation but source evidence still consists of ASK and ANSWER about the BOOK. Give role marker to child, let them originate a who/where/how problem question to uninformed listener; partner responds without prompting child script. Swap roles in the supported practice: child now independently responds to their earlier own authentic book question or a separate real book-relevant question. The role swap is pedagogical scaffolding and not an official requirement. If child's wording is unclear, ask neutrally what they meant, then preserve their actual idea rather than invent words. The partner may use sign/AAC and home-language interpretation while child controls meanings.

FADING: Remove adult-supplied complete semantic utterances, interviewer answer cues and adult's “ask how it crossed” hints, and fade blocks giving away one answer, but retain child-origin independent use of familiar/preprogrammed AAC phrases or conventional question frames when the learner controls referent and inquiry intention. Teach on new practice story as needed rather than prompting exact EXIT. Adult moving a duck to communicate learner intent is neutral A only if the content and question/answer are child-authored; supplied content is H.

EXIT: With a never-shown genuine complete book, PK3.III.D.3 requires child-owned asking and answering age-appropriate book questions; role-switch performance is instructional support, NOT an additional published assessment bar. PK4.III.D.3 requires genuine relevant text read aloud first, then independent ASK and RESPONSE grounded in THAT actually read text; this workshop chooses its complete short first-party story, not a universally imposed completion quota. Two different child communicative acts are documented, not partner dialogue attributed to child. No printed answer cues.

REVISIT: Later calendar date, fresh original text and separate child-generated ask/answer without rehearsed puppet lines; PK4 new complete read-aloud, status NOT_OBSERVED until real.

Diagnostic options

Child only moves duck: Appreciate play but explain on other source that question requests information; invite new practice book question, then fresh EXIT. Puppet asks but child replies: Good receptive practice; do not credit the child ASK, explicitly shift interviewer role. Adult gives completed AAC question: H, not source origin; preserve child-independent meaningful selection of familiar, previously stored AAC question messages with child-chosen book referent and curiosity, rather than requiring new lexical composition. Teacher asks while giving the answer: Neither child action; redo without leading response. Child asks about real bridge rather than book: PK3 actual about-book relevance required; PK4 actual read-aloud-text relevance mandatory. A child-origin open interpretation about Jo's choices can still be text-relevant even if not answered in one literal sentence; document which details were genuinely read and which conclusions are reasoned rather than explicitly stated. Learner cannot tolerate a real partner: Quiet adult-assisted, solo rehearsal or low-pressure exchange; source evidence still recorded separately and actual software not claimed.

Seven access profiles

Attention/executive: predictable one interviewer/one respondent turn, fade role card; language/communication: independent semantic sign/AAC/home-language turns without adult script; literacy: never require decoding words on role cards; numeracy: no measuring bridge or exact number of turns quota; autism/sensory: quiet predictable partner, opt-in puppet/no puppet; intellectual/developmental: model clear real question-response contrast then fade narrator hints; physical/motor/hearing/vision: ground-level safe materials, adapted book/read-aloud access, child-directed symbol or gaze and qualified input for non-equivalent speech/hearing access. Do not infer disability from reluctance to role-play.

Mode-specific adult responsibilities

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: A virtual partner would need genuinely responsive dialogic listening and independent child-origin semantic evaluation; toy taps cannot substitute for real ASK/RESPONSE. CO-PLAY: Caregiver changes roles without supplying words. ADULT-LED: Educator provides supported role-switch practice and logs independent ask+answer separately. CLASSROOM: Partners rotate, but each learner's words/expressive AAC are privately observed, not credited from group chorus. External educator approval, software parity, later retention and real child learning remain NOT VERIFIED.
