Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 03 Rain Book Evidence Detective

Pair: Rain informational lesson for PK3.III.D.3 and PK4.III.D.3; overarching objective guide. The guide gives lesson-specific reasoning and errors, not a counted independent lesson.

Why this is a third instructional approach

Unlike Lesson 01's question-generation workshop and Lesson 02's live role exchange, this mechanism teaches a child to identify where an informational text provides an answer, distinguish a meaningful text-rooted question from unrelated world guesses, and respond from original read facts without demanding advanced causal science. A visible three-panel cloud→rain→puddle→drier sequence supports evidence search, but do not equate selecting highlighted picture cards with generating a question or answer. PK3 asks and answers about a book; PK4 must genuinely ask and respond about content ACTUALLY read aloud. This is reading dialogue, not proof of science VI learning outcomes.

Preflight and safe material assignment

Use the complete original Rain mini-book passages in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. In the selected lesson the adult checks the genuine source and reads all three short Rain panels aloud to PK4 with accessible narration/sign and relevant professional access; this is an intentional rich method, not an official full-book qualification quota; one abbreviated image slide alone does not prove source text read aloud. Prepare neutral cloud/puddle/sun illustration panels, no standing-water activity, no real dangerous storm exposure and no exact-answer-coded labels. Adult MODEL on completely different original Duck story; DURING a different fact/event from practice or additional fresh first-party factual passage; EXIT an entirely unfamiliar actual complete Garden Seed or other original full book, not a memorized Rain card; later REVISIT a new unrelated book and real later calendar date. Separate original ask and answer records; prep observer with actual text facts.

Interpretation and accessibility: A child's genuine PK4 why/how question anchored in actual read Rain text can be relevant even without a single literal book-key answer; distinguish a valid reasoning inference from a factual claim the source never stated. A child who independently selects familiar preprogrammed AAC for this new water question may still have authored it, and a separately child-owned answer to that exact question is valid. An adult/narrator supplying full question/answer is not. The story states some water goes into ground or air and warm sun can help puddles shrink; do not impose exact scientific jargon or claim every puddle vanishes instantly.

Instruction, assessment and feedback sequence

ENTRY: Provide authentic full informational book access. For PK4 actually read it ALOUD before the first source probe. Let learner ASK what they truly want to know and independently ANSWER their earlier authentic question after meaningful inquiry or another real factual/sequence book question. PK3 a full read-aloud is supported but not a published requirement. A question about an adult's shoes is conversation, not text-source evidence.

MODEL: On adult-only Duck Bridge, demonstrate asking “How did the duck get across?” and finding a plausible reply in the actual book rather than guessing that real ducks fly. Point to supporting text illustration while adult works; remove the model from child's new material.

DURING: On learner practice Rain book, invite a question about what happened before and after rain, and separately respond to their earlier own genuine book question after source inquiry or another relevant adult question. If learner says “Because of magic,” educator returns to a separate read passage and asks which detail supports the claim; do not punish imagination, but distinguish a story invention from source-specific response. Use a simple three-panel evidence map as scaffold only, then fade preordered sequence cues and picture pointers. Respect acceptable “it went into the air” without requiring “evaporation.” Ensure learner creates a question, not just selects “puddle” icon or repeats educator's prompt.

FADING: Remove highlighted sentences and answer-key pictures, question stems and yes/no forced guesses. Preserve genuine accommodations (replay, described figures, independently selected existing or composed AAC, sign, language access) without adult selecting the correct answer. A child who generated a relevant real question with a short answer may still meet source semantics; do not invent a word-count quota.

EXIT: With a wholly new actual full book, PK3.III.D.3 requires own age-appropriate book question and independently relevant answer to their own earlier question or another book question, without compulsory read-aloud exposure. PK4.III.D.3 needs actually relevant age-appropriate TEXT READ ALOUD with sufficient context for the child's genuine relevant ASK and RESPONSE; this workshop prefers a complete short story, not an official arbitrary whole-book completion threshold. Record individual exact content, date, book, actual read-aloud, conceptual H versus neutral A, not only pointing to three ordered cards.

REVISIT: Return another real day with different complete original book/text; perform novel first-response question and answer checks, PK4 aloud text again. At authoring REVISIT remains NOT_OBSERVED.

Diagnosis and real feedback

Asks interesting but off-text question: Accept curiosity, show an unrelated adult model of a text-rooted question, re-practice relevance and recheck on fresh text; PK4 source cannot credit irrelevant asks. Only answers teacher questions: Separately teach learner to ask for genuinely unknown information, then new independent probe. Rehearses a memorized rain sentence: Present unexpected fresh content and remove repeated exact frame. Confuses puddle water disappearance with “vanished into nothing”: Model accurate age-appropriate descriptive account using adult-owned example, then assess meaningful book response without imposing a specific science model. Difficult hearing or speech: Provide individual signed/accessible means with professional input, avoid asserting source-equivalent spoken read-aloud hearing without evidence. Adult points to the only matching card: H not U, reset with new material.

Seven learning/access profiles

Attention/executive: visible three-event scaffold for practice, faded before exit; language/communication: independently child-selected familiar/stored AAC or composed meaningful sign/home language, with child-chosen source intent and no partner-selected complete question/answer; literacy/phonology: actual accessible book rather than decoding test; numeracy: no sequencing-count requirement beyond understanding book relevance; autism/sensory: predictable quiet book exploration and noncoercive first turn; intellectual/developmental: begin two clear book events, model on adult text and fade location-of-answer prompts; physical/motor/hearing/vision: high contrast/tactile/described pages, safe switch/gaze access and verified text exposure with qualified individual assessment where needed.

Delivery modes and external validation

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Future engine must narrate genuinely relevant contextualized real text for PK4 (preferably the complete short selected source), allow independent question authorship and semantic response without prefilled answers. CO-PLAY: Caregiver listens to genuine questions, repeats text for access but does not highlight answer at EXIT. ADULT-LED: Educator keeps source and story comprehension separate, records both acts and interventions. CLASSROOM: Individual child turn, not collective picture selection or repeated teacher sentence; PK4 must have actual read-aloud text. No real child observation, qualified professional approval, physical-print access validation, completed delayed REVISIT or equal four-mode effectiveness is claimed.
