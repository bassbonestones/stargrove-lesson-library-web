Objective-level educator guide: needs and helpers

PK4.VII.B.1 concerns all people needing food, clothing and shelter. PK4.VII.B.3 concerns discussion of roles and responsibilities of family, school and community helpers. See the developmental sequence for the exact wording, pinned source hashes and flexible teaching steps. The archived official 2022 TEA PDF, printed page 67, was checked for these two outcomes and no PK3 outcomes. PK3 participation is enrichment only. This is an objective guide, not a counted lesson or a standardized assessment.

Keep the two objectives separate. A single food preference is not universal-needs understanding; a job label is not discussion of responsibility. Conversely a familiar learned explanation, fictional context, sign/AAC or home language does not invalidate meaningful source evidence. The child need not invent a story or an unfamiliar response. One adult can offer a neutral setting without supplying the target category or helper action. The learner may choose, change or decline it. Never require a new context merely to earn source credit; optional transfer and retention are distinct observations.

Use Needs and Support Studio and its facilitator run sheet for the DOT09 candidate. The sibling Helper Roundtable (not yet in reviewed library) and Needs Support Pathways (not yet in reviewed library) have separate mechanisms and still require their own full applied examinations. The shared directions here constrain evidence claims but do not certify sibling quality.

ENTRY welcomes a child into safe fiction with an open invitation. MODEL illustrates needs or a helper role and action on the adult's own example. DURING lets the child draw, tell, sign, use AAC or direct marks and receive needed support. EXIT invites a manageable source-relevant response without current answer cues; familiar settings and learned responses are allowed, while immediate copying and adult-fed categories remain helped. REVISIT is an actual later encounter, never a planned future score.

For B.1, explore food, clothing and shelter across people and acknowledge that needs persist when access differs. Do not rank homes, foods or family resources. For B.3, discuss a plausible role and responsibility in each of family, school and community contexts over suitable sessions. Different people may share tasks; exact titles, gender roles and paid employment are not requirements. Actual child discussion about fictional people counts as discussion; a planned software dialogue supplies no observed child evidence. Neither objective requires contacting unknown adults or visiting helpers.

Use blank paper and adult narration/sketches, not unavailable elaborate cards. Printable example pages are teaching aids with answers, not independent-check sheets. Read needed text aloud. Preserve neutral access; fade only conceptual hints. If a child provides one category, teach missing ideas without cross-credit. If a stereotype appears, explore another plausible arrangement without shame. If overloaded, simplify, stop or return later. Silence, ambiguous gestures, no opportunity and unknown help do not prove absence of knowledge.

Observation notes include only date, fictional context, actual communicated meaning, component and help. U child-owned, A access, H content help, X unclear. Record unobserved components NOT_OBSERVED; do not infer mastery, diagnoses or percent thresholds. Keep family/school/community components distinct and do not force the entire objective into one sitting. Do not collect real household details, photos or audio. If hardship is volunteered, thank the child, avoid public probing, stop or return to fiction and follow the setting's safeguarding/support procedures.

ADULT-LED uses one adult and one child, roughly 5–12 minutes flexibly. CO-PLAY includes real adult contributions with attribution. CLASSROOM uses a safely supervised shared scene and meaningful paper/discussion activity while the teacher listens to one focal child; rotate later and leave others NOT_OBSERVED. No group chorus or copied product proves everyone's understanding. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED; future game accessibility, learner outcomes and parity are unverified. Physical-print accessibility and external teacher/child usability have not been tested. These shared revisions are written candidate design work only.
