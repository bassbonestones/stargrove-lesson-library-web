Facilitator run sheet: Needs and Support Studio

For the full lesson. PK4.VII.B.1 is understanding that all people need food, clothing and shelter. PK4.VII.B.3 is discussing family, school and community helper roles and responsibilities. They are separate objectives; one does not prove the other. PK3 has no published Social Studies outcome here and may join as enrichment. These written materials are a candidate design, not evidence of a child's learning.

Before teaching

Prepare two blank sheets, a marker, and optional safe drawing tools. Draw or narrate anonymous fictional people; detailed picture cards are not supplied or required. Offer a pretend home, school or library and let the child change it. Do not use real family photos, food, addresses, income questions or household comparisons. Read or describe necessary material. Allow AAC, sign, home language, child-directed scribing, gaze or a hands-free route using the child's established supports. Clarify ambiguous communication without inventing an answer. Keep help with content separate from help accessing materials.

The original printable example pages contain answers. Use those for teaching only, not as proof from a completed page. Blank paper is sufficient. See the full plan for source boundaries, safe disclosure response and minimal observation notes. Familiar learned responses are legitimate; novelty and inventing a story are not source requirements.

ENTRY: invite rather than test without warning

“Would you like to make up a story at home, school or the library?” Accept another safe choice or offer to start a story. Ask “What kinds of things do all people need?” or “Who might help here, and what could they do?” Begin with one focus. Wait, offer access, or teach. Silence and choosing not to act are not diagnostic. Stop when wanted; no prescribed number of successful trials.

MODEL: make your contribution visible

On your own sheet, explain that people can eat different foods and wear different clothing while all needing food, clothing and shelter. Needs exist even when access is unequal. For helpers, explain a plausible role and action, such as a custodian keeping a school usable. Different people can hold these roles. Do not demand a particular family structure, occupation title or gender. Invite another reasonable possibility. An echoed model is supported practice, not independently observed knowledge.

DURING: let the child make a meaningful decision

Let the child draw, tell, sign, use AAC or direct marks. Explore one need or helper context at a time. If a child names one food, acknowledge it as food, then teach other categories without cross-crediting your prompts. Avoid good/bad food judgments or a nutrition lesson. If the child names a job only, ask “What might they do?” If a stereotype appears, give a non-stereotyped possibility and discuss rather than shame. If a child is ready, offer an optional contrasting setting; if overwhelmed, simplify and return later. Do not require every helper to satisfy every need.

EXIT: make a limited inference

Put away answer examples and invite an open response. Familiar contexts are acceptable. Record prior modeling and actual help; immediate copying does not establish independence. Needs evidence concerns all people and food, clothing and shelter, not a prompted chant. Helper evidence concerns actual child discussion of a role and responsibility in each of family, school and community contexts across suitable opportunities. A child discussing fictional people is having an actual discussion; a hypothetical future app supplies no child evidence. Partial components remain partial, not failure of the whole child.

A private note records date, setting, exact contribution, which component it supports, and adult help. U means child-owned content; A means neutral access; H means conceptual help; X means unclear provenance. X cannot warrant independence but is not proof of inability. Missing/inaccessible opportunity is NOT_OBSERVED. No mastery threshold or diagnosis is authorized. If a child discloses hardship, thank them, do not probe publicly or record it as curriculum evidence, and follow established safeguarding/support procedures.

REVISIT and stop

Thank the child and finish before fatigue or frustration. On a genuinely later date, offer another suitable encounter, familiar or varied, and record what occurs then. REVISIT remains NOT_OBSERVED until actual. A future date or same-session repeat is not retention.

Routes

ADULT-LED: One adult and one child can use this run sheet in a flexible 5–12 minute encounter, shorter if appropriate. Teaching one component is worthwhile; the complete objectives can develop over multiple sessions. CLASSROOM: One teacher uses one shared fictional scene and safe paper materials in a supervised area. Others draw or discuss their own made-up scenes while one focal child has a brief conversation. Observe only what safe supervision allows, rotate focal turns later, and record unobserved pupils as NOT_OBSERVED. If oversight is compromised, do group practice only. Choral answers and copied peer products are not individual evidence. Quiet/private alternatives and passing are allowed. CO-PLAY: Adult and child genuinely contribute different ideas and can revise them together. Attribute help rather than pretend an adult idea originated with the learner. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: No prereader application or outcome-equivalent digital route exists here. Human teaching does not depend on game development.

No real teacher usability trial, child outcomes, physical print accessibility acceptance, completed retention visit, or parity across routes is claimed. The full plan is authoritative for this candidate, and shared family materials do not confer acceptance on sibling lessons.
