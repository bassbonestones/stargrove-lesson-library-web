Optional directions for punctuation exploration

PK4.IV.C.5: “Child begins to experiment with punctuation when writing.” Research-cell SHA f35dd0e0c5a9b4335009a5d5d7e83f657ee4f77b0fc314be6e20469bd26ae7cf. PK3.IV.C.5 is an explicit non-outcome research note, not a mandatory target. Direct publisher comparison is in SOURCE_CONCORDANCE_DOT23.md.

The following are teacher choices, not validated developmental stages, required levels or mastery prerequisites. Revisit earlier choices, combine them or omit them according to the child's interest and access.

PK4 P4-1 — Notice and try

ENTRY can be naturally occurring writing, not a required diagnostic pretest. MODEL a dot in your writing if welcome. DURING invite a mark in personal or interactive writing. EXIT describes the actual contribution and help; familiar writing is valid. REVISIT on a later occasion only if it occurs.

PK4 P4-2 — Explore a thought-ending mark

Try a dot in a meaningful note; approximate placement is welcome. A teacher prompt is permitted. Do not require a spoken explanation, original sentence or conventional grammar. Keep decorative dots and teacher-made marks distinguishable from observed child experimentation.

PK4 P4-3 — Explore another expressive possibility

A child may try a question-like or excitement-like mark if useful to their message or curiosity. No child must use both types, label them or master the conventions. Home-language writing practices and accessible choices remain valid.

PK4 P4-4 — Return to a useful writing purpose

Return to the same observation, friendly note or safe toy direction. Offer another purpose if the child wants it. New contexts can reveal transfer but are not necessary to recognize beginning experimentation in a familiar context.

PK4 P4-5 — Explore with different amounts of help

Offer less help when appropriate and record what actually occurred. Independent performance is a different claim from supported participation, not an eligibility gate for the source. A child can learn through repeated models and prompted interactive writing.

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: no app parity claim. CO-PLAY can use shared text with separate contributions. ADULT-LED responds to one child's actual interest. CLASSROOM requires realistic supervision and attributable observations; unobserved turns remain NOT_OBSERVED. These written routes do not establish empirical effectiveness. No PK3 punctuation track is invented.

These teaching choices can recur in any order; the numbering organizes this guide and is not an age ranking or developmental diagnostic scale.
