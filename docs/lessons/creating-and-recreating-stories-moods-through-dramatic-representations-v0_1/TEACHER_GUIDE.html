Objective-level family guide — Meaningful dramatic play

PK4.VIII.C.1 in the 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines reads: “Child creates or recreates stories, moods, or experiences through dramatic representations.” Printed pages 73–74 identify no PK3 outcome here and include acting a read-aloud, creating with peers, using props and recreating everyday events. Page 8 says these guidelines are developmental guidance, not a curriculum, assessment tool or checklist. Source-cell SHA-256: 65c74d87275652b9e9df4a721b1a8b83d73c1a69769a6118b605b13326a265dc.

What the source permits

Creates OR recreates; stories OR moods OR experiences. One meaningful portrayal can be relevant. Neither all combinations, completely new material, polished acting, fluent speech, a stereotyped facial display nor public performance is required. Familiar pretend routines, culturally meaningful roles, repeated lines used intentionally as a character and borrowed gestures can be genuine dramatic representation. Record how teaching, adult/peer direction and access supports contributed; do not turn re-creation into automatic false credit.

Watching alone, static scenery or a teacher moving every prop without child meaning do not by themselves demonstrate the child's representation. Conversely, voice in role, sign/AAC character dialogue, a small meaningful gesture and intentionally directed neutral prop assistance may do so. Ask if meaning is unclear; don't force verbal explanation or invent intent. Never diagnose a child from performance, refusal or fictional expression.

Three routes and acceptance boundaries

Lesson 01 invites a child-owned little adventure, allowing familiar scenes and recreation. Its facilitator and two human guides contain the current scoped repair. Lesson 02 starts from a heard story and Lesson 03 from a fictional mood/experience. Those sibling plans remain historical drafts, not accepted by this lesson's review. Their stricter novelty phrasing must not govern Lesson 01. Family-level repairs here are source-bound corrections, not earned review of every sibling.

Teaching choices

ENTRY invites voluntary meaning but is not a prerequisite role-choice test before help. MODEL, if wanted, briefly makes pretend meaning visible in a safe unrelated example; a child may meaningfully recreate it. DURING follows purposeful play with responsive questions and neutral assistance. EXIT is a fresh observation opportunity without immediate answer-leading cues, not necessarily a new scene. REVISIT means an actual later return, currently NOT_OBSERVED. Changed context is optional transfer exploration. Reduce instructional prompts, never required communication or motor access. See DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md for flexible possibilities, not a compulsory ladder.

Materials and practical organization

No mandatory kit, script, costume or printing. Optional props must be large, stable, lightweight and safe for actual learners; avoid small pieces, neck ties, covered faces and climbing. Use the first page of the two family printables for Lesson 01; pages 2–3 are other invitations, not extra required checks. The forms are optional private adult records, not scored child worksheets. Keep real child information outside Git and public sites.

One-child play can be brief and child-led. CLASSROOM delivery must maintain one teacher's real supervision: visible in-place/familiar play, optional quiet participation, a few focal observations and later equitable rotation. Do not require all children to be assessed in one session or use group output as individual proof. Stop/simplify if notes compromise safety. CO-PLAY follows child meaning; ADULT-LED does not prescribe a correct plot. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED; no working app, classroom outcome or mode-equivalence claim.

Official passage concordance for pages 8, 73–74 is recorded in DOT06 source evidence; this does not certify every sibling. Tests, hashes and written simulations are not observed learning, an external educator trial or physical print validation.
