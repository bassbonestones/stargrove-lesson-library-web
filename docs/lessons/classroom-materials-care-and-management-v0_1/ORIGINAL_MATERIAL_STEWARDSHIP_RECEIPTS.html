01 · Care means action, not a cleanup badge

Adult observation and misconception correction

A child can name the correct storage shelf on an illustrated card but never actually manage the real materials. Conversely an adult may independently manage all classroom items while a child watches. Both can support instruction, yet neither alone proves the learner taking care of and managing actual supplies.

PK3 permits meaningful real adult assistance. PK4 does not forbid needed child-directed access supports. Document who selected the safe action and who actually contributed to caring and organizing usable real supplies.

Use only clean oversized safe stable materials. Child should not handle sharp, electrical, chemical, hot or heavy objects, and must not be punished or forced to “earn” access through cleanup. Requesting adult repair help is appropriate.

02 · A real project uses shared safe supplies

Teacher's separate adult-owned MODEL, not independent child source

One teacher makes a benign large-card display with broad clean mats. Adult first chooses usable materials, protects items during use, and returns them after the work is complete, asking for help with anything difficult.

During an existing supervised activity, invite the child to choose/participate with real familiar materials, caring for and managing supplies, with full PK3 adult assistance where necessary and PK4 authentic learner decision and actual source action.

A “supply keeper” sticker, puppet tool, or teacher making every decision does not become child material management. Respect peer property and permissions, safe individually accessible tasks, sensory and motor supports.
