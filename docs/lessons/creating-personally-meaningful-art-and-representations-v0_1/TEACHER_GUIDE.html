Three different artistic opportunities

  1. Child-authored imaginary place: child makes or directs personally meaningful marks about an invented, remembered or mixed place. A single mark can suffice. The meaningful operation is choosing a spatial symbol or detail, not drawing an accurate map.
  2. Expressive mark exploration: child may vary stroke, spacing, color or texture to express a personally chosen idea or fictional mood. There is no universal “blue means sad” rule and no forced emotional disclosure.
  3. Relational collage: child may arrange safe broad pieces to express a meaningful relation, event or story. A teacher-composed layout is not the child's creation merely because the child applies glue.

These are local planning alternatives, not sequential levels. Tool changes alone do not establish different cognitive mechanisms; the relevant distinction is what the child chooses and means. A child can combine routes without being forced to demonstrate all three. A remembered or culturally familiar pattern can be personally expressive; novelty is not a competition.

Shared teaching principles, applied concretely in Lesson 01

ENTRY: Offer a first opportunity for child-owned action or choice. The child may make, choose a tool, watch or pause. Do not require a named theme, spoken plan or already meaningful symbol before help is offered.

MODEL: If helpful and the child chooses to watch, an adult may make a brief separate example before the child chooses a theme. Explain the adult's own choice and meaning; do not assign the child's intended content. If the child already has a theme, use a different adult example. Do not interrupt absorbed making just to complete a model phase.

DURING: Follow the child's own making/direction. Invite meaning at a natural pause without guessing it. Accept own language, sign, communication device or interpretable gesture. A point can locate a mark without revealing meaning; uncertainty or privacy is not inability. A child can assign meaning after making or change it. Ask consent for physical assistance, and distinguish access help from adult artistic decisions.

EXIT: For Lesson 01, an optional fresh uncued choice can use the same theme and familiar accessible tools, on blank paper or unmodeled space. Remove answer-bearing content hints, not access support. A new sheet alone does not establish independence. Declining an end task does not erase actual earlier evidence.

REVISIT: Offer a later actual opportunity when appropriate. Familiar continuation can support practice; a changed medium/setting is a separate optional accessible transfer opportunity, not mandatory evidence of authorship. Later learning is NOT_OBSERVED until actual action/date is recorded. Neither date nor new material alone proves retention or generalization.

Ownership, access and observation

Actual child creation or directed action with personally attributed meaning is relevant to this goal. Adult labeling, prompted assent and an attractive final product without known origin do not establish child authorship. A borrowed technique plus the child's own meaningful choices can be valid; copying with no child-owned meaning is not the same evidence.

Keep a brief private note of actual action, child's meaning as communicated, adult/peer content suggestions, neutral motor/communication access and a useful next step. Do not demand a story to complete a form. No public child records, family photographs or diagnoses from art are needed. Routine approved private educator records suffice.

Use familiar age-safe nontoxic tools, stable positioning and perceptible marks. Established adapted grips, tactile systems or consented child-directed proxy may preserve authorship. Do not improvise sharp equipment or assume a verbal description replaces access to making. If essential access or supervision is unavailable, offer an accessible opportunity and defer the particular inference.

Human organization and limits

CO-PLAY / ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: a caregiver/educator listens and responds, with flexible time and pause/return options. CLASSROOM: ongoing individual art, safely supervised focal observations and fair rotation across sessions; no compulsory gallery or group product substituted for individual evidence. See the current Lesson 01 guides for complete quantities, transitions and staffing constraints. Do not infer an extra adult.
