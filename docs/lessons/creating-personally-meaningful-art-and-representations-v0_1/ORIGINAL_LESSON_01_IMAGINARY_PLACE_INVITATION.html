Lesson 01 · optional aid for adult planning

Make a place from your imagination

Invite the child to make or direct art and choose what its marks mean. A remembered, invented, mixed or abstract place is welcome. One meaningful mark can be enough.

Adult planning illustration only. Keep this picture out of the child's working space. It is not a template, a required subject or a picture the child must match.

Invite, then follow the maker

Offer blank paper and familiar safe drawing tools: “You can make a place from your imagination. What would you like to try?” Let the child make, choose a tool, watch or pause. They may decide what a mark means after making it.

If a demonstration would help, use your own paper and a different idea. A child who chooses to watch does not have to name a theme first. Put your example aside afterward; do not interrupt a child already absorbed in art.

At a natural pause, offer one invitation:

“Is there anything you want me to know about this part?”

Listen to the child's words, home language, sign, communication device or clear gesture. Do not guess a meaning from color or shape. Quiet or private meaning is welcome.

Let the child stop, keep working or use fresh space with the same theme and tools. Keep real earlier observations if they decline another turn. Borrowing a technique can support their own meaning; an adult's picture or label is not evidence of the child's authorship.
