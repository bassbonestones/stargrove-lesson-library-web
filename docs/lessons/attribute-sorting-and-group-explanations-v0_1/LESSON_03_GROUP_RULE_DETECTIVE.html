Teach the Secret Sorting Rule Detective

Teacher-friendly guide. The fun is discovering why objects belong together and helping someone else understand a meaningful rule.

What the child learns

A child sorts objects by a feature that genuinely fits each group. The detective can then explain how someone could place a new object correctly.

For the 3-year-old group, the important goal is real same/different sorting, even through action or gesture. For the 4-year-old group, the child also describes what is alike within a group and how groups differ. The explanation may be in spoken language, sign, home language or expressive communication aid.

Prepare a good mystery

Have large safe tactile or visual objects with noticeable consistent properties—perhaps some smooth and some bumpy, or some with rounded edges and some with corners. Keep an adult-only model set and a new unfamiliar independent set. A pretend listener who cannot see the child's groups can motivate explanation, but no actual person should be tricked or excluded.

The detective game

1. Let the child make groups first

Say: “Can you make groups of things that belong together? Show me how you decided.”

Wait for the child's placements. If ready, ask “What is alike in this group? How is the other group different?” Do not supply a feature label beforehand.

2. Show an unrelated mystery

Sort different adult-owned objects, such as smooth pieces together and bumpy ones elsewhere.

Say: “I used how these feel. If I find another bumpy piece, this rule tells me which group it belongs with.”

Demonstrate explaining the actual shared property, not a memorized bin title.

3. Let the child help a listener

Offer a new safe group of objects and ask:

“Imagine I cannot see where you placed them. How would you help me know which group this new piece belongs in?”

Let the child choose the feature and guide the new item's placement. If their explanation is unclear, explore the actual properties on separate practice pieces. Do not expect an older child to say one prescribed sentence.

4. Try a new secret set

Clear model items and give an unseen collection plus a fresh item to classify.

Observe the independently chosen meaningful rule, placement and—when part of the age goal—an actual child-origin explanation of likeness and contrast. For a 3-year-old, avoid marking genuine sorting deficient for not speaking a full rule.

5. Revisit a new set another day

Ask about an unfamiliar real object on a genuine later date. Do not backfill future evidence.

Respond

Observation Next move
The child sorts consistently using a feature different from yours Accept the valid rule and ask about their choice
They switch between unrelated features unintentionally Model one feature at a time on different objects
They point correctly but cannot say the rule Value genuine younger-stage sorting; provide supported language opportunities
Their sign or AAC message accurately explains the difference Accept the semantic explanation
They simply repeat the adult's rule Offer fresh objects and a child-authored choice

Teach the Secret Sorting Rule Detective with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

A child sorts objects by a feature that genuinely fits each group. The detective can then explain how someone could place a new object correctly.

For the 3-year-old group, the important goal is real same/different sorting, even through action or gesture. For the 4-year-old group, the child also describes what is alike within a group and how groups differ. The explanation may be in spoken language, sign, home language or expressive communication aid.

Prepare the classroom

Gather a mixed set of safe objects that can be grouped by more than one legitimate attribute. Prepare an unrelated adult-only demonstration. Allow enough shared pieces for children to create mystery groups without requiring writing or reading.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “Could you make secret groups and give us clues so someone can work out the rule?” Show, with separate example pieces, how a description should explain why one object fits and another doesn't.

Give children meaningful practice

Small groups alternate as sorter and detective: the sorter chooses a coherent rule and arranges objects; the detective observes and explains a possible rule, then checks with the sorter. Rotate so every willing child can make and explain a grouping. Teacher sees original child decisions before peers supply an explanation.

Observe individual learning fairly

In an individual fresh example, notice whether the child makes an attribute-based grouping, identifies a plausible consistent rule for someone else's grouping, or revises a proposed rule when shown a counterexample. These are different actions—don't mark full flexible explanation from a correct guess alone.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Do not reject a coherent, culturally familiar or sensory-based grouping merely because it differs from the educator's intended rule. Offer accessible tactile or high-contrast items and an option to point to a counterexample. A child may observe or return later; do not force a spoken secret.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

Method 03 — Secret Sorting Rule and Blind Listener Explanation

Original E2.2 fully written third teaching mechanism; read DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. Official PK3.V.E.1 sorts safe same/different objects (SHA 8b2b6b110dc7c30ae2d79582e51fcfdde89db00528fdbfce233b1afbf8702f93); PK4.V.E.1 sorts objects into groups and uses language to describe how those groups are alike/different (SHA f15247bc25c6278ea982d8489dce31711cd0731ad926895ef8993fe064d36a05). Mechanism: learner constructs or deduces a grouping rule from real perceptible evidence and communicates it meaningfully to a listener who cannot see the answer, then tests a new counterexample. Different from hands-on sensory matching (method 01) and explicit same-set changing-rule comparison (method 02). For PK3, the inference element is simple: find the pair that belong together and place contrasting item aside, without mandated verbal rule-giving or complex guessing games.

Original test objects and meaningful game constraints

Prepare safe oversized nonchoking blunt clean tangible pieces or original accessible large tactile cards varying in two independently perceivable ways (shape/color/raised texture), under adult-known but child-not-labeled attribute relationships. A novice 3-piece set: two same rounded shapes differing in color plus one unlike polygon. A later 4–6 object set supports defensible grouping by at least one clear rule and one ambiguous shared feature to prevent teaching a brittle memorized picture-key. Alternate input/set differs between first ENTRY, teacher's MODEL, DURING and fresh EXIT. Provide a neutral “hidden listener” represented by a non-identifiable toy microphone/stuffed prop or actual adult who turns away; a peer does not need to know private child data. Do not preprint answer labels or make success hinge on a mystery rule known only to teacher when child's alternate sorting is logically coherent. Use original ORIGINAL_RULE_DETECTIVE_CARDS.html only as illustration; source EXIT must include learner-origin physical object grouping or accessible equivalent, not memorized graphic position. No tiny blocks, food or real child classification.

ENTRY before MODEL: Offer unseen big set, “Can you make groups of objects that belong together? Show me how you decided.” Learner chooses own feature. PK3 moves similar vs different real pieces; PK4 child sorts at least two meaningful groups, then explains to blind listener “How could I tell which objects belong in each group, and how are the groups different?” Record exactly first placements and language before any model answer.

MODEL on DIFFERENT teacher-only objects: Adult makes a physical 2-group arrangement with separate novel safe set, then turns toward pretend blind listener: “These two all have a smooth side. These other pieces all have a bumpy side. Both are toys, but their surfaces feel different. If you feel a new bumpy piece, you can tell it belongs over here.” Teacher does not use the same features/objects as child's later independent EXIT if they create answer-leak shortcuts. The teacher demonstrates explanation based on observable property rather than a fixed bin title.

DURING: Learner child-directed handles own safe props to sort, then tells an adult or imaginary listener how to classify a new safe object. A genuine rule must decide where that example belongs under the same feature, not change from color to shape opportunistically. Adult can ask open “What is alike?” and “How is the other group different?” but if adult suggests criterion or phrase (“say all round”), record H and re-probe different untouched set. PK3 can point/act to demonstrate matching under chosen feature; no mandatory spoken class labels. PK4 own semantic speech, sign, home language or expressive AAC must actually convey within-/between-group relationship, not merely tap an adult label.

Fresh independent EXIT: entirely different safe real array, visual layout rotated and one ambiguous item sharing multiple features. Learner independently makes coherent alike/different grouping; PK4 additionally independently communicates meaningful similarity within actual groups and difference between them, in child-chosen speech, home language, sign or meaningful AAC. A blind listener may help give this communication genuine purpose, and predicting where an extra unseen object belongs under a chosen rule is valuable optional transfer enrichment; neither another listener understanding the clue nor correctly classifying an additional item is a mandatory grade-source qualification hurdle. A novel counterexample is internal stronger evidence, not official requirement that child solve all paradoxes. EXIT recorded U/A/H/X and child-origin actual trait; if unsupported, mark incomplete/unclear. REVISIT genuinely later calendar date new object set, NOT_OBSERVED.

PK3 developmental stages

PK3 S3-1: ENTRY two matching safe tactile outlines and unlike object, child groups; MODEL different teacher-safe pair tells pretend listener “these are alike,” DURING child gestures group, EXIT novel similar vs different physical pieces placed correctly. False-positive: chooses two nearest objects; teach distinct spread-out objects and new uncued example. REVISIT later date.

PK3 S3-2: ENTRY new set with clear texture difference, learner groups one property; MODEL adult-owned smooth-vs-bumpy pieces, DURING learner points/touches own unlabelled groups, EXIT new set under stable property. If child copies adult precolored bins, hide color/labels and new probe. REVISIT later date.

PK3 S3-3: ENTRY new two round differently colored and one red square, child may choose valid shape or color criterion; MODEL different adult-only objects sharing outline despite contrasting colors, DURING child places one new object under own consistent feature, EXIT new mixed-trait independent group. No official requirement to explain the chosen rule in English. REVISIT later date.

PK3 S3-4: ENTRY unfamiliar safe real object set with likeness and difference, child independently arranges; MODEL separate teacher example, DURING new assisted trial with physical/tactile access, full unmodeled source EXIT new real three+ objects divided into defensible alike vs different grouping, not random guess. No requirement to label abstract sorting-rule concept. REVISIT different calendar date, NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 developmental stages with true language

PK4 S4-1: ENTRY novel grouping before labels, own “these alike because…” if able; MODEL teacher-only safe features and blind listener explanation, DURING child practices expressing shared trait, EXIT new actual groups with meaningful own language about similarity. If echo only, different object sample and new lexical no-cue probe. REVISIT later.

PK4 S4-2: ENTRY new set requiring groups with shared attribute and meaningful difference; MODEL teacher distinct smooth vs bumpy props, describing inside group AND between groups; DURING child tells listener how groups similar/differ, EXIT unseen set sorting plus relational explanation. Isolated “red, blue” words do not always demonstrate descriptive comparison; accept “this group red, those blue” when clearly tied to feature. REVISIT later.

PK4 S4-3: ENTRY new two-feature set (e.g. color/shape) without adult secret rule; learner chooses consistent arrangement, MODELS unrelated valid grouping, DURING listener tests one new safe item against child rule, EXIT novel array and child own language explaining why new member belongs. Rule changing is optional only, not a required state standard. REVISIT later.

PK4 S4-4: ENTRY new ambiguous item sharing different feature with different candidate groups, child selects currently intended shared attribute and says how groups alike and different. MODEL on distinct teacher objects “it belongs with the corners group under shape, but with yellow under color”; DURING child explains own current consistent choice and where new piece goes; EXIT new ambiguous set independent explanation. If child changes rule only after placing a wrong piece, teach other set then novel first response, no post hoc credit. REVISIT later.

PK4 S4-5: ENTRY new unlabelled safe real object set, learner independently sorts and gives descriptive reason to listener. MODEL unrelated source if needed; DURING responds to teacher critique or checks another property, full source EXIT new physical objects grouped meaningfully with child-origin language describing a shared within-group property and a between-group difference. Responding by semantically composed AAC/sign/home language meets expressive action; teacher-fed sentence does not. Independent later-date REVISIT with new objects, NOT_OBSERVED.

Four fully written mode routes, not claims of equal effectiveness

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Future prereader tablet features a blind virtual listener and high-contrast accessible textured/shape visual/tactile object representations. PK3 user independently assigns own same/different piles; PK4 chooses groups then independently chooses semantically accurate expressive language including preprogrammed AAC descriptor words/phrases to communicate rule explanation in expressive AAC/sign/voice/home language rather than selecting an answer-key bin or repeating synthetic narrator. Virtual listener presents one NEW object and asks where it belongs under child rule. Input supports gaze/switch/keyboard/tactile/large buttons and narration; interaction must record actual first response, blind prompts, hints H vs access A, actual new EXIT and real future-date REVISIT. A well-written spec is not a built app; sign/AAC semantic assessment and real-object transfer require actual usability trials. No demonstrated mode parity.

CO-PLAY: Family member prepares oversized safe actual objects with hidden extra trait; child selects and physically groups while adult uses separate model first. Then caregiver acts blind listener and follows child-origin group explanation; PK3 may communicate placement/gestures, PK4 independently describes shared feature plus how groups differ, in home language/AAC/sign. Adult hands may move pieces only under actual child direction (A); adult grouping and words H. Novel unmodeled EXIT and later different-date revisit.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: Tutor first checks real sensory access/feature meaning, then child-origin ENTRY, models on other safe object family, guides DURING group-placement and nuanced why questions, then new untouched EXIT with self-chosen defensible rule. Child must not be marked inaccurate for a different valid grouping, and PK4 must provide meaningful own expressive similarity/difference relation. Provide sign/AAC/home-language, visual/tactile and motor proxy, family/team accommodations as applicable; no diagnosis inferred from unfamiliar rules.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Whole class sees unrelated teacher demonstration, teacher models grouping clues to a blind listener; each student then receives their own safe oversized novel object set away from peers’ answer hints, sorts or directs motor proxy, and (PK4) independently explains grouping evidence to another child/adult without choral phrases. Record individual first-source response and language semantics; never sort children by personal traits. PK3 accepted action may be nonverbal. The new future-date revisit is scheduled, not actually observed.

Adversarial audit, correction and boundary

False-positive child 1: learner copies two teacher-labeled piles and says “green group” without recognizing any feature shared inside; listener already knows correct arrangement. Correction: remove answer labels, model DIFFERENT safe attribute, new unmodeled physical grouping plus blind listener test of a new object; child responds independently.

False-positive child 2: teacher says “they are all round,” PK4 repeats exact words or picks precomposed AAC response; educator treats as original language. Correction: teacher demonstrates on different feature, then learner creates new own explanation for different actual groups using independently chosen meaningful expressive AAC/sign/home language, including stored AAC phrases when the child controls correct relational semantics; original adult-supplied H remains documented.

False-positive child 3: child groups two things by size but says by color afterward, yet teacher accepts any claimed answer. Correction: check actual property versus placements under the child-declared or consistent inferable criterion; use new real set with conflicting secondary attribute, ask blind listener to place one new member, and record new unhinted first action. No arbitrary “all possible rules” standard.

Limitations: Blind-listener language adds cognitive demand; PK3 must first have simple accessible sorting success rather than a sophisticated guess game. The actual materials' shape and color accessibility require real-world validation. These methods remain author-reviewed draft only; no actual child evidence, externally qualified educator assessment, physical/tactile print, implemented SOLO, genuinely delayed revisit or equal four-mode effectiveness.

Retrospective learning-differences remediation — 2026-11-01

This is an INTERNAL AUTHOR WRITTEN instructional revision, not observed child efficacy or delivered software.

PK3: actually sorted safe real objects by meaningful same/different attribute

Source action: Child SORTS objects that are SAME and DIFFERENT using a defensible observable property; does not need PK4 verbal group explanation or a compulsory two-bin/color-only test. Teaching/access and fading: Keep detective's attribute evidence concrete: learner actually groups safe objects by coherent shared property; language explanation not mandatory PK3. Fresh child-origin check: Unseen everyday objects: learner independently distinguishes objects sharing a function even when sizes differ. False-credit adversarial case: Adult detective declares rule and child names it but never actually sorts a set, so PK3 source verb not observed. Passive watching, educator piles prearranged, grouping by size of numerals, color mat revealing answer or mandatory explanation cannot be credited as PK3 independent sorting.

PK4: independent attribute groups plus expressed similarity and difference grounded in actual objects

Source action: Child sorts safe objects into meaningfully SAME/DIFFERENT groups AND uses real expressive LANGUAGE (speech, home language, sign or own AAC) explaining how groups are SIMILAR AND DIFFERENT. Teaching/access and fading: Use original detective challenge where child independently infers defensible object property, sorts full novel collection and explains both within-group commonality and between-group contrast. Fresh child-origin check: Fresh brush/spoon/cup items: child explains sorting by function plus difference rather than guessed bin label. False-credit adversarial case: Teacher's group-rule hint is also the exact answer, or child can merely name category but never independently sort objects. Placing objects without reason, saying 'red/blue' from printed bins, a single identical duplicate, or teacher-composed AAC sentence does not demonstrate PK4's sorting PLUS both explanations. Seven access profiles: attention_and_executive_function: Present a small safe set with shared and contrasting attributes, neutral uncued bin spaces and predictable turns; model change of rule on DIFFERENT objects then fade colored bin edges.; language_processing_and_communication: Accept learner-chosen sign, home-language, semantic AAC or original photo descriptions of group similarities AND differences; neutral translation, not adult-completed scripted reason.; literacy_and_phonological_processing: Use real original safe tangible objects that differ in color, shape and use, with unlabeled sorting spaces; no written word decoding prerequisites.; numeracy_and_mathematical_processing: Vary group sizes and allow more than two defensible piles; contrast actual color, form or purpose while removing numbered bin markers.; autism_predictability_and_sensory_needs: Offer predictable quiet visual or sensory-safe tactile objects, no compulsory touch, learner-directed private response and preview a new sorting criterion before practice.; intellectual_and_developmental_support: Model sorting on different safe items with explicit 'same round despite colors'; fade picture outlines, adult sorting and single correct bin.; physical_motor_hearing_vision_access: Use large nonchoking high-contrast/tactile shapes with reliable audible descriptions and child-controlled gaze/switch proxy, preserving real category decisions. Delivery routes (written, not executable): SOLO must not supply the answer or imply working accessible software; CO-PLAY permits neutral caregiver facilitation, not conceptual answers; ADULT-LED uses a different modeled example before an uncued source probe; CLASSROOM checks each learner independently, not choral compliance. Individualize based on real child/family and relevant specialist plans; retain modality access while fading answer hints.

Explicit objective-specific EXIT and later transfer interpretation

PK3.V.E.1: On the completely unused real-object EXIT bank, invite the learner to independently sort all same/different items into a coherent grouping. A first-response child-selected rule is valid if the actual objects support it; no second-sort criterion, blind-listener explanation, spoken comparative sentence, or category label is required by this published outcome. Later-calendar-date REVISIT is fresh physical sorting and remains NOT_OBSERVED until witnessed.

PK4.V.E.1: On a different untouched real-object EXIT bank, first observe the learner grouping objects, then elicit an independently formulated similarity property within groups and meaningful difference between groups. Both the child's actual sorting and source-specific original language (home language, child-origin sign/AAC or speech, including independently selected preprogrammed descriptive AAC words/phrases accurately communicating the actual group similarities and differences) are necessary; another person's guessed secret rule, rote category labels, or adult-composed message cannot stand in. Genuinely later-date REVISIT observes both physical grouping and meaningful relational language without hints; remains NOT_OBSERVED.

Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 03: Group Rule Detective and Blind Listener

Pair: Lesson 03, linked to PK3.V.E.1 and PK4.V.E.1. Operational teaching guidance, not a lesson-plan count. Refer to objective guide for source scope.

Teaching mechanism and guardrails

The learner builds real groups under a coherent perceivable property, then tests whether another person who did not see the placement can use child-origin clues to identify the grouping rule. This social “information gap” targets PK4's meaningful language: describing a shared property and a contrast between groups. It must NOT become an artificial PK3 verbal puzzle. For PK3, the detective story is optional motivation for a real source-eligible object sort without mandatory explanation. This lesson is independent of Stargrove lore and fictional characters.

Prepare actual investigation

Provide adult-inspected safe oversized physical items with deliberately confounded attributes: e.g., two round textured outlines in different colors and two angular textured outlines with matching colors; accessible alternative sets may contrast surface or function. Prepare four distinct material banks for ENTRY, MODEL, DURING and novel EXIT, plus different safe items for later-calendar-date REVISIT. A peer/adult blind listener may actually turn away; do not claim a simulated or preprogrammed listener verifies a human conversation. Have plain mat, unsorted item IDs, note page for first physical groupings, exact child's explanation and access/hint codes. No name/family/personality classification, consent pressure or diagnosis.

Instructional sequence for the facilitator

  1. ENTRY: Child receives a clean unfamiliar physical set. Ask “Show me the groups you would make.” Note first sort without covert colored answer spaces. With PK4, let a listener ask “How are pieces in this group alike, and how is this group different from that one?” No prewritten answer choices.
  2. MODEL: Adult groups a different set, then tells a truly uninformed adult listener “These all have corners; those have curved edges.” The listener uses the statement to place a separate demonstration piece. Explicitly show the difference between saying a label and giving evidence about the groups.
  3. DURING: Child chooses their own sort of new practice objects. A partner can ask “What do they have in common?” and “What's different about the other group?” but may not name the attribute, complete AAC phrases, place pieces or tell a secret rule. For PK3, let learner communicate choices by physical placement, gaze or gestures; explanatory performance is enrichment only.
  4. FADING: Remove detective picture hints, spoken comparison stems, labeled bins and visual answer shadows. The learner must make the full actual sort; not merely identify an adult pile. If the blind listener guesses wrongly because the original clue was vague, offer feedback about missing detail on practice pieces, then move to an untouched set.
  5. EXIT: Present independent unseen objects; PK3.V.E.1 requires a coherent child-origin sort of same/different objects with no forced language. PK4.V.E.1 requires the sort PLUS meaningful child-controlled expression of similarity within groups and difference across groups in speech, home language, sign or AAC, including independently selected preprogrammed descriptive words/phrases. A successful blind-listener guess or classifying an extra new item is an optional transfer challenge, not a condition of official source evidence. When speech is not the child's expression channel, capture semantically child-selected sign, home language or AAC, including independently chosen meaningful stored descriptive vocabulary but not an adult-selected correct complete answer, with any interpretation accurately attributed. A listener's accurate guess does not substitute for actual child-created groups or actual relational language.
  6. REVISIT: On a genuinely later date use new unlabeled physical objects and a different listener if appropriate. Capture unassisted first response separately from reteaching; retention NOT_OBSERVED at authoring.

Mistake-specific decision tree

Child only points to adult groups: On a different adult example model that the detective must make their own full piles; guide on new practice pieces; use fresh independent EXIT. PK4 supplies category word without comparison: Adult models “all rough / other smooth” on unrelated teacher objects, then fades prompts and repeats with fresh child-generated comparisons. Teacher/peer tells secret rule: Record H for affected practice; do not retrospectively call language independent. One item fits multiple criteria: Ask which selected rule applies for this sort; both coherent answers can be valid, while a child changing rules silently deserves further support. No viable blind listener: Teacher can provide private unprimed listening role; avoid inventing actual human interaction or attributing a synthetic narrator response as observed. Sensory barrier: Offer alternate safe high-contrast/tactile/use traits rather than scoring a false concept failure.

Seven learning/access profiles, with fading

Attention/executive — short predictable investigator turn with real pieces and gradual removal of turn cards; language/communication — PK4 must actually choose relational meaning using speech/sign/AAC/home language, including independently selected preprogrammed descriptors, no adult-supplied full answer; literacy — no reading or written mystery labels; numeracy — no exact bin-count quotas; autism/sensory — quiet listener, non-touch choice and optional reduced social load; intellectual/developmental — one reliable obvious feature before mixed-feature challenges, fade semantic scaffolds; motor/hearing/vision — large safe pieces and learner-directed switch/gaze proxy, reliably described object properties and accessible private communication, with access assistance distinguished from teacher-selected rule.

Four written routes and assessment boundaries

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Proposed blind-listener UI would need genuinely child-selected meaningful within/between relational expression and false-credit detection, including correctly chosen accessible preprogrammed AAC symbols or phrases, never a prerecorded/system-selected full correct answer. Virtual grouping does not automatically establish real physical categorization or language performance. CO-PLAY: Caregiver can play listener without whispering the answer and records what the learner says/signs/chooses. ADULT-LED: Educator separates genuine relational explanation from repetition and records U/H/A/X. CLASSROOM: Learners sort separate unseen sets and respond privately to a listener; no answer-sharing or chorally rehearsed sentences count.
