Objective-level Educator Guide — V.E.1 Classification by Observable Attributes

Role: Cross-lesson educator reference, NOT a counted third lesson. Source-linked to the printed 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Mathematics V.E.1 (Classification and Patterns), PDF printed p.57. The source research cells await independent visual concordance with the original PDF layout.

Official PK3.V.E.1 — sorting action (SHA 8b2b6b110dc7c30ae2d79582e51fcfdde89db00528fdbfce233b1afbf8702f93)

“Child sorts objects that are the same and different.” This asks a learner to originate a meaningful actual grouping of objects under a stable defensible SAME/DIFFERENT criterion. TEA's own child-behavior examples allow animals grouped by ocean versus land and writing utensils by type, so an independently chosen coherent function/habitat/meaning category can be valid without one directly perceptible shared visual/tactile trait; sensory attention is an accessible learning pathway, not a blanket official sorting restriction. The child need not produce explanatory language to count as a source-action candidate. Neutral motor access under child direction can permit a child to originate placement decisions, but do not infer an independently executed physical movement without observation. Do not add number, exactly-two-pile, spoken English, or arbitrary alternate-rule thresholds to the official objective.

Developmental foundation: Attend to large safe objects, notice visual/tactile sameness and difference, understand that an object's membership depends on a selected trait, and personally assign a whole unfamiliar set. Begin with obvious differences; later let incidental color, texture or size differ while the selected trait stays alike. A failed color-based task may mean color is inaccessible, not that classification is impossible. Progress through PK3 S3-1 to S3-4 in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md.

Independent PK3 evidence: On untouched novel items, the learner groups defensibly alike/different pieces under a consistent personally chosen directly perceivable property OR coherent familiar meaningful use/habitat/function relation, without adult answer labels, precolored bins or prearranged piles. Document first placements, chosen/observable rule, item identities, access support and conceptual hints separately. A verbally stated property, pointing to an adult pile, or sorting only one teacher-chosen final object is not enough.

Official PK4.V.E.1 — grouped sorting PLUS similarity/difference language (SHA f15247bc25c6278ea982d8489dce31711cd0731ad926895ef8993fe064d36a05)

“Child sorts objects that are the same and different into groups and uses language to describe how the groups are similar and different.” PK4 builds on source-aligned actual sorting, then expresses both how members share a property AND how groups differ. Speech, child-composed sign, meaningful augmentative and alternative communication, and home language may provide language access; record precisely what the child expressed and whether a partner interpreted or supplied the response. A preselected answer button or repeated teacher phrase is not self-origin explanatory language.

Developmental foundation: Once the learner can place objects under a stable chosen criterion, introduce within-group descriptions and contrasting statements grounded in the real objects. Practice explaining to someone who has not seen the piles. PK4 S4-1 through S4-5 gradually fade sentence stems and prompt-induced rules; selecting a second sort method is an optional enrichment, not extra state-source attainment.

Independent PK4 evidence: With a fresh unmodeled accessible full set, learner forms coherent groups, communicates an actual property shared within groups and a meaningful contrast between groups. All components must be child-origin, not inferred from a correct shape of piles or adult-supplied speech. Log both first placement sequence and verbatim/faithfully interpreted explanation, distinguishing U (independent), H (conceptual help), A (neutral access assistance), X (unclear/adult-supplied); an A-coded trial is not automatically equivalent to independent unassisted movement.

Why the three different lesson plans complement one another

  1. Lesson 01 — direct attribute sensing and actual physical sorting uses felt/seen properties and bodily placement decisions as the initial concrete classification mechanism. Companion 01.
  2. Lesson 02 — same-set rule reclassification makes the selected criterion visible as an idea, not a property permanently attached to the object. Rule switching demonstrates conceptual flexibility but is NOT required for either official EXIT. Companion 02.
  3. Lesson 03 — blind-listener group detective uses communication with someone who lacks the answer to sharpen relation-based explanations; PK3 still receives a real nonverbal sorting EXIT without a verbal hurdle. Companion 03.

The sequence is not three checkboxes: start with accessible concrete grouping, intervene with criterion reclassification only when it clarifies a real misconception, and offer listener explanation for PK4 relational language. A child may benefit from a different order. Keep each plan's ENTRY, MODEL, DURING, independent EXIT, and true later-date REVISIT separate: success on modeled pieces is practice, not independently observed transfer.

Common misconceptions and targeted teaching

  • “Same means identical in every way.” Show adult-only two differently colored round shapes grouped by outline; fade the reminder and offer a fresh learner-chosen-rule trial.
  • “The rule is the tray color or the piece location.” Remove colored answer mats, rotate spatial orientation, and present a cross-cutting attribute set; do not infer a rule from random adjacency.
  • “Any pile is valid.” Accept multiple coherent child-chosen rules, but verify every item fits the SAME selected criterion rather than switching mid-pile.
  • “A named feature equals PK4 explanation.” A word such as “blue” without a grounded within-group similarity AND between-group contrast needs further practice with a different adult example and an untouched exit.

Inclusion and delivery safeguards

Across both grades provide seven individually planned access profiles: attention/executive functioning; language processing/communication; literacy/phonological processing; numeracy/mathematical processing; autism/predictability/sensory needs; intellectual/developmental support; and physical/motor/hearing/vision access. Favor oversized, nonchoking, clean, high-contrast, tactile and nonsensory-aversive safe objects; check perceivability before interpreting performance. Use learner-directed gaze/switch or a neutral motor proxy, not teacher-origin grouping. Consult individualized family and qualified specialist plans as applicable without asserting equivalent outcomes. All four SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes are WRITTEN DESIGNS; SOLO NOT IMPLEMENTED, delivery parity NOT VERIFIED.

Assess source action on unseen objects, preserve uncertainty and actual language records, and repeat on a genuinely different calendar date; REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED until done. Synthetic source checks, print receipts, and author review are not evidence of child achievement, physical-print access, external educator approval or pedagogical efficacy.
