Teach Sorting by What Objects Have in Common

Reader-friendly teacher and family guide. Use safe real objects and preserve the child's reasoning rather than requiring one predetermined sorting rule.

What the child is learning

For a 3-year-old, the goal is to physically notice which objects are alike and which are different, and sort meaningfully. For a 4-year-old, the child also uses language, sign or another meaningful communication route to describe how their groups are similar and different. An adult naming the rule is not a substitute for the child's own explanation.

Prepare an inviting set

Place three large, safe blunt objects on a plain mat: for example, two objects with similar rounded outlines and one with noticeable corners. Keep the objects unlabeled and ungrouped. Have a different set ready for your model and another for a fresh later check. Use objects the child can see, feel or access; vision or motor restrictions should not masquerade as inability to sort.

Avoid sharp edges, small parts and a requirement to use a specific school vocabulary word where the child can genuinely explain the feature another way.

The teaching path

1. Ask what could go together

Show the new three-piece set without sorting it.

Say: “Which of these could go together? Can you show me?”

Wait for the child's own grouping. For a child ready for deeper explanation, ask “How are these alike? What makes the other group different?” Observe without naming the correct rule beforehand.

2. Model on a different set

Arrange other safe objects by their edges or shape, while varying their colors.

Say: “These have smooth round edges. These other pieces have corners. I grouped them by shape even though the colors are different.”

For an older learner, explain a true similarity within each group and a difference between them. This is your model, not evidence that the child has supplied those ideas.

3. Invite the child's own sorting choice

Provide another collection, allowing the child to move pieces or direct an accessible adult mover.

Say: “What could be one rule for your groups? How could you check whether each item belongs?”

The child may choose a defensible feature different from your example. For the younger group, observe meaningful sorting. For the older group, invite a genuine child-origin explanation of the group property and meaningful contrast. If the rule seems inconsistent, ask about a different practice item rather than silently sorting for the child.

4. Offer a fresh independent set

Remove model and practice materials. Bring out an unseen collection with more than one plausible feature.

Say: “Make groups that make sense to you. What is the same in each group? How are your groups different?”

For a younger child, an independent meaningful sort is the target; do not force advanced descriptive language. For an older child, observe both the grouping and a genuine explanatory response, not a sentence repeated after the adult.

5. Revisit later with changed materials

On another real day, use a new safe collection and see which rule the child proposes. Don't assume retention from the written plan.

Teach Sorting by What Objects Have in Common with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

For a 3-year-old, the goal is to physically notice which objects are alike and which are different, and sort meaningfully. For a 4-year-old, the child also uses language, sign or another meaningful communication route to describe how their groups are similar and different. An adult naming the rule is not a substitute for the child's own explanation.

Prepare the classroom

Set up several bowls or trays of safe objects varying in one clear attribute and a separate adult model group; avoid sharp or allergenic surprises. Give children accessible ways to examine color, shape, size or another meaningful property. Keep enough pieces for real re-sorting.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “Which things might belong together? What makes them belong?” Invite a child-first arrangement, then model a different object set grouped by a property you can actually verify.

Give children meaningful practice

In table groups, children choose a sorting rule, make groups and tell a willing partner why, then have a turn listening to a peer's different valid rule. Educators visit individual workspaces to observe actual child-owned decisions; a pre-sorted teacher example can introduce an idea but not establish independent categorizing.

Observe individual learning fairly

Give a child a fresh varied selection and ask for a grouping and reason, without dictating the attribute. Notice whether the rule consistently explains the included items and whether the child can communicate it through pointing, sign or words. A correct-looking group made by a friend isn't that child's independent sorting evidence.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Do not treat a culturally different but consistently grounded classification as wrong. Use tactile/visual aids or fewer items when needed, ensuring the target remains an explainable shared attribute. Keep unanswered reasoning as needing another chance, not as proof of inability.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

Lesson 01 — Attribute Sensing and Safe Physical Sort

Universe-neutral E2.2 WRITTEN standalone lesson plan, 2026-10-30. Begin with DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md and its own Lesson 01 companion facilitator guide; the objective educator guide covers the entire V.E.1 progression. Printed 2022 Texas Mathematics V.E.1 PK3.V.E.1 sorts same/different objects (SHA 8b2b6b110dc7c30ae2d79582e51fcfdde89db00528fdbfce233b1afbf8702f93) versus PK4.V.E.1 forms groups and uses language to describe group similarities and differences (SHA f15247bc25c6278ea982d8489dce31711cd0731ad926895ef8993fe064d36a05). Mechanism: the learner directly explores perceivable properties of real safe objects and actively organizes consistent alike/different groups. This is not a chart, matching two pictures only, forced English speech, or teacher-created piles.

Exact original materials and separation for assessment

Choose clean, large nonchoking rounded tactile or smooth safe solid pieces, with adult-validated visible and accessible feature variation: two blue circles, two gold circles, two blue squares, two gold squares, oversized enough for children and physically safe (no magnets, beads, sharp edges, detached wheels or food). Avoid color as sole cue for a child with color-perception difference: provide tactile smooth/raised curves, shape/card texture, high-contrast boundaries and educator-accessible original ORIGINAL_SORTING_MATS.html. Prepare one CLEAR novice array with a single salient difference and a later cross-feature array where one trait remains same despite another difference. Distinct objects for ENTRY, teacher-only MODEL, guided DURING and untouched independent EXIT. A prop sharing a feature with multiple groups is not automatically a wrong piece; explicitly retain selected sorting criterion. Official TEA p.57 child examples include knowledge-based functional or habitat classification (real toy sea animals versus land animals, safe writing tools grouped by kind): a child-led coherent USE or HABITAT relation can be valid even if toy objects do not share directly visible, tactile, or color attributes. This direct-sensory lesson begins with perceivable properties but does not make such a sensory feature a universal PK3/PK4 source-eligibility condition. Ask for neutral clarification if actual meaning remains uncertain; never impose PK4 spoken explanation on PK3 or assume prior taxonomy learning.

ENTRY before MODEL: Place a new unlabeled trio of large blunt objects (two same simple tactile outlines and one different) on a low mat, with random orientation and distance. “Which could go together? Show me.” Wait silently and record child’s first natural placement, gaze/AAC/gesture or child-directed proxy. For PK4 additionally ask after child sorts, “How are these alike? How are those groups different?” Do not put correct-label bins on table first. If child cannot perceive feature, test accessibility separately before judging.

MODEL on DIFFERENT teacher-only set: Adult touches two matching curved safe outlines, points to unlike angular outline, physically groups: “These have no corners; this one has corners. They are alike in shape even though the colors can be different.” For PK4 teacher explains two groups with full relational content, “In this group all edges are curved; in that group the edges have corners, so the groups differ in outline.” Demonstrate on adult's DIFFERENT objects, never on child's EXIT set.

DURING: Child actively moves their own pieces into same/different or multiple groups, chooses one shared feature, and may use proprioceptive/tactile exploration and child-directed motor proxy. Teacher gives specific feedback only on different practice pieces, e.g. “If you are sorting by shape, what do their edges feel like?” Then child independently fixes/rechecks their practice array. PK3 goal is meaningful sorting, not naming the geometry; PK4 must independently supply a contextual language explanation after support but new EXIT must be without sentence cues.

Fresh independent EXIT: New unmodeled physical four+ safe objects with an intentional misleading second attribute (same shape different colors; same color different shapes). No preprinted labels or teacher-preselected rule. PK3 independently organizes the actual offered objects under a coherent child-selected alike/different relation—two or more meaningful groups OR a matching cluster with an intentionally separate unlike item, all objects considered rather than a lucky isolated pair: directly visible/tactile attributes such as shape, texture and color are the taught route, but consistent familiar meaningful purpose or use of safe real objects is also legitimate source sorting if the grouping actually follows that relation. Do not preannounce the category or demand PK3 explain it in words; observe original placements and, when useful, child-directed action showing purpose. PK4 independently forms coherent meaningful groups AND uses own language speech/sign/AAC/home language to convey how group members are similar and groups differ. Record actual object IDs, chosen feature, first placements, explanation, adult help, U/H/A/X. A lucky copied pile is not meaningful independent source evidence. REVISIT on actually different calendar date with fresh objects, NOT_OBSERVED now.

PK3 staged teaching script

PK3 S3-1: ENTRY unseen pair round + one large square with same color, child groups touching same outline; MODEL teacher different outline objects, “these feel alike,” DURING child traces own edges and moves them; new EXIT novel two round/one square, no hints. Misconception: groups objects based only on proximity on mat; teacher spaces unrelated props and sorts by real outline, then new probe.

PK3 S3-2: ENTRY new safe smooth/smooth/ribbed/ribbed set; MODEL different large tactile contrasts, “same feeling together; bumpy together”; DURING child builds two piles by touch or gaze under access support, EXIT untouched safe textured objects genuinely sorted. If child switches smooth → color halfway through, teacher shows different simple stable trait and new uncued pair. No vocabulary recitation requirement.

PK3 S3-3: ENTRY new red round, blue round, red square; if child sorts by color or shape, accept a consistent child-selected rule and document exactly which. MODEL different teacher-own two-tone shapes emphasizes “same round edge even different colors.” DURING child compares competing features with safe props, EXIT another unmodeled mixed-attribute trio, coherent sorting. Misconception: two differently colored round objects always “different” no matter shape; tutor models different round pair and independent new case.

PK3 S3-4: ENTRY new flat large safe toy tools or wooden objects, child sorts shared feature vs unlike; MODEL different adult-safe objects first teaching common criterion, DURING exploratory rearrangement, full unmodeled source EXIT new set with confounded secondary features where learner independently sorts same/different in a coherent fashion. Later actual-date REVISIT with new family of safe objects, NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 staged teaching script — actual language, not teacher's label

PK4 S4-1: ENTRY new unlabelled mixed large objects, child groups then starts own “these match because…” response; MODEL teacher unrelated safe shapes with one similarity explanation, DURING learner forms own groups; EXIT untouched set plus independent brief speech/sign/AAC stating shared trait. A memorized location/colored tray not valid.

PK4 S4-2: ENTRY new cross-feature set, child groups and explains similarity AND difference. MODEL unrelated safe forms “these have curved edges and those have corners.” DURING child adds own comparison to their real grouping, teacher not supplying answer sentence, EXIT new cross-feature set with “alike because X / other different because Y” in any expressive language; child may use brief grammar, but meaning must be real. If says only “round/blue” as isolated names, ask on teacher's distinct practice then new open exit.

PK4 S4-3: ENTRY new red/blue round/square set, adult does not preassign shape vs color; child chooses feature and explains. MODEL other teacher set physically sorted by one property, then regrouped by a different one; DURING child may re-sort if ready, explaining rule. EXIT new set one coherent rule and contextual group comparison is enough; alternate sorting is pedagogical extension, not extra state bar.

PK4 S4-4: ENTRY new ambiguous piece sharing two possible different traits; ask independent group decision and reason. MODEL adult-owned example of one object fitting a color group under color rule, and shape group under shape rule. DURING learner checks own selection consistent with stated rule, EXIT novel ambiguous item and independent comparison language (why alike in one feature and different in another). If child keeps placing piece in two piles simultaneously without explaining rule, teacher models separate prop then new probe.

PK4 S4-5: ENTRY novel realistic object array, child independently sorts and explains before instruction; MODEL entirely distinct object grouping if help needed; DURING teacher responds to any confound and learner practices with new pair; full source EXIT unseen safe object set sorted into coherent groups and learner's own expressed similarity AND difference. New same-looking variation/changed positions defeats teacher-cued colors. REVISIT new real date and unseen set, NOT_OBSERVED.

False-positive analysis and honest boundaries

False-positive child 1: two pieces are nearest together, child pushes them into a pile; teacher calls it correct even though one circle and one square happen to be adjacent. Repair: model unrelated tactile feature on other set, rearrange positions and novel independent physical sort. No first-origin semantic evidence from distance cue.

False-positive child 2: adult supplies precolored matching bins and all correct cards share that same tray color; child follows adult label without distinguishing real attributes. Repair: remove prelabels, use mixed secondary colors and child-selected trait; redo with unseen safe materials. H cannot count as U.

False-positive child 3: PK4 says “round, square” after teacher points, but never independently says which group is alike or different and by what observable property. Repair: model an alternate trait with unrelated pieces, then new group + child-origin explanatory language (sign/AAC/home language valid). A word count/rule-switch threshold isn't official. PK3: actually sorted safe real objects by meaningful same/different attribute

Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 01: Attribute Sensing and Physical Sorting

Pair: Lesson 01; separate official outcomes: PK3.V.E.1 / PK4.V.E.1. This is adult pedagogy and troubleshooting, not a counted lesson. Read the objective-level guide for scope and source boundaries.

Pedagogical mechanism

Physical examination precedes naming: learner perceives real safe properties, compares across objects, and personally moves or directs every item into coherent categories. Body-based/tactile exploration is a strategy, not a sensory prerequisite; no child must touch an uncomfortable texture. PK3 success is an independently organized actual sort. Direct tactile/visible attributes are the core TEACHING mechanism, but consistent child-selected familiar purpose, use or habitat groupings of real safe objects are also source-valid even without shared outlines/colors/textures. The TEA p.57 original example explicitly includes toy animals organized by ocean versus land. Do not falsely reject a child-owned coherent conceptual category because it does not match the adult's planned sensory rule, and do not demand PK4 speech from PK3. PK4 adds learner-authored language about similarities within and differences between their actual groups; do not give PK3 a PK4-only language test.

Preflight and adult-only staging

Check the actual child's access, reach, hygiene, choking hazards and sensory willingness. Prepare separate ENTRY three large clear pieces, MODEL four unlike large teacher-only shapes, DURING six cross-feature oversized pieces, untouched EXIT 4–6 new safe objects with multiple defensible sensory OR child-meaningful use/habitat relations, and later real-date REVISIT another different bank. For 24 simultaneous novice three-piece ENTRY sorts provide about 72 large child-safe pieces (plus teacher MODEL stock), or supervise 4–6-child groups with 12–18 reused large pieces and new unseen reserved EXIT material. Teacher observes only 2–3 original source acts per realistic interval; other individual evidence is NOT_OBSERVED until assessed. No small detachable pieces, magnets, food or precolored answer labels. Neutral blank mats; first-party sorting mats must be independently checked before physical use. Prepare response record for grade, item identities, first placements, inferred/chosen rule, PK4 actual words/sign/AAC, assistance U/H/A/X and date.

Adult moves in instructional order

  1. ENTRY: “Which could go together? Show me.” Let the child begin without pointing to an answer space. For PK4 only, ask after sorting “How are these alike? What is different about the groups?” Record spontaneous response, including uncertainty.
  2. MODEL: On adult-only different pieces, demonstrate one property. Say “These both have curved edges; they can go together even though their colors are different.” For PK4 model both within-group and between-group statements on this adult-owned set. Put the demonstration away.
  3. DURING: Let the learner actively assign a new full practice set. If one item is misplaced under the chosen rule, ask “Look at the edges of these two: what do you notice?” rather than silently fixing it. For PK4, have child express each group's relation; accept concise sign/AAC/home language with recorded semantics. Independently selected preprogrammed AAC vocabulary or phrases count as real expressive language when the child chooses correct group similarity AND contrast; a whole correct answer played automatically or selected by adult is H, not source-independent child meaning. Gradually remove pointed hints, labeled positions, a sentence starter and physical help that supplies conceptual answers.
  4. FADING: If a child needs access assistance, move only the item and destination the child unmistakably selects, record A. If the adult selects category or phrase, record H, not U. Rearrange locations and secondary features so rote color matching does not masquerade as sorting.
  5. EXIT: Present a fully novel, unmodeled bank and blank spaces. PK3.V.E.1 requires child-origin coherent sorting of same/different real objects, including child clustering matching objects while deliberately separating an unlike piece with every offered item considered, not a fixed two-homogeneous-bin format; don't require verbal explanation or count only a lucky isolated pair. PK4.V.E.1 additionally requires genuine independent similarity AND difference meaning attached to groups; one concise original utterance, child-selected sign or AAC expression may convey BOTH, without two separate English sentences. Record actual first attempt, not teacher-corrected final piles. Clean evidence is a source-action candidate, not mastery.
  6. REVISIT: Schedule new safe objects on another real date, with new positions and a new first attempt. Until observed, write NOT_OBSERVED rather than implying retention.

Misconception decision tree

Copies spatial proximity or answer-coded BIN colors: Spread pieces apart, hide precolored answer-bin markers, model adult-only unlike/alike sets, then offer wholly new learner practice and untouched EXIT. A child-chosen rule based on actual OBJECT color is legitimate classification, not a shortcut. Likewise ocean/land animal figures or writing tool USE may be coherent even when toys share no visible feature; investigate child-origin rule without mandatory PK3 speech. Sorts by color rather than expected shape: First check if color is a defensible coherent rule. If yes, accept it; don't penalize adult expectation. Coherent child-origin object-purpose/use sorting is also legitimate even though visual/tactile classification remains this method's central modeled mechanism; record the child-made placements and basis without requiring PK3 speech. If inconsistent, explain an unchanging attribute using DIFFERENT model pieces. Can sort but cannot articulate PK4 contrasts: Keep physical sorting credit separate from the missing language evidence. On another set model “both smooth / these rough,” practice child's own explanation, then fresh unbiased exit. Unable to perceive proposed trait: Change to accessible tactile/contrast properties with qualified family/specialist input. Do not call a perceptual barrier a concept deficit. Adult moves all pieces or says groups: Treat as supported exposure, not first-origin evidence. Let the child control an entirely new accessible set.

Seven differentiated access pathways

Attention/executive: small initial sets and predictable turn structure, fading placement arrows; language: personally composed AAC/sign/home-language for PK4 without scripted answers; literacy: no print decoding; numeracy: no counts or numbered bins; autism/sensory: choice to look instead of touch, quiet consistent workspace; developmental: clear adult-only opposite-feature contrast then gradually increase mixed attributes; motor/vision/hearing: large tactile/high contrast, reliably described properties, learner-directed neutral proxy or switch, never proxy-chosen groups.
