Companion Teacher/Facilitator Guide — Lesson 03: Group Rule Detective and Blind Listener

Pair: Lesson 03, linked to PK3.V.E.1 and PK4.V.E.1. Operational teaching guidance, not a lesson-plan count. Refer to objective guide for source scope.

Teaching mechanism and guardrails

The learner builds real groups under a coherent perceivable property, then tests whether another person who did not see the placement can use child-origin clues to identify the grouping rule. This social “information gap” targets PK4's meaningful language: describing a shared property and a contrast between groups. It must NOT become an artificial PK3 verbal puzzle. For PK3, the detective story is optional motivation for a real source-eligible object sort without mandatory explanation. This lesson is independent of Stargrove lore and fictional characters.

Prepare actual investigation

Provide adult-inspected safe oversized physical items with deliberately confounded attributes: e.g., two round textured outlines in different colors and two angular textured outlines with matching colors; accessible alternative sets may contrast surface or function. Prepare four distinct material banks for ENTRY, MODEL, DURING and novel EXIT, plus different safe items for later-calendar-date REVISIT. A peer/adult blind listener may actually turn away; do not claim a simulated or preprogrammed listener verifies a human conversation. Have plain mat, unsorted item IDs, note page for first physical groupings, exact child's explanation and access/hint codes. No name/family/personality classification, consent pressure or diagnosis.

Instructional sequence for the facilitator

  1. ENTRY: Child receives a clean unfamiliar physical set. Ask “Show me the groups you would make.” Note first sort without covert colored answer spaces. With PK4, let a listener ask “How are pieces in this group alike, and how is this group different from that one?” No prewritten answer choices.
  2. MODEL: Adult groups a different set, then tells a truly uninformed adult listener “These all have corners; those have curved edges.” The listener uses the statement to place a separate demonstration piece. Explicitly show the difference between saying a label and giving evidence about the groups.
  3. DURING: Child chooses their own sort of new practice objects. A partner can ask “What do they have in common?” and “What's different about the other group?” but may not name the attribute, complete AAC phrases, place pieces or tell a secret rule. For PK3, let learner communicate choices by physical placement, gaze or gestures; explanatory performance is enrichment only.
  4. FADING: Remove detective picture hints, spoken comparison stems, labeled bins and visual answer shadows. The learner must make the full actual sort; not merely identify an adult pile. If the blind listener guesses wrongly because the original clue was vague, offer feedback about missing detail on practice pieces, then move to an untouched set.
  5. EXIT: Present independent unseen objects; PK3.V.E.1 requires a coherent child-origin sort of same/different objects with no forced language. PK4.V.E.1 requires the sort PLUS meaningful child-controlled expression of similarity within groups and difference across groups in speech, home language, sign or AAC, including independently selected preprogrammed descriptive words/phrases. A successful blind-listener guess or classifying an extra new item is an optional transfer challenge, not a condition of official source evidence. When speech is not the child's expression channel, capture semantically child-selected sign, home language or AAC, including independently chosen meaningful stored descriptive vocabulary but not an adult-selected correct complete answer, with any interpretation accurately attributed. A listener's accurate guess does not substitute for actual child-created groups or actual relational language.
  6. REVISIT: On a genuinely later date use new unlabeled physical objects and a different listener if appropriate. Capture unassisted first response separately from reteaching; retention NOT_OBSERVED at authoring.

Mistake-specific decision tree

Child only points to adult groups: On a different adult example model that the detective must make their own full piles; guide on new practice pieces; use fresh independent EXIT. PK4 supplies category word without comparison: Adult models “all rough / other smooth” on unrelated teacher objects, then fades prompts and repeats with fresh child-generated comparisons. Teacher/peer tells secret rule: Record H for affected practice; do not retrospectively call language independent. One item fits multiple criteria: Ask which selected rule applies for this sort; both coherent answers can be valid, while a child changing rules silently deserves further support. No viable blind listener: Teacher can provide private unprimed listening role; avoid inventing actual human interaction or attributing a synthetic narrator response as observed. Sensory barrier: Offer alternate safe high-contrast/tactile/use traits rather than scoring a false concept failure.

Seven learning/access profiles, with fading

Attention/executive — short predictable investigator turn with real pieces and gradual removal of turn cards; language/communication — PK4 must actually choose relational meaning using speech/sign/AAC/home language, including independently selected preprogrammed descriptors, no adult-supplied full answer; literacy — no reading or written mystery labels; numeracy — no exact bin-count quotas; autism/sensory — quiet listener, non-touch choice and optional reduced social load; intellectual/developmental — one reliable obvious feature before mixed-feature challenges, fade semantic scaffolds; motor/hearing/vision — large safe pieces and learner-directed switch/gaze proxy, reliably described object properties and accessible private communication, with access assistance distinguished from teacher-selected rule.

Four written routes and assessment boundaries

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Proposed blind-listener UI would need genuinely child-selected meaningful within/between relational expression and false-credit detection, including correctly chosen accessible preprogrammed AAC symbols or phrases, never a prerecorded/system-selected full correct answer. Virtual grouping does not automatically establish real physical categorization or language performance. CO-PLAY: Caregiver can play listener without whispering the answer and records what the learner says/signs/chooses. ADULT-LED: Educator separates genuine relational explanation from repetition and records U/H/A/X. CLASSROOM: Learners sort separate unseen sets and respond privately to a listener; no answer-sharing or chorally rehearsed sentences count. No real child observation, specialist approval, cross-mode parity or completed delayed revisit is claimed.
