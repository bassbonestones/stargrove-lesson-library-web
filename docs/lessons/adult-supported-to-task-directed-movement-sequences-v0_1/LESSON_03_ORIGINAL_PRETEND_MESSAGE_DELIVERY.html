Teach Delivering a Pretend Message With Safe Body Movements

Teacher and parent guide. Use a harmless large pretend paper note and a low accessible destination—never real mail or a high obstacle.

What the child learns

The child connects real comfortable body actions to complete a simple task, such as move toward a low box, turn to face it and deliver or indicate a pretend message. A 3-year-old may genuinely participate with adult sequence guidance and help; a 4-year-old can coordinate their own purposeful physical route with accessible safety support.

Moving a digital arrow or simply telling the adult “put it there” is not by itself child-performed body movement. If touching paper is inaccessible, authentic child body shift/reach/reorient/indicate may preserve a relevant meaningful physical route.

Prepare an accessible destination

Use a large, clean, harmless blank paper note, a broad, stable low empty box and a flat accessible space with no hazards. Place note and box where the child may safely approach, turn, reach or gesture from an appropriate supported position. No forced gait, grip, balancing challenge or dangerous raised mailbox. The adult must check individualized mobility needs and honor the child's choice to stop.

Five gentle moves

1. Ask the child to choose a route

Say: “Our pretend message can go to this safe box. Would you like to show how your body could help?”

Observe actual child-controlled movement before giving exact movement order. If a child just gives a verbal direction, note communication without inventing bodily source performance.

2. Model a different adult-only body task

On a separate stable adult chair, demonstrate your own gentle approach, safe sitting and reorientation toward comfort.

Say: “My movements work together to help me settle safely.”

Do not demonstrate the child's actual note-delivery sequence, carry their paper for them or move the child's limbs.

3. Let the child deliver their own way

Say: “How could you move or turn toward the box? Would you like to put the message in, or show where it should go?”

Help with sequence cues for younger children, and encourage child-owned linked movement for older learners. If fabric/paper grip is inaccessible, a child may physically turn, reach and indicate the destination while an adult separately carries the paper. Document that difference honestly. Stop at discomfort.

4. Explore a different safe movement goal

Put away the note and box; show a new large safe story-spot marker.

Say: “Can you find our story spot with your body and show you're ready?”

Observe actual voluntary child-generated shift, turn, reach or movement toward the new marker. A copied adult motion or an adult carrying the child to a spot is not proof of independent coordination.

5. Revisit another real day

Offer another gentle meaningful movement opportunity only when safe and chosen.

When to adjust

What happens Helpful next step
Child needs an adult to sequence reminders Support authentically, particularly for younger children, without performing all movements
Child does not want to touch paper Offer a no-grip physical reach/reorient/indicate alternative
Adult moves child or note without child bodily participation Describe adult work accurately; don't invent child motor skill
Child chooses a seated or mobility-assisted position Accept genuine safe child-generated body sequence
Child declines or tires End the activity without a deficit label

Teach Delivering a Pretend Message With Safe Body Movements with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

The child connects real comfortable body actions to complete a simple task, such as move toward a low box, turn to face it and deliver or indicate a pretend message. A 3-year-old may genuinely participate with adult sequence guidance and help; a 4-year-old can coordinate their own purposeful physical route with accessible safety support.

Moving a digital arrow or simply telling the adult “put it there” is not by itself child-performed body movement. If touching paper is inaccessible, authentic child body shift/reach/reorient/indicate may preserve a relevant meaningful physical route.

Prepare the classroom

Mark an accessible short route to a stable low pretend message destination using broad paper or a safe box. Check hazards, turning space and whether the child can genuinely reach or indicate the destination. Keep a separate adult demonstration route; no racing, carrying fragile items or travel near doors.

Introduce the idea together

Invite, “Where would you like to deliver a pretend message? How could your body get it there safely?” Let children first choose or indicate a route. Show a different adult-only movement task on your own clearly separate destination.

Give children meaningful practice

A small number of focal children take individual real movement turns in a safe supervised bay, choosing how to orient, approach and place or indicate delivery. The rest of the class needs genuinely supervised quiet or seated exploration; one adult must not supervise unsafe concurrent movement stations while assisting a single child.

Observe individual learning fairly

Watch actual child-directed linked physical actions, not the pretend story alone. For younger learners, note supported real body movements; for older learners, observe coordinated goal-directed action at the appropriate level. A paper note moved entirely by an adult, a toy animation or a teacher's scripted mime doesn't prove the learner performed the sequence. For the planned changed-purpose check, put away paper and box entirely and introduce a new broad reachable high-contrast/tactile story-spot marker. Observe the child's own linked bodily orienting, shifting, reaching and indication toward that spot, with appropriate younger-child adult support. Repeating delivery to the same box may be meaningful practice but is not the particular novel no-transport transfer.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Allow child-directed movement aids, seated reach or appropriate gestures and alternate routes that preserve real bodily action. Children can decline handling the paper and still demonstrate their own destination-oriented motion. If safe staff coverage or mobility space is missing, postpone the movement check and mark it unobserved rather than unsafe.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

Sensory: Preview paper and box; no forced touch, sounds, bright flashes or public audience. Mobility/vision/hearing: Stable reachable broad low open box, clear access for mobility aids, tangible large target marker/neutral descriptions, safe seated/axial turns, neutral adult steadying, never claim adult-moved limbs as pupil. Cultural and social choice: Fictional harmless blank notes avoid assuming a particular home address, postal practice or stranger greetings; learner can choose neutral task meaning. Physical safety: No narrow slot/choking pieces, sharp fasteners, unsecured box, busy station routes, running or forced reaching.

Four complete prospective delivery routes

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: Future narrated device may give a child choices about a REAL supervised physical note task but cannot verify actual motor performance from avatar, on-screen icons or Next taps, and cannot make a child's room safe without a responsible adult. CO-PLAY: A trusted caregiver checks actual space, offers authentic entry first, provides PK3 responsive support/PK4 neutral safety and observes first real child bodily sequence. ADULT-LED: Distinct adult safe stable-chair model, child physically practices the preferred paper task or source-faithful no-touch alternative, then a truly different new nontransport story-spot EXIT. CLASSROOM: One safely supervised moving learner at a time in a clear bay, all other children in genuinely supervised calm stationary activities under real existing staffing; if unavailable, defer and rotate across separately staffed sessions. Record only individually witnessed first bodily actions (perhaps 2–3 focus pupils per feasible observation period), never infer 24 from group demonstration or projected cartoon. No public scoring, peer video, timed race or fabricated staffing.

Teacher note: Grade and official source, task/context, child's actually observed physical movement sequence, child/adult paper handling, adult support/cues and U/H/A/X, actual task endpoint, familiar vs truly changed task, safe alternative used and honest later date. Document task action without diagnosising motor limitations.

Limitations: This full guide is written design only. No actual child delivery/alternate movements, field room/staff supervision, external teacher reliability, physical first-party print QA, later retention, functioning SOLO or measured four-mode effectiveness has occurred.
