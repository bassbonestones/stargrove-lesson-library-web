Lesson 02 — Original Soft Cloth Resting Sequence: Complete Facilitator Guide

Paired main: LESSON_02_ORIGINAL_SOFT_CLOTH_RESTING_SEQUENCE.md. Family teacher guide: TEACHER_GUIDE.md. These are written teaching resources, not measured learner experience. From 2022 Texas Education Agency Prekindergarten Guidelines printed p.76 (original PDF page index 75): PK3.IX.A.2 child engages in movement sequences with adult support (source-cell SHA-256 f748de7e4b034e5263511d0773315285b4e3143b8eb855013ccfd54706452426) and PK4.IX.A.2 child coordinates sequence of movements to perform tasks (SHA fc198498ea4448cb9c6c34e9dea81b73b469dd42edea5780b9fc877f0ca73cb5). Official examples include nonlocomotor body bending, reaching, turning, twisting and movement within boundaries; carrying an object or gripping cloth is not a universal IX.A.2 condition.

This lesson's particular teaching idea

A large, soft, clean cloth needs a comfortable safe resting place. The learner explores how their own real body can reach toward it, reorient gently and move with purpose. Preferred concrete route: learner chooses how to bring the cloth a short safe distance to an age-appropriate broad low stable resting mat. This gives the motor sequence an immediately meaningful cause and result: the cloth goes from here to where the child decides it can rest. Equally source-respecting motor route when grasp/texture is inaccessible: with the same large safe resting spot as a genuine goal, child initiates/coordinates meaningful comfortable seated reach–reorient–point/gesture movements to direct or indicate the resting place without pretending they themselves carried the fabric. Record cloth endpoint and who moved cloth separately; do not claim this alternate is identical fabric transport. A teacher may place fabric according to the child's choice as neutral context support, but the child must truly move their own body in a linked physical goal-directed sequence for IX.A.2.

PK3 support means adult stays safely available, physically prepares and supervises materials and responds to learner when cues/regulated pauses are useful; step-by-step verbal cues are allowed but not compulsory for a child who willingly makes authentic linked movements while an adult remains appropriately responsive. PK4 coordination means the child selects/directs actual bodily transitions toward a task such as resting fabric or physically indicating the safe resting target, even if the fabric slips before being fully laid down. A single reach, paper-step plan, avatar or complete adult-performed carrying is not sufficient child motor sequence. Neither a specific walking gait nor a flawless paper/cloth grip is an official test.

Safe, reachable setup and consent

Prepare one large intact washable soft cloth (no loose tassels, detachable buttons, magnets or small parts), a wide stable low reachable resting surface, clean unobstructed flat floor or comfortable safe seated reach zone, and enough accessible turn/assistive-device space. Ask the child whether they want to handle cloth; never force touching a texture, stretching painfully, twisting beyond comfortable range, a public performance, stranger contact, climbing, running or racing. Alternative goal markers should be broad and tactile/high-contrast when needed. Check age-appropriate supervision, accessible vision/hearing, clean shared textiles, possible sensory dislikes and local staffing, and follow existing individualized supports with appropriate caregivers/educators if applicable. Do not require medical clearance as a written-design qualification condition; if real implementation would be unsafe, defer and log NOT_OBSERVED. No real child identities, body or health details belong in shared Git artifacts.

Exact five instructional phases

ENTRY — observe original child actions first. Put cloth and broad resting area within a safe short reachable space. Ask, “Where could this soft cloth rest? Show me a comfortable way your body could help.” Child may choose actual short carry, safe seated reorient/reach–release, or a meaningful linked reach–turn–indicate movement when grip is inaccessible. Record exactly what child physically did first, the purpose, adult availability/cues, if anyone else handled fabric and actual endpoint. A spoken “over there,” gesture alone or a whole projected picture sequence with no linked real body motion is not physical source action.

MODEL — genuinely different teacher-owned settling posture. On a safe ADULT-appropriate stable chair, adult demonstrates ordered body actions toward a different purpose: approach if needed, gently lower into a comfortable seat, shift/align shoulders and settle safely. This is not cloth transport, greeting, or the child's later marker-finding task. Adult never moves pupil limbs or uses child clothes/exit materials, and seated movement is not mandated on pupils; the adult model shows that real body actions work together toward a goal.

DURING — support authentic real source. Let child explore the cloth-resting task. Child can independently move toward a large broad cloth, reorient bodily toward low mat and lay it gently there. A neutral helper may stabilize the mat or make fabric available at child's reach; record if a caregiver performs fabric movement. If touching cloth/holding is difficult, replace the mandatory carry with a physically meaningful reachable goal: child turns toward the chosen resting surface, reaches to indicate it and reorients in a comfortable sequence; adult may place fabric there, but only learner's own actual movement supports motor source. For PK3 allow responsive support and child participation; for PK4 encourage child to select a purposeful linked movement route. Slow pace, calm encouragement, child-chosen pause, brief sign/home-language/AAC requests and adaptive seated/wheelchair/axial movement are valid. A fully adult moved child, a stationary verbal instruction, or a prerecorded avatar is not source.

Feedback and causal repair. If a child makes only one movement, invite a second genuinely physical comfortable action connected to their goal; model on unrelated adult marker, not their later task. If the cloth drops or slips, document whether the child actually coordinated meaningful reaching/reorienting body sequence regardless of endpoint; change cloth size/position/goal and offer child a fresh safe opportunity. If a child declines touch, allow a genuinely physical alternative goal rather than inventing inability. If adult cues the next movement, note H or supported PK3; if adult only provides neutral physical environment/AAC access, note A; if source attribution is unclear use X. Never call adult carrying a child-produced fabric transfer.

EXIT — different physical purpose and honest transfer. Present a new, genuinely non-transport task unlike cloth movement and adult's distinct chair-settling model: child discovers a large safe high-contrast/tactile marker across the reachable body area and chooses how to reorient or shift body, reach toward it and return/indicate its location to help locate the marker (no object to carry or deliver, no required gait or fine grip). Ask, “Can your body help find and show where this new big marker is?” For PK3 record actual comfortable connected body movement with responsive adult support; for PK4 record personally coordinated physical movement toward that real locating/indicating task, not a sequence of picture cards. Keep any necessary access supports. The independent new goal earns a stronger prospective novel-transfer observation only if actually witnessed. A legitimate familiar cloth task with real child-origin physical motor sequence may still show a cautious source-action candidate and must not be falsely failed solely for lacking novel transfer. Document changed context and help/actual action separately.

REVISIT — true later calendar day. Only on a different actual date, offer another safe goal (for instance a broad marker repositioned to a different comfortable reach direction), observe the child's genuine bodily movement with age-correct support/goal and record new date and provenance. Present status NOT_OBSERVED. A date written on a card in advance cannot prove delayed motor retention.

Staged and responsive progression

PK3 Q3-1 to Q3-4: Start with gentle two-action supported actual seated/standing motor links and trusted adult availability; optionally move cloth at Q3-3 and progress toward changed comfortable nonlocomotor physical goals. No mandatory step count, clipboard completed fabric transport, extra novel source requirement or every-motion verbal cue before recognizing real linked motor participation. PK4 Q4-1 to Q4-5: Child coordinates comfortable actual linked physical movements to pursue meaningful goal, initially greeting/cloth rest, then a distinct safe marker-locating task. Judge original physical coordination separately from fabric placement, speed and fine-muscle grip; distinct new task supports stronger transfer evidence but not a mandatory official source cutoff. See DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md for stages; these are developmental teaching choices, not fixed publisher cutoffs.

Access and interaction decisions

Attention: One clear short reachable goal, few moves and a child-directed pause. Communication: Sign, home language and self-selected AAC invite requests or task orientation; naming actions never replaces actual motor action. Literacy: no print reading needed. Numeracy: no count/speed gate. Autism or sensory needs: preview cloth before touch, allow contact-free meaningful movement, no unexpected tactile input or public greeting. Developmental supports: small actual movement links and differentiated model, gentle fade of answer-bearing prompts, PK3 responsive adult availability maintained. Mobility/hearing/vision: stable flat reachable setting, wheelchair-compatible and seated axial movement, broad contrast/tactile cue, neutral physical setup support and meaningful language access; no compulsory walking or complete cloth grasp. Record whose limbs moved and what adult did, not just final fabric location.

Four WRITTEN routes, without unsupported parity

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. A future narrated device could invite actual safe body actions under responsible available adult supervision; an avatar carrying cloth or a tap on screen does not verify child physically moving. CO-PLAY. Caregiver makes accessible safe real cloth/target available, invites original child bodily participation, provides PK3 responsive support and records actual child action/grade boundary privately. ADULT-LED. Teacher observes child ENTRY, models distinct adult settling-body goal, offers supported cloth task DURING and different locating/indicating goal EXIT. CLASSROOM. Only one active learner at a time in an actual safe, clear, supervised movement bay; other children have calm stationary meaningful activities with genuinely available supervision under real staffing, or use later staffed rotations/sessions. Do not leave other pupils unattended while providing body support, invent additional assistants, assume 24 separate source acts from one demonstration, or force a single period for all. Record the 2–3 individuals whose actual safe body actions and help were witnessed per feasible observation interval; all others NOT_OBSERVED until a real turn. No class competition, public ability ranking or recorded mobility video.

Assessment record: actual safe task and child consent, child's bodily first and later linked actions, who physically moved fabric, PK3 responsive support versus content cues, PK4 personally chosen purposeful transition, actual fabric endpoint, U/H/A/X, whether task was familiar or genuinely new and a real later date if any. Explicitly distinguish source-action candidate from mastery or demonstrated effectiveness.

Limitations: No child has performed this written lesson here; no real room/material safety trial, individual access effectiveness, physical/tactile print QA, independent educator reliability, later retention or working SOLO/equivalent four-mode outcome exists.
