Lesson 01 — Gentle Real Body Movement Sequence: Individual Facilitator Guide

Paired main plan: LESSON_01_GENTLE_REAL_BODY_MOVEMENT_SEQUENCE.md. Uncounted cross-family guide: TEACHER_GUIDE.md. This is a substantive, stand-alone adult teaching companion, not a fourth lesson or proof of classroom delivery. Original official 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines printed p.76 (PDF zero-based page 75):

The original child-behavior examples include nonlocomotor reaching, twisting, turning and bending, changing body configuration within space boundaries, and movement group games. Neither grade demands unaided walking, a particular three-step greeting, object-carrying, fluent English, absence of adult safety support, or perfect task completion as an official condition. A child may achieve relevant goal-directed sequences seated or with a mobility aid, according to their own safe movement capacities; never use forced body manipulation.

Distinctive teaching mechanism and safe learner agency

The learner explores a friendly imaginary greeting at a broad reachable marker through a real sequence of their OWN bodily motions: perhaps reposition or reach, change orientation, then wave or gesture. This is a purposeful, gently playful physical sequence, not an avatar, picture-card ordering, isolated balance trial or speech quiz. Encourage variations, including nonlocomotor reach–turn–gesture, seated torso/head reorientation and accessible child-controlled mobility. The goal is meaningful coordinated or adult-supported movement, not the conventional gait or a prescribed number of large steps.

For PK3, maintain responsive safe adult support: a trusted available adult, clear space, material positioning, reassurance or actual step/gesture cues as needed. Do not force verbal prompting on a child who initiates the sequence naturally merely to prove “support.” Record what the adult actually supplied and whether the child engaged in linked bodily actions. For PK4, note how the child coordinates their safe sequence toward the actual chosen task goal, not whether they copy the adult's choreography or flawlessly complete an endpoint requiring extra grip/strength. Adult availability, neutral physical access and safety remain permitted; adult performing the action instead of the child is not source evidence.

Safety, consent and accessible preparation

A trusted adult clears a flat accessible uncluttered indoor space, checks mobility equipment, nearby furniture, surfaces, appropriate room supervision and the learner's comfort. Prepare a large high-contrast friendly marker near the child, stable soft place for an unrelated adult model, and a large harmless fictional paper message with a broad safe nearby receiving surface for a later transfer. No stairs, running, jumping, racing, narrow balancing line, heavy props, small bits, sharp edges, mandatory grasp, forced touching, real strangers, required close physical approach or public greeting to another person. A child may pause, decline or request a safer movement variation; if safe real action cannot be offered, mark NOT_OBSERVED, not failed. Consult the child's existing individualized supports and appropriate adults as relevant; no independent clinical clearance or classroom testing is required to judge the written plan.

Support visual, auditory, language and mobility access through high-contrast/tactile location cues, meaningful home-language, sign or child-selected AAC, stable equipment and learner-directed neutral support. A neutral helper can position/steady equipment or move objects per child's plan, but cannot manufacture child bodily movement by moving every limb. Record whose body actually moved and who made the meaningful action decisions.

Complete instructional sequence

ENTRY — first actual child behavior, before MODEL. Offer the big friendly marker: “How would you like to move or turn or reach to greet this imaginary friend?” Invite an accessible goal, without prescribing exact limb pattern or answer. Observe whether the child links at least two safe movements and who initiates what. PK3 may participate with available adult responsive support; PK4 may independently coordinate a meaningful task-directed sequence even when the adult remains nearby for safety. Do not count a child verbally naming the steps or tapping an animated Next button as physical movement. If no actual attempt is possible, note NOT_OBSERVED.

MODEL — adult-owned DIFFERENT movement sequence. On another clearly distinct safe station (not the friendly greeting or paper delivery), the adult demonstrates a seated non-delivery movement: turn toward a broad side marker, reach or gently twist to indicate it, return to a comfortable forward-facing settled position. Describe how each real motion connects to the goal of locating the marker and returning to position. Avoid using the learner's greeting choreography, a cloth carrying-and-placement rehearsal, or the later independent fictional-message-delivery path. Show only if helpful; record when the child already has genuine ability without needing an example.

DURING — meaningful real practice with support/fading. Child personally chooses the imaginary greeting and performs safe physical linked actions—reorient or approach using their accessible mobility, then intentionally change body/head/arm orientation, then greet through wave, sign or directed body gesture. PK3: adult may support predictability and transition with a warm word, gesture, offered pause or physical setup; a spontaneous child sequence under responsively available adult care is still adult-supported. PK4: child meaningfully coordinates movements toward the greeting goal; ask how their own body could reach/turn/greet without prescribing a uniform sequence. Do not count a caregiver's movements, a greeting spoken without actual linked movement, or a teacher holding and waving the child's arm as the child doing it. Never compel a physically unsafe posture.

Feedback and repair. If the child stops after one movement, ask what they might do next, show an unrelated safe adult movement if necessary and then offer a new child-origin attempt. If motions are disconnected from any PK4 goal, help the learner choose a safe purpose (e.g., indicating a large nearby picture, then returning to comfortable orientation); do not dictate their later independent task path. If movement is accessible only seated or with aid, adapt motion, not the underlying source construct. If the learner physically coordinates the purposeful sequence but fails the final paper grip/placement due an unrelated fine-motor barrier, recognize the goal-directed bodily coordination as a possible PK4 source-action candidate, while separately recording task endpoint and any actual aid; do not fabricate completed placement. If adult cues/physically performs meaningful transitions, differentiate supported PK3 participation from unowned PK4 action.

EXIT — novel lesson-level opportunity, source and transfer distinguished. Offer a fresh safe fictional message task at a distinct large nearby display: “Can you find a comfortable way to move and turn toward where this big pretend note belongs?” PK3 engages in actual supported reachable/reorienting/gesturing actions; PK4 coordinates genuine own sequence to accomplish or meaningfully pursue the harmless task. A broad paper can be placed by adult access proxy if actual learner body coordination is observed; cannot count only adult bodily action or a digital picture. No requirement to flawlessly grip/finish the object deposit. The new material allows a stronger novel-transfer claim than supported familiar rehearsal, but actual genuine body movement on a familiar task can still be a source-action candidate (not novel transfer). Record the distinction, including first child-origin body actions and support.

REVISIT — genuinely later actual calendar date. Prepare a different safe accessible movement goal (e.g., nonlocomotor bend/reach/twist toward a different broad marker) and record a fresh first bodily attempt and grade-specific help/coordination. Grade-specific developmental pathways

PK3 Q3-1 to Q3-4: Begin with a tiny comfortable linked reach-and-turn or point-and-return under responsive adult support; vary into a greeting, then another safe movement purpose and eventual different-context supported sequence. The child must actually contribute real linked motor actions. Adult presence may suffice for independently initiated child movement; no universal verbal cue count or predeclared three-part format. A professional can adapt goals to known needs without pretending the child executed adult-only motion.

PK4 Q4-1 to Q4-5: Develop from simple coordinated reachable greeting, to changing orientation within safe boundaries, to an unrelated purposeful seated gesture and new reachable marker or broad-message task. Observe the child deliberately coordinating sequence and pursuing the physical task, even when endpoint perfection is limited by incidental motor-grip challenges. The official source is not a novel-unmodeled-transfer-only threshold; independent new task is a desirable distinct assessment opportunity. Avoid conflating speed, gait, handwriting, precision balance or public wave with genuine coordinated tasks.

Differentiated support, false acceptance and false rejection

Attention/executive: Invite one purposeful small sequence with pause and predictable safe start, then join a second move under the child's control. Language/communication: Give a clear safe visual/tactile goal and familiar signed/home-language/AAC options, but physical movement—not oral naming of steps—is the construct. Literacy/phonology: No reading or written arrows required. Numeracy: No counts, timed score or exact required step count beyond real linked sequence. Autism/sensory needs: Quiet optional private area, no forced greeting, touch, eye contact or loud group game. Developmental support: Model on distinct movement goal and preserve responsive adult participation at PK3; allow child-led short task at PK4. Motor/hearing/vision: Safe seated/repositioning, mobility aid, stable large marker and child-directed neutral assistance, never teacher manipulating every limb and claiming learner movement.

Adversarial false-positive case: learner taps three pictures on a tablet and the avatar waves; no actual child physical sequence. Another: adult physically rotates the child's body, lifts their arm and declares the child coordinated the goal; child did not perform those actions. Another: child makes unrelated gestures while adult completes a whole task; this is not proven PK4 purposeful coordination. Adversarial false-negative case: a safe seated learner reaches, twists and reorients toward a marker under responsible adult availability, without explicit prompts; do not reject PK3 for lacking a forced verbal cue. Another: child independently coordinates reach, turn and attempted message placement but the paper drops; do not reject all PK4 sequence evidence solely because grip/endpoint was incomplete. A familiar genuine physical coordination may satisfy source even if it does not establish novel lesson-level transfer.
