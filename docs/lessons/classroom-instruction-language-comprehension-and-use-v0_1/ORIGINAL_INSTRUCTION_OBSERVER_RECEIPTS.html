01 · Understanding is not blind compliance

Teacher-original contextual language detective

An adult asks a learner to “choose the large card,” then separately to “check whether it fits.” The actions differ. A child may communicate the difference through a chosen safe AAC/sign/voice explanation, or noncompulsory independent choice with accessible support.

Teacher takes their own demonstration turn; separate items are optional and the same safe materials may be reused. A child moving the same toy each time may have learned a motor routine, not the spoken words.

No teacher gesture, highlighted answer, adult device-autoselection, peer imitation or public behavioral judgment should be scored as independent word comprehension. A child may decline a task safely. PK3 expression is enrichment, not new official source; PK4 requires child-owned E language separately.

Adult preparation

What actual concept is the instruction word conveying?
Could same movement look correct without comprehension?
Does child have home-language/sign/AAC listening access?

R = receptive understanding; E = expressive use. U = child-selected meaning; H = supplied answer; A = access-only help; X = uncertain. AAC = augmentative and alternative communication. Original adult-only aid, no child observations. PK3 source requires receptive word understanding only; PK4 adds child-origin expressive instructional language, evidenced distinctly but not necessarily in a separate event. 02 · Child guides a voluntary safe project

Teacher MODEL of a different own task, not learner's scripted response

Adult demonstrates with their own large safe materials; a second adult is optional. Adult narrates “I choose my wide mat. I check whether this card fits.” The adult may narrate their own action and show its meaning to the child. For PK4, identify both understanding and intentional use. They may occur in one actual episode; a useful description or direction can supply expression.

Words “share” or “take turns” do not justify forcing a child to give away personal items, to touch others, to perform a task or to make eye contact.

An adult operating a learner's AAC device under exact child-directed word selection may be A access; adult deciding the semantic word/position is H. A chorus repeating “check” is MODEL/DURING, not authentic PK4 E source.

Teacher-only false-credit correction

Did teacher supply intended word/content or only motor access?
Did learner indicate actual word meaning or copy motor routine?
Is current meaning clear? If not, what natural contrast could clarify it?
