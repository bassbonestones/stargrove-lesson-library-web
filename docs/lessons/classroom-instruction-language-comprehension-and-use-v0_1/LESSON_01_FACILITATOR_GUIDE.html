Facilitator guide: words that help classroom learning

Focused action-word component of PK3.II.D.2 (understands receptive classroom instructional language) and PK4.II.D.2 (understands and uses expressive classroom instructional language). Read the technical plan for exact official source and detailed optional progression. This is not exhaustive classroom vocabulary, a compliance test or observed efficacy.

Materials and preparation

Two different familiar large safe objects and a stable reachable space suffice for one adult/child; optional mats and separate model/transfer pairs are conveniences, not prerequisites. Use the same pair in turns if needed. Check names/referents, safe texture, age suitability, choking/sharp/loose-fiber hazards, willingness and communication access. Keep the child’s familiar language/sign/AAC vocabulary and usual access positioning; verify uncertain translations before drawing meaning conclusions. No printer, six-object quota or second adult required. Optional word mats and observer receipts are adult aids, not a displayed answer key.

Source context and evidence

Use actual classroom instructional language in a real learning activity. One-to-one tutoring or genuine home-learning instruction can supply that context; a general household game is related practice, not automatically evidence of classroom language. State the real context rather than stage a fake school scene. Action words are examples: meaningful descriptive/content-area terms also belong to the broader source.

PK3 needs receptive meaning, not expressive speech. PK4 needs both receptive and intentional expressive meaning; the two may be shown in one authentic episode or different natural opportunities, with evidence and help for each identified separately. A child’s meaningful description “I checked it” can be expressive use, not only directions to a helper. No changed material/room, unheard word, additional test or later date is needed after a valid familiar-context current observation. Changed contexts can strengthen optional transfer evidence. Immediate answer-cued echo, gaze-following or fixed motor habits are not automatically understanding.

ENTRY → MODEL → DURING → current EXIT → later REVISIT

ENTRY: Invite a real learning purpose: choosing a classroom book or making a small display. “Choose one.” At a useful moment: “Show me your choice.” Either option can be valid; do not point at a preferred answer. A grab might be preference rather than word meaning. A refusal can demonstrate comprehension when it communicates that meaning; silence/refusal alone is not a failure.

MODEL: Take your own turn: “I choose this book. Choosing means deciding which I want. I show you so you know my choice.” One adult can narrate their own action; no fictional colleague response is needed. If using check, state the criterion before examining it. Make the demonstration accessible without forced eye contact.

DURING: Follow the child’s learning project. Explain a useful contrast when needed and note semantic help. Return choices rather than moving the child’s hand or selecting their device answer. Fade answer hints, not access. Child may clarify, pause or decline. If meaning is already genuine and clear, acknowledge it without requiring a new test.

EXIT/current check: A meaningful observation already made can count. If uncertainty remains and the child is willing, ask in a natural opportunity with familiar materials, varying only what helps rule out a particular habit/gesture/echo explanation. “How can I help?” may invite expressive use if needed, but a genuine description may already supply it. Record receptive/expressive evidence separately, not necessarily in separate events. A peer model does not erase all later independent meaning; record copying/uncertainty only when warranted. No whole-standard mastery from one word.

REVISIT: A later-day opportunity may use the same or a different routine. Record the actual date/action only when it happens; until then retention is NOT_OBSERVED. An immediate retry is practice. No forced delayed test before recognizing current meaning.

CLASSROOM organization

Use two familiar objects per participating child or plentiful shared materials with voluntary turns; model with available items. All children remain in the usual safely supervised area, continuing chosen learning projects while a teacher briefly observes one focal child. No queue, isolation screen, compulsory display or fixed observation quota. A private rotation list includes quiet/AAC learners in later opportunities. Unobserved children remain not yet observed, not failed. Stop notes whenever supervision/care needs attention; reduce simultaneous participation or defer observation rather than invent another adult.

Record honestly and privately

Date/local code, actual learning context, term/language, exact prompt, actual response and help/uncertainty for receptive and expressive dimensions. U = child-selected meaning; A = access without semantic answer; H = supplied meaning; X = unclear. Labels do not replace description. Supported learning stays valuable but is not independent proof. No photos, diagnosis, medical/family details or public ranking required; use setting consent/storage rules.

Four routes and limitations

CO-PLAY: follow a child’s interest and keep context/meaning claims honest. ADULT-LED: teach a useful contrast, return semantic choice and observe proportionately. CLASSROOM: supervise meaningful participation and attribute evidence individually. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: no working accessible app or equal-effects claim. No actual child learning, educator field trial, physical/tactile print trial, delayed retention or measured mode parity is established.
