Teach the Honest Data Detective

Reader-friendly guide. The child checks that every graph mark is backed by a real object or real voluntary answer.

What children learn

An honest graph does not invent items, count the same source twice, or treat silence as a “no.” The learner investigates where each mark came from. For a 3-year-old, meaningful real-group collecting and organizing matter; for a 4-year-old, personally gathering actual data and constructing a faithful graphic can suffice.

Prepare safely

Use a small genuine source—large safe physical objects or a harmless optional question offered to real willing participants. Keep people's identities out of the public graph. Have a blank picture/mark mat, enough large markers and a different teacher-only example containing an intentional discrepancy. Never stage a child's socially embarrassing mistake or fabricate real student answers.

If a real small group is unavailable for the younger goal, practice with objects and honestly leave group-participation evidence unobserved.

A friendly detective process

1. Ask where the data really comes from

Say: “What things or real answers could we collect? How will we know which real thing each mark shows?”

Let the child help inspect actual sources and start their own blank graph before the adult shows an example.

2. Model a harmless made-up error on your own objects

Put out three real teacher-owned objects but draw four marks in a separate example.

Say: “My picture shows four, but I only have three actual pieces. Something's wrong. I need to check each real piece.”

Correct the excess mark. Use another adult-only illustration to show why a person who didn't answer is not a ‘no’ vote.

3. Invite real child investigation

Offer different real objects or truly voluntary harmless group responses.

Say: “Could you show which real piece or answer belongs to this mark? Did we use one twice? Did someone choose not to answer?”

During practice, support child-generated checking without handing over the correct totals. Accessible movement or communication assistance can remain child-directed.

4. Give a fresh uncoached source

With a different actual collection and empty graph, invite the child to gather and represent real information.

Say: “Can you collect what is really here and show it truthfully?”

Observe the child's own object or answer gathering, real graph marks and whether categories match the sources. Do not count a copied correction of your demo as independent evidence; a separate new sample is necessary.

5. Revisit a real new occasion

A different source on an actual later date can test transfer. Planned future poll responses do not exist until recorded.

Respond to what you see

What happens Your move
A child adds a mark for someone who did not answer Keep nonresponse separate, not falsely marked “no”
One object produces two marks Show an independent one-at-a-time source check

A child's category choice differs from yours Accept it if the source relation is coherent and honest

Teach the Honest Data Detective with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

An honest graph does not invent items, count the same source twice, or treat silence as a “no.” The learner investigates where each mark came from. For a 3-year-old, meaningful real-group collecting and organizing matter; for a 4-year-old, personally gathering actual data and constructing a faithful graphic can suffice.

Prepare the classroom

Prepare a truly verifiable simple real-object collection, blank chart space and a distinct teacher-owned fictional error demonstration with clearly labeled model pieces. Keep classmates' private choices out of any investigative game unless actually freely supplied and relevant.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “How can we tell if a graph really shows what happened?” Demonstrate deliberately making one extra mark on your own model objects, then checking and repairing it against the known source. Make clear this is a teaching example, not actual corrupted class data.

Give children meaningful practice

Groups take turns as source checker, chart maker, questioner and repair explainer using genuine bounded objects or consenting harmless contributions. They trace any mark to a real source before claiming a mismatch; no child should be rewarded for accusing a peer of a made-up error.

Observe individual learning fairly

Observe each focal learner with a new real source/chart comparison: can they identify a genuinely unsupported or missing mark when present, or accurately conclude that a truthful chart matches its source? Do not invent a flaw in the independent real dataset. A child repeating 'check the votes' without actually comparing sources is guided vocabulary, not demonstrated provenance reasoning.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Accept pointing, recounting and child-directed accessible marking. Protect privacy and normalize the valid answer 'the chart matches.' If the data source cannot be verified, don't label a child's inference correct or wrong with fabricated certainty. Revisit a different true collection another day.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

Method 03 — Data-Source Detective: Optional Polls, Missing Marks, Honest Charts

Original E2.2 written lesson method; grade-specific preparation in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. PK3.V.E.2 participates in real group collection/graphic organization (SHA d96dbdfe74f4165f743a188f142a8e4a6754385750c5ba3c3fe34e0a117a16db). PK4.V.E.2 personally collects real data and organizes graphic (SHA 8e242aa02657364c902288ec9d00fd6162493bb5193fac8d9e35e9671059dd13). This method's distinct mechanism is provenance and discrepancy reasoning: what question was asked, which real observation produced each mark, who (if anyone) declined, which mark is duplicated/missing/mislabeled, and how the child independently repairs the graph using an authentic source. Not Method 01 social voting or Method 02 object inventory alone. The fraudulent chart exists only in a clearly labeled teacher MODEL, never independent assessment.

Safe real-world preparation

Use a trivial optional real-group question with two safe nonpersonal choices: e.g. “Which of these two safe oversized art shapes did you choose for the shared construction today?” Do not ask about real family, health, food/allergies, pets, transport, religious life, economic status or school absence. Participants may not choose, and nonresponse is distinct from NO. For each eligible source, capture one original answer (A/B) without recording a name: an adult may issue each real willing respondent one large harmless nonidentifying turn token and collect it once when the person chooses, or neutrally witness one response per willing person without keeping identities. For objects, use separate uncounted/checked trays and move each distinct safe actual item exactly once. Record only real source category/count, never assign an absent/declining person a NO vote. The educator verifies unique sources without preassigning the child's graphic answer. Do not publish individual classmates' answers or names. Count only people who actually participated; a drawn character is not a participant. Alternately use a bounded actual object source (large safe circles/squares) as physically traceable observations, particularly for PK4 child-only practice. The printed ORIGINAL_DATA_INVESTIGATION_CARDS.html contains only blank recording forms, except visually flagged fictional MODEL cases. Question/category labels must be learner-perceivable in pictures/tactile/sign/AAC and mutually exclusive initially. If adult cannot verify source or a voluntary group isn't possible, record no credible group EXIT rather than guess data.

ENTRY before MODEL: Give learner a fresh real source with a blank two-category mat and ask “Which actual things/answers should we collect, and how can we make sure every mark belongs to something we really found?” First child-led collection and graphic action logged. For PK3 this occurs in a genuine group where learner cooperatively acquires and organizes response marks; for PK4 a privately bounded actual physical-object census is sufficient. An adult placing already collected marks then asking “what does this mean?” is only graph reading.

MODEL on DIFFERENT teacher-owned objects: Adult deliberately draws four marks for only three separate teacher-demo large pieces and pauses: “Our model graph says four but I only found three real pieces. We must not add a pretend extra piece; I re-check one by one.” Correct the discrepancy from actual model source; demonstrate missing mark and a mismatched category, one error at a time. For a fictional survey model, narrate that a child has not answered and cannot be counted as “no”; do not show names or claim model votes are real. Teacher says “An honest graph shows only what we really collected.” Never embed intentional fraud in children's scored data.

DURING: Learner directs actual collection of a new real object sample or a separate real group option response, makes their own picture graph. Adult asks “Can you show which real item made THIS mark? Which person actually answered? Did any item get used twice? Was anybody silent?” These open questions guide supported DURING only; in independent EXIT ask simply to collect/show the facts. If a learner overcounts due to double markers, adult returns to teacher-only source example, does not fix their scored chart for them.

Fresh independent EXIT: New source that teacher has verified but not told child: a clean bounded tray of actual distinct oversized shapes, or a new consenting real group of people with unique optional responses. Child PK4 selects real category/question, genuinely collects each source, and represents it in a faithful new picture/floor graph with no adult numerical hints or prefilled columns, then if requested locates actual source for a mark. Child PK3 must genuinely participate in a group collecting and organizing real data in a graphic, with individually attributable child action; a sibling's prompted fictional responses and chat-bot “classmates” are NOT valid. A sourced discrepancy check strengthens evidence, but perfect graph error-detection is an author-added teaching extension, not an official V.E.2 substandard. REVISIT on later actual calendar date, NOT_OBSERVED until performed.

Four PK3 stages — collaboratively source, not just watch

PK3 G3-1: ENTRY with actual cooperating small group and genuine safe object/response; child helps make source-connected mark. MODEL a DIFFERENT teacher-only object source with deliberate extra mark, DURING group records real items, EXIT new genuine group child collects and properly places one response. False-positive adult supplies mark and child echoes: model other example and new group action. Later REVISIT. PK3 G3-2: ENTRY real optional safe two-choice group question, child helps gather true responses and place picture symbols in columns; MODEL separate teacher object census and what one mark signifies, DURING real willing group contributors (adults and/or peers) one independently checked response per person using a one-use anonymous token or neutral adult witnessed turn, EXIT different actual group/question child helps both collect and arrange. Duplicate speaker not duplicate vote. Later day. PK3 G3-3: ENTRY actual group chooses how to verify own source after a teacher-model discrepancy, MODEL unrelated erroneous set, DURING child assists with group recount/repair of a missing mark, EXIT brand new source group graphic child-origin accurate placement and optional check without leading cue. No mandatory numerals or full formal audit. Later date. PK3 G3-4: ENTRY new real group question/contributed objects with learner meaningful active role, MODEL unrelated honest-graph teacher sample, DURING child helps gather and organize actual data, full independent group source EXIT different real group/collection where child genuinely does both and the graphic faithfully depicts records. If no group exists, NOT_OBSERVED, not synthetic pass. New-day REVISIT.

Five PK4 stages — child-owned provenance and graphic

PK4 G4-1: ENTRY fresh real bounded object sample and blank mat, child tries one-mark-to-object connection, MODEL other adult source with extra misleading mark, DURING child moves own source to checked zone, EXIT new true object sample faithfully graphed by child. Later-day REVISIT. PK4 G4-2: ENTRY completely unseen large safe physical-object set, learner chooses which two categories to observe and captures data, MODEL separate sample with one repeated object, DURING child verifies each once, EXIT new real set and faithful graph. “I saw three in picture” not proof of real observation. REVISIT later. PK4 G4-3: ENTRY learner gets new source with potential category ambiguity (two colors and two shapes), chooses meaningful category rule and constructs a graphic under its own labels. MODEL teacher-owned false mismatched category and repair; DURING child checks each mark against actual item, EXIT new actual object collection organized into a correctly labeled graph without answer-coded colors. A defensible different attribute rule is valid; wrong to insist one teacher answer. Later-date REVISIT. PK4 G4-4: ENTRY with authentic optional small group poll if real consenters exist, learner asks one clear exclusive question per real respondent and notes no answer separately. MODEL teacher-only fictional survey with duplicate/blank, DURING learner collects and charts own real responses or chooses independently safe physical items where a poll cannot be safely gathered, EXIT fresh actual source child collects and represents. A yes/no chart may include no-response field but NO is not a nonresponse. Later date. PK4 G4-5: ENTRY new bounded real-world set or real consenting poll and blank original graphic, learner chooses categories/collects before any teacher model. MODEL unrelated teacher-only source if needed, DURING child finds/fixes actual mistaken mark with real source, full source EXIT new actual data source gathered and correctly graphically represented by child with clear question/categories and true source fidelity (one mark per real unit is the preferred concrete model; an independently child-owned, accurate, explicitly keyed different graphical scale is also a valid source display, not a required advanced task). Read/interpret graph is optional enrichment; no invented statistical mastery. REVISIT genuinely different date NOT_OBSERVED.

Specific false-credit, correction, and remaining limits

False-positive 1: teacher produces source tally of six although only four objects/answers exist; learner copies bar of six perfectly. Why false: graphic not faithful, data not collected by child. Repair: model extra-mark error with separate real teacher objects, then untouched real source where child acquires and accurately charts one by one. False-positive 2: a real child declines a harmless poll, learner marks NO and tells adult all group members answered. Why false: a nonresponse is not NO, category and source invalid. Repair: model separate no-answer teacher scenario, child creates new authentic poll/other real bounded object graph and keeps unknown separate; never force answer. False-positive 3: online software displays robot votes under character names and says “our group participated”; assessed PK3 clicks a bar. Why false: group was fictional, the child's collecting role absent. Repair: design true consented, privacy-safe human contributors and verify actual child graph participation; if unavailable only scaffold and NOT_OBSERVED. Remaining limitations: provenance and accessibility under real classrooms are genuinely difficult and need independent review, and privacy/unique source records must not turn into surveillance. Retrospective learning-differences remediation — 2026-11-01

This is an INTERNAL AUTHOR WRITTEN instructional revision, not observed child efficacy or delivered software.

PK3: real group contributions carried through child-involved collection and graphic organization

Source action: Child PARTICIPATES IN A REAL GROUP'S activity of COLLECTING actual data AND ORGANIZING it in a genuine graphic representation; ordinary single-child chart or adult-only data collection is not this official action. Teaching/access and fading: Use non-sensitive voluntary genuine peer response collection ONLY with actual group and explicit missing/no response state; child participates in adding source-traceable marks. Fresh child-origin check: New real willing small-group survey about two harmless choices, child receives real answer and assists organizing corresponding honest graphic mark. False-credit adversarial case: Teacher invents nonresponders' preferences or uses scripted pretend classmates while claiming PK3 actual group collection. Teacher fills completed chart, invented classmates and votes, passive student audience, graph of one person masquerading as group, or copying decorations do not prove actual PK3 participation.

PK4: child-collected actual source observations with honest source-to-graphic correspondence

Source action: Child COLLECTS genuinely observed data (objects or authentic voluntary responses) AND ORGANIZES the real results in a graphic representation; no compulsory class survey, axes, numeral notation or statistical standard. Teaching/access and fading: If survey is appropriate, learner collects real voluntary non-sensitive answers, labels missing/declined distinctly, and graphics only observed responses; otherwise use legitimate object census. Fresh child-origin check: New willing group's actual responses: learner independently gathers and categorizes them, marks each once and leaves silent child as missing, not no vote. False-credit adversarial case: Script invents 12 pretend classmates, simulates replies or uses adult-created tallies, then claims authentic PK4 source collection. Teacher-created data set, preprinted answered graph, a chart interpreted but not constructed, fabricated survey replies, duplicates or count-only score omit required child collection plus organization. Seven access profiles: attention_and_executive_function: Use neutral one-object/one-marker exchange and unnumbered broad source tray with actual object return, learner-controlled pause, and vary which data type arrives first.; language_processing_and_communication: Present consent-based broad categories with clear meaningful pictures, optional home-language/AAC responses, and individual private accessible contribution without forced disclosure.; literacy_and_phonological_processing: Use real manipulatives, large image category heads and movable graph spaces without reading/spelling requirements; verify true category membership of each observed source.; numeracy_and_mathematical_processing: Use one tangible countable mark per VERIFIED observation with independent source tracking; allow simple nonnumerical bars or object pictures; distinguish missing responses from zero preference.; autism_predictability_and_sensory_needs: Offer predictable small-group roles and private neutral answer/motor choice, non-sensitive object-count alternatives and freedom not to respond to surveys.; intellectual_and_developmental_support: Demonstrate genuine collection from a DIFFERENT object set, then fade category-highlighting, have child receive actual objects and place graphic marks from each source.; physical_motor_hearing_vision_access: Provide large accessible high-contrast/tactile graph spaces, meaningful spoken/signed/auditory category access, child-directed switch/gaze placement and adult motor assistance without source decisions. Delivery routes (written, not executable): SOLO must not supply the answer or imply working accessible software; CO-PLAY permits neutral caregiver facilitation, not conceptual answers; ADULT-LED uses a different modeled example before an uncued source probe; CLASSROOM checks each learner independently, not choral compliance. Individualize based on real child/family and relevant specialist plans; retain modality access while fading answer hints.

Source-bound real data acquisition, beyond the fictional error example

The duplicate response and missing category are adult-only MODEL/teaching exercises. Never plant false votes in real children's data or take a correct detective answer as actual data collection. For PK3.V.E.2 independent EXIT, use a NEW real group making voluntary harmless choices: the assessed child actively helps COLLECT real distinct contributing group answers AND helps ORGANIZE a genuinely new blank group graphic. Fake classmate responses, a single child-only answer, or a group leader doing all marking cannot satisfy PK3 group source. For PK4.V.E.2 independent EXIT, the child may independently COLLECT real safe objects or real voluntary responses and ORGANIZE authentic categories into a newly made authentic graphic (one mark per unit is the taught default, but an independently created accurately keyed alternate scale is equally legitimate); a real peer group is not mandated. If source units are missing, keep unanswered separate from a no response and never create invented totals. Fade adult hints from DURING before independent EXIT. Companion 03 — Facilitating genuine survey provenance

Paired plan: Lesson 03 survey provenance detective, PK3.V.E.2 and PK4.V.E.2. Objective guide remains uncounted. This companion is specific to error analysis and genuine data provenance.

Why investigating false data teaches an important graph skill

A fictional ADULT model with deliberately duplicated mark, missing response mistakenly recorded as “no,” or column assigned to incorrect category teaches that graphics have real accountable sources. Unlike Lesson 01’s communal gathering or Lesson 02’s direct object-to-mark census, the investigation distinguishes each source's eligibility and whether marks fairly reflect it. Spotting an adult mistake is NOT itself the published outcome; independent EXIT must still contain the child’s actual data collection and organization. Do not plant false records in authentic children's data as an experiment.

Prep and consent

Build a separate fictional teacher-only survey with three safe toy symbols, one intentionally duplicated on the demo. Maintain a verified answer key ONLY for teacher MODEL. For subsequent genuine practice and EXIT, use separate blank graphs and honest data: voluntary harmless group opinions or real object contributions from an actual multi-person group for PK3 (including genuinely contributing adults, not solely same-age peers); real independently observed objects/consented answers for PK4. For group responses, an adult issues one harmless large anonymous turn token to each willing responder and collects it once or discreetly witnesses that person's one turn without recording identity. For object units, each distinct safe item moves once from uncounted to checked zone. Adult verifies provenance rather than choosing the learner's group/graphic answer. If a respondent declines, mark UNKNOWN/NOT_ANSWERED, not negative vote; never solicit protected personal details or record child identities in curriculum repository.

Teaching decisions and source phases

ENTRY: Show a genuinely new neutral real source situation before the detective MODEL and record any child-first actual collection and organization. For PK3 lacking real group, report pending source evidence. MODEL: On a wholly separate FICTIONAL adult-owned chart, demonstrate a doubled picture token and ask how a responsible person verifies against existing safe source units. Correct it visibly as teacher work; never claim a child's own survey. DURING: Child examines another nonidentifying toy graph mismatch, distinguishes actual one-per-response from duplicates, then participates in collecting genuinely new source data and personally plotting those marks. Fade adult source key, visible correction circles and hints before an independent attempt. EXIT: PK3.V.E.2 new genuinely willing group of multiple independently contributing people (adults or peers), where child meaningfully helps gather actual group source AND organize authentic graphic; a sole adult-prepared tray does not create a group; detecting a fictional duplicate alone never qualifies. PK4.V.E.2 child independently gathers actual safe observations or voluntary responses and makes a true graphic, keeping unanswered distinct from NO. One source unit to one mark is our taught beginning mechanism; an independently child-made accurate and meaningful explicit alternate symbol scale also constitutes graphic organization, but is never mandatory or credited when adult selected the answer. REVISIT: Genuinely later calendar date and new honest source; NOT_OBSERVED.

Feedback and adverse case handling

If child says a column has five but only four real answered objects exist, ask which distinct source supports EACH mark during practice, then use new source for assessment. If child mistakes no response for a NO, demonstrate the difference on other safe adult-only examples; do not force respondent to answer. If child suspects duplicate solely because a bar looks tall, show why real data sometimes are unequal and provenance, not visual symmetry, is the check. If an adult prefilled “correct” chart then child colors it, report completed chart reading practice, not child-origin data acquisition/organization.

Seven differentiated profiles and four modes

Attention/executive: one source discrepancy at a time, fade key; language/communication: composed AAC/sign/home language to inquire about source units; literacy/phonology: picture symbols rather than written survey answers; numeracy: mark-per-unit concrete check, no required formal statistics; autism/sensory: noncoercive privacy-safe choice and ability not to answer; intellectual/developmental: one fictional duplicate first then distinguish unknown and wrong column; motor/hearing/vision: accessible large truthful source tokens, child-directed proxy and qualified individualized support. Preserve actual source integrity instead of adult speaking the learner's answer. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: program can show fictional mistakes as practice but no claimed real group PK3 evidence. CO-PLAY: adult helps set up genuine voluntary source but not learner's mark/category meaning. ADULT-LED: model on separate fictional props, learner independently organizes real fresh source. CLASSROOM: small consenting authentic group, record individual collection and marking roles.
