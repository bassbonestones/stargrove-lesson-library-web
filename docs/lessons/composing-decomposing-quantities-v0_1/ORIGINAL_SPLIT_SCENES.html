01 · Whole fixed / parts change

First-party supported practice. Child chooses parts; not a standardized test.

Original library scene

Four large paper picture books stay in the library. The child may put them on either of two shelves. No book arrives or leaves.

Learner chooses a split and checks the whole. Invite another way if useful.
Original art gallery scene

Five large paper drawings are displayed in two safe areas. A curator moves a drawing from one wall to the other. The gallery still owns the same five drawings.

How could two different-size groups describe the same five? Does moving change whole?

Blue dots are countable objects. For five, 4+1 versus 3+2 is genuinely distinct; merely reversing trays is not a different unordered size combination.

02 · Empty whole and larger whole

Teaching cards: learner decides parts, not prefilled correct equations.

Original empty story

Two clean boxes sit on a table. No toy or paper shape has been brought to either box yet.

How many objects are in each part and in the whole? A printed empty box is NOT an object.
Original one paper star

One large cut-out paper star is in the story and two blank trays are available. No new star is introduced.

Can one part be empty? What is whole?
Original seven library cards

Seven big paper cards remain in two library trays. The child chooses how to redistribute these SAME seven cards without introducing a new one.

Invite child-chosen parts and a check. Teacher may MODEL 5 and 2, then 4 and 3 as practice; never score adult-selected parts as independent.

Scale to 6–10 only after concrete fixed-whole concept is accessible. Educator must not count pictures of boxes as tokens or force every possible partition.

03 · Moving is not adding/removing

First-party contrast stories and blank response record.

Original moving

Three original large paper squares start on a shelf. One is moved to a second shelf; none enter or leave the library.

Did the whole change? What are the two parts before/after?
Original new arrival

Three original large paper squares start on a shelf. A NEW paper square is delivered from outside the library.

Does this represent *repartitioning the same three*? Why not?
Original removal

Three large paper squares start on a shelf. One square leaves the library collection entirely.

Does the WHOLE stay three? Why not?

These are practice scenes. Familiar materials may show local evidence; note prior teaching. Only actual changed contexts support transfer, and a later real date supports revisit claims. No real child data or validated mode parity.
