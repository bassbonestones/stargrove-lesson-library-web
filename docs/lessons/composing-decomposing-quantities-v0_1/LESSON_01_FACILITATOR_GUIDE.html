Facilitator guide: Two trays, one whole

Use with the Lesson 01 plan. This companion supports decisions, not an extra lesson. TEACHER_GUIDE.md is the uncounted family overview.

What this experience is for

The child chooses how a fixed collection goes into two places, puts the parts back together and sees the same whole. This physical mechanism differs from a frame divider or a spoken story. Texas 2022 PK4.V.A.7 says “Child begins to understand that numbers 0-10 can be composed and decomposed in various ways to represent a quantity.” The source research cell (without terminal period) has SHA-256 f516c6263cd777db194ae66fe60757491e4e68630b8f02cff8125cec7f3d0876. Archived official PDF page 51 text has been compared. PK3.V.A.7 is explicitly a NON-OUTCOME research note; optional play is not a PK3 grade standard.

“Various ways” is a direction across experiences, not a mandate to show every pair, all numbers or two different unordered pairs for zero/one. Correct 4+1 and 1+4 are valid arrangements; they repeat the same sizes. Describe that limit rather than calling the child wrong. Local evidence and broad transfer are different.

Before the child arrives

One child needs two broad shallow trays, a nonslip enclosing mat and two large blunt nontoxic blocks to begin, with up to ten available in the adult box for later choices. Adult model uses a separate three-block set, two trays and mat. No small loose objects, food, printer, screen or numeral writing is needed. Flat marked areas can replace trays. Keep original items visible and unused ones outside the whole. Offer tactile boundaries, home language, sign, AAC, neutral descriptive audio or a precisely child-directed proxy as required. Ask about comfort and allow a pause.

For a class of twelve in six pairs, prepare fourteen trays, seven mats and thirty-three blocks including the adult model. Initially place two blocks on each child mat. Others remain in the adult-controlled box. Use the existing supervised table/circle. For an odd number, provide an individual set; individual parallel work is also a valid alternative to sharing. Never leave the rest of the group to test one child privately.

Five teaching phases with real decisions

ENTRY before any answer: “These are all our blocks. Choose where they go in these two places. What happens when we bring them together?” A whole in one tray and an empty part is valid. Start with two, or accept a comfortable chosen total up to ten. Record the first actual response before help. Refusal or no visible response is NOT_OBSERVED, not a score.

MODEL: set the child's whole aside visibly. With the adult's three, show one and two, rejoin, then none and three. Say what stayed the same and why. Do not show merely a reversal and call it different sizes. If only one set exists, mark the model as teaching and its later use as familiar, not novel transfer.

DURING: child moves or directs the pieces and checks them. “What would you like to try? How can you check?” Permit repetition. If they count one tray only, point around the whole mat and reunite; mark conceptual help. If a block enters accidentally, identify it openly and restore or recount the whole. If they reverse sides, acknowledge validity before inviting a different size pair. If they count the tray itself, distinguish contents from container using an empty adult tray. If they already find several ways, invite a larger familiar whole or a new context without imposing either.

EXIT: offer an unprompted choice/check after support is faded. Familiar objects/quantities are valid for local evidence. Document exposure rather than requiring a new size or buying new materials. The child should own the arrangement and check, not copy the adult's supplied answer. A changed context can sample transfer separately. Keep essential access. Stop when interest or safety ends; no timed threshold or mandatory number of trials.

REVISIT: plan a later actual date and record it only if performed. Familiar context checks return to the idea; changed context can sample transfer. Immediate retry is practice, not retention. REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED in this authored package.

Observe individual learning fairly

Record the actual whole, ordered pairs, check/rejoining action, original membership and assistance. U = child-origin without conceptual help; A = access-only help following the child's decision; H = conceptual or answer-bearing help; X = unclear provenance. H is worthwhile supported learning. X is independence NOT_ASSESSABLE. A partner's result and class chorus are never individual evidence. No observation is NOT_OBSERVED.

One valid child-chosen split with a meaningful whole check is local evidence. Two different unordered pairs for totals where possible add evidence of size variety. Neither proves all 0–10 or retention. Zero permits 0+0 only; one permits 1+0/0+1 but only one unordered pair. Counting a memorized equation without showing the relationship leaves understanding uncertain. Do not convert these descriptions into a diagnostic or readiness cutoff.

In CLASSROOM, the first partner chooses while the second checks; then switch. Keep every pair within ordinary supervision. Observe two or three focus learners only if safe, rotate later, and mark the rest NOT_OBSERVED. If partner prompting is unavoidable, record supported/uncertain evidence. Pause the whole group when materials scatter; write minimal notes only after safe cleanup. No additional adult is assumed.

Seven access decisions

  1. Attention/executive: offer a small visible whole, one invitation, unhurried turns and a break, not mandatory persistence.
  2. Language/communication: accept child-authored home-language, sign or augmentative and alternative communication; adult translation must not choose the math.
  3. Literacy: no reading, digit writing or equation recital required.
  4. Numeracy: reduce the whole for meaningful exploration; preserve actual membership. Zero/one do not have impossible second size pairs.
  5. Sensory/autism: quiet large objects, tactile/visual contrast and predictable transparent moves; no surprise “trick,” forced touch or eye contact.
  6. Intellectual/developmental: model slowly and repeat as useful; label prompted practice honestly rather than denying its teaching value.
  7. Motor/hearing/vision: use stable boundaries, meaningful description and a proxy moving exactly as directed. Adult-chosen parts are conceptual help, not access.

Apply actual individualized education/support plans with the relevant team. These materials do not diagnose disability or establish adaptation effectiveness.

False-positive and false-rejection checks

False-positive: adult chooses both pairs, peer arranges while focal child repeats “five,” or a sixth item arrives while observer marks “same five.” Correction: describe assistance/membership, teach openly, and offer a new child decision without pretending the earlier trial passed. False rejection: child gives 0+0, reverses two correct parts, uses familiar blocks or directs a proxy. Correction: credit actual local meaning and distinguish limited variety/transfer from failure.
