Composing & Decomposing Quantities · original teaching resource

Composing and decomposing the same whole: responsive PK4 progression

Revised shared guidance, DOT10, 13 November 2026. This is a shared boundary repair, not acceptance of sibling lessons. Lesson 01 and its companion are the currently repaired physical route. The older frame/story plans require their own applied reviews; their stricter novelty/multiple-pair wording must not override these source boundaries.

Official source and scope

2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Mathematics, printed/PDF page 51: PK4.V.A.7, “Child begins to understand that numbers 0-10 can be composed and decomposed in various ways to represent a quantity”. The printed source adds a terminal period. Research-cell SHA-256 f516c6263cd777db194ae66fe60757491e4e68630b8f02cff8125cec7f3d0876. Archived official page text has been compared. PK3.V.A.7 is an explicit research-insufficiency NON-OUTCOME note, not an official learning outcome. Source examples such as five shown as 4+1, 3+2 or 5+0 illustrate possibilities rather than mandatory modalities.

The goal is beginning understanding of composition and decomposition, not memorized algebra, all partitions, every number in one sitting or a developmental diagnostic. Fixed membership makes this physical lesson's whole clear. A correct child-chosen arrangement and meaningful rejoining/check can be local evidence. Varied size pairs across opportunities add evidence of variety. Reversals remain mathematically valid; 1+4 and 4+1 have the same unordered sizes. Zero only has 0+0 and one only the unordered pair 0+1. Do not invent an impossible second-pair requirement.

Shared teaching cycle

ENTRY: invite a first child-owned action before showing an answer. MODEL: demonstrate and explain on a distinct adult example when available, openly identifying teaching. DURING: allow repeated supported exploration and contingent feedback. EXIT: invite a fresh child decision without supplying the parts; familiar numbers/materials can show local understanding. Record previous modeling, prompts and who chose the arrangement. A changed context can sample transfer but novelty is not a prerequisite to local evidence. REVISIT: a genuinely later actual date, familiar or changed context labeled honestly. Current learner and revisit evidence is NOT_OBSERVED.

U means child-origin without conceptual help; A means access-only child-directed assistance; H means conceptual/answer-bearing help; X means uncertain provenance and independence NOT_ASSESSABLE, not failure. No class chorus, adult demo or partner result becomes individual evidence.

Optional PK3 enrichment

E-1: move two large safe toys between places and reunite. E-2: play with three, noticing the same toys remain. No official PK3.V.A.7 credit, age placement, failure or diagnosis follows. Older learners may revisit a prerequisite without stigma.

PK4 C4-1: small whole, one to three

ENTRY starts with two large blocks and two broad shallow trays inside a mat. “Choose where they go; what happens when we bring them together?” Empty parts are allowed. MODEL with three adult blocks: 1+2, reunite, then 0+3. Explain that no new block arrived. DURING let the child move/direct their small set. If they count one tray only, reunite both visibly and model again. EXIT invite a new unprompted arrangement and meaningful check. Same objects are valid; note rehearsal. One meaningful arrangement is local progress, not generalized 0–10. REVISIT later, not an immediate retry called retention.

PK4 C4-2: variety within two to five

ENTRY may be four when familiar; do not force a larger whole on a novice. MODEL 1+2 then 0+3 on three. These differ in unordered size, unlike 1+2 then 2+1. DURING invite another way. A child may choose 4+0, 3+1 or 2+2, or five as 4+1 and 2+3. Do not require all combinations. A reversal is correct but does not add a new size pair. EXIT record what was actually child-chosen, what remained constant and how checked. Two different size pairs strengthen variety evidence, not a pass/fail cut score. REVISIT may use familiar objects; reserve transfer claims for actual changed-context responses.

PK4 C4-3: empty parts, zero and one

ENTRY with empty trays: “What is in them?” With one block, offer either place. MODEL distinguish containers from contents; then show an empty part alongside three blocks. DURING allow 0+0, 1+0 and 0+1; no second unordered pair exists for zero/one. EXIT record meaningful none/one and rejoining or other accessible confirmation. Never count a drawn empty box or physical tray as a block. REVISIT in a later real opportunity. No formal numeral requirement.

PK4 C4-4: six through ten when useful

ENTRY offers a familiar manageable larger whole only when meaningful. MODEL seven as 5+2 and 4+3, checking the same seven. This example is teaching, not a supplied child answer. DURING child selects parts and checks the original set. Optional five-group organization can reduce counting load. Empty frame cells are capacity, not objects. If an item arrives or leaves, describe the changed whole openly. EXIT can use a familiar total 6–10 with a fresh child decision; record scope, help and actual pairs. No exhaustive enumeration. REVISIT when readiness and interest allow; returning to small numbers is legitimate support.

PK4 C4-5: context and later return

ENTRY can invite a pretend library, garage or simply two places, chosen without culturally specific knowledge. MODEL/DURING use examples openly as teaching and distinguish moving an original object from adding a new one. Never secretly alter the child's set. EXIT changed-context, child-owned actions may sample transfer. Familiar local actions remain valuable but do not establish transfer. REVISIT on a later actual date may show return to an idea; label the context and support. No real return has occurred in this written package.

Mechanisms and human delivery

Method 01: physical movement of a bounded original set into two child-chosen trays and recombination. Method 02: a divider/ten-frame makes occupied subgroups visible; empty capacity must not be counted. Its own legacy plan is not accepted by this shared repair. Method 03: a child-authored redistribution story checks meanings of moving versus actual arrival/removal. Its own legacy plan is not accepted here.

CO-PLAY: adult sets up safe materials, child chooses; role exchange optional. ADULT-LED: one child receives ENTRY, explicit MODEL, responsive DURING, bounded EXIT and planned later REVISIT. CLASSROOM: one educator maintains ordinary group supervision, temporary pair roles switch, individual evidence only for focus learners actually observed. For twelve in six pairs the Lesson 01 route uses fourteen trays, seven mats and thirty-three large blocks including adult model; initially two blocks per child mat. Others are NOT_OBSERVED, not failed. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. Static teacher pages are not a working child app or evidence of route parity.

Access, safeguarding and claims

Large blunt nontoxic age-appropriate blocks, shallow stable trays, enclosing mat and adult-controlled spare pieces. No beads, coins, food, secret substitutions or forced touch. Tactile/high contrast boundaries, home language, AAC/sign, meaningful description, breaks and precise child-directed motor proxy preserve agency. Adult part selection is conceptual help, not access. Respect actual individualized education plans; do not diagnose from speed, speech or counting.

Print only optional pages actually needed. All family printable pages are first-party practice resources, not a standardized test. Minimal local instructional notes suffice; no uploaded child records or images. No real teacher/child trial, physical printing, later retention, specialist access validation, operational SOLO or four-mode parity is claimed.
