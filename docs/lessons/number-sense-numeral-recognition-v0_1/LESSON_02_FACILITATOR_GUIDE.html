Exactly which distinct practical mechanism to teach

Identify stable numeral across two readable typefaces while designing and interpreting novel signs.

Full exact printed research source, separate grade boundaries and evidence

Original authored method's concrete learner actions and feedback model

  • Not assumed: Ability to count a set, reproduce a digit by handwriting, understand place value, or know an English-only digit name. First use entry evidence to choose contrasts and access supports.

Fairness checks: A serif 1 can legitimately look different from a sans 1. Rotation/mirroring is not fair font variation, especially for 6/9. Readable 10 must appear together on one complete card. A child's matching two shapes merely because they look alike is not proof that the child knows what either is called. Zero is printed 0, not a blank sign. Use accessible standard fonts, high contrast, no color-number code, no fake novelty glyphs or timed flashes.

B. Lesson sequence — entry, model, during, independent exit, later revisit

ENTRY E-PRINT, 2–3 min. Before introducing the print shop, the adult quietly shows an unknown standard printed 2 then an unfamiliar 0 to PK3; PK4 adds previously unseen 6, 9 and 10. “What number do you read on this sign?” If speech is not a usable response, narrate a target name and offer 3–4 neutral upright numeral-only choices, with positions changed between trials. Write down first actual response, access mode and whether any clue preceded it. Entry covers only samples, not the entire official range.

MODEL M-PRINT, 4 min. Place two sample signs with 2 in different fonts/sizes alongside a 3 foil. “These two signs say two even though the shape changes a little. This one says three. We don't decide by color or card size.” Show a real 0 on one sign and a blank sign next to it: “Only this printed shape is the numeral zero.” For PK4 demonstrate upright 6 versus 9, then two distinctly styled whole 10 signs versus single 1. Also model PK4 7 and 8 on separately labeled practice signs. Adult labels are teaching, not independent learner evidence. Child chooses the next font/size transformation.

DURING D-PRINT, 8–10 min. Child becomes the printer: adult verbally commissions a numeral and child selects the corresponding unsigned sample from a rotating 3–4-card field to place in blank sign frame A or B on PRINT_SHOP_MATS.html. Then adult replaces it with a different font of the same numeral plus a different numeral. Child decides what still says the same number and explains/chooses how they know. After several turns reverse roles: child commissions, adult prints, then **child read

Method-specific instructional rationale and preparation: Genuine distinct readable original numeral sign forms in two practical first-party noncommercial printable typography styles on age-safe original mock learning supply labels, with consistent identity. This is a benign purposeful safe source, not a decontextualized card matching/timing drill, and must be actually available if the official source requires real objects/people/child movement.

ENTRY — uncoached original child source, context and provenance

Before the educator models, E-PRINT first shows one uncoached conventional upright numeral at a time: PK3 2 then 0; PK4 2 and 0, then 6, 9 and whole 10. Record the child's first independent numeral name/sign/AAC response to an actual shown glyph, or a receptive target-name choice among neutral unlabelled glyphs (not teacher-supplied answer). A voluntary shorter opening is valid but missing planned symbols are NOT_OBSERVED. Cross-font recognition is the supported MODEL/DURING teaching mechanism, not an additional two-font skill prerequisite for initial ENTRY. A different genuinely held-out readable font supplies independent EXIT opportunities.

Document actual child first response (spoken/sign/AAC/meaningful self-directed physical action, as appropriate to printed source), grade, which physical/real-source materials or true willing people were available, and what adult said/did beforehand. If no safe real source or no real learner observation, record NOT_OBSERVED, not false child learning or lack of capability.

MODEL — adult demonstrates on different real owned source

Adult on unrelated class-owned adult sample sign demonstrates identifying same numeral in two visually distinct adult-only letter styles, never child source sign.

Never model the exact learner EXIT example or pre-answer with a pointing finger/answer cue. The adult's true demonstration is a teaching event and carries ZERO child independent evidence.

DURING — actual child activity, contingent instructional repair and faded hints

Learner compares meaningful digits despite font stroke and sign/background changes, creates own neutral sign optionally; verify PK3 0–5 or PK4 0–10 range and fade font feature prompt.

Track U (original learner decision), H (teacher supplied answer/semantic next move), A (neutral learner-directed motor/communication/sensory accessibility), X (unknown). Respond to partial attempts with source-specific feedback and a completely unrelated adult-owned misconception example, then let learner practice without blame. Fade H cues before new EXIT, retain essential disability and physical safety A access.

EXIT — genuinely different independent source action

New genuine readable numeral form under unseen original typeface/context: PK3 source-appropriate 0–5 recognition, PK4 full 0–10 recognition without font/location/color shortcut or mandatory writing.

The test is NOT the same adult modeled material, prerecorded answer, outline tracing or identical stimulus shuffled. Record child-owned action independently against each exact printed source ID; an observed age-specific action is a source candidate, not verified developmental mastery.
