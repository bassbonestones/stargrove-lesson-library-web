Numeral Recognition — explicit PK3 foundation and PK4 extension sequence

Mandatory teaching companion for MATH-PK-NUM-01/02/03 · E2.2 original universe-neutral draft · 2026-10-30. This corrects an instructional-design weakness: a shared method that can assess either grade is not automatically an adequate progression for a child learning the symbols for the first time. Each method's ENTRY/MODEL/DURING/EXIT/REVISIT remains in force, but teach the sessions below before applying the full-range exit. The sessions are invitations and formative opportunities, not a timed timetable, developmental diagnosis or mandatory promotion score. All SOLO modes are written future specifications, NOT IMPLEMENTED.

Exact published source: PK3.V.A.5 “Child recognizes numerals 0-5” (cell SHA-256 6c517169f3955c076d6845dfe0de5cb23a8d14e12d8974a4638945a90a0bcbeb) and PK4.V.A.5 “Child recognizes numerals 0-10” (SHA-256 698470dd2c2c165e382009b27a35d4ed882e67662e43066cb56ee2e9ba14e675). Research reference: 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Mathematics, page 51. The PK4 range includes 0–5, not just the new 6–10 glyphs. PK3 children with higher readiness can explore additional numerals voluntarily; do not imply they have failed PK3 for not knowing 6–10.

Developmental sequence and original teacher language

Each short session can be repeated across days in several contexts; stop before fatigue, change the approach after actual confusion, and vary taught examples. Use 2–4 previously unknown practice glyphs per turn so attention is on interpreting them; a “session” here is a focused mini-lesson, not necessarily a full 20-minute block. Entry happens before teaching, MODEL then coached DURING. Record a short uncued mini-check using fresh items not modeled immediately to choose next instruction; the later formal EXIT must still examine all grade-specific identities on different items, and the scheduled REVISIT is another actual day.

Pathway Session and explicit teaching move Genuine child response, formative check, and next instructional decision
PK3 / Stage P3-1 0, 1, 2: Show a printed 0 beside a truly empty card: “The loop on this card is the numeral zero. An empty space isn't a zero.” Name a clear 1 and 2 in both upright readable sizes. In a new shuffled 3-card array invite child to find spoken 0, 1 and 2; separately show an unfamiliar form of each and invite naming/sign/AAC. Record initial independent vs helped responses. If 0 is mistaken for empty paper, return to explicit real-glyph contrast next session.
PK3 / Stage P3-2 3, 4, 5: Keep one or two earlier numerals in each round, explicitly show visual distinctions 2/3/5 and 4/5 where confusable. “This shape has its own number name. We don't need toys here to count.” Child independently identifies new, upright 3, 4, 5 among mixed known and unfamiliar shapes; change order/position. If child depends on teacher's pointing/color, remove the cue and teach again with clean cards.
PK3 / Stage P3-3 Mixed 0–5 transfer: Show all six in changing size/font and mixed locations, with functional signs only as a second context. “Tell me which numeral you see; there is no row to memorize.” Full six-symbol novel uncued EXIT, with per-glyph raw first response and modality, plus a real later-date REVISIT in a different font/context. If only four have been assessed, record remaining two as NOT_OBSERVED and schedule another small session; never claim full range.
PK4 / Stage P4-1 Re-establish 0–5 first: Begin with changed-font 0–5 mixed probe. Re-teach any missing identities using P3-1/P3-2 (not as a punishment or a claim that age predicts ability). Check each earlier symbol with different array/position; previously known names should transfer to new examples. A PK4 child strong at 6–10 but confusing 0/3 still needs independent evidence for the complete 0–10 range.
PK4 / Stage P4-2 New 6, 7, 8: Pair a previously known 3 or 5 with 6/7/8. Model upright 6 then 7 then 8, adding one new contrast at a time. “This one is six. This printed seven is shaped differently.” Fresh mixed fields with 0–8 (not a new fixed ascending row); distinguish 6 from a genuinely printed 9 only after 9 has been taught or adult introduces it as an explained foil. If 6 is misread as 9, model upright shape contrast, not rotate card.
PK4 / Stage P4-3 New 9, whole 10: Teach upright 9 versus 6; show 1, 0, 10 on separate cards. “This whole card is ten; two printed characters together name one number.” No need for place-value lessons. Show unknown standard fonts with 9 and complete 10 in a scrambled selection including single 1 and 0; do not infer 10 identity from reading the digits separately. Helped response is practice, not fresh EXIT.
PK4 / Stage P4-4 Mixed 0–10 transfer: Randomize all eleven new upright readable numerals including 0/6/9/10 with no surrounding countable objects; use changed sign contexts on later turns. Full eleven-symbol novel uncued EXIT, grade-PK4 independent 0–10, not “only five new ones.” Later actual-date all-eleven REVISIT in another fair font; retain NOT_OBSERVED for gaps/opt-outs and note any assistance.

Formative instructional decision (not an invented Texas cutoff): Advance the teaching variety when the child shows independent recognition of the current small set in more than one fair layout and does not rely on answer clues. If confusion persists, repeat a different meaningful contrast and allow a later short attempt. A strong first response is encouraging but not proof of retention; a wrong response or need for AAC is a teaching signal, not a diagnosis. Children need not clear a numerical “80% gate” to be offered rich experiences; source-range evidence remains separate from teaching decisions.

Make this sequence pedagogically different for each authored method

Method 01 — Numeral Signal Studio (TEACHER_GUIDE.md)

  • PK3 P3-1: Adult models real 0 versus blank, 1 and 2 with original unsigned loose cards. Child responds “Find zero” using a freshly shuffled 0/1/2 field. For expressive checking, show new single 0, 1 or 2 and ask its name without reading it aloud first. P3-2: Introduce 3/4/5 individually; child compares actual confusing strokes in 2/3/5 and independently chooses among shuffled neutral shapes. P3-3: Use separate hidden never-modeled 0–5 exit cards in changed readable typeface.
  • PK4 P4-1: Do not jump directly into only 6–10: replay mixed 0–5 as a genuine recognition baseline. P4-2: Add 6/7/8 progressively into existing 0–5 field, changing position on every turn. P4-3: Model 6-versus-9 upright and whole 10 versus 1/0 on separate practice specimens. P4-4: Eleven fresh independent single-glyph prompts, then actual later font transfer.
  • Counterexample: A child correctly guesses 2 from a fixed left-hand slot across five turns but fails changed position: revisit P3-1 changed-position practice, not P3-3 completed credit.

Method 02 — Number Sign Print Shop (LESSON_02_PRINT_SHOP.md)

  • PK3 P3-1: Commission a 0 sign using an actual printed glyph and a different-font 0 (the empty shop window is not zero); then choose two correctly printed variants of 1 and 2, distinguishing from a foil. P3-2: Child commissions 3/4/5; adult supplies different-font practiced samples and asks which still says the requested numeral. Simple matching of two unnamed shapes is NOT enough: child must read/name/select the numeric identity. P3-3: New-font sealed exit from Print Shop's own third page, full 0–5.
  • PK4 P4-1: Run the same-varied-font sign checks on all 0–5; the shop isn't only about new glyphs. P4-2: New 6/7/8 signs in two legible typefaces with changed sizes; don't call mirrored/rotated 6 a new font. P4-3: Two fonts for upright 9 and whole 10; reject a child's attempt to “print ten” by placing separate 1 card and separate 0 card. P4-4: Use unmodeled monospace whole exit cards for 0–10; child reads them without a matching labeled model.
  • Counterexample: Child produces a perfect imitation of 5 from a model sign but cannot read the unseen 5: valid copy participation, return to independent P3-2 identity reading.

Method 03 — Sign-Finder Walk (LESSON_03_SIGN_HUNT.md)

  • PK3 P3-1: Choose three safe, fictional and readable number-bearing location signs containing 0, 1, 2; adult narrates surrounding context (“room sign”), not the numeral. Child both locates the actual printed glyph and identifies it. P3-2: Introduce 3/4/5 on unfamiliar signs interleaved with earlier known ones; shuffle where the sign appears. P3-3: Six independently identified numeral-bearing unfamiliar signs, followed by bare glyph transfer; locating a shelf sign without naming its numeral is incomplete.
  • PK4 P4-1: Re-check all prior 0–5 in functional prints with varied surroundings. P4-2: Distinct sign contexts for 6/7/8 mixed with known 0–5, no six pictured objects or other count cues. P4-3: Unfamiliar upright 9 sign versus 6, and an entire 10 room sign rather than separate '1'/'0' items. P4-4: Novel sign contexts and bare glyph transfer across all 0–10; record separately which dimension was observed.
  • Counterexample: Child knows “the green shelf always means four” but cannot identify changed-color glyph 4: address location/color memory before source-range exit.

Four-mode delivery of the sequence, not merely the task

  • ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD: A caregiver prepares only today's relevant small-set practice glyphs and hides full-range exit materials, carries out the method-specific dialogue above, observes raw independent response and chooses a subsequent mini-lesson according to that evidence. Do not demand eleven unfamiliar numerals in the first PK4 session.
  • CO-PLAY: Child chooses a sign/font/room to inspect and commissions a numeral; adult names only adult model exemplars. Child's separately fresh read must precede partner clues. Repeat P3 or P4 stage when relevant, without relegating the child to passive watching.
  • TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Use weekly stations: P3-1 or P4-1 focus group, P3-2/P4-2 emerging group, independent transfer group. All may have meaningful tasks on the same day at different stages. Teacher privately collects individual symbol responses rather than chorally inferring mastery; schedule novel full-range exit in small appointments, leaving missing cells NOT_OBSERVED.
  • SOLO future spec: Narrated stage selector introduces 0–2 before 3–5, and for PK4 reviews 0–5 before 6–8, 9/10. Child can choose another stage or repeat; audio questions are independent of print reading, with no automatic answer label on scored item. Store first responses, numerical hints and each symbol's stage separately only if future privacy authorization exists. No existing functioning software, actual child data or tested parity.

Explicit phase and evidence integration

For each stage, ENTRY asks unfamiliar small-set identities before today's model; MODEL labels distinct examples, DURING permits aided correction, EXIT mini-check asks new uncued glyphs for that stage and records exactly which were observed, REVISIT is a genuinely later actual date. The full PK3/P4 EXIT and later full-range REVISIT from each original lesson are additional to those formative mini-checks, not replaced by them. Distinguish receptive name→symbol choice from expressive printed-symbol→name; both can evidence recognition within their mode but are not the same observation. Access support for IEP/IFSP/Section 504 is separate from teaching the answer.

Acceptance boundary: A complete authored sequence is still not child trial, independent qualified educator acceptance, all four-mode functional parity, or validated SOLO app. It makes the instructional order usable; results of future teaching remain NOT_OBSERVED.

Learning-differences stage-by-stage teaching adjustments — November 1, 2026

Instructional inclusion audit, not diagnostic evaluation. For each stage, plan IEP/IFSP/504 and family/team consultation where relevant; allow AAC, gaze, home-language signed naming, low-clutter genuine printed symbols, child-selected look time and breaks, while retaining source distinction between access and adult label cues. An inaccessible stimulus is NOT_OBSERVED, not incorrect.

PK3 / Stage P3-1

Practical support and fade: A child may confuse zero with an empty card or rely on 0–2 being presented in fixed ascending order. Introduce 0/1/2 as upright equal-contrast printed symbols, with explicit ZERO-versus-BLANK distinction, a two-choice request then a three-choice task, and an optional child-controlled AAC or gaze response. Fade pointing cues after modeled DIFFERENT cards.

New accessible uncued check: Use a never-seen font of 0/1/2 in a shuffled fair field without objects or adult shape-hinting. Record each first independent label or selection; zero-from-empty by a guessed blank is not valid.

PK3 / Stage P3-2

Practical support and fade: The 2/3/5 or 4/5 similarities can be overwhelmed by clutter; introduce one new glyph against already understood upright known examples, allow time to compare true printed curves, and replace rapid-fire demands with short spaced practice. Do not use distinct colours that make identity trivial.

New accessible uncued check: Present three, four and five on new neutral cards mixed with familiar symbols, randomizing location. Count a correct first recognition, not echoing a teacher label or reading an adult pointing cue.

PK3 / Stage P3-3

Practical support and fade: Change readable typeface and size deliberately after rehearsal, with a visual preview of how the activity works and an accessible one-card option for visual crowding. Spread six independent identity checks across sessions if attention or endurance requires it; do not convert an abbreviated assessment to a full-range pass.

New accessible uncued check: Check all SIX 0–5 glyphs on newly printed cards without numerical context, prompt labels or fixed ordered row; separately mark I/H/N and truly later-date new context.

PK4 / Stage P4-1

Practical support and fade: A PK4 learner may have uneven 0–5 symbol experience, not a learning-disability diagnosis. Begin with a nonthreatening accessible new-font baseline and reteach only actual missed identities using the earlier three-stage PK3 ladder, not child-to-child comparison.

New accessible uncued check: Use separate previously unseen upright 0–5 identities in a shuffled field. A numeral recognized only after counting matching dots is source practice, not independent printed recognition.

PK4 / Stage P4-2

Practical support and fade: Six, seven and eight present new visual contrasts, especially six versus nine. Offer predictable one-at-a-time explicit modeling; make the 6/9 rotation/shape distinction only once the child has genuinely met nine, never trick with upside-down cards or a surprise timer.

New accessible uncued check: Use never-seen upright 6/7/8 mixed with familiar 0–5 and fairly introduced foil 9 only if previously taught. Observe child response before any adult correction.

PK4 / Stage P4-3

Practical support and fade: Ten is ONE printed numeral with two characters, and six/nine confusions may increase under clutter. Teach whole 10 on an original card alongside separately printed 1 and 0, then fade spatial highlighting of the pair; provide extra processing time without saying the word ten during an exit.

New accessible uncued check: Present novel WHOLE 10 and upright 9, 6, 1, 0 in shuffled fair locations. Do not count “one-zero” digit segmentation or copied adult speech as whole-ten identification.

PK4 / Stage P4-4

Practical support and fade: An eleven-glyph check can exhaust attention or overload a child with sensory needs. Use learner-chosen short sessions, stable access modality and fresh readable fonts, but retain individual raw evidence for ALL 0–10 glyphs and a genuine later date for retention.

New accessible uncued check: Use new upright legible print for every 0–10 identity without number-line order or answer-audio prompts; missing identities remain N, not a diagnosis or arbitrary adjusted PK4 range.

All four modes inherit these same stage/construct safeguards; group choral correct responses and caregiver-originated labels do not replace individually attributable child recognition. This is internal written remediation only; no specialist signoff, real learner observation or running SOLO.
