Original authored method's concrete learner actions and feedback model

Entry reveals whether a child can already recognize a few written symbols. It does not require previous counting-to-ten fluency or pencil control. Starting with familiar identity marks (a birthday numeral, building number, play-room tag) can provide motivation, but age, prior vocabulary, familiarity with Arabic numerals and visual/motor access are not diagnostic. A numeral naming task can also load language retrieval; preserve receptive and expressive evidence separately. Do not confuse blank space with 0: zero is a real printed oval glyph. For 6 vs 9, preserve upright card orientation and vary font only within legible conventional forms; rotations can genuinely turn one into the other and are not a fair recognition assessment. For 1 vs 10, present the complete card without a cropping boundary.

B. Precise sequence (child evidence independent of teacher demonstrations)

Phase Materials and exact language Meaningful child action and observation
ENTRY E-NUM, 2–3 min Without practicing first, present new neutral cards in scrambled locations. PK3 probe 0, 2, 5; PK4 probe 0, 6, 9, 10 plus one chosen familiar digit. “What number is this?” For children who do not name digits orally, say a numeral name and offer 3–4 unlabelled-symbol choices. Record first identity response, symbol shown, distractors, font, answer modality, access supports, any number-name help. Unasked symbols = NOT_OBSERVED. This screening is not range completion.
MODEL M-NUM, 4 min On different practice cards (not entry/exit exemplars) point to printed 0 and an empty neutral card: “This printed loop is zero. The blank card is not the numeral zero.” Then show 2 beside 3 (“They look different; this is two, this is three”), 4 and 5; for PK4 explicitly compare 6/9 upright and whole 10 versus 1. Model a different typeface of 1 and explain “both are one.” Child watches and chooses which near-shape is interesting; no claim of recognition from the adult's labeling. For PK4 explicitly teach 7/8 as well; neither 10 nor 0 is optional.

MODEL — adult demonstrates on different real owned source

Teacher on different adult-only real numerals 3 and 8 shows their own recognition process from visible glyph shapes, never pointing to child's exit target.

Never model the exact learner EXIT example or pre-answer with a pointing finger/answer cue. The adult's true demonstration is a teaching event and carries ZERO child independent evidence.

DURING — actual child activity, contingent instructional repair and faded hints

Teach discriminating glyph identity from color, page location and accompanying dots; invite original name/selection in randomized changed positions and explain true zero shape, fade adult visual cue. Child-chosen signal-purpose option: within existing supported DURING time, a toy follows an authentic printed numeral sign to a numbered pretend room/shelf of the child's choosing; adult either states the number for receptive symbol selection or shows an unknown glyph for independent expressive naming/AAC. The child sees a meaningful consequence of their choice, while the adult waits until first response to help. Child-origin designed signs are reciprocal practice only, and a quiet plain-card alternative is valid; neither is substituted for unmodelled source EXIT.
