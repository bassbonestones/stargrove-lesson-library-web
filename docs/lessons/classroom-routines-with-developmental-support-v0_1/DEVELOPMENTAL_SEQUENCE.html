Responsive teaching choices for safe routines

These are optional planning choices, not a published age-stage ladder, prerequisite checklist, separate counted lessons or compulsory tests. Move among them according to the child's actual opportunity, interest, access and response. Do not require every child to complete all stages. The Lesson 01 art plan supplies the complete materials and moment-to-moment route.

For PK3, meaningful participation in a simple real routine can include substantial adult assistance. It is not limited by the printed wording to a classroom; record a home setting honestly and do not claim school transfer. For PK4, actual classroom context and occasional rather than continuous next-step direction matter. There is no numeric reminder quota or demand for zero help. A familiar directly taught routine can supply relevant evidence in either case. Novelty is not a prerequisite for the published action.

Meaningful contribution includes a child directing a helper's movements through their established communication. Adult physical execution is not itself disqualifying. Adult choice and execution with a passive watching child is different. Record who chose and who moved, and do not infer broad mastery from one action, one clean table, one routine, a repeated rule, or a finished picture.

Teaching sequence without an artificial testing gate

ENTRY: Explain the real places and purpose, offer accessible materials and an authentic invitation, then notice what the child does or directs. Help, demonstrations and familiar routines need not be withheld to protect an uncued baseline. Mark any prior teaching accurately.

MODEL: Demonstrate with your own book or art materials when useful. A different example may preserve a later independent opportunity; a direct example may be exactly the teaching needed. Neither the adult demonstration nor a passive child watching is evidence of child routine-following. An immediately copied action is not independent transfer, but assisted action must not automatically be discarded as irrelevant to PK3.

DURING: Follow the child's making and respond to uncertainty, comfort, access and interest. Explain the useful step, demonstrate or offer help. Reduce unnecessary next-step cues when the child can direct more, but retain communication, motor, sensory and other access supports. Never withhold needed help to create a score.

EXIT: At a natural ending, observe a real child contribution with its actual help level. A familiar routine is legitimate. A separate changed-context opportunity is optional and should occur only when useful; explain any changed locations rather than hiding rules. Record a copied or prompted action honestly instead of calling it uncued transfer.

REVISIT: On another actual calendar day, notice a real routine and record new evidence and assistance. Leave later-date retention/transfer NOT_OBSERVED until observed. Stored work or a planned revisit is not evidence. These stage labels organize adult notes; they are not five compulsory child tests.

PK3 responsive choices

PK3 R3-1 — One meaningful step with assistance

Invite the child to choose a paper, use a tool or direct a helper. Explain and model as needed. Note the child's contribution and exact adult help, not just the completed table. Simplify access or one action if needed; do not demand unaided performance.

PK3 R3-2 — A simple beginning and ending

Help the child connect a real beginning to a gentle stopping point: choose a book, then give it a findable resting place. Use a familiar routine and appropriate support. A missed opportunity means not observed, not a failed transition. Keep necessary aids.

PK3 R3-3 — A useful shared boundary

With a willing participant, practice asking before moving another person's project or leaving clear space. The teacher may explain directly; no second adult demonstration is required. Respect a child's refusal to join. A solo alternative can practice material care but does not prove interaction with a real partner.

PK3 R3-4 — Optional application in another real occasion

If useful, introduce another safe material or explain a different storage place. The child may still need adult assistance. Distinguish learning under direct teaching from any claimed uncued transfer. No forced new activity or later-date mastery claim.

PK4 responsive choices

PK4 R4-1 — A familiar classroom routine

Explain accessible classroom locations and invite a meaningful start or ending. Ordinary reminders are allowed. If every step still needs direction, keep teaching and describe that support rather than claiming the occasional-reminder pattern.

PK4 R4-2 — A familiar transition

At a real art/book transition, preview the ending and allow time to save unfinished work. Observe how the child contributes with the reminders actually used. Do not fabricate an exact reminder count or require uniform speed.

PK4 R4-3 — A relevant shared-space rule

In a willing real classroom interaction, notice a useful agreed boundary. One teacher can explain or demonstrate directly with their own materials while supervising the group; two adults are not required. Adapt motor execution while preserving the child's decision. Passive watching is not personal rule-following; directing a helper can be.

PK4 R4-4 — Respond to a missed cue kindly

When a noncritical cue is naturally missed, offer a timely reminder and help. Never manufacture distress to test recovery. Record whether the child can direct the next useful step or still needs each-step instruction; both inform teaching without moral judgment.

PK4 R4-5 — Broader opportunities over time

Across actual classroom occasions, notice different meaningful rules and routines and document context and help. A single step does not establish broad mastery. More variation can support a wider inference only when it is actually observed, not because this stage is listed. Retention on another day remains NOT_OBSERVED until a real revisit.

Choose practice, support or challenge

If the child cannot act comfortably, change access or the invitation first. If a useful step is unclear, explain or demonstrate it. If the child can meaningfully contribute with support, continue real practice. If a familiar routine is manageable, an optional useful variation may be appropriate. Reduce unnecessary instructional prompting responsively, not according to a fixed fading timetable; never remove essential access. Keep a child's purpose, creative choices and ownership of artwork central.

Delivery, safety and provenance

CO-PLAY: One caregiver and child can use ordinary safe art supplies. A home observation has its actual context, not automatic PK4 classroom credit. ADULT-LED: One educator can follow the Lesson 01 plan without a second adult or a separate room. CLASSROOM: Use only the places and group arrangement one educator can safely supervise; choose brief focal observations, rotate fairly and leave missing individual observations unobserved. Care takes priority over note-taking. Do not replace individual evidence with group cleanup or peer policing.

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. No independently child-operable software, narrated access route or verified mode parity exists. An imagined app or puppet routine cannot substitute for observed real action. A child using ordinary AAC with an adult is not an implemented standalone lesson.

Use stable reachable surfaces, intact age-safe tools, suitable communication and sensory supports, and safe storage. Check mouthing and broken pieces. Allow processing time, breaks and declining; no forced hands, speech, gaze, rushed transitions, punitive rewards or compelled peer interaction. Ask before moving, displaying or discarding child-owned art. Save unfinished pictures separately from shared tools. Adapt with the child and their established support team; do not diagnose from this activity.

Keep minimal private notes: date and actual setting, the child's specific contribution, who moved materials, exact help, missed steps and next support.
