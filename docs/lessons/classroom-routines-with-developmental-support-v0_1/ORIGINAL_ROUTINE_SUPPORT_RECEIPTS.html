01 · Does the child actually follow the routine?

Teacher observer guide — planning or compliance alone isn't source

A child can recite a rule while the adult chooses and performs every step and the child only watches: that is not observed child-following. A child meaningfully directing a helper in the real routine is a legitimate accessible contribution; record who chose and who moved. To observe source, a learner must meaningfully contribute to a real classroom-safe activity through feasible action, intentional choice and appropriate assistance.

PK3 actual adult help is allowed by the published standard. PK4 allows occasional adult reminders; continual commands/teacher task takeover are not the same. Do not invent a fixed number of allowed reminders or demand never needing help.

A classroom rule must be safe, understandable, accessibility-respecting and kind. Declining peer interaction or needing a break is not automatically noncompliance. No forced gaze, timed transitions, restraints or punishment.

02 · A gentle, changed transition

Original adult-only instructional model for flexible routines

Teacher finishes their OWN benign art activity and starts a quiet book. They demonstrate asking “What comes next?” and calmly putting a large clean paper on a safe shelf. The child may follow a familiar taught routine with legitimate help. Do not call an immediately copied answer independently transferred learning.

Allow nonreading signs/AAC and stable motor tools, individual transition pace, privacy and a chance to request clarification. An adult reminder may be suitable, while a child physically moving or meaningfully choosing a feasible next step remains the observation.

No sudden loud alarm, surprise sensory distress, forced peer touch or inaccessible task to test “following rules.” If actual meaningful child participation absent, record NOT_OBSERVED, not a character flaw or clinical deficit.
