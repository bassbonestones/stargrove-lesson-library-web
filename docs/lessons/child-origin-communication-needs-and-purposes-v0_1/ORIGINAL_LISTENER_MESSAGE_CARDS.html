01 · An uninformed listener can learn

Safe information-gap adult preparation, NOT a child script · Original II.B.1 first-party teacher materials · NOT observed child outcomes

Actual low-risk observation, volunteer adult listener

With a real willing listener briefly unable to see, a child observes where a safe large circle moved. When listener returns, the child may independently REPORT its new place; listener checks. Or child may ASK a genuine question about a safe unfamiliar item. Adult does not whisper what to say.

PK4 can use information sharing, requests, questions, suggestions and explanations as **different meaningful functions**. PK3 merely reporting a fictional puppet's need is not their own child-origin need/want; separately observe a real freely chosen want/help/stop when natural, never stage need by deprivation.

Real response provenance

Did the listener genuinely lack information before hearing child?
Exactly what did child independently communicate?
Did listener actually respond or clarify without supplying content?

Never withhold essential care, force a request or judge child by eye contact/English speech. A deliberate child-selected stored AAC phrase can be legitimate language. Physical print, real child source, different-day REVISIT and independently operable SOLO remain unverified.

02 · Meaning can be clarified

Teacher-only ambiguous-message repair demonstration · Original II.B.1 first-party teacher materials · NOT observed child outcomes

DEMONSTRATION ONLY — adult example

A teacher sees two harmless large art pads. Teacher tells a volunteer colleague “I want that one,” but the colleague genuinely cannot identify which. Teacher clarifies their OWN intended item: “I meant my wide textured pad.” This models purposeful clarification—never intentionally misunderstand or refuse a child's genuine essential need.

Contrast original purposes without prescribing child's response

“I WANT the pad” = request. “I SAW the pad move” = report. “WHERE did it go?” = question. “WE COULD put it near the tray” = suggestion. These adult examples are not a PK4 answer checklist or 4 required official state actions.

A child can select a preprogrammed AAC word intentionally to express meaning. If an adult automatically chooses and presses “I need help” for them, source authorship belongs to adult, not child. Correct model-card matching cannot substitute for authentic new child language.
