Teach a Child to Ask and Share What Someone Doesn't Know

One adult and one child; no second teacher or staged secrets required. A child’s genuine question can be an excellent beginning.

The two age-specific learning goals

Younger preschoolers use language to communicate a real need or want of their own. During this lesson, your child might spontaneously say “I want to know what this is,” “Can you help me?”, “I want the smooth one,” “I'm tired,” or independently choose their own meaningful sign or stored communication-device message. A report such as “the circle is under the box” is useful language practice but doesn't automatically demonstrate a younger child's own basic need/want.

Older preschoolers use language for genuinely different purposes: asking a sincere question to learn a fact, sharing an original observation, requesting something or suggesting an idea. Repeating three location facts is still reporting; asking for three different colors is still requesting. Different purposes may emerge naturally at different moments or on different days.

An information gap means one person truly knows something the other doesn't. A question the child genuinely wants answered by an adult who knows is one kind of gap. A child sharing a harmless real fact with someone who actually didn't see it is another. A child-authored report remains meaningful language even if you already knew the fact; just don't call it new information transferred to an uninformed person.

Other genuine reasons to speak count, too. An older child may independently greet someone, respectfully refuse, invite a willing person to play, comfort a peer, joke, tell a story or negotiate—not only request, question and report. These are possible different purposes, not a list the child must perform. Repeating a refusal as a protest (or two requests with different objects) does not automatically show two purposes. Never force greeting, apologies, friendship, comforting or public speech; honor genuine child meaning, accessible language and consent.

One clarified request is not automatically two purposes. If a child first says “I want that” and later clarifies “I mean the wide mat,” the clarification may be valuable, but it is still mainly the same request. Don't award an older-child second purpose just because an observer labels the elaboration “clarify”; judge actual meaning in context. A genuinely different original report, question, invitation or refusal may show another function naturally. Never deliberately misunderstand a clear message to elicit extra speech.

Prepare a safe, curious space

Put two or three broad, stable, safe everyday objects within reach: perhaps a large soft felt piece, a chunky drawing board, and an adult-held safe textured book or large child-safe tool. Offer a quiet place to stop and usual home-language, sign or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) supports.

Check safety, grip/reach, sensory comfort, and anything small that could be swallowed. Never use blindfolds, make the child handle unsafe hidden objects, withdraw supervision, or conceal water, toilet access, food, care or comfort to create a “need.”

Only one adult is necessary. You already know something about your safe object that your child may not know. For example, your child may sincerely wonder what a safe unusual tool is called or what a page texture feels like. Do not tell them they have to ask, and don't invent the child's curiosity. Your answer should be accurate. A colleague or another child is not required.

Five steps you can teach now

1. Begin with what the child genuinely wonders or wants

Say: “We can explore these if you like. Is there anything you want to know, tell me, use, or ask for help with?”

Wait. An older child might genuinely ask “What is that for?” about a safe tool. If you actually know the answer, respond truthfully. That is a real child-unknown/adult-known question, with just you and the child.

The child may instead report something they notice: “This one feels bumpy!” You may already know that fact; the child has still produced a real original report, but they did not newly inform you if you knew. For a younger child, notice separately if a true child need/want is communicated in language, such as “I want that board” or “I need a break,” not just whether they report an object.

If no question or personal want arises, keep the materials genuinely available, let the child play or pause, and record the specific source opportunity not yet observed.

2. Demonstrate different adult-owned examples

Using your own different book or cloth, say: “I noticed the star picture is on this cloth.” That is an adult report about what you know. Then, on another safe object about which you really have a question, demonstrate: “I wonder what this part is used for.”

These are your messages, not the child's. You can model them alone with the child; an imagined second adult or reply is pretend teaching, not proof a real listener learned a secret. Do not show the exact answer to a later child check or decide which question the child must ask.

3. Let an actual exchange happen

If the child genuinely asks a question you know the answer to, listen and answer truthfully. They have communicated a sincere question without anyone hiding an object. If you don't know the answer, say so rather than guess: “I don't know yet. What could we look at together to learn?” The child still asked a real original question (a valid older-child language purpose), even though nobody yet had that information. You may explore together with the safe materials available; don't falsely call this a transfer from someone who already knew, and no website, app or external research is compulsory for this lesson.

For an optional stronger reporting activity, a willing child who can safely manipulate broad harmless pieces may change one large object arrangement while you briefly aren't looking but remain safely supervising. The child can later choose to tell you what changed. If you really didn't know, and the child reports truthfully in their own language, you may have a real newly shared fact. If you saw the change, don't pretend to be surprised: it can still be child-owned reporting but not previously unknown information.

A child who can't safely rearrange objects, or doesn't want to, can still ask real questions or report their own observations. Don't require fine-motor performance, blindfolds, a second adult, public speech or a secret.

4. Notice different purposes and respond to actual meaning

For a younger child, a real need/want might emerge in either activity: “I want the larger pad,” “Help,” or “I'm tired.” They can say, sign or independently choose meaningful stored AAC vocabulary. Respond immediately to actual comfort and safety needs, even if they never “ask politely.” An adult saying the sentence for them or selecting their exact AAC answer is helped practice, not independent child-authored meaning.

For an older child, record authentic different purposes when they happen: an original question about an unknown tool and later original report about texture or suggestion about a game are different functions. It is not essential that all happen in the same session.

If you initially misunderstand, say “I want to understand. Can you show me or tell me another way?” Their original child-authored meaningful expression still occurred. Record whether you ultimately understood separately; do not claim successful new information transfer until you truly learned what the child communicated.

Stories count when the child really authors the message. A sincere question about a dragon or puppet can be genuine language, even though the topic is imagined; a puppet's pre-recorded “I'm thirsty” isn't the child's own basic need. A younger child truly wanting to play with that puppet may express their own real want.

5. Try another chance later if welcome

An optional new safe book, object or caregiver listener can offer a stronger test of the child's independent question/report in a different context. But a child-authored genuine need/want or different purpose on familiar materials already matters, without extra novelty or staff.

On an actual different day, return to a familiar safe object or another optional experience. Record only real child messages and real listener reactions witnessed then. No number of questions or new object quota. Never score a scheduled future visit as completed.

Teach Genuine Questions and Information Sharing with a Class

Whole-class inquiry with a truthful listening partner, not a guessing trick. Children may choose their own questions, reports, private needs and communication channels.

What children are learning

Younger preschoolers use meaningful language for their own genuine basic needs and wants. A child may ask for an available broad art material, express “I need help,” sign “I'm tired,” or deliberately choose an appropriate stored communication-device phrase. Telling everyone where a circle is hidden is a fact report, not automatically the child's own basic need or want.

Older preschoolers use different meaningful functions of language: a sincere question, an original fact report, request, suggestion or explanation. Many location reports are still reports; many material requests are still requests. Different purposes can arise over real separate activities and days, without an arbitrary simultaneous performance quota.

An information gap means someone's genuine knowledge differs from someone else's. A child who sincerely asks a teacher for a fact the teacher actually knows uses a real child-unknown/adult-known gap with only one adult. A child who truthfully tells a harmless fact to a willing classmate who really had not seen it may also share genuinely new information. A teacher must not pretend ignorance when they already know. A child-authored report to a knowledgeable listener remains language, but cannot be scored as new fact transfer.

Other genuine reasons to speak count, too. An older child may independently greet someone, respectfully refuse, invite a willing person to play, comfort a peer, joke, tell a story or negotiate—not only request, question and report. These are possible different purposes, not a list the child must perform. Repeating a refusal as a protest (or two requests with different objects) does not automatically show two purposes. Never force greeting, apologies, friendship, comforting or public speech; honor genuine child meaning, accessible language and consent.

One clarified request is not automatically two purposes. If a child first says “I want that” and later clarifies “I mean the wide mat,” the clarification may be valuable, but it is still mainly the same request. Don't award an older-child second purpose just because an observer labels the elaboration “clarify”; judge actual meaning in context. A genuinely different original report, question, invitation or refusal may show another function naturally. Never deliberately misunderstand a clear message to elicit extra speech.

Prepare the classroom

Arrange broad safe visible or tactile real objects (for example, giant felt shapes, a big box with no hazardous concealment, large stable drawing board and safe unfamiliar child-rated tool). Ensure none are small or sharp, and check each child's reach, safety, sensory and communication needs. Provide quiet independent options and the normal home-language, sign and augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) devices children already use.

One teacher can facilitate: have an accurate fact to answer if a child genuinely wants to ask about an actual safe book or object. Do not invent uncertainty or force a child to ask. An optional pair/group report activity requires only genuinely willing participants and ordinary classroom coverage. Nobody must be blindfolded, left without supervision, paired with a stranger or made to share a private concern.

All water, meals, toilet access, comfort, medicine, safety support and ordinary class materials remain available regardless of whether a child says anything. Do not stage scarcity, an upset peer, inaccessible object or distress to elicit a target phrase.

Invite real curiosity before showing the teacher's answer

Say: “We can look at these safe objects today. Is there anything you truly want to know, tell somebody, use, or ask for help with? It's also okay just to explore.”

Let pupils speak, sign, use familiar AAC, gesture with meaningful language or approach privately. Pause without immediately telling them what to ask.

To younger children: Attend to an actual child-owned need/want expressed with meaningful language, not simply a teacher-directed group phrase or a fact about where a shape is. If no personal need arises, the child can still learn reporting/questions without having that particular source outcome observed.

To older children: Accept a genuinely original question about an object the teacher actually knows, or an authentic observation, even if it isn't in the teacher's expected order. A pupil who asks about a fictional story can genuinely ask a question of a real listener; a puppet's scripted “I'm thirsty” isn't the child's own real need.

Demonstrate two adult-owned purposes

On completely separate teacher-owned materials, explain your own actual knowledge: “I noticed the star picture on this cloth.” Then demonstrate how you might ask about an unrelated safe object you honestly don't understand: “I wonder what this tool is for.”

If there's only one adult, those are explicitly teacher examples; do not make up a real second adult's response. If two adults genuinely consent to a harmless demonstration, they can optionally model a real unshared fact: one witnesses a safe change and the other actually hasn't. Neither the second adult nor a previously uninformed peer is a prerequisite for a classroom language lesson.

Never give the child their exact independent answer during the demonstration or whisper what to say to their listener.

Give children meaningful, safe practice

At supervised stations, offer calm object exploration and optional inquiry. Some children may truly want to know a safe tool's name or purpose; respond truthfully. When you do not know the answer, say “I don't know yet; how might we find out?” and allow safe shared exploration rather than making up a fact. A child’s authentic question remains a real language purpose even when teacher and child both lack the answer; just don't score it as successful transfer of a fact that someone already knew. Others may report a texture, suggest how to use a broad mat or request a different already available item. These all can be authentic when the child originates meaning. A website, computer or research exercise is not required for a true original language question.

For a stronger optional report-to-uninformed-listener experience, small voluntary groups can observe a harmless change while another willing listener is genuinely occupied elsewhere under appropriate supervision. Later, if the listener actually did not know and the child gives a truthful self-authored report they can understand, that may be genuine new fact sharing. If the listener already saw or heard, don't fake a gap or stage ignorance. If no genuinely uninformed willing person can safely participate, use teacher-answered sincere questions or real original reports instead. A child who can't manipulate pieces can still ask real questions and express needs.

An adult may stabilize AAC or offer language access without selecting actual meaning. If teacher feeds exact “the circle is under the box,” it is helped/suggested practice, not independently created content. A child-selected stored “help” can be authentic, while teacher-pressed help is adult-origin.

When a child remembers the harmless fact incorrectly: They may still be using real original reporting language, even though their report isn't factually accurate. Do not count that as successful transfer of the correct newly unknown information. Say: “You shared what you remember. Let's check it together.” Look at the safe object without shaming the child; model a factual correction if useful, but do not score a child echo of the teacher's exact correction as independently generated. Record original purpose, fact accuracy and real listener understanding separately; the child is not judged unable to communicate because a detail was mistaken.

Observe individual learning fairly

The class may model a report or listen to a story together, but a group chorus doesn't prove which child genuinely wanted something, asked an original question or used a different function. Choose a manageable number of focal individual observations while supervising all learners. If intensive access support would leave others unsafe, defer individual scoring; do not invent additional staff or events.

For each younger learner, distinguish a genuine own need/want in meaningful language from a simple fact report; a safe need could be expressed as “I'm cold,” not just “please give me…” For older children, distinguish real child-authored requests, questions, observations and proposals over separate authentic opportunities. No exact English phrase, count of utterances, unfamiliar partner or new prop is an official source cut score.

Record who actually knew the fact and whether a recipient was really newly informed; do not assert successful transfer if teacher or classmate already knew. A listener who initially misunderstands hasn't erased the child's real original expressive act: note comprehension separately, and help clarification. Neither group worksheet nor next-day schedule establishes observed retention.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

When a child says “I need help,” “I'm tired,” or “I want to stop,” honor their actual need immediately. Never require “please,” a public demonstration, eye contact or classroom applause. If a pupil declines telling a fact to a peer, respect refusal and offer another meaningful role or private teacher interaction. Public classroom resources remain equitable; private family/health/communication information stays private.

A new safe object or genuinely uninformed partner can offer stronger optional transfer evidence but is not a prerequisite for a legitimate familiar-context language message. On a genuinely different actual date, revisit a familiar safe book or a new optional activity, recording only child-authored purposes and listener response actually witnessed then. No forced new fact, secret, number of reports or scheduled retention credit.

Note whether listener genuinely responded, access-only A and conceptual H, privacy/actual IEP/IFSP/504 team where relevant. Later-date REVISIT not yet observed. TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Adult makes optional art/science/build centers with real child-selected objects, offers responsive consented listeners without forcing peers, and meets every health/bathroom/comfort need regardless of language. Teacher may illustrate teacher-owned requests/reports/questions but never score group chorus or puppet lines as individual meaning. At student-specific new EXIT, observe child independently communicate true own need/want to real listener for PK3; for PK4 genuine different purposes in at least contrasting opportunities with meaningful listener response and child-authored content, including AAC/sign/home language. Record H/A/X, do not publish private needs, no public ranking. Real later calendar-day REVISIT required for retention.

Provenance, safety, and remaining limitations

Each stage has ENTRY before other-context MODEL, helped DURING, new genuinely unmodeled EXIT, and new genuine later-calendar-day REVISIT (currently NOT_OBSERVED). U first independent child-origin meaning, H adult conceptual/message content, A child-directed access-only assistance, X uncertain origin. An adult-generated phrase, teacher's puppet want, adult clicking an AAC icon or child merely pointing to already chosen correct answer is not independent child authorship. A child deliberately selecting a relevant stored AAC word/phrase for their own meaning may be authentic child language—do NOT forbid legitimate expressive AAC just because vocabulary is preprogrammed. Human listener optional safe question; right to decline, stop and receive essential needs immediately. No staged hunger/thirst/toileting or distress; no forced peer contact, eye contact, standard-English-only grammar, diagnosis, private health/family disclosures, or pressured turn-taking. Independent qualified educator approval, actual child learning, later true retention, physical printed/tactile usability, functioning SOLO and four-mode equivalence remain unverified.

Method-specific adversarial false credit and concrete repair

False-positive: Adult listener watches hidden object's placement, then child says “there” after seeing pointing and assessor claims child communicated novel basic information, or teacher gives child's entire answer. Why: no real information gap and no child-origin meaning. Repair: separate true safe unobserved listener scenario, adult MODEL on another set and leaves real exit unknown, child chooses independently accurate informative language; PK4 also new contrasting request/question/suggestion that is actually their own. PK3 additional false credit: child reports where a fictional puppet's cup is and scorer calls it child's basic need; must observe independently child-authored own genuine need/want with actual responsive listener, no staged thirst. Limit: real listeners and genuine unknown events require truthful live observation; paper cards cannot demonstrate these.

Neither memorized echo nor listener guessing from prior observation establishes independent transfer.

COMM54-01–03 source-level applicability and adult availability guard

One adult is enough to teach. A sincere child-authored question to a familiar real caregiver who really knows a safe answer produces an actual information difference, with no colleague, blindfold, manufactured peer ignorance or unsafe unattended child task. A child-origin real factual report can be meaningful language even if the caregiver already knew it—but it cannot be credited as new information to an actually uninformed listener. Only claim such transfer if the other actual willing person genuinely had not witnessed or known it and actually heard/interpreted the child. Adult explanatory modeling is on different adult-owned materials; any imaginary second respondent is fictional and not source evidence.

Published source versus stronger optional task: PK3 genuinely communicates their own real basic need/want even as a statement (“I'm tired”), home-language sign or child-selected stored AAC; child need not phrase an imperative request or use a novel prop. PK4 uses meaningful language for multiple genuinely different purposes (question, report, request, suggest, explain), possibly at separate actually occurring safe times; multiple requests or repeated reports remain one purpose. Original child questions/reports about imagined stories can be real language, while a puppet's pre-scripted wants aren't the child's own need. A real listener's initial misunderstanding doesn't erase child-authored expressive meaning but does leave successful mutual comprehension not yet verified. Genuine optional changed contexts may challenge transfer, not qualify source by novelty alone; actual later calendar day is unobserved until it occurs. Children may decline; medical/comfort needs always supplied immediately with no staged crisis.

COMM-06 — Language-purpose examples are not an exclusive list: The printed PK4 outcome says language is used for multiple purposes, so a child may authentically greet/welcome, refuse or set a boundary, invite, comfort/reassure, joke, narrate a story, express gratitude or negotiate, as well as genuinely request, question, report, suggest or explain. Distinguish actually different pragmatic functions rather than awarding two just because they have different names: repeated requests are one kind, and “refuse”/“protest” may represent the same kind for a particular observation. Never force a social greeting, apology, comforting act or refusal as a staged performance; protect child consent, context and sign/home language/child-selected stored AAC. An authentic meaningful yet unforeseen purpose should be described for fair human judgment, not rejected simply because an example list did not name it. The synthetic evidence helper conservatively normalizes semantic aliases and flags emerging purpose classes for human adjudication instead of treating arbitrary unrecognized labels as child deficiency.

COMM55-05 cross-family score interpretation — clarification is not automatically a new purpose: For PK4, if child originally requests an item, then rephrases that same request after listener asks “which one?”, this may be valuable authentic clarification and improved mutual understanding, but is not automatically a second different language purpose. Labeling the second utterance `clarify` cannot manufacture purpose variety in the source helper: if that category is needed for PK4 source, it now returns pending contextual human adjudication unless a responsible observer can substantively establish a genuinely separate communicative purpose beyond additional request details. Another truly independent report, question, greeting, refusal, invitation, story or other genuine function can provide contrast. Do not coerce a repair or deny care, and do not treat all original clarifications as invalid language; the question is whether they show a distinct function.

Companion facilitator 02 — Truthful information-gap report and original inquiry

Paired complete lesson: Lesson 02; separately uncounted objective educator guide.

Specific instructional mechanism

A child may genuinely ask a willing familiar adult a question the child doesn't know and the adult actually knows, OR tell a real willing listener a truthful harmless fact not previously known to that listener when such a safe event occurs. A real two-direction knowledge gap needs one adult and child, not an extra staff member. Actual source-valid child language is possible even if the adult already knew the report; in that case don't claim new unshared fact transfer. For PK3 separate actual own need/want expression from neutral fact report, and for PK4 identify different authentic purpose types rather than rote phrase repetition. Fictional topics are legitimate for original child questions, but character scripts aren't child intent.

Adult preflight, real materials, genuine child agency

Set up broad safe accessible objects/books and a quiet optional exit. With a single caregiver, the child may sincerely ask a real question the adult actually knows the answer to, offering a genuine child-unknown/adult-known information gap. A truly unwitnessed harmless shape change is another optional report context only when child can safely make it and one caregiver maintains adequate supervision while briefly looking away; never blindfold, physically abandon or invent adult ignorance. Extra willing adults/class peers are optional, never required. A child's true original report when listener already knew the fact may be valid PK4 language, but don't falsely label it newly transferred information. Use distinct adult-owned model, no staged personal secrets, forced questioning, small hazards or hidden essential care.

ENTRY, MODEL, DURING, truly fresh independent EXIT and later REVISIT

ENTRY: first genuine child-authored own want/question/report in a safe activity to real willing adult, before adult teaching example; a true information gap exists if child sincerely asks what adult actually knows, or child truthfully tells an unwitnessed fact to a genuinely uninformed willing listener. MODEL: with one adult, narrate a different adult-owned report/question as explicit teacher example, any pretend reply labeled hypothetical; an optional second willing adult can genuinely model an unrelated fact exchange, never necessary. DURING: support original child wording/sign/AAC and distinguish real information novelty, recipient initially misunderstanding and actual successful exchange rather than infer from a prop. SOURCE: PK3 a real own basic want/need message may be a genuine statement (“I'm cold”) as well as request, not merely neutral report. PK4 different language purposes can emerge at separate real safe times, including an original question about fictional story. EXIT: optional changed safe object/real informed or uninformed context can provide a stronger mechanism/transfer check, but neither new prop nor a second human is a publisher prerequisite and an adult-supplied answer never qualifies as independent. REVISIT only on actual later calendar date, familiar safe scenario acceptable, NOT_OBSERVED before performed.

False-credit and misconception-specific correction

If the teacher already whispered the change to child and says “tell the listener,” it is teacher-origin H. If listener already saw change, no meaningful knowledge gap; use new genuine consented fact. PK3 only reporting observation without communicating an original actual want/need is preparatory, not PK3 source completion: include independent actual child-chosen need/want occasion rather than laundering the report as a need. Child-origin safe curiosity must not force question if child has none.

Mistaken-but-original report contingency: A child can independently communicate a genuine PK4 reporting purpose while making an ordinary factual memory error about a harmless object placement. Respond gently “Let's check together” and preserve the child's authentic language act; do not claim the factually incorrect message accurately informed a genuinely uninformed listener. A corrected message is independent only if child actually originates it, not by echoing adult's exact supplied correction. Source purpose, factual correctness and mutual understanding are different observations; don't manufacture a mistake to test the child.

Grade-specific source boundaries and honest original-language evidence

PK3.II.B.1: child genuinely independently uses expressive LANGUAGE to communicate a real own BASIC NEED or WANT, including chosen stored AAC, meaningful sign or home language to responsive real listener. Silent grasp, puppet answer, fabricated deprivation and adult-selected phrase cannot independently prove this source. Never withhold actual essential care or communication access. PK4.II.B.1: actual original language for MULTIPLE DISTINCT PURPOSES, such as genuinely different request, report, question, suggestion, explanation; not merely three identical requests, copied frames or speaker-selected icons. No fixed word count, sentence length or English-only voice criterion; source functions can be evidenced across appropriate genuine opportunities. Track child-original U, adult-written H, access-only A, uncertain origin X, true recipient, actual purpose and whether listener really responded. If no genuine need or relevant purpose arises, mark NOT_OBSERVED, no child deficit. Genuinely later calendar REVISIT only, no fabricated sessions.
