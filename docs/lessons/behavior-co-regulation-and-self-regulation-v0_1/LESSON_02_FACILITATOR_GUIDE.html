Facilitator Guide 02 — Respectful Real Shared-Resource Response, Never Manufactured Scarcity

Paired original counted method: Our Shared Maker's Table. Family TEACHER_GUIDE.md, graded DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. This facilitator resource is not a fourth counted lesson or actual recorded learner outcome.

Exact printed 2022 Texas source: - PK3.I.B.1.c: “Child manages own behavior with adult guidance and assistance.” Frozen cell SHA 7ce96073d9895dbe53d059a144e6fa7873c195d3262ab1cc801bc87b442d5d7c. - PK4.I.B.1.c: “Child regulates own behavior with occasional reminders or assistance from adults.” Frozen cell SHA 43c84a7d7cc3217606c337031a3803012c739f7f473cea574ed4ec36c0c79ca3.

The lesson engages with real optional classroom-owned shared material actually in use for legitimate ordinary reasons, not an adult-scheduled “unavailable thing” test. A pupil may want to use the shared material and respond with practical real adult help, genuine safe alternative, willing later turn, choosing to do another part of artwork, safe pause, or leaving a shared task. The child may continue to want the original item and voice disappointment without any source failure. A specific replacement object, wait time, requested phrase, unfamiliar context or silent mood is not a Texas source requirement. The method differs from a simple personal pause (Lesson01) and a genuinely willing peer-boundary/transition adjustment (Lesson03).

Safe materials and true access/consent

Prepare optional accessible creative activity (perhaps a maker's neighborhood collage), large nonhazardous art materials, genuine school-shared optional stencil or another tool that might already be in valid use, a trusted responsive adult and multiple honest safe ways to continue/pause/help. The adult must not intentionally hide or withdraw materials, withhold needed communication/motor/sensory aids, create a fake scarcity, force peers to share personal belongings or end someone's actual legitimate turn. The phrase “equally attractive” is not meaningful across children; instead make truly usable optional alternatives available as judged by child needs and preferences, without pretending they must want an adult-selected replacement.

A real current user's permission and material ownership remain protected. If a tool belongs personally to a learner, it is not an optional classroom-share example. If an item is a required accommodation or health/safety tool, it is never used as a scarcity challenge. A willing turn request is negotiable only with honest actual availability and consent, never a false promise of immediate access. Offer sign/home-language/augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), appropriate personal-space/movement/quiet choice, safety support and child-led exit. No peer rejection, stress induction, shame, competitive “fastest sharing” or punitive waiting. Adult care always takes priority.

Five actionable teaching phases

ENTRY: Begin actual voluntary safe ordinary project. If a genuinely optional shared item is naturally occupied, neutrally say “The classroom stencil is being used. You could do something else you enjoy, ask about a later turn, get my help or take some space. What feels useful?” Watch if child meaningfully enacts or accepts a safe action. PK3 can genuinely contribute with adult guidance and assistance (G); teacher may first suggest choices. PK4 can personally direct with occasional behavior cue and ongoing neutral access (U+A). If all items are free, a child doesn't need a turn, or an invitation is declined, NO NATURAL OPPORTUNITY / NOT_OBSERVED, not failure or inability.

MODEL: With different actual adult-owned materials, teacher notices a colleague genuinely using a separate adult card/book or pen. Teacher asks about timing or opts for another reasonable adult project, without taking anything or staging pupil disappointment. Narrate allowing other person's valid use and honoring own preferences without forced sharing. Teacher/puppet modeled response is instruction, not learner source.

DURING: When real gentle choice naturally occurs, educator respects desired item and current legitimate user while offering feasible optional ways forward: request willing later turn, use accessible different tool, choose another part of creative work, ask adult to help or take pause/stop. For PK3 adult guidance can substantially suggest/assist while child genuinely participates in response; for PK4 behavior planning remains child-directed with occasional content help, despite ongoing neutral interpretation, motor/sensory support. No mandatory forced sharing or turn compliance. Describe actual enacted behavior beyond selecting a choice mat icon, including normal expressed disappointment if safely handled. Avoid prompting exact scripted “please” or a timed wait.

EXIT: On same familiar table or a different legitimately available safe context only if a real optional shared-tool moment naturally exists, observe a genuine child-owned/co-managed safe response. A PK3 pupil may voluntarily accept an adult-suggested alternative and enact it; PK4 may direct a respectful request with occasional reminder. A context change offers optional more generalization but is NOT source gate. No staging another unavailable tool to get two score opportunities. Nothing meaningful to manage → NOT_OBSERVED. A caregiver/teacher withholds no basic need and respects ownership.

REVISIT: Another later real calendar date may present genuine ordinary shared-resource moment and child choice; record actual observed event/support. At written authoring this is NOT_OBSERVED, never invented retention, compliance or stable trait.

Fair developmental source decision

PK3 real contribution: Adult explains shared tool is naturally occupied and suggests safe options; child chooses/accepts and implements a reasonable response, perhaps asks to use it later, does another project, waits voluntarily or pauses. Adult proposing plan does not invalidate source, because original grade allows guidance. H if adult merely takes child's personal material, hands substitute and physically directs everything without child meaningful action.

PK4 real direction: Child personally directs a respectful actual choice, e.g. asks through a communication device whether it might be available later, chooses a comfortable other task while respecting current use, with occasional real behavior-content adult reminders. Continuous neutral communication/mobility/sensory help isn't continuous behavior directing. Don't invent numeric cue maximum or require no adult help.

No real source: A child answers a hypothetical puppet “share” question, taps an alternative card but does not enact a behavior in real context, teacher creates artificial scarcity, coerce-silences a disappointed child, or all optional tools were genuinely available (no meaningful need). Last scenario is NOT_OBSERVED, never deficient.

Acceptable safe alternatives: Child says “I still want this later,” signs for help, chooses another part of activity, takes break, safely continues with familiar materials, waits of their own accord or declines voluntary participation. A child refusing to hand over personally owned work isn't misregulation; adult doesn't force that conflict. A child can be unhappy and still manage behavior appropriately. The decision is not based on compliant facial expression, unwilling wait or a required substitute.

Observation details: Real item/context, actual optional shareability/owner and reason unavailable, child's expressed need/consent, real personally enacted safe response, adult behavior help versus neutral AAC/motor/sensory access, effect on current user's consent, presence/absence of natural opportunity, child right to stop. U child-selected and enacted; G authentic PK3 adult-guided co-regulation with meaningful child action; A neutral necessary access without adult choosing the behavior; H adult controls response or manufactures it by force; X ownership of actual behavior unclear. G+A and U+A possible. No labels of diagnosis, no direct published numeric cutscore and a single example isn't mastery.

Seven access pathways, stage differentiation and teacher logistics

PK3 C3-1–C3-4: Adult attends to real optional tool need and child response, suggests workable options, co-manages gentle transitions with child, and may later encounter another context for stronger generalization. PK4 C4-1–C4-5: child manages a familiar real moment, identifies useful response, handles another willing peer's consent, adapts to ordinary availability, optionally transfers under occasional behavioral support. Different grade does not imply zero ongoing access or forbidden help; stages aren't high-stakes tests.

Attention/executive: a small number of actual usable choices with unhurried pace; language/communication: home-language, sign or AAC as real child meaning; literacy: no rule reading; numeracy: no waiting stopwatch/turn count; autism/sensory: safe accessible alternatives without forced change/masking; intellectual/developmental: PK3 supported meaningful choices with adult plan allowed; motor/vision/hearing: true accessible material reach, qualified child-directed action aid and necessary neutral translation. None involves removing a necessary accommodation to simulate scarcity.

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: a hypothetical simple screen scenario can rehearse respectful responses, not make a real school tool unavailable, observe pupil regulation or establish equal effectiveness. CO-PLAY: caregiver respects actual consensual shared ownership, protects child comfort and supports real acted behavior without hiding tools. ADULT-LED: watches only natural safe meaningful choice, models on adult different material, co-regulates and records actual grade-specific behavior and help. CLASSROOM: a small feasible number of naturally occurring real child responses may be privately observed while all other pupils genuinely supervised; don't force simultaneous sharing puzzles, make each child wait for scarce equipment, infer universal source from group chant or publicly rate behavior. If no source, defer.

Reality limits

First-party original three-page choice mats and protected blank educator observation sheets are authored supports. They do not certify actual child regulation, material ownership, peer consent, family acceptance or any learner development. No actual class, child, independent specialist, real physical/tactile paper, actual later-date retention, operational SOLO or four-mode measured educational parity has been verified.
