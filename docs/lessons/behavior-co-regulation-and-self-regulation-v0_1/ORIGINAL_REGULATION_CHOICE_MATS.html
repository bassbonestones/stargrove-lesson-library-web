01 · I can ask for help, space, or a pause

First-party teacher visual/communication resource. Use only in a safe real voluntary activity; the picture itself is never behavioral source evidence.

◯ A different way

Choose another safe large material, change position, or make a new gentle plan.

□ A quiet space

Ask for an available, safe, accessible quiet area or short break.

◇ Help please

Ask a trusted adult for supportive co-regulation, translation, or access assistance.

△ Continue safely

Keep doing a chosen activity if it feels okay; no distress has to be produced.

Adult instructions: Offer these as optional accessible communication supports, including sign, home language, and child-selected AAC. Never require a child to look happy, breathe a particular way, stop expressing discomfort, or stay where they feel unsafe. A selection alone is not source evidence: observe a meaningful actual child action or advocacy.

PK3 teaching move

Attend to the child’s choice. Say “I can help.” Model safe adult support on a different example, then guide the child’s actual response without taking it over.

PK4 teaching move

Offer real choice and respectful space; occasional simple reminders or assistance remain allowed. Record whether the child directed an actual safe response.

02 · Three authentic ordinary contexts

Set up comfort and access first. No engineered conflict, frustration, distress, or compelled peer interaction.

01 · A learner-owned pause

During optional quiet drawing or looking at a book, the learner may request a break, alternate seat, continued activity, or help. Give the requested safe support. Notice whether the learner directs an actual regulation step.

02 · A real, benign available-resource choice

Only IF a genuinely OPTIONAL SCHOOL-SHARED art material is already being used for its actual ordinary purpose, protect the present willing user and never take anyone's personal supplies or accommodations. Do NOT make up shortage, cause upset or promise another child's turn. The learner may request a willing later turn, choose a genuinely ACCESSIBLE acceptable approach, continue another part, ask a trusted adult, rest or decline; disappointment does not erase safe behavior.

03 · A respectful space request or gentle transition

ONLY if a real willing partner OR child naturally expresses their OWN/other person SPACE need, respect BOTH people and no forced pair, staged rejection, scripted apology, hug or gaze. OR during a normal forewarned gentle routine transition, child may ask for time, safe accessible route, help, rest or chosen space. These are independent possible opportunities; do NOT engineer distress, force contact or move child to create scored evidence.

Educator MODEL on separate adult-owned benign situation, but a genuine safe child-managed response in familiar everyday context remains valid source evidence. A changed real setting may strengthen transfer, never a publisher prerequisite. If no natural relevant event, NOT_OBSERVED; never fabricate distress or refusal.

03 · Real child-managed behavior and optional context transfer

PK3

1. Child notices a safe need with adult attunement.

2. Child chooses a safe option with adult guidance.

3. Child and adult co-regulate a quiet transition.

4. Child contributes a changed-context safe response with assistance available.

PK4

1. Child retrieves a familiar response.

2. Child considers own strategy.

3. Child responds to real respectful boundary.

4. Child repairs benign change with occasional help.

5. Child manages a new situation with own-directed strategy.

ENTRY → MODEL → DURING → EXIT → REVISIT

ENTRY: First child-origin observation. MODEL: Unrelated adult example. DURING: Safe authentic practice and supports. EXIT: Familiar real safe response allowed; changed setting optional transfer. REVISIT: Actual future date; until then NOT_OBSERVED.

Accessibility and consent

Provide high contrast, large tactile supports, quiet space, accessible movement, sign, AAC, home language, adaptive seating, individualized plans, and opt-out. A child-directed access device or motor proxy can realize the child's choice; an adult-scripted response cannot.

Never award evidence for a picture tap alone, forced obedience, prescribed breathing, emotional suppression, adult body control, engineered upset, or a puppet's behavior. A safe self-advocacy action can be legitimate regulation.
