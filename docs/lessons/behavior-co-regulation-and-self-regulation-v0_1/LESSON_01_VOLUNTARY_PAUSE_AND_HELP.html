Teach Choosing a Pause, Help or Safe Way Forward

Practical parent and educator guide. This is responsive everyday co-regulation, not a staged distress test, compliance drill or mandatory calm-down routine.

What the child is learning

Children begin making personally meaningful safe choices when everyday activities become difficult or they need something—such as help, a break, more space or a way to continue.

For a 3-year-old, doing this with adult guidance and assistance is entirely appropriate. For a 4-year-old, the child may sometimes direct a useful choice with occasional reminders or assistance. Needed ongoing communication, sensory or mobility access support must not be mistaken for the adult supplying the child's behavioral decision.

Prepare a trustworthy, ordinary activity

Set out something the child genuinely wants to do, such as safe drawing supplies, a picture book or a familiar play choice. Provide honest access to a quiet space, a trusted responsive adult, suitable material alternatives and meaningful ability to stop or change the activity.

Do not deliberately upset, frustrate, deprive or surprise the child to create an assessment opportunity. If no natural need or manageable interruption occurs, that is fine: enjoy the activity and leave regulation evidence not observed.

Facilitate when a real moment arises

1. Let the child choose their activity

Say: “Would you like to draw, look at a book, or choose something else?”

Notice the child's self-directed interests without imposing an artificial challenge. A happy child quietly completing a task is not by itself evidence of navigating a regulation need.

2. Model options without making the child distressed

On an unrelated adult-only benign example, demonstrate how someone might request help or pause.

Say: “Sometimes I want more space. I can ask for it, take a comfortable break, or ask someone to help me.”

This prepares a choice; it is not proof the child already used it.

3. If a real manageable need occurs, offer a genuine choice

Say: “Would you like help, another way to do this, a little space, or to keep going?”

Support the child's own response. The younger child can choose with guidance and co-regulation. The older child can initiate or accept a useful action with occasional content-bearing reminders; necessary neutral access still remains available. Respect an actual break, safe movement preference or communication of “no.”

4. Notice what the child actually decides

When a genuine new or continuing everyday need occurs, give another chance to choose without telling the child exactly which behavior to perform.

Observe the real safe action: asking for help, deciding to pause, using an accessible alternative, or thoughtfully continuing. Distinguish the child's choice from an adult physically moving the child or simply giving the entire response.

If no authentic occasion occurs, do not generate one. A child isn't failing by having a calm day or by declining the optional activity.

5. Revisit a different real day if appropriate

Only record an authentic future event when it happens. The lesson's written revisit is not evidence of actual retention.

Practical responses

What happens Helpful adult response
Child asks for a break, space or help Honor the request when safe; support their chosen next action
Child chooses not to continue Respect the boundary and safety; don't score it as poor self-regulation

Technical reference

The detailed plan separates adult-supported development from increasing child direction. Nothing here requires inducing distress. No child responses, later-day outcomes or functioning solo program have been observed simply by writing this guide.

Lesson 01 — A Place for Pause: Genuine Co-Regulation, Help and Safe Continuation

Distinct method: In an authentic welcoming, unforced art, picture-book or other familiar child-selected everyday activity, a learner has a genuine opportunity to manage a small personally meaningful need—such as continuing safely after a mild natural interruption, requesting help/access/space, selecting a comfortable pause or choosing how to return. A trusted adult responds rather than dictating a predetermined “calm” performance. This method differs from Method02's real material-resource alternative and Method03's consensual interpersonal boundary/transition repair. No distress, unwanted waiting, task deprivation or emotional expression is manufactured to generate assessment evidence.

What counts, and what must remain unknown

PK3: A child may meaningfully contribute to managing their own behavior in collaboration with adult guidance and assistance. Adults can suggest two real safe options, prompt a request, offer co-regulation or accompany a child-selected or child-accepted safe step. The child need not invent the strategy or independently initiate every action. The child must, however, actually participate in a meaningful safe self-management response rather than being physically positioned, silenced or moved wholly by the adult. A genuine child-accepted guided pause/return, appropriately communicated help request or self-directed safe continuation when an actual choice is present can be bounded evidence.

PK4: The child meaningfully directs an appropriate own behavior response in an authentic ordinary context with occasional content-bearing adult reminders or assistance. Occasional is not zero and has no published numeric maximum. Ongoing neutral communication interpretation, visual access, safe mobility support or sensory accommodation is not the same thing as continuously telling the child which behavior/choice to perform. Conversely, an adult dictating every decision does not become occasional support by calling it AAC assistance. A child can choose to ask for help, rest, alter pace, set a safe boundary, or continue and re-engage—no forced stillness, smiling, eye contact or hidden discomfort.

A real regulation opportunity means an actual, naturally occurring manageable need, choice, impulse, boundary, transition or moment where the learner's enacted response is relevant. Neither calm appearance nor a correct choice-mat icon proves regulation in the absence of such an occasion. A familiar genuinely encountered context is publisher-source-relevant; a different naturally safe context can strengthen generalization when truly observed but is not a mandatory Texas criterion. If no real opportunity arises, or the child declines participation, NOT_OBSERVED, never “unable” or behavioral deficit. Emotional crisis is neither required nor appropriate to provoke.

Preflight — trust, access and real choices

The educator prepares a voluntary comfortable ordinary invitation such as selecting between nontoxic large safe drawing materials, browsing picture books or deciding whether to return to a child-chosen work area, with actual access to a trusted responsive adult and optional accessible break/space. Provide safe sensory comfort, water/toilet needs, physical mobility, quiet or movement choice and usable communication in home language, sign, augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) or other authentic mode. A quiet area is chosen and safe, never a punitive removal/time-out or locked isolation; a child can request adult presence, leave, or return with reasonable safeguarding. Respect peer privacy and bodily boundaries.

Do not take away a desired accessible material, deny break/water, engineer an “unavailable” favorite item, suddenly frighten or interrupt, invent peer rejection, require eye contact, enforce breathing counts or induce upset to test behavior. Adults retain responsibility for immediate actual safety and supportive co-regulation during distress; a needed adult response is not postponed to obtain an unassisted learner performance. No audio/video surveillance, diagnoses, identity records in public source, or high-stakes behavior labels.

Five meaningfully distinct instructional phases

ENTRY — a real ordinary occasion before teacher MODEL. Invite the child to choose an accessible safe art/book/play activity. Do not make a “problem.” If naturally the child encounters a manageable need for different space, support, pause or continuation, quietly observe the child’s actual response and any adult support. PK3 might accept an adult's “Would it help to take a break together or get the blue paper?” and enact their own safe response. PK4 might ask for quiet via sign, adjust their own pace, ask for help, or decide to continue after noticing a real change with occasional suggestion. If no personally meaningful decision occurs, record NO AUTHENTIC OPPORTUNITY → NOT_OBSERVED instead of interpreting compliance or happiness as regulation. A child may decline the invitation without a deficit finding.

MODEL — educator demonstrates on THEIR own unrelated real mild example. After any true entry opportunity, an adult demonstrates a helpful choice in their own other activity, e.g. “My pencil isn't where I expected; I can ask a colleague for another or take a short pause. I am choosing to ask.” A colleague responds naturally. No forced child emotion label, scripted learner response or staged upset. Adult demonstration is instruction, never child evidence; the teacher can teach by modeling even if a source observation is unavailable.

DURING — genuinely responsive supported co-regulation. At actual safe benign moments that naturally arise, offer meaningful accessible choices and gentle feedback: “Would you like help, another way, to keep going, or a little space?” PK3 may manage the response with adult guidance and assistance, including co-planning and real adult supportive help while the child meaningfully participates. PK4 may respond by personally directing safe action with occasional behavior reminders/assistance, not a teacher giving every behavioral step; constant neutral sign/AAC interpretation/mobility/sensory support is retained. A child can directly request an adult's help and still have meaningful agency. Observe an actual safe chosen/accepted action or relevant safe continuation only if a real need or choice arose. Do not extinguish self-advocacy, sensory stimming that is safe, movement preferences or emotional expression to make a “calm” score. If a child becomes upset, prioritize actual support and dignity, not data capture.

EXIT — genuine contextual child contribution, familiar source allowed. On the same familiar safe actual activity or another naturally arising appropriate setting, an adult neutrally invites an available choice, without scripting exactly how the child must behave. Record what really happened if there was a meaningful ordinary source occasion: PK3 child participates in safe help/pause/return/continuation while an adult offers allowed guidance; PK4 child directs an appropriate action with occasional adult content-bearing reminder or assistance, while necessary continuous neutral access remains. A routine repeated with child's genuine action is meaningful evidence; a truly different safe event can probe stronger transfer but is not a compulsory “new” gate. Teacher-selected calm icon, puppet acting, adult physically walking child to corner, forced silence or an unobserved hypothetical scenario do not qualify. With no authentic need/opportunity, NOT_OBSERVED, not a failure.

REVISIT — genuinely later actual calendar day. Another naturally occurring safe manageable moment with real learner action and accurately recorded support could strengthen retention/generalization. At authoring, REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED; written future instructions are not delivered learning or field evidence.

Evidence anchors that protect both valid alternatives and genuine construct

Record: (a) actual ordinary trigger/choice (or none); (b) what the child actually contributed, even where adult first suggested a safe option; (c) the observed behavior beyond choosing an icon; (d) the actual nature of adult behavior guidance versus neutral access; (e) assent/decline, environment and how safety and dignity were preserved; (f) whether the child re-engaged, requested help/space, took a needed pause or appropriately continued given that real trigger. Examples:

  • Valid PK3 candidate: During ordinary drawing, the child indicates a genuine need to pause, or a trusted adult recognizes early signs of needing another material and offers an accessible two-option support; the child voluntarily accepts or selects and meaningfully enacts a safe pause or adjustment. Adult co-regulation is allowed. No need for spontaneous strategy invention.
  • Valid PK4 candidate: In a familiar, genuinely manageable moment, the child directs a small safe change, asks a trusted adult for help, sets appropriate space boundary or returns to work with an occasional brief behavioral reminder. Continuous neutral sign/AAC/motor assistance does not convert such self-direction into continual adult behavioral control.
  • Legitimate but no assessable event: Child calmly enjoys drawing with no actual decision/behavior management occasion. The teacher celebrates engagement but cannot automatically score regulation from calm posture, compliance or inferred feelings; no child deficit should be inferred.
  • False credit: Teacher manufactures conflict, dictates “take a break,” physically moves child to a corner and praises “child regulated,” or child taps “calm” on a paper mat while never making any actual response. Similarly, forcing “happy face,” breath counts or stillness is not reliable behavior management.
  • False rejection: Requiring a changed context, self-created strategy without adult guidance for PK3, no requested adult help for PK4, absence of all continuous AAC/sensory support or forced verbalized feelings.

Provenance: U learner meaningfully directs and enacts a safe response without specific adult behavior direction; G PK3 adult-guided/co-decided response with genuine learner contribution; A neutral child-directed communication/motor/sensory access without adult selecting the behavioral next step; H adult solely dictates/performs the decisive behavior (or coerces performance); X unclear. An action can have G plus A, or U plus A; record separately as needed. PK4 assistance may be occasional while child remains directing; no fabricated count of prompts is an official cutscore. An observable learning action is a source candidate, not mastery, diagnosis, classroom effectiveness or a stable trait judgment.

Developmentally differentiated grade pathways

PK3 C3-1: in a gentle ordinary available task, recognize/communicate or respond with adult attunement to a real need and co-manage an accessible pause/help/continuation; C3-2: try safe adult-guided option; C3-3: participate in a naturally occurring supportive transition; C3-4: optional generalization to another ordinary real context with adult still available. PK4 C4-1: direct a familiar safe choice with occasional behavior assistance; C4-2: choose a personally useful regulation strategy in an actual mild context; C4-3: respectfully manage another's safe consent/space when a real willing partner is present; C4-4: manage an authentic benign change without induced stress; C4-5: optional broader generalization with only occasional content-bearing reminders and continuous required neutral access. Stage descriptions are teacher planning opportunities, not a mandated series of high-stakes tests.

Seven access pathways: Short patient prompts/rest for attention/executive needs; personally directed home-language/sign/AAC requests; no literacy decoding or spoken explanation; no timer/warning quota for numerical tasks; nonpunitive calm sensory alternatives, no masking of safe stimming; developmentally adjusted collaborative adult planning for intellectual supports; safe motor/vision/hearing access, nearby trusted adult and appropriate individualized plans. Protect genuine child agency, avoid prejudice against normal culturally diverse emotion/behavior expression, and retain access supports without mislabeling them behavioral prompts.

Four written delivery routes — different actual observation strength

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. A future standalone child-friendly digital activity might practice recognizing legitimate options through accessible icons/voice, but simulated selection cannot demonstrate actual regulation in a real social/task setting. An app cannot observe real child support, safety or emotions simply from a cartoon; no functional SOLO or measured parity exists.

CO-PLAY. Caregiver offers a naturally enjoyable comfortable real book/art/other ordinary activity and responds when a genuine need arises. PK3 co-regulation may be substantial with child actually participating; PK4 child may self-direct and ask for occasional assistance. Caregiver must not invent upset or interpret choosing to stop as behavior failure.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD. Qualified educator prepares genuine access, observes voluntary real event if present, models separate adult issue, offers supported practice, and records actual independent or adult-guided child-contributed safe behavior with honest prompting and privacy. No source event → NOT_OBSERVED rather than artificial test.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM. Teachers may teach options to whole group, but any actual source candidate needs individual naturally witnessed child response under real achievable adult staffing while the rest of class remains safely supported. Private observations need consent and authorized local protection, not named/public charts or universal “quiet class” scores. Rotate attention or defer; never engineer conflict between pupils, use forced isolation or count a puppet's regulation as a child's.

Documentation and limitations

The original first-party choice mats and protected blank observer, individual facilitator guide, and stage progression support adults and are not actual child behavior observations. No real child assessment, qualified independent educator evaluation, classroom safety implementation, physical/tactile print validation, future retention, implemented digital SOLO or four-mode causal effectiveness has been demonstrated. Written design can be reviewed without inventing or demanding field results.

Facilitator Guide 01 — Genuine Child Agency with Support, Not “Calm” Compliance

Paired original counted lesson: A Place for Pause — Voluntary Help, Safe Continuation and Real Co-Regulation. This is a method-specific uncounted adult resource alongside family teacher guide and graded developmental sequence.

Original Texas publisher boundaries

PK3.I.B.1.c: “Child manages own behavior with adult guidance and assistance.” Research original source-cell SHA-256 7ce96073d9895dbe53d059a144e6fa7873c195d3262ab1cc801bc87b442d5d7c. An adult can recognize the need, propose real safe options, give meaningful guidance and assist physical/communication implementation; the child must genuinely contribute to managing their behavior, not invent every strategy themselves. The teacher completing a physical action without child participation is not the child managing behavior.

PK4.I.B.1.c: “Child regulates own behavior with occasional reminders or assistance from adults.” SHA-256 43c84a7d7cc3217606c337031a3803012c739f7f473cea574ed4ec36c0c79ca3. The child meaningfully directs behavior with occasional behavior-content reminders/assistance permitted. Do not impose “zero help” or an invented maximum prompt number. Ongoing neutral sign/AAC translation, physical access, sensory regulation tools or safe supervision are not continuous teacher decisions about what behavior to select. Equally, a teacher giving exact behavioral commands every step doesn't become neutral accessibility support merely because a device is involved.

Both sources concern a child's actual safe ordinary behavior in context, not an emotion label, compliance, perfect calm face, forced smile, obedient eye contact, silence, public class rank, psychiatric diagnosis or cartoon-choice score. Familiar legitimate context remains direct source; a different appropriate safe context provides optional more demanding generalization but isn't stated by publisher as mandatory.

Actual preparation — protect each learner and the right to stop

Offer a comfortably accessible, entirely optional quiet book, drawing, construction, movement-choice or other ordinary real play activity. A trusted adult is truly available, and reasonable appropriate rest, water, toilet, safe break/space, communication in home language/sign/augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), mobility assistance and sensory accommodations remain available throughout. Prepare real reasonable option to continue the child-chosen activity or request help. A pause area is welcoming, protected and never a forced time-out or isolation; when appropriate child can keep the adult nearby, choose an alternate area or resume.

Do not create frustration, abruptly remove a favorite material, withhold needed help, make a false “no” rule, provoke peer rejection, artificially startle, subject child to unsafe waiting, demand a performance of emotion, inhibit safe stimming/movement or pressure child to sit still. A child may refuse an offered “assessment activity” or seek a break; their participation should not be coerced. Basic needs and safeguarding are provided first, even if that prevents a scorable moment. No public behavioral labels, identifiable videos, hidden emotion recognition or social comparisons.

Five-phase authentic educator workflow

ENTRY: Invite an ordinary child-chosen comfortable activity and observe without engineering any need. Only if a naturally relevant mild behavior choice arises—child wishes a quieter seat, a book changes, a family/teacher offers help, a normal interruption occurs, a child chooses to keep going—record what the child truly does, adult support and actual context. A calm child merely drawing with no meaningful choice isn't an observed regulation achievement and isn't a child deficiency. PK3 adult may gently offer a supportive real choice; the child can accept and actively manage behavior. PK4 child can choose safe own behavior after an occasional reminder. The absence of an opportunity is NOT_OBSERVED.

MODEL: In the teacher's separate own safe book/drawing or play setup, demonstrate recognizing a real mild need and respectfully taking a short pause, requesting a colleague's help, adjusting a safe approach, or choosing to continue. The adult may model their own communication and return, not script the child's exact response or force the child to imitate. The model is teaching evidence about the adult, never field evidence of child regulation.

DURING: Co-regulate responsively. For PK3, adult may say “Would you like me to help you take some space, bring another item, or stay with you?” and the child may voluntarily accept/participate; a genuine adult-guided child contribution can be a valid PK3 response. For PK4, the child should meaningfully own the behavioral next step with only occasional content-bearing adult reminders or help; ongoing interpretation/AAC/physical support remains available. Listen to a signed request, allow movement or safe expression, and never require emotional suppression. Guide safe action rather than compliance theater; offer factual response-anchored feedback without praise for silently masking a need. A request for adult help is itself a legitimate self-advocacy option where actually contextual and enacted.

EXIT: At the naturally encountered same familiar or another truly safe accessible everyday situation, without prescribing the child response, record the relevant enacted behavior if an authentic opportunity arises. A child who accepts a PK3 adult suggestion then actually follows through may show guided self-management even if they didn't invent the idea. A PK4 child may independently ask for help or choose safe continuation after occasional cue, while retaining continuous neutral communication support. New setting NOT required for a bounded real publisher source candidate; new setting is optional transfer. Without real need/action or if child declines, NOT_OBSERVED rather than failed. Do not manipulate child's body to create apparent behavior or mark picture tap itself as regulation.

REVISIT: On a genuinely later real calendar day, when ordinary safe relevant occasion arises, the adult can document actual child behavior/help. At authoring all such future acts are NOT_OBSERVED, never presumed retention or clinical treatment outcomes.

Practical stage and differentiated access examples

PK3 C3-1: Recognize/respond to a genuine ordinary need with an adult, even if adult first notices and offers real choice. C3-2: manage optional real material alternatives with support. C3-3: negotiate a genuinely emerging transition with co-regulation and right to break. C3-4: optionally apply valid co-regulation to another ordinary safe context. PK4 C4-1: personally direct a familiar safe response with occasional help; C4-2: choose a useful strategy in real mild need; C4-3: respect a genuinely willing peer's boundary without forced proximity; C4-4: respond to a natural benign change; C4-5: optional safe different-context generalization, with ongoing neutral access and occasional behavior help allowed.

Attention/executive: simple authentic choices without timed silence or transition surprise; language/AAC: personally selected sign/home-language/request with adult translation and proper response; literacy/phonology: no read-the-rule card; numeracy: no count of warnings or seconds of calm; sensory/autism: choice of quiet/movement/retreat, safe non-disruptive stimming, predictable setting and breaks; developmental support: real adult-led co-regulation especially PK3, without adult physically manufacturing the child's response; motor/hearing/vision: accessible ordinary task, neutral physical action assistance, tactile/signed information, reasonable safe body positioning and appropriate individualized plans. No education-quality claim substitutes for real specialist decisions when needed.

Four written modes with honest status

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. Future gentle choices in a digital story may teach vocabulary/options, not real-world source regulation by tapping icons, and no system for independent child use or behavioral outcome measurement exists.

CO-PLAY: Caregiver in ordinary chosen safe activity responds naturally to child need, invites real adjustment or help and records only actual safe child-managed response with PK3 guidance or PK4 occasional behavioral help. Do not manufacture conflict.

ADULT-LED: Trusted educator observes child if real source occurs, models on separate adult task, gives appropriate co-regulation, retains neutral access and accepts familiar real EXIT, with accurate child/action/adult-support provenance.

CLASSROOM: Full group may learn by adult example/visual mat, while only actual observed individual child contributions under genuine classroom staffing can become source candidates. No mandatory simultaneous peer conflict; no public behavior ranking, punitive time-out or invasive recording. Safely rotate/defer unobserved occasions.

Professional and source evidence limits

This is a written teaching plan, not proof any student actually managed behavior or learned regulation, without actual child observations, independent educator approval, implemented child SOLO, real material/visual/tactile field validation, actual later-day retention or equal efficacy among modes.
