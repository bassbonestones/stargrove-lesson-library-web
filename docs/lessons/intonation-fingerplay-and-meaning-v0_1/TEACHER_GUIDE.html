Shared source and access boundaries

A familiar repeated line is valid; the teacher's heard stimulus is necessary for mimicry, not automatically answer-bearing help. Notice the child's own approximate linguistic contour rather than perfect musical notes, exact pronunciation, loudness or English accent. Musical humming with no identifiable relation to language intonation does not establish the whole target. PK4 meaningful expressive adaptation or interpretation can show growing understanding; mere repetition without meaning is a narrower observation. Do not require singing as the only PK4 route. Meaningful spontaneous PK4 expression does not require an adult model; verify its actual language/context. A claimed imitation or receptive response still needs its accessible heard stimulus. If you only heard the child imitate the sung line, note that actual voice; there is no listener answer to judge. If you also saw a visually guided answer, note which act the cue explains: the same one, a separate one, or unclear. A visually guided action is not vocal imitation. Note a child’s purposeful greeting separately from an answer they give by looking at the paper. The second does not show hearing-based interpretation; it does not erase the first when you observed it separately. If you only observed expression, there is no listener answer to judge. If the two are unclear in your notes, clarify before attributing either. If you supplied the meaning or answer, note which act got that help. If notes disagree about whether you supplied it, keep both reports and don’t count that act as independent. It is not the child’s own meaning on that act. Keep a separate note for a genuinely different act when the help, who did it or whether the child could hear was different. If you explained the meaning to older children, keep that separate from a younger child’s own audible imitation. Pause or otherwise make the child’s vocal contribution distinguishable; a supplied meaning is not independent PK4 evidence.

Keep natural context, face visibility and needed communication/hearing supports. No universal rise-equals-question rule or diagnosis of feelings from volume. When a response may be explained by a picture or another child's answer alone, record uncertainty rather than strip away access. Do not treat different plausible cultural interpretations as errors. Signing, augmentative and alternative communication (AAC, such as a communication board or device) and movement are welcome; identify the actual response channel without labeling another person's voice/device playback the child's vocal mimicry.

For expressive evidence, notice what the child changed in the language's expression and what that change did in context. A finger tracing a rise or a suggestion signed or selected on a device is not by itself that expressive change. A meaningful action, sign or AAC response can instead show what the child made of an accessible heard contrast. Describe the actual contribution; when a note says both “only traced the pitch” and “changed the expression,” clarify it rather than credit expression.

Use a clear seated space and optional large paper card held by the adult. No small loose items, cutting, breath exercises, shouting or physical contact. Ordinary volume, familiar language and the child's usual access plan come first. Invite watching, still hands, joining or stopping. If the adult cannot interpret language-specific prosody, use knowledgeable language support and avoid an unsupported score. Breaks, quiet watching and nonparticipation are not misconduct.

EXIT, support and REVISIT

Offer a preferred familiar phrase or meaningful situation; change it only if useful and wanted. Pause the adult voice when distinguishing a child's contribution. In shared singing, a clearly distinguishable child contribution may be described, but indistinguishable chorus is practice, not individual evidence. U: attributable child response; H: supplied meaning/answer assistance; A: access support; X: uncertain attribution. Ordinary repetition and necessary auditory stimulus are not automatic H. No numerical cutscore or mastery claim.

Record privately date, child code, phrase/context, language/access, actual response/channel, help and uncertainty. Do not record voices or publish names. On another actual day, REVISIT a favorite or optional different situation; NOT_OBSERVED until it occurs. Familiar return is legitimate; optional new-context transfer is a separate inference. Stop after a few enjoyable minutes, often 3–8, or earlier for fatigue, distress or refusal. Do not force a performance to finish a form.

Human routes and development

CO-PLAY and ADULT-LED: one adult follows one child's chosen example and response, repeats and scaffolds warmly, and describes actual evidence. CLASSROOM: one teacher keeps children seated in view, offers short group play and notices two or three willing nearby contributions when supervision permits. Others may watch or use silent hand motions. Note a clearly identifiable child contribution even if voices overlap, with the help and context. If you cannot tell whose voice you heard, leave it as shared practice. If someone needs you or declines, stop listening for new evidence and keep any earlier distinct observation separate. No distant quiet station, second-adult assumption, compulsory solo or public ranking. Rotate opportunities later. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: no audio/game or speech recognition software exists here.

See DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. PK3 I3-1 comfortable inflection; I3-2 preferred sung repetition; I3-3 optional gesture choices; I3-4 optional extension. PK4 I4-1 notice expression; I4-2 meaningful question/statement or expressive purpose; I4-3 clarify ambiguity; I4-4 appropriate emphasis; I4-5 optional transfer. These are flexible teaching choices, not required stages or a state testing ladder.

False-positive: the teacher supplies all sound or tells the intended meaning and credits the child as independent. Record supported practice instead, then invite a later attributable contribution. False rejection: familiar repeated imitation or culturally plausible expressive meaning is discarded because it was not novel or not a listener response. Keep the limited evidence.
