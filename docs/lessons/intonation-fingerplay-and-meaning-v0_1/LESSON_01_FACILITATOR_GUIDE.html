Voice and Language Meaning · original teaching resource

Facilitator: little leaf, different voices

Use the complete lesson. PK3.II.C.2 concerns emerging intonation imitation through songs/fingerplay; PK4.II.C.2 concerns growing understanding, which can be expressive or receptive. Do not require listener inference as the only PK4 route.

Prepare

One adult, clear seated space, optional large paper leaf, pencil for private notes. No purchased supplies, small loose objects, cutting or recordings. Choose comfortable language, ordinary volume and established access supports. Face visibility and communication aids stay. Avoid compulsory singing, touch, breath strain or emotion performance.

ENTRY, MODEL, DURING

Invite watching, hand play or voice play. Gently sing “Leaf up high, leaf down low,” then pause for an optional response. This heard stimulus is necessary for imitation; familiar repetition is valid. Model “Leaf rests here, soft and slow.” Let the child choose repeats, still hands, another image or leading the adult. Shorten the phrase, slow down or sing together when helpful. Record distinguishable child contributions; group or adult sound alone is not child evidence.

PK4 may create a meaningful inviting voice or interpret a genuine question/statement in paper play. Do not assign emotions by loudness or a universal rise/fall rule. Accept contextually plausible meanings and requests to clarify. Echo alone without demonstrated meaning supports imitation, not automatically the stronger PK4 interpretation. If you only heard the child imitate the sung line, note that actual voice; there is no listener answer to judge. If you also saw a visually guided answer, note which act the cue explains: the same one, a separate one, or unclear. A visually guided action is not vocal imitation. Note a child’s purposeful greeting separately from an answer they give by looking at the paper. The second does not show hearing-based interpretation; it does not erase the first when you observed it separately. If you only observed expression, there is no listener answer to judge. If the two are unclear in your notes, clarify before attributing either. If you supplied the meaning or answer, note which act got that help. If notes disagree about whether you supplied it, keep both reports and don’t count that act as independent. It is not the child’s own meaning on that act. Keep a separate note for a genuinely different act when the help, who did it or whether the child could hear was different. In an optional private note, say which act a visual cue could explain and whether it was the same act, a separate act, or unclear. Leave an unobserved act blank or mark it “not observed”; don’t ask for another response to complete the form. If you explained the meaning to older children, keep that separate from a younger child’s own audible imitation. Pause or otherwise make the child’s vocal contribution distinguishable; a supplied meaning is not independent PK4 evidence.

For expressive evidence, notice what the child changed in the language's expression and what that change did in context. A finger tracing a rise or a suggestion signed or selected on a device is not by itself that expressive change. A meaningful action, sign or AAC response can instead show what the child made of an accessible heard contrast. Describe the actual contribution; when a note says both “only traced the pitch” and “changed the expression,” clarify it rather than credit expression.

EXIT and response

Offer the favorite familiar line or a child-chosen alternative. Pause adult voice if distinguishing the child's response. Note approximate contour rather than exact notes. PK4 can show purposeful expression or meaningful interpretation. Keep natural context and access. If only hands move, welcome participation and record vocal evidence unobserved. If attribution is unclear, say so; do not force another attempt. If the child declines or tires, stop. Roughly 3–8 minutes is a flexible planning range.

Write date, child code, actual phrase/context, language/access, response/channel, help and uncertainty. A sung eliciting model is not inherently answer help; co-performance that obscures contribution remains shared practice. A meaningful response with adult-supplied interpretation must not be called independent. No recordings needed. Keep notes private.

One adult with a class

Seat children in view with space for gentle optional hands. Offer whole-group practice briefly; then notice two or three willing nearby children while others continue quiet optional leaf movement. Keep everyone supervised. No distant assessment station or second adult. Note a child’s contribution if you can actually distinguish it, even during overlap; mark indistinguishable sound as shared practice. If a child declines or the group needs attention, stop trying to gather evidence and keep any earlier clearly heard contribution separate. Rotate focus later. No chorus-to-individual inference, public ranking or compulsory solo.

REVISIT and limits

Return on another actual day to familiar play or an optional different situation. No changed-context or novelty requirement. REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED until it happens. Both routes are written design, not tested classroom efficacy or diagnosis. SOLO is NOT IMPLEMENTED. Shared family repairs and sibling lessons need separate review; this guide does not qualify siblings.
