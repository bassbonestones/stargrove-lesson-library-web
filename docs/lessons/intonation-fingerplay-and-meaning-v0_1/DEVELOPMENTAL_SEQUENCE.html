Source interpretation and progression

PK3 begins to mimic linguistic intonation through songs/fingerplay. It is not a music-pitch exam or an articulation target imported from adjacent II.C.1. PK4 growing understanding may be expressed in purposeful voice use or meaningful interpretation, not exclusively listener inference. These flexible options help educators adjust challenge; they are not mandatory ordered subskills or cutoffs. Preserve a child's familiar songs and repeated favorite material. The newly authored sample lines avoid dependence on commercial audio, but do not prohibit legitimate familiar/family songs.

PK3 I3-1: one comfortable inflection

Offer “little leaf” in a gentle sung fingerplay; wait for a voluntary approximate linguistic contour. Hands can remain still. If the adult alone makes the sound, note participation but not child's voice evidence. Shorten or repeat without demand.

PK3 I3-2: favorite recurring line

Sing “Leaf up high, leaf down low.” Let the child repeat a favorite part, suggest a phrase or lead the adult. Repetition supports learning; source does not require an unheard phrase. Co-singing is teaching and can yield attributable evidence only when the child's own contribution is clearly distinguishable.

PK3 I3-3: gesture choice

Offer slow optional hand opening or still hands with the same phrase. This helps the adult distinguish motor participation from audible mimicry without withholding access. Do not require changed gestures or a new motor skill.

PK3 I3-4: optional extension

Return to the line or try a child-chosen song. A changed phrase is an optional transfer opportunity, not a prerequisite for recognizing current emerging evidence. No note accuracy or word-memory score.

PK4 I4-1: notice expression

Compare comfortable locally meaningful voices during genuine play. A child can try an expression or respond to it without naming pitch or emotion.

PK4 I4-2: expression for a purpose

Invite a welcoming leaf voice or make a real clarification question and later statement. A purposeful expressive choice or contextually meaningful reply can show growing understanding. Echo alone remains a narrower observation.

PK4 I4-3: clarify uncertainty

Accept “Are you asking?” or another communication repair. Do not demand mind reading or stereotype quiet voices as sadness. Explain your own intended meaning when useful, labeling that teaching help honestly.

PK4 I4-4: meaningful emphasis

Explore emphasis only when naturally meaningful in the child's language and actual context. Retain faces and access; a bold word or adult pointing alone cannot prove prosodic understanding.

PK4 I4-5: optional later application

A later familiar greeting, story or new setting can extend learning. Newness is not required and later evidence must be actually observed.

Shared source and access boundaries

A familiar repeated line is valid; the teacher's heard stimulus is necessary for mimicry, not automatically answer-bearing help. Notice the child's own approximate linguistic contour rather than perfect musical notes, exact pronunciation, loudness or English accent. Musical humming with no identifiable relation to language intonation does not establish the whole target. PK4 meaningful expressive adaptation or interpretation can show growing understanding; mere repetition without meaning is a narrower observation. Do not require singing as the only PK4 route. Meaningful spontaneous PK4 expression does not require an adult model; verify its actual language/context. A claimed imitation or receptive response still needs its accessible heard stimulus.

Keep natural context, face visibility and needed communication/hearing supports. No universal rise-equals-question rule or diagnosis of feelings from volume. When a response may be explained by a picture or another child's answer alone, record uncertainty rather than strip away access. Do not treat different plausible cultural interpretations as errors. Signing, augmentative and alternative communication (AAC, such as a communication board or device) and movement are welcome; identify the actual response channel without labeling another person's voice/device playback the child's vocal mimicry.

Use a clear seated space and optional large paper card held by the adult. No small loose items, cutting, breath exercises, shouting or physical contact. Ordinary volume, familiar language and the child's usual access plan come first. Invite watching, still hands, joining or stopping. If the adult cannot interpret language-specific prosody, use knowledgeable language support and avoid an unsupported score. Breaks, quiet watching and nonparticipation are not misconduct.

EXIT, support and REVISIT

Offer a preferred familiar phrase or meaningful situation; change it only if useful and wanted. Pause the adult voice when distinguishing a child's contribution. In shared singing, a clearly distinguishable child contribution may be described, but indistinguishable chorus is practice, not individual evidence. U: attributable child response; H: supplied meaning/answer assistance; A: access support; X: uncertain attribution. Ordinary repetition and necessary auditory stimulus are not automatic H. No numerical cutscore or mastery claim.

Record privately date, child code, phrase/context, language/access, actual response/channel, help and uncertainty. Do not record voices or publish names. On another actual day, REVISIT a favorite or optional different situation; NOT_OBSERVED until it occurs. Familiar return is legitimate; optional new-context transfer is a separate inference. Stop after a few enjoyable minutes, often 3–8, or earlier for fatigue, distress or refusal. Do not force a performance to finish a form.

Human routes and development

CO-PLAY and ADULT-LED: one adult follows one child's chosen example and response, repeats and scaffolds warmly, and describes actual evidence. CLASSROOM: one teacher keeps children seated in view, offers short group play and notices two or three willing nearby contributions when supervision permits. Others may watch or use silent hand motions. Note a clearly identifiable child contribution even if voices overlap, with the help and context. If you cannot tell whose voice you heard, leave it as shared practice. If someone needs you or declines, stop listening for new evidence and keep any earlier distinct observation separate. No distant quiet station, second-adult assumption, compulsory solo or public ranking. Rotate opportunities later. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: no audio/game or speech recognition software exists here.

False-positive: the teacher supplies all sound or tells the intended meaning and credits the child as independent. Record supported practice instead, then invite a later attributable contribution. False rejection: familiar repeated imitation or culturally plausible expressive meaning is discarded because it was not novel or not a listener response. Keep the limited evidence. Flexible teaching phases

ENTRY invites the child's preferred participation and records what occurs, without making a pretest compulsory. MODEL gives a gentle meaningful vocal example; the heard stimulus can also elicit imitation. DURING supports repetition, co-singing or clarification. EXIT offers a bounded attributable observation on familiar or optionally different material. REVISIT occurs on a real later day, not on paper at authoring. Meaning-bearing help is described, not denied or confused with access.
