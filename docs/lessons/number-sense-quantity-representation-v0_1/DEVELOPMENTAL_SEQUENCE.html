Representing Quantities — teach PK3 foundation before PK4 extension

Required developmental pathway for MATH-PK-REP-01/02/03 · 2026-10-30 original draft. These are three reusable methods, not three one-sitting courses that simultaneously teach all values 0–10. Explicit instruction must grow the child's understanding over real sessions. This pathway modifies delivery of each existing ENTRY, MODEL, DURING, EXIT and REVISIT, but preserves source-specific independent final evidence. SOLO is NOT IMPLEMENTED, and mode equivalence is not validated.

Source fidelity: PK3.V.A.6 “Child represents quantities up to 5” — SHA-256 0be2d9b0e2a809832878d3dfcf9f1434baa73f69331c3d22eebb0510e2ae974f. PK4.V.A.6 “Child represents quantities up to 10” — SHA-256 b2febe0ac880a52306209b7046cab1ef66ae1213ea3eae64151d3a39e39e9625; 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines Mathematics p.51. A quantity representation may use objects, marks or discrete actions; our three methods intentionally assess child-authored production, not selecting a prebuilt collection. Printed symbol knowledge, serial-count word recitation and adult-built set cardinality are different outcomes. PK4 still must work with 0–5, not only 6–10.

Stage-by-stage mini-lessons, not one full-range exposure

Use responsive 8–15-minute teaching segments and revisit familiar quantities over different actual sessions. The bands below organize concepts rather than assign a fixed “children must learn X by Tuesday” deadline. When working in PK4, check 0–5 before adding 6–10; if those foundations are uncertain, teach them meaningfully rather than rushing. A child's advanced interest is welcome, but complete source-range observation still needs all individual requested values.

Stage Teaching, exact sample language and genuine child action Mini-check and next response
PK3 R3-1: 0, 1, 2 Adult says “Make one in any arrangement you choose” on a truly blank workspace, then “Now make two in your own way.” After modeled examples on different material, deliberately introduce zero: “For zero, choose not to put any pieces. Let me know when you are done so I know you chose zero.” Fresh blank mat or new action surface: request 2, 0, 1 in scrambled order; child must create each amount and clearly signal intentional zero. A silent untouched mat after any prompt is NOT_OBSERVED unless zero intention is communicated.
PK3 R3-2: 3, 4, 5 “Can you make four in your own layout, without watching me make it first?” Begin with three, then four/five, interleaving a known small amount and zero. Child selects pieces, marks or safe action, decides when group/sequence is finished. New material and unseen order, child independently produces 3/4/5, plus 1–2 familiar values. If adult has to tell child each time to add “one more” or physically make the final unit, tag HELPED; teach on a different example then retry another day.
PK3 R3-3: flexible mixed 0–5 Compare two self-created forms of the same requested amount (e.g. four in a loose line versus four as separate daubs), but do not require pictorial neatness, counting-strategy explanation or a fixed pattern. Formal novel 0–5 uncoached EXIT with all six actual requested amounts; later-date REVISIT with changed medium and full range. Unproduced quantities stay NOT_OBSERVED; no “5 mastered” from naming the glyph five.
PK4 R4-1: retrieve complete 0–5 Ask new creation requests 0–5 in scrambled order, beginning with a comfortable self-chosen method, then another. Teach any gaps using R3-1/2; advancing grade does not mean skipping foundation. Fresh independent child-authored 0–5 candidate baseline, distinguishing exact first amount, help, and intentional zero. A PK4 child can revisit earlier skills without an age label or deficit inference.
PK4 R4-2: 6, 7, 8 “This time try six. You can organize your pieces however helps you keep track. You choose when you have six.” After an adult MODEL on a different total, child creates new 6/7/8 without a prebuilt row to copy; optional one-to-one checking afterward as practice. Fresh unseen requested 6/7/8 among interleaved 1–5; record original actual output before any coached count. If child only knows six when copying a six-dot template, provide independent blank-space practice.
PK4 R4-3: 9, 10 Add nine and ten individual units/actions using larger/comfortable materials or short gentle press series. “Make ten separate marks (or presses), then tell me when you are done.” A printed 10 alone is not ten created units. Repeated unmodeled child-owned nine and ten creations with known 5/6/8 interleaved. Correct one-button auto-fill or ten adult-made pieces earns no independent child-production evidence.
PK4 R4-4: flexible mixed 0–10 Revisit all values with child choice of original material/format; correct narrow confusions from actual evidence, not a forced eleven-repetition drill. Full 0–10 separate novel uncued EXIT (eleven amounts) can be split among manageable real sessions; full actual later-date 0–10 REVISIT with new material. Missing, aided or ambiguous produced quantities retain their own honest statuses.

Formative decision rule, not a mandated Texas mastery score: When a child has independently made the current handful of requested amounts using a new blank surface and without a copied model, expand teaching or offer another method. Where one quantity remains confusing, model a different count then reattempt a new target; don't treat two identical rehearsed attempts as validated transfer. Progress and persistence can be noted without blocking access to richer ideas or diagnosing a child. Revisit is an actual later date, never a checkbox completed by writing this plan.

Stage adaptations for three genuinely different teaching methods

Method 01 — Make It Yours (TEACHER_GUIDE.md): physical set creation

  • PK3 R3-1: Give a blank tray and at least two safe nonswallowable large blocks. Adult models different quantities on an adult mat; child makes own one then two and explicitly demonstrates zero by deliberately leaving mat empty and signalling done. R3-2: Child builds 3/4/5 without the adult starting the pattern, moves objects into a personally selected arrangement. R3-3: Full uncued 0–5 on fresh surfaces/changed materials, all six individual initial end-states recorded.
  • PK4 R4-1: Fresh mixed 0–5 build baseline, not a “PK4 starts at six” assumption. R4-2: Build six/eight with self-chosen safe grouping and seven new shape, not copied piles. R4-3: Deliberately create nine and ten distinct objects with self-stopping. R4-4: Full 0–10 novel physical group creation; some trials may be carried into later sessions but remain NOT_OBSERVED until actually completed.
  • False credit: A child correctly reports number of adult-set counters is not a child-produced set; original count and final help must remain separately recorded.

Method 02 — Mark Makers (LESSON_02_MARK_MAKERS.md): child-generated visual notation

  • PK3 R3-1: Child invents their own one-mark symbol (large daub/dash) and then independently creates two marks; zero is intentionally blank + child confirmation. R3-2: Child makes three/four/five marks in different self-chosen layouts; adult may show how two different marks can be same count on different model material. R3-3: Request all six 0–5 quantities on fresh untemplated pages in scrambled order, not traceable dot patterns.
  • PK4 R4-1: Retrieve all 0–5 without visual templates. R4-2: Child independently authors six/seven/eight marks, perhaps grouping spatially to check them; teach how to separate overlapping strokes if count becomes unobservable. R4-3: Create nine/ten distinct personally chosen units, not writing glyph “10” or an auto-stamping macro. R4-4: Mixed 0–10 fresh independently generated marks, changed tool and separate later-day revisit.
  • False credit: Matching two preprinted eight-dot sheets by visual appearance or tracing an adult's row is not independent amount creation.

Method 03 — Quantity Composer (LESSON_03_ACTION_COMPOSER.md): child-originated temporal action sequences

  • PK3 R3-1: Show one discrete soft press and two separate presses on adult-only pad, then child chooses a gentle accessible action to perform 1, 2 and intentional 0 (confirm “done with none”). R3-2: Child independently initiates and stops at three/four/five safe quiet squeezes/taps with no adult rhythmic pulse; interleave one/two. R3-3: New self-selected action and all 0–5 values, real individual observation, no group chorusing inference.
  • PK4 R4-1: Check actual self-controlled 0–5 actions, not just replaying a familiar adult rhythm. R4-2: Add 6/7/8 gentle presses at self-chosen tempo, with optional quiet observer tally; avoid motor fatigue by breaking into multiple sittings. R4-3: Child makes nine/ten individually controlled events, not one “play ten” button; if units cannot be distinguished, mark unknown and choose another accessible event. R4-4: Changed safe action/tempo, full independent 0–10, later session transfer.
  • False credit: Copying adult’s 5-clap pattern with no new request, automatically generated audio events or 20 simultaneous classroom taps do not independently establish each child's representation.

Four-mode progressive teaching commitments

  • ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: Prepare only current stage's blank materials and a small group of values, not an eleven-amount quiz upfront. Adult privately records first child-authored amount and a separately helped retry. Offer next small-step experience based on what child really made; accommodate IEP/IFSP/504, home-language words and child-directed motor proxies.
  • CO-PLAY: Child leads selection of mark/action/material; adult demonstrates a different amount and takes turns as a fellow maker, but only child's independent fresh creation after adult examples are hidden constitutes child evidence. Do PK3 R3-1→3 or PK4 R4-1→4 as appropriate.
  • TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Different groups can work at different concept stages in parallel: some intentional zero/one, some flexible 3–5, some extending through 10. Give each child clean blank individual work and record authentic self-generated first attempts rather than group totals. Schedule full-range EXIT across small sessions; never equate all-class copy with individual understanding.
  • SOLO future spec: Narrated requests and blank touch/mark/pad workspaces must offer 0–2 then 3–5 and PK4 refresh 0–5 before 6–8 and 9–10. Child must generate one object/mark/press per child initiation, and explicitly choose zero/no action; no autofill or visible target count on exit. Data security, narration, prereader usability and functional parity remain NOT IMPLEMENTED.

Source versus teaching distinction: The PK3 and PK4 authored teaching ladders are intentionally distinct while allowing reuse of materials; the assessment for each published source still requires complete 0–5 or 0–10 genuine independent observation. Stage mini-checks do not confer source-level credit or child mastery automatically. This document is internally authored draft, not independently educator validated.

Learning-differences stage adaptations — retrospective review November 1, 2026

Access, sensory comfort, communication, processing speed and executive function must be checked against the CHILD-ORIGINATED representation action on every stage, not mistaken for number deficit. Consulting actual child/family and authorized IEP/IFSP/504 educators is required where relevant; do not diagnose. Short individualized instruction and assistive access can preserve the official PK3 0–5 and PK4 0–10 targets, but a smaller partial range does not qualify as full-range mastery.

PK3 R3-1

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: For 0/1/2, place two large nonchoking units and a completely blank mat in comfortable reach or child-controlled gaze space. Model one on ANOTHER mat; then child chooses whether to add zero, one or two and uses their reliable DONE/NONE signal. Fade any adult demonstration of stopping; no silent empty response is credited as intentional zero.

Fresh source-equivalent check: On a new blank mat or new quiet press medium request 2, 0, 1 in novel order. Record exact child-created units and positive intention of zero before feedback; adult-produced units and ambiguous blanks remain N/H.

PK3 R3-2

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: To reach 3/4/5, offer short spaced opportunities and a stable finish zone; for lost-track difficulty practice child-selected touching/counting on DIFFERENT targets, then remove number-synchronous adult cues. Use separated stamps or large blocks if fine-motor marks merge.

Fresh source-equivalent check: Use new blank medium for 3/4/5 interspersed with a familiar value and intentional zero; observe first produced total, independent control of each unit and end signal without a prefilled five-box template.

PK3 R3-3

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: For mixed 0–5, invite child-selected physical or mark tools after predictable preview of choices. Allow stage-sized short sessions and access supports while ensuring all six actual amounts are assessed; prevent accidental double presses through safe debounce without autosuggesting target count.

Fresh source-equivalent check: Use novel requests 0–5 across changed materials, no adult-made arrangement or auto-generated dots; missing quantities are NOT_OBSERVED, and a separate actual later-date transfer remains required.

PK4 R4-1

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: Check the earlier 0–5 as child-produced quantities, rather than asking only numeral naming. If child struggles, reteach the real missing production stages with dignified learner-selected materials and no assumed age-linked deficit.

Fresh source-equivalent check: In new blank media and randomized 0–5 requested amounts record self-originated item count, independent stop and zero signal; a five-symbol card choice is not representation.

PK4 R4-2

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: For six/seven/eight the child may lose one-to-one tracking. Practice grouping on a DIFFERENT total (e.g., known four) with larger safe separated units, then fade the adult group, step counter and number-synchronous prompts. Offer short physical/switch runs with break when needed.

Fresh source-equivalent check: Ask for new 6/7/8 child-authored sets/marks or genuinely independent presses, interleave known small amounts; record unaided first total and identify if the adult suggested the final unit.

PK4 R4-3

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: For nine/ten, provide accessible workspace sized for ten separate units without forcing ten loud claps or tiring hand movement. Use one action per intentional child control; ensure any switch does not auto-repeat or auto-fill ten, and model another target before removing cues.

Fresh source-equivalent check: New blank area and separate nine/ten requested amounts: count ten separate learner-originated units/actions, not a written glyph “10”, premarked ten-frame copy or automatic repeated switch output.

PK4 R4-4

Access-aware instruction and gradual cue removal: For mixed 0–10, avoid one-session endurance overload and let the child choose among genuinely accessible methods with equivalent countable outputs. Split the eleven target requests across planned sessions if needed but keep raw per-target observations and later-date transfer.

Fresh source-equivalent check: Each of 0–10 requires an actual new child-authored amount, including independently intentional zero, with individual first attempt, access/hint provenance and later fresh transfer; untried targets stay NOT_OBSERVED.

All four written delivery modes inherit child-originated-unit and adult-hint separation; in groups, individual turns with clean blank spaces are needed. Actual children, professional special educators, SOLO software and equivalence are not observed.
