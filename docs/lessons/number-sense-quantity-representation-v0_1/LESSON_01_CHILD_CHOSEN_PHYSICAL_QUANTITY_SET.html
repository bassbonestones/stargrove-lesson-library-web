Teach Making Your Own Number of Objects

Plain-language teacher and family guide. The original technical plan remains available in the other tab. No actual child outcomes or working solo software are implied.

What the child is learning

The child creates the requested number of things themselves. This is different from recognizing a printed digit, copying an adult's prearranged pattern or merely counting an already-made set. Children may arrange safe pieces, draw marks, stamp, or direct an accessible tool while still making their own decisions.

Prepare and choose the stage

Bring a blank mat, a tray of large safe counters or shapes, and perhaps an accessible mark-making alternative. The mat must really start empty. Do not prebuild the number for the child or use a printed numeral answer as a template.

Begin according to actual readiness:

Child Start with Gradually explore
3-year-old new to making quantities Zero, one and two Three to five, then mixed zero to five
4-year-old Revisit zero to five when useful Six to eight, then nine and ten; mixed zero to ten

Zero is a genuine quantity: making zero means intentionally leaving the collection empty, not forgetting to do the activity. The later full-range prompts in the technical plan are not a mandatory first-day series.

Teach with the child's own choices

1. Invite an initial creation

Set out an empty mat and a few large pieces.

Say: “Can you make [a small suitable number] of things, any way you like? Tell me when your group is ready.”

Wait for the child's own selections and placements. Record whether the actual amount was child-produced, rather than built or corrected by an adult.

2. Demonstrate separately

On a different adult-only mat, select three objects one at a time. Show that changing their position does not change the amount.

Say: “I made three. If I move them around, I still have three.” Then give a separate zero example: “If I choose to put none on this mat, that shows zero.”

The adult's work is an example, not proof of the child's skill.

3. Let the child invent more groups

Clear the child's mat. Invite different new quantities at their current stage.

Say: “Show me [the amount] your own way. How will you know when you're finished?”

Allow creative patterns and child-directed use of safe counters or marks. The child can change their plan. If they make too many or too few, help them inspect a different practice set and then give a fresh chance. Adult physical access assistance is okay if the child controls the choice of what to add.

4. Offer new independent amounts

Clear the mat between tasks and hide all demonstrated patterns. Request a fresh quantity without supplying the corresponding pieces, marks or answer.

Observe the child's actual number of created objects, whether they chose placements and whether any adult did the decisive counting or making.

A complete later source-range assessment includes all numbers zero to five for the 3-year-old group, or zero to ten for the 4-year-old group, presented in mixed order and split across sessions if appropriate. One successful example is not an entire range.

5. Revisit another genuine day

With another safe material or work surface, invite fresh child-owned quantities. Only actual later creations can be reported as observed.

Adjust your teaching

Observation Response
Child copies your finished arrangement Clear it, demonstrate on different pieces, and offer a truly new amount
Child adds one more by accident Invite a check of actual pieces; do not silently repair it for them
Child correctly leaves a mat blank for zero Recognize the intentional empty representation
Child needs adult hands for placement Let the child decide each addition; record access assistance honestly
Child prefers drawings or stamps Accept them if each mark genuinely represents child-produced quantity

Teach Making Your Own Number of Objects with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

The child creates the requested number of things themselves. This is different from recognizing a printed digit, copying an adult's prearranged pattern or merely counting an already-made set. Children may arrange safe pieces, draw marks, stamp, or direct an accessible tool while still making their own decisions.

Prepare the classroom

Prepare safe large counters in shared trays, an individual blank build space for each child, and a separate teacher model; never rely on tiny pieces. Offer reachable quantities: younger learners work gradually with zero through five, older learners later with zero through ten, not every amount at once.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “Could you make a number of objects your own way? How will you know you made the amount you wanted?” Invite an initial child-selected creation, then model a different amount on different objects and explain what a truly empty set means.

Give children meaningful practice

During group workshops, children choose placement and build individual physical groups, sometimes comparing designs with a partner as practice. The teacher visits a feasible few children to notice creation and checking, while others continue independent constructive exploration. Spread later required amounts across appropriate rotations or days.

Observe individual learning fairly

Give a focal child a fresh blank space and a suitable new requested amount, then observe the child's actual independent physical construction and communication. A correct group made for them by a peer or teacher is not independent production. Separately note which amounts were genuinely sampled and whether access assistance altered the target thinking.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Accept a child's arrangement, pointing, AAC and directed adult mover when the quantity decisions remain the child's. Make early amounts small and playful; protect zero as zero real objects, not an arbitrary symbol. Record missing later-range checks as pending rather than assigning all children mastery from a finished shared display.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

01 — Make It Yours: independently represent a requested quantity

Required developmental teaching sequence: PK3 foundation → PK4 extension. PK3: child intentionally creates 0–2, then 3–5, then mixed 0–5; PK4: retrieve 0–5, then child-creates 6–8, then 9–10, then mixed 0–10. Do not replace authored quantity with numeral reading, matching or adult-produced sets. The existing full-grade EXIT is a later source-range check, not the first teaching session. Apply this ordered sequence in SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes; SOLO remains a written specification only.

Official illustrative child behaviors include moving objects, drawing dots, tapping/clapping, and matching symbols with sets. The published action is representing quantities; this original lesson deliberately seeks independently child-created representations to avoid a mere matching task falsely earning productive-action credit.
  • Concept: A child is asked for an amount (spoken, sign or accessible quantity-word), chooses and independently makes that amount using safe countable counters or authored marks on an initially blank mat, communicates that the creation is complete, and can reconstruct a different amount with fresh material. This is not simply recognizing a written numeral, counting an adult-created group, imitating a template, or pointing at a preconstructed dot card.
  • Grade-specific scope: PK3 can work through the full 0,1,2,3,4,5 range; PK4 through 0–10, including 0 and 10. It is developmentally acceptable to spread instruction and assessment across several short real sessions. An untested value is NOT_OBSERVED, never automatic full-range source recognition. The guidelines themselves are not a test instrument or mandatory cut score.
  • Estimated time: 18–22 minutes (PK4 extended across sessions as needed). No timed production. Later distinct date revisit planned, never credited before real observation.
  • Original material: ORIGINAL_MAKE_MATS.html; large safe washable objects, or child-chosen marks/stickers/digital dots, with adult handling any cutting; blank entry/practice/exit workspaces and an adult-private per-quantity observation. Printable layout contains no sample preconstructed answer pattern or numeral-number line visible to child. No small beads, choking hazards or demanded paper-pencil skills.
  • Prerequisites: Child can communicate a response or reliable intentional choice in some accessible mode, with meaningful opportunity to practice an amount; no prerequisite handwriting or printed numeral recognition. Clarify number word in child's home language/sign/AAC and provide a neutral accessible way to request repetition. Child may count while constructing; method of verifying the amount is not substituted for making it.

Two fundamentally distinct skills: Adult asks “make four,” child places four large blocks in their chosen arrangement = child represents. Adult already placed four blocks, child correctly says “four” = adult-built set with child counting/cardinality, not independent production. Child taps a picture labelled 4 that causes the app to auto-fill four objects = app produced representation, not child. A child who places no pieces after hearing zero might be responding correctly or refusing; assess zero via explicit intentional communication (“zero means none; this mat should stay empty”) and a changed later task, not silence.

B. Concrete source-bound sequence

Phase Prepared child-facing experience and exact language Authentic evidence and guardrail
ENTRY E-REP (2–3 min) Present a blank mat and large safe counters. Request PK3 2 then 4, or PK4 3 then 7 using an understood spoken/signed/AAC number word. “Can you make this amount for me in any arrangement you choose? Tell me when your group is ready.” No objects preplaced. Observe whether child initiated and controlled each placed piece, exact final count verified later, adult guidance, requested help, and intentional completion signal. Starting with an adult sample would destroy baseline. Only a short entry sample, no full-range claim.
MODEL M-REP (4 min) On an unrelated adult-only demonstration mat, teacher says “I'm making three objects. One, two, three. I chose where to put them. I can show three another way with three simple marks.” Demonstrate a separate zero model: explicitly say zero and leave blank on purpose while indicating none. For PK4 show how ten pieces could be put in two easy-to-check groups, but don't leave this as child's exit reference. Modeling is teaching, not child independent performance. Explain that different arrangements can still show the same amount. Do not require decomposition, writing numeral 3 or a specific pattern; these have separate outcomes.
DURING D-REP (8–9 min) Child chooses counters versus drawing/large stamps or placing digital objects; adult requests different quantities, PK3 through 0–5 / PK4 0–10 over short sessions, without making any amount first. “Show me five in your own way. How will you know when you are finished?” After child stops, ask them first whether they are satisfied and why, then count privately/with child's consent to check. Let child change arrangement without adult supplying objects. Log child-created amount, first end-state, any self-correction, whether adult physically added pieces or supplied the final answer, and optional child-generated explanation. If adult demonstrates correction, classify that example as HELPED; use a different new prompt for later unprompted evidence.
EXIT X-REP (4–7 min; split for PK4 as needed) Hide model and practice materials. Give a newly cleared and genuinely blank mat with no printed numeral/amount template. The child may continue using the same familiar large safe accessible pieces or mark-making tool used in practice, as long as the new quantity request and child-owned creation are not a copy of a demonstrated answer; a different safe material is an optional transfer challenge, not a prerequisite for independent source evidence. No printed numeral answer or auto-filled group is shown. Request each in-grade quantity in an unfamiliar nonnumeric order: PK3 4,0,2,5,1,3; PK4 8,0,4,10,2,7,5,1,9,3,6. One fresh workspace per amount (clear mat between trials) and no adult preplacement, arranging or revealing the answer. “Make [amount] so I can see the amount you chose. Let me know when it's finished.” For zero explicitly ask how the empty mat shows zero rather than assuming silence. Separate first child-completed output for every identity with actual produced amount, original request, exact type of adult help, intentional completion and how zero was interpreted. Mark no answer, incomplete session and adult-supplied group honestly. Do not treat a successful named-numeral selection as produced quantity.
REVISIT R-REP (new actual date) Use new materials and different work surface: e.g. large safe felt shapes instead of blocks, a tactile mark board instead of crayon. Re-request all age-range amounts across manageable sittings, with zero and PK4 ten actually included. Record actual date, independent initiated action, final produced count/representation and help. Until real session, NOT_OBSERVED. Do not invent retention from same-day practice or from the written plan.

Error-specific instruction:

  • Child names numeral without creating anything: “You named a number. Now show me that many with objects or marks that you put here.” If unavailable, offer an alternate accessible child-controlled representation.
  • Adult physically places objects while child watches: This is a teacher demonstration. Clear the mat and give child a new amount, using a child-directed motor proxy only if child commands each placement rather than adult deciding amount/positions.
  • One too many or too few: “You wanted five. Let's check what you made. You can change your group.” Count together on a practice example, then offer another fresh amount with no numeric hint for independent evidence. Helped corrections never replace first independent response.
  • Zero confused with abandonment: “The amount zero means no pieces. Can you choose to show zero and tell me how the blank mat shows that?” If child declines or cannot indicate deliberate zero, mark not observed, do not guess.
  • Child can reproduce exact adult arrangement but not new arrangement: Allow creative placement of objects and change spatial layout/material, not a dotted template. A cloned shape may be imitation only.
  • Child keeps adding after saying finished: Ask what finished means, invite child to settle on final group, record initial and revised states separately; don't edit the original log to pretend an unprompted first success.
  • PK4 10 shown via 1+0 written symbols: Ask for ten child-placed items or marks, not a printed 10 sign; recognition of the numeral is the different V.A.5 objective.
  • Child counts aloud while making: This is allowed; production of set itself matters. It does not imply all counting/cardinality standards met unless separately observed with their own conditions.

C. Full ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD script

0–3 min — entry without model: Put out a blank safe work mat, 12 large nonswallowable counters (PK3 may need up to five, PK4 up to ten), extra pieces out of reach if distraction matters. “You get to make a group. I'll say how many, and you make the group your way. Say stop when you're done.” Say 2 and later 4 (PK3), or 3 and 7 (PK4). Record first result. Adult can repeat task wording without pointing to correct count. Child can move to another format if access fails.

3–7 min — direct demonstration: On a different adult-only mat, adult makes a three-object group; points out that adult made the representation, so this is example, not child test. Rearrange same three objects to show amount not dependent on picture shape. Demonstrate zero with explicitly intended empty adult mat. With PK4 show ten large objects briefly and how to verify they total ten, then remove before child tries. “Now you get to decide how your own group looks.”

7–15 min — responsive independent construction with practice feedback: Child chooses material (counters, broad marker dots, large stamps); ask for one amount at a time without displaying or constructing the answer. Child places/draws as many as desired and signals completion. Let child revise voluntarily. To inspect, ask “How did you know you had enough?” This explanation is valuable but is not a handwriting/language requirement. If incorrect, compare actual group to request, invite change and model on another mat if needed. Record first independent state and supported revision separately. Mix easy and challenging amounts, including intentional zero and ten if PK4. Encourage child-owned shape and placement, not copying.

15–21 min — new uncued independent production: Clear all practice. Present a truly blank exit surface and an unfamiliar, unmodeled quantity request; safely reuse the same accessible large object type if that best preserves the child's control. An optional changed safe object type can probe transfer, never a condition of independent representation. Ask each grade-specific target in scrambled order, including zero, without any printed correct answer, model, app auto-fill or adult placements. Across more sittings as needed. Adult privately checks produced marks/pieces after child announces completion, records actual final amount, support and child’s choice. If adult has to physically move pieces, they do so only at explicit child direction and tag access-only support. A child who simply points to a finished adult set has not independently made the amount.

21–22 min — closure and future revisit: Child picks one optional amount to make for a toy figure using their preferred material; this is creative practice. Log a true future-day revisit plan, not an achieved result.

CO-PLAY — adult and child each get genuine roles

Adult prepares a blank area and gives the child an unbuilt quantity request; child chooses safe material and acts as maker, adult serves as verifier only after child says finished, not the person placing the answer. Model uses adult's different sample group and explains making three versus intentionally leaving zero; adult then invites child to create an original amount without any copy. During, child may challenge adult with a requested amount, and adult must build it; this is meaningful reciprocal inquiry but is not child-source evidence. Return to child-as-maker using newly spoken amount to observe child-owned action. Exit is unseen requests on different clean mats; adult covers their own examples and keeps hands off child's quantity. Capture all in-grade amounts over short sessions, including intentional zero and PK4 ten, with separate first result and coached result. Later on a true different date switch material and repeat full source-range creation. Child may opt out.

ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD — complete guided creation

Execute the detailed minute-by-minute section C exactly; individual parent/tutor chooses pace and access material. Explicitly use blank space before each independent request, not a dot template. Record quantity spoken, child's raw final number of placed/drawn elements before checking, whether zero was intentionally signaled, and any adult-set corrections. Child's home language and child-controlled motor proxy count as access, not failed oral English. No number writing is required.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM — feasible whole-group logistics and independently attributable evidence

For an illustrative 20-child group, supply five group trays with 12 large safe counters each, one large adult-only modeling tray, twenty blank reusable wipe mats or blank sheet spaces, adult private group checklist (one child row × six/eleven amounts) and accessible marker/switch/raised counters. 0–3 min: teacher privately prompts two baseline construction requests; all children get blank mats, but teacher documents only individually observed outputs or collected named/anonymous-as-policy artifacts rather than attributing group response to each child. 3–7 min: whole group teacher models making three and deliberate zero, optionally ten in PK4, then clears model. 7–16 min: children choose their own placement and build amounts individually; partners can discuss while practicing, but do not pass peer-built sets off as individual learner work. Teacher rotates, asks actual children why they decided they were finished, gives misconception-specific feedback and records aided versus independent production. 16–24 min: novel independent request rounds with fresh blank mats/marker styles, each child produces own quantity and signals completion before others reveal answers; teacher collects individually attributable original marks or direct observation. PK4 eleven amounts can be distributed across different short sessions, and any inaccessible/unobserved amount is recorded as such. No arbitrary single-day group pass. Later REVISIT with new source-neutral physical medium and all required quantities, capture real student-specific evidence only under classroom policy; not a digital student database.

E. Assessment rubric, differentiation and accommodations

Per-target record: actual date and grade; exact spoken/signed/AAC quantity request; material (large counters, learner-authored marks, tactile shape/child-controlled digital pieces); first uncorrected child-produced item count, intentional zero confirmation; whether child independently controlled each piece or directed a motor proxy; a different adult/peer's interference; actual support and later attempt. Code I = independent correct original production and deliberate completion; E = independent but different produced amount; H = numerical help or adult-generated set; N = NOT_OBSERVED/inaccessible or refusal; A = neutral access-only support annotated separately. A request word repeated in a home language or using AAC is not an answer cue. Record child's self-corrections separately so first observations aren't rewritten. For a full-range candidate PK3 requires individually examined values 0–5; PK4 0–10. These internal observations are not a Texas mandated mastery threshold, professional developmental evaluation, automatically durable learning evidence, or solo equivalence proof.

Counterfactual failure tests: Correctly matching five existing dots to a numeral card = not self-created quantity. Singing to ten = no created quantity. Choosing an already-completed six-set from three options = receptive matching, not child-owned representation. Child watches adult place five objects then says five = adult-produced. Child traces a provided five-dot outline precisely = copy skill, independent production uncertain. Child leaves mat blank without responding after a zero prompt = ambiguous; zero must be intentionally shown. Child creates ten on a familiar arrangement but fails another material at later date = partial transfer. Child independently constructs three while adult count-word sequence was dictated for each piece = helped, not fully independent.

Differentiation: Use one or two accessible amounts at first, but never silently remove unattempted in-grade numbers from claimed source range. Encourage varied shapes, counters, large child-generated dots, intentional empty mat for zero; child who knows PK3 band may try PK4 amount naturally without acceleration demand. Children who can count but not construct: simplify motor action with safe large pieces and observe actual child-directed placement. For a child who can create an amount but cannot explain orally, permit pointing, AAC, sign or demonstrating; no speech-only gate.

Accommodations: Review relevant IEP, IFSP and Section 504 plans with appropriate educator/family; child-directed adult motor proxy, flexible manipulation and no required precision drawing; low-vision high-contrast/tactile large stable shapes; seating and sensory break options; home-language quantity words; limited noise and short sessions; no timed demands, inaccessible teeny beads or impossible handwriting. Do not diagnose disabilities, demand movement, force verbal explanation or assume a lack of speech is a lack of understanding. Print in accessible size; validate safe material selection for actual age and individual needs. In SOLO an accessible switch may author one object at a time, not auto-fill an adult-prescribed final amount.
