Original authored method's concrete learner actions and feedback model

ENTRY E-MARK (3 min): Give completely blank original maker space and at least two safe choices of marking tools. PK3 request one and four marks; PK4 request two and seven. “Can you show that amount with marks you make, any shapes you choose? Let me know when you have made the amount.” Child can use home-language quantity request or AAC. Record first independently authored quantity and whether adult started or supplied each mark. Do not require an explanation before evaluating visible production. Only two entry amounts have been probed, not whole grade range.

MODEL M-MARK (4 min): On an adult-only sheet, make three large marks as unlike dots/dashes; say “I made three of these. My shapes don't have to line up.” On a second adult-only sheet make three different marks, discuss how each is still three. For zero, intentionally create no marks and explicitly explain, “I chose zero, so I made no marks; my sheet is empty on purpose.” With PK4, show ten adult-made large marks arranged in two rows for counting safety only, without needing the child to copy the exact design. Adult's created example cannot be child-source evidence.

DURING D-MARK (9 min): Let child invent an easily repeatable mark (fat dot, slash, loop, press), asking them to make an amount requested aloud. Child decides whether marks are spread out, circled, or clustered, and can choose to switch mark styles while keeping quantity. For a second demonstration, invite “Can you show the same amount a different way without looking back at your first picture?” If child wants to count to verify, welcome it after they declare finished; recording is of first self-created end state. Offer practice requests through all 0–5 (PK3) or 0–10 (PK4) over manageable opportunities. When child begins to copy a previous row, introduce a new blank section and change arrangement/material; a copied row is practice, not unprompted generalization. Adult may guide motor movement only on child's specific direction, not decide number of marks.

Specific response to errors: Too many marks: “You were asked for four. Let's look at what you made and think about how to change it.” Child chooses whether to cross out an extra mark, clear and restart, or use a different tool. MODEL — adult demonstrates on different real owned source

Adult on different adult-owned paper draws a distinct number of broad tally-like marks for adult story, not child's quantity or symbol style.

Never model the exact learner EXIT example or pre-answer with a pointing finger/answer cue. The adult's true demonstration is a teaching event and carries ZERO child independent evidence.

DURING — actual child activity, contingent instructional repair and faded hints

Allow child to explain that each self-authored mark stands for a counted item, verify true representation not decorative total ambiguity, neutral proxy may draw exact child-chosen marks, PK3 0–5 PK4 0–10.

Track U (original learner decision), H (teacher supplied answer/semantic next move), A (neutral learner-directed motor/communication/sensory accessibility), X (unknown). Respond to partial attempts with source-specific feedback and a completely unrelated adult-owned misconception example, then let learner practice without blame. Fade H cues before new EXIT, retain essential disability and physical safety A access.

EXIT — genuinely different independent source action

A truly fresh blank quantity-unit area and an unmodeled age-appropriate amount; the child may reuse the same safe accessible marking tool while originating every distinct requested unit. For zero, the quantity-unit area intentionally contains no units; a child-originated sign, AAC/home-language confirmation or personal separately identified symbol for zero may communicate intention without being counted as one of the quantity marks. Ask neutrally if any mark in the unit area is ambiguous; do not miscredit silence or one unit-mark as zero. No preprinted dots, copied teacher picture or adult-generated amount.

The test is NOT the same adult modeled material, prerecorded answer, outline tracing or identical stimulus shuffled. Record child-owned action independently against each exact printed source ID; an observed age-specific action is a source candidate, not verified developmental mastery.
