Teach Showing Numbers With Your Own Marks

Practical teaching guide. The child must make the quantity—not copy a finished example or trace a grown-up's answer.

What the child learns

Children intentionally create a chosen number of visible marks, using dots, lines, loops, stamps or another accessible repeatable mark. The arrangement and artwork can be their own. A deliberately blank sheet can represent zero. This is quantity-making, not a handwriting test or merely recognizing printed numerals.

Prepare and choose a first amount

Gather a blank spacious sheet or erasable board, large safe crayons/stamps or accessible switches and an entirely separate adult-only example sheet. Never pre-fill the child's marking area. Speech, home language, sign and communication aids can convey the requested amount; physical assistance may support a child-directed mark without choosing its number.

A 3-year-old learning amounts can start with zero, one and two, then three through five. A 4-year-old can revisit smaller values before six to eight, then nine and ten. The full zero-to-five or zero-to-ten later review is not a first-session demand.

Five moves

1. Invite a few marks

Say: “Can you make [a small number] marks, any shapes you like? Tell me when you're done.”

Watch the child's first self-made set before you count or demonstrate. For zero, ask whether they intentionally chose to make no marks.

2. Show a different adult example

On your own sheet, make three visible separate marks and show three made with a different kind of stroke.

Say: “These look different, but I made three marks in each group.” Leave another separate adult sheet intentionally blank: “This is how I made zero.”

Your marks are not the child's learning evidence.

3. Let the child invent a way

Clear the child's work area and offer new suitable requests.

Say: “How would you make [amount] marks your way?”

Let the child choose shapes, spacing and tool. If they overshoot, note the first self-made amount, offer supportive counting afterward, then try a different request without tracing the correct answer for them.

4. Try an unmodeled new amount

Hide earlier example and practice sheets. On a truly blank work area, request another amount with no printed model.

Observe and quietly check how many individual marks the child created, whether zero was intentional, and whether the adult supplied any decisive marks. The complete later assessment covers all age-range values in mixed order across manageable sittings—not one repeated pattern.

5. Revisit with another safe marking surface

On a real later date, offer a new sequence of suitable amounts. Record only real child-created marks and supports.

Respond to what happens

Observation Response
Child copies your exact row instead of creating their own Use a new blank page and genuinely different amount
Child makes several overlapping strokes hard to distinguish Invite neutral clarification without showing target quantity
Child makes no marks and says they chose zero Record intentional empty representation, not nonparticipation

Teach Showing Numbers With Your Own Marks with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

Children intentionally create a chosen number of visible marks, using dots, lines, loops, stamps or another accessible repeatable mark. The arrangement and artwork can be their own. A deliberately blank sheet can represent zero. This is quantity-making, not a handwriting test or merely recognizing printed numerals.

Prepare the classroom

Prepare blank individual mark-making surfaces, broad safe crayons/stamps or tactile alternatives, and a visibly separate adult demonstration sheet. Younger learners gradually work with zero through five; older learners eventually through ten. Don't display a completed answer board next to private practice.

Introduce the idea together

Ask, “How could you make marks that show this many?” Offer a first child-owned attempt. On your own separate sheet, model three different marks all representing the same amount, including that zero means making no quantity marks.

Give children meaningful practice

Children run a mark-making workshop at their own tables, experimenting with dots, lines, broad stamps or accessible marks. They may discuss their representations during supported practice. The educator checks a manageable set of individual original creations and decisions rather than scoring a class mural as twenty independent results.

Observe individual learning fairly

For an independent check, hand out fresh blank surfaces and personally appropriate unfamiliar amounts. See whether each selected child originates the right number of marks; an accurate tracing of a teacher-drawn model is not independent quantity representation. Record the actual amount tested and any help, leaving untaught later-range amounts unassessed.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

Accept varied size, arrangement and mark style; accommodate alternate motor/communication routes while preserving child-origin quantity decisions. If control of a marker is the barrier, use accessible stamping or child-directed placement instead of treating motor access as math failure. Return with different materials on a genuinely later occasion.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

02 — Mark Makers: design a quantity with your own symbols

Required developmental teaching sequence: PK3 foundation → PK4 extension. PK3: child intentionally creates 0–2, then 3–5, then mixed 0–5; PK4: retrieve 0–5, then child-creates 6–8, then 9–10, then mixed 0–10. Do not replace authored quantity with numeral reading, matching or adult-produced sets. The existing full-grade EXIT is a later source-range check, not the first teaching session. Apply this ordered sequence in SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes; SOLO remains a written specification only.

MATH-PK-REP-02 · October 30, 2026 · Original E2.2 universe-neutral written lesson. SOLO NOT IMPLEMENTED. This method is not simply replacing plastic counters with pen strokes in Lesson 01. A child invents a readable-for-the-child representation of an amount, experiments with how its format can change without changing amount, and can independently produce a new quantity using self-authored marks when no object model is present. Every source amount must still be constructed through an actual child-owned action, not traced, copied, or matched.

False equivalences: A child tracing five adult dots has reproduced a pattern rather than authored a quantity. A child copying the numeral “5” onto a poster may show numeral writing, not producing five quantity marks. A child counting pre-existing stickers gives a correct answer but does not make a representation. A self-authored group of five very irregular marks, however, does show quantity production even if their penmanship is not neat.

B. Clear instructional progression

ENTRY E-MARK (3 min): Give completely blank original maker space and at least two safe choices of marking tools. PK3 request one and four marks; PK4 request two and seven. “Can you show that amount with marks you make, any shapes you choose? Let me know when you have made the amount.” Child can use home-language quantity request or AAC. Record first independently authored quantity and whether adult started or supplied each mark. Do not require an explanation before evaluating visible production. Only two entry amounts have been probed, not whole grade range.

MODEL M-MARK (4 min): On an adult-only sheet, make three large marks as unlike dots/dashes; say “I made three of these. My shapes don't have to line up.” On a second adult-only sheet make three different marks, discuss how each is still three. For zero, intentionally create no marks and explicitly explain, “I chose zero, so I made no marks; my sheet is empty on purpose.” With PK4, show ten adult-made large marks arranged in two rows for counting safety only, without needing the child to copy the exact design. Adult's created example cannot be child-source evidence.

DURING D-MARK (9 min): Let child invent an easily repeatable mark (fat dot, slash, loop, press), asking them to make an amount requested aloud. Child decides whether marks are spread out, circled, or clustered, and can choose to switch mark styles while keeping quantity. For a second demonstration, invite “Can you show the same amount a different way without looking back at your first picture?” If child wants to count to verify, welcome it after they declare finished; recording is of first self-created end state. Offer practice requests through all 0–5 (PK3) or 0–10 (PK4) over manageable opportunities. When child begins to copy a previous row, introduce a new blank section and change arrangement/material; a copied row is practice, not unprompted generalization. Adult may guide motor movement only on child's specific direction, not decide number of marks.

Specific response to errors: Too many marks: “You were asked for four. Let's look at what you made and think about how to change it.” Child chooses whether to cross out an extra mark, clear and restart, or use a different tool. Adult-generated answer after counting is HELPED. Too few: count together on the practice mat, then give a fresh prompt for independent follow-up. Merged marks that are visually ambiguous: ask child to indicate the separate intended units or space them with their own choice; do not impose a handwriting quality gate. Zero confused with refusing: child must intentionally communicate that empty quantity-unit area represents zero; unresponsive silence remains NOT_OBSERVED. A sign, AAC selection, home-language word or child-authored separately identified notation may communicate the zero intention, but must not be counted as a positive quantity mark in the unit area. If a child writes one symbol on the area, ask what it means without supplying the number; a clearly labeled zero symbol in a separate response area is valid communication, while a single unit daub means one and an ambiguous mark means unknown until clarified. Child writes a printed “5” once: celebrate symbol knowledge but request five child-authored marks, because the target of this method is quantity representation, not V.A.5 printed numeral recognition.

EXIT X-MARK (5–7 min, split across sessions where needed): Completely remove adult samples and copied practice pictures. Present a genuinely fresh blank quantity-mark area and a safe child-chosen tool; the same accessible familiar stamp or adapted grip may be reused provided that no template, model count or finished quantity appears. A different mark material can be an optional transfer opportunity, not a prerequisite for independent child-origin output. Ask all required in-grade amounts in unannounced, nonascending order: PK3 5,0,2,4,1,3; PK4 7,0,9,4,10,1,6,2,8,3,5. The adult says only “Please make [amount] marks on this blank space and signal when you are finished.” Child initiates and produces marks, then adult counts/separates/checks quietly. Record raw first end state, how many units child says they made if communicated, who physically made marks, and any access/support; if a single mark is unclear, ask a neutral clarification without announcing desired final number. Correct result after a teacher's numerically guided correction does not count as independent. All six/eleven identities should be observed for whole-range candidate; do not invent absent rows.

REVISIT R-MARK (later real date): Fresh safe tactile stamping surface or erasable board, no modeled shapes in sight, different target order; invite child to make each in-grade amount independently in a chosen marking style. Record actual date and first completed artifact, support, intentional zero and format. Never backfill later evidence from the original materials.

C. Fully scripted ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD route

0–3: Hide adult examples, place plain blank practice page and two usable big mark tools. “Let's make marks that show numbers. You can choose the shape.” Ask unseen E-MARK amounts before the child watches any example. Log initial amount and child motor authorship; no demands for tidy handwriting.

3–7: Adult demonstrates on a different plain sheet three marks as dots and three as dashes, saying these are distinct pictures of the same amount. Demonstrate deliberate zero as intentionally empty paper. Let child choose whether to draw or stamp, ask how they might show three in their own way as practice, not an exit. For PK4 show ten as ten marks briefly on adult sheet, not a preprint to trace.

7–16: Child designs a personal mark and generates different quantities. Adult asks one at a time, offering an alternate tool if strokes are hard. Encourage decisions about spatial layout; ask “Did you want three marks or more? Would you change anything?” only after first end state is recorded. Use actual count checks to give constructive feedback on a separate supported turn, not to silently improve source credit. The child can explain using pointing, sign, AAC or direct demonstration.

16–22: Put practice out of view. New blank exit sheet, child-selected safe tool (including the same familiar accessible one), unknown target order and no adult-supplied marks. Ask each 0–5 or 0–10 identity in multiple short blocks if necessary. The child must author requested amount and intentionally indicate zero rather than remain inactive. Record independently made quantity per target, raw first output and helped correction separately; keep any unfinished amount NOT_OBSERVED.

22–23: Child chooses a mark to decorate a non-assessed pretend page and discusses where marks might help people remember amounts. Schedule real later revisit; no “all mastered” praise on incomplete evidence.

D. Four substantive instructional modes

SOLO — independent accessible mark canvas production spec; NOT IMPLEMENTED

ENTRY: Future tablet independently narrates a number word, not a visible printed target numeral or prefilled array, with replay/nonreading buttons, pause and not-sure, AAC or switch operation. Show a blank canvas; child stamps or drags each original mark individually and chooses “done” when amount is ready. Child can choose shapes independently; app records first actual number of self-authored marks before any feedback. MODEL: Original narrated example makes three large strokes of different kinds and explicitly leaves a blank canvas intentionally for zero; model is in a separate screen/asset, unavailable as copied template in exit. DURING: Child switches marking tools and creates same amount in a new form if they want; only on a practice turn does system optionally count completed marks aloud and explain correction. Do not auto-fill number of requested marks or use a “make five” button. EXIT: Fresh blank canvas and alternate mark tool, independent spoken requests covering all 0–5/0–10, first completed mark count, authorship, intentional zero via child “I made zero” confirmation, any assistance and skipped identities logged separately. REVISIT: Fresh later actual session with no unauthorized personally identifiable records. Implement screen-reader/switch mode carefully: do not narrate the desired amount per individual object during production; it can repeat request, not supply piece-count answer. No program, audio, privacy design or validated prereader solo functioning exists.

CO-PLAY — choose the mark language, compare representations

Adult and child each invent their own mark (adult does not require imitation). ENTRY child creates original amount on blank page before adult demonstrates. MODEL adult creates three dots and three dashes, emphasizing equality of represented quantity while shapes differ. DURING child requests that adult make a quantity using adult's mark and then child makes it in their own mark system from the spoken amount, not a copied visual pattern; reverse who requests after several turns so both are meaningful participants. Adult verifies only after child completes; practice peer feedback welcomed but tagged H when it gives numerical solution. EXIT: adult puts away all co-made signs and asks full grade-range newly scrambled amounts on clean material, child authoring own groups with first result captured independently. REVISIT: real different day, alternative safe medium, same source range without copying. Talking and comparing patterns makes engagement richer but is not by itself source-production evidence.

ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD — explicit mark-invention sequence

Follow section C with motor-access adaptations. Count marks independently only after the child's end-state to avoid adult-coached final mark. Observe child-generated completed quantities rather than tracing neat numerals. Record each source amount and especially zero/ten explicitly. Don't demand English naming or fine-motor precision; child-directed motor proxy acceptable when child chooses every mark quantity and location. Separate amount errors from unreadable very small pencil marks.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM — mark makers workshop

For ~20 children prepare 20 blank reusable wipe-mats or original print pages, several trays of broad crayons/safe large stamps, one teacher-only demonstration sheet, simple blank novel exit sheets and 20 × six/eleven private observation columns. 0–3: kids independently make one small entry amount at desk; teacher can collect private first picture, never infer independence from a shared group picture. 3–7: large teacher modeling contrasts three dots/three strokes, intentional zero and ten (PK4) without presenting exit patterns. 7–16: children choose stamps, paint daubs or markers and make requested quantities, then show a same-amount different-style example; teacher moves among children, observes child authorship and response to guidance, keeps first attempt separate from helped revision. 16–24: use fresh blank sheets and unseen quantities; each child must actually author their own set of marks, collected with individual identification according to school policy, not 20 copying a correct teacher poster. Different PK4 quantities may be assessed in subsequent small-group blocks due attention; log unobserved quantities rather than assuming full-range. True later REVISIT: another material on a different date, with same six/eleven target identities individually checked. Protect child privacy and respect opt-outs.

Codes I independent correct child-created quantity, E child-created different count, H correct after numeric/adult placement help or copying, N unobserved/ambiguous/access unavailable, A access-only help separate from understanding. Only independently observed 0–5 or 0–10 source identities can be marked as full-range candidate, never durable mastery, psychometric validation, teacher acceptance or V.A.2/.3/.5 source credit automatically.

Needs-based differentiation: If child cannot hold a marker, switch to large safe stamps or individually child-commanded motor-proxy placements. If visually crowded, make larger separated marks and fewer competing materials. If sequencing or response vocabulary is emerging, simplify practice amount without lowering official range for an unsupported final claim. Home-language quantity words, sign and AAC count as valid requests. For children with higher readiness, invite creative nonuniform mark systems and unfamiliar material transfer, not a formal algebra or decomposition standard claim. Allow short sessions, seating/breaks, and safe nonswallowable tools. Consult relevant IEP, IFSP, Section 504 and family/educator supports for actual pupils; do not diagnose disability. Mark stroke perfection, color choices, pace and oral explanation are not graded.
