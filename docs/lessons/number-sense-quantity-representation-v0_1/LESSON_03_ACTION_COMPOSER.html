03 — Quantity Composer: make an amount across actions in time

Required developmental teaching sequence: PK3 foundation → PK4 extension. PK3: child intentionally creates 0–2, then 3–5, then mixed 0–5; PK4: retrieve 0–5, then child-creates 6–8, then 9–10, then mixed 0–10. Do not replace authored quantity with numeral reading, matching or adult-produced sets. The existing full-grade EXIT is a later source-range check, not the first teaching session. Apply this ordered sequence in SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes; SOLO remains a written specification only.

MATH-PK-REP-03 · October 30, 2026 · Original standalone four-context E2.2 written lesson. Unlike constructing a persistent set (Method 01) or drawing spatial marks (Method 02), this method asks the child to author a quantity as a self-started, self-stopped series of deliberate actions—for example safe table taps, hand squeezes, gentle instrument strikes, or accessible switch presses. The central challenge is that actions disappear over time: the child must decide when to stop at the requested quantity without simply echoing a teacher rhythm. SOLO is NOT IMPLEMENTED; no wearable, camera, audio recognition, or exercise telemetry is presumed.

Important interpretation of zero: Deliberately choosing no action when requested zero and clearly confirming “I did zero; I'm done” or communicating via AAC is a zero representation. A child silent/still due to refusal, lack of access or confusion is not presumed to have represented zero. Protect the right to decline.

B. Explicit authentic sequence and misconception responses

ENTRY E-COMP (2–3 minutes): Before teacher demonstrates a target action sequence, offer child two comfortable ways to respond: gentle tabletop tap or large hand-squeeze/switch activation. Request verbally/signed/AAC an amount, e.g. PK3 two then four, PK4 three then seven. “Make that many taps [or squeezes] and you choose when you're finished.” Adult stays quiet and does not clap an answer or count the taps aloud in sync. Observe actual independently initiated actions and stops. Write down first number of taps, access and assistance. Entry of two numbers is only baseline, not full source coverage.

MODEL M-COMP (4 minutes): With an adult-only instrument adult explicitly states “I'll make three gentle taps. Listen: tap—tap—tap. I decided when to stop. Those were three separate actions.” Show an alternative three gentle squeezes so child understands action choice does not change quantity. For zero adult intentionally does no tap, then says, “I chose zero actions, so I stayed quiet on purpose.” With PK4 also demonstrate ten manageable quiet finger taps as a model only—child need not copy or maintain a beat. Explain that loudness, rhythm style, length and speed are not the quantity; count of distinct events is.

DURING D-COMP (8–10 minutes): Child chooses their own quiet action. Adult requests quantities (PK3 across 0–5; PK4 0–10, perhaps spread over sessions) and gives a neutral “start when ready.” Child initiates actions at any comfortable pace and stops voluntarily; teacher can count silently for observation but should not give audible cues or pulse a matching beat. After child declares done ask “Would you make the same amount using a different action?” If child chooses, they can translate three taps into three squeezes—this is practice transfer, not automatically a decomposing-number claim. On an incorrect first amount, teacher after recording says “Let's check together. I counted four separate sounds, but the request was three. How could you change a new try?” On a fresh supported trial teacher may count with child, but that is H aided; restore independence using another different request.

Misconception-specific repair: Child copies adult’s rhythm exactly → adult asks different quantity and lets child initiate without the adult tapping; imitation is not independent representation. Child repeatedly taps continuously without a clear event boundary → agree on one gentle action as one unit, slow if child prefers, record ambiguity; do not punish movement difficulty. Child taps five when asked four → reflect actual five in practice and allow child-directed correction on a separate new task. Child makes no action when requested zero but cannot indicate intentionality → offer a “finished / zero” accessible option; classify ambiguous first response N. Child makes ten by pressing an app shortcut that auto-plays ten sounds → software, not child, authored ten; give individually controlled presses. Child cannot reliably clap/hear → choose tactile soft squeezes/switch activation with explicit response confirmation, not an audio/hearing test. Child counts aloud while tapping each time → source may still be representation because they produce actions, but no automatic V.A.1/.2/.3 inference.

EXIT X-COMP (5–7 minutes or across sittings): Remove all adult examples and any replayable sequence. Present a new unmodeled quantity request and fresh independent sequence; the child may use the same familiar, safe accessible action or switch as in practice, with no answer-bearing adult pace. Changing action type or station is an optional transfer extension, not a condition for child-origin quantity evidence. Randomly request independent amounts PK3 5,0,3,1,4,2 or PK4 8,0,6,3,10,2,7,1,9,4,5. “Make [amount] separate actions. You decide when you've done it.” No printed numeral answer, preplayed tapping, adult-paced demonstration, synchronized count aloud, vibration for each correct step, or auto-filled action track. An adult private observer logs actual count of the child-authored first sequence, if separable, and child's intentional stop; for zero require explicit “I chose none” signal. Correct helped retry doesn't overwrite original attempt. When only a few amounts fit child's attention, defer the rest honestly as NOT_OBSERVED.

REVISIT R-COMP (real later date): A genuinely new independent requested series with fresh order and no demonstrated answer; keep the same reliably accessible quiet action if needed, or voluntarily explore a different safe action (e.g., gentle switch press versus finger tap) to probe modality transfer, for all 0–5 or 0–10. Document actual date, support, whether events distinct, first child-made quantity and confirmed zero. Not a same-day pretend return.

C. Full ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD guide (parent/tutor/homeschool)

0–3: Child chooses tabletop tapping or gentle large soft-object squeezes without any physical exertion requirement. “I’ll tell you an amount; you make that many actions when ready. You get to stop.” Adult privately records first response to two, four (PK3) or three, seven (PK4), before teaching. No ticking metronome, teacher beat, repeated answer pulses or automatic app count display.

3–7: On a separate model surface demonstrate exactly three soft taps with verbal number after the action; same amount as three gentle squeezes. Demonstrate explicit zero by silence with intention communicated, not merely doing nothing. For PK4 model ten safe light taps but don't force any child to perform ten jumps. Discuss “how many actions” versus “how loud/fast,” and accept child’s selection of action method.

7–16: Adult requests novel quantities in a mixed order; child controls tempo, count and ending. If child wants to stop after a hard turn, respect it. After child signals done, count actual distinct events from adult log and, only then, offer useful check. If number differs, use a second practice request or small new amount with explicit guided one-to-one marking, logged H. Invite another independent request later without the adult’s rhythm. Child may replace tapping with pointing to an accessible big pad or pressing a switch. Invite child to ask adult to do a quantity; adult produces it and child checks, but adult performance does not count as the child's source production.

16–22: Clear adult exemplars and give a new unmodeled quantity request, letting the child retain the same comfortable independent safe action if desired; switching actions is optional transfer. Ask every source amount in a shuffled sequence over short sessions; zero gets a deliberate “my series is empty” confirmation. Teacher privately records actual number of unique actions before giving any correctness feedback, and notes answer if the action boundaries were ambiguous. If adult had to guide one switch press each time, separate child decision from adult numeric pacing; do not score as independent. Child may choose a break and record N for missing amounts.

22–23: Close with child’s free rhythmic/nonrhythmic play not attached to a score; agree to new-day transfer check in another modality. Never call a planned observation completed.

D. Four fully written modes with equivalent instructional goal but not proven parity

SOLO — child-authored quiet-action tablet spec, NOT IMPLEMENTED

ENTRY: Future original app uses independent narrated spoken amount requests with nonreading, replay, pause/not-sure, home-language/AAC/switch access and a blank single big action pad. CO-PLAY — take turns as conductor and maker, with independent child exit

At entry the child picks action method and performs a requested quantity without adult making matching sounds. At model the adult demonstrates three (and zero) and invites child to explain if they want. During child acts as conductor and requests a number of adult taps; adult plays and child can check, then child acts as maker for a different adult-requested amount; both roles are meaningful. Make sure the adult does not give a rhythmic answer demonstration just before child performs it. Discussion/rehearsal may be aided practice, recorded separately. At exit adult uses a fresh unknown quantity request with no demonstrated answer; the same accessible pad/instrument may be reused, and a different action is optional transfer. The child controls each action and stopping; the adult keeps quiet and records first independent results. On a later actual date the child chooses a safe accessible action (the same type is permitted) and responds to new requests; changed modality may be explored when appropriate. Child can decline and never be forced to move or perform for peers.

ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD — full temporal series

Follow the script in section C; choose soft quiet motion/press or one accessible switch event per child initiation, never infer disability from coordination speed or sound volume. Adult response cues are neutral instruction to start and child controls completion. Adult logs first actual produced amount before helped revisions and intentional zero. All 0–5 or 0–10 results required for a complete-range candidate, which is still not educator or child-mastery validation.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM — individualized observation in a shared quiet room

For illustrative 20 children arrange five groups with a set of large soft quiet pads per group, a teacher-only demonstration station, neutral original action tally sheets for adults, accessible switches, and one row per learner × six/eleven amount columns. 0–3 entry: teacher privately watches selected students make small independent series before model; 20 children simultaneously tapping cannot provide individually attributable counts if teacher did not observe. 3–7 model three soft taps, deliberate zero, and optionally ten, without requiring exercise or rhythm expertise. 7–16 learning stations let children choose tap, squeeze or pad press; adult/peer can be a quiet listener/verifier after first action series and offer practice feedback, but group echo is not independent evidence. 16–24 teacher rotates private individual trials with a new quiet surface and new amounts; other children undertake unassessed choice play without counting each other's answers aloud. Teacher logs only actually observed unique events per child, true first answer, cue and access; for PK4 eleven values may need later small-group sessions. A twenty-person synchronous sound does not prove twenty individual source actions. Later REVISIT: different real date and alternate accessible mode, genuinely private child-authored series, recording all grade values/unknowns and applicable permissions.

E. Evidence scoring, fairness, accommodations and no-credit rules

Per learner/target: date, requested amount (spoken/home-language/sign/AAC), child-selected action modality, actual number of distinguishable child-originated events in the first attempt, whether child independently started/stopped, whether “no actions” was intentionally communicated, any numeric beat/gesture/physical coaching, ambiguous boundaries, access-only proxy, and delayed actual result. Codes I = correct independent produced quantity, E = different unprompted produced count, H = assisted pace/content answer or adult-originated events, N = not observed/ambiguous/no access, A = neutral motor/communication access. Don't equate approximate tempo, loudness, athletic effort or speech with source correctness. Candidate full grade-range only when all 0–5 / 0–10 independently observed; no official mastery cutoff or validated educator verdict follows.

Differentiation: Start with one/two/three gentle easy actions; gradually extend amounts without forcing an eleven-action marathon. Choose optional sitting, no jump or mandatory clapping, low sound/stimulus level, child-selected soft press/squeeze, tactile cues for learners who don't hear sound, home-language spoken quantity requests, child-directed switch controls. For motor disability, a neutral motor proxy can act only on discrete child commands and should not introduce their own counting rhythm; if child can neither initiate nor intentionally direct independent events, mark output dimension genuinely unobserved rather than making up a pass. Review relevant IEP, IFSP, Section 504 and clinical/educational accommodations with family/professionals before real use. Do not diagnose a child or compel repetitive movement that causes fatigue/pain. Child may stop for safety.

Also don't claim PK3/PK4.V.A.1/.2/.3 (rote, one-to-one, cardinality), V.A.5 (printed numeral) or arts/rhythm source credit without their independent requirements.
