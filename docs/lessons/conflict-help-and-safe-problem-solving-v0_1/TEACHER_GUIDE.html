Seven access profiles and four WRITTEN routes

Adult safeguarding is always required. Calm preparation needs no peer. Authentic PK3 evidence requires a real conflict with another child; PK4 evidence requires a real conflict with others, not necessarily a peer. For the relevant context: attention/executive offer brief safe choices and time, language use individual sign/AAC help messages, literacy no written “conflict rules” prerequisite, numeracy no turn count quota, sensory/autism no touch/gaze/overcrowding, developmental adult neutral coaching without choosing solution, and physical/motor/hearing/vision real accessible peer statements and child-directed proxy only. A fictional/puppet peer may teach but cannot replace real child conflicts for PK3.

November 13 applied clarification: source action, teaching and transfer

A genuine child-owned help request or safe strategy during a familiar, naturally occurring real conflict can demonstrate the relevant source action. A changed context is an optional transfer observation, not a prerequisite for source-action credit. "Unmodeled" means the adult did not supply the child's decision in that event; it does not prohibit a familiar learned phrase, a previously taught strategy or a stable communication aid. Record immediate answer prompts separately from access support. Later calendar-day evidence supports a separate revisit claim; it is never fabricated to complete this lesson.

The staged lists are flexible teaching examples, not mandatory developmental ranks or a diagnostic ladder. PK3 help-seeking does not require self-resolution. PK4 may initiate a request for necessary adult help as a meaningful problem-solving strategy; do not demand a failed solo attempt first. An unavailable or unresponsive adult is an adult/context failure, not proof the child failed to seek help. Remedy adult access immediately. If the adult must intervene before any child action, record support provided and source action not observed, without a negative child judgment.

One adult can teach this lesson: narrate the original fictional paper story aloud, identify it as pretend, and think aloud: "I could ask a trusted adult for help. The adult listens and helps keep everyone safe." There is no need for a second adult, conflict actor or real peer during calm preparation. Children may rehearse an actual help signal for an ordinary harmless need and receive a real response; this establishes a usable communication route, not real-conflict evidence. Stop rehearsal on refusal, fatigue or unease. A child may choose another trusted adult; never insist that trust is owed to the educator. Follow safeguarding procedures if a child reports feeling unsafe with an adult, and do not promise secrecy.
