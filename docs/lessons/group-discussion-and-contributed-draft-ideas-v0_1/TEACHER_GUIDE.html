IV.B.1 shared-writing progression: optional teaching choices

PK3.IV.B.1: Child discusses and contributes ideas for drafts composed in whole/small group writing activities with adult prompting. PK4.IV.B.1: Child discusses and contributes ideas for drafts composed in whole/small group writing activities.

Child behaviors explicitly include brainstorming, sharing the pen, and contributing relevant ideas. Draft inclusion of every child's idea, novelty, promptlessness and exact adult-language transcription are not source gates.

These choices are NOT mandatory stages, a diagnostic scale or additional source requirements. Start with what the child can access and wants to communicate. A child can move between routes, use an easier entry or remain with a successful familiar activity.

PK3 G3-1: discuss a shared purpose

ENTRY: invite a real reason to write together; MODEL if useful by labeling your own idea; DURING invite an idea with neutral adult prompting and accessible choices. EXIT: note this child's discussion and contribution during the same draft, including support. REVISIT another day only if it happens, currently NOT_OBSERVED.

PK3 G3-2: expand an idea when useful

Offer a responsive question such as “What should readers know about that?” One meaningful idea can be enough. Do not mandate two ideas or longer speech. Keep child meaning while scribing/translation supports expression.

PK3 G3-3: contribute using chosen communication

Accept home language, sign, AAC, gesture, marks or supported choice. Verify intended meaning with the child rather than infer from a physical movement. Keep access; reduce answer-supplying prompts only when helpful.

PK3 G3-4: optional later purpose

Try a welcome message or play plan if welcome. New group/topic is optional transfer evidence, not a prerequisite. Spontaneous contributions remain valid; PK3's allowed prompting is not an obligation to interrupt spontaneous authorship.

PK4 G4-1: discuss ideas for a shared draft

Invite and respond to this child's meaning, not a chorus. Adult help may support discussion without becoming the child's claimed contribution.

PK4 G4-2: build on or choose among ideas

Explore how a child's suggestion relates to a peer's. Familiar or shared ideas are not automatically imitation. Child intention matters; no compulsory unique wording.

PK4 G4-3: clarify meaning for an audience

Read back the draft or communicate it accessibly. Child may clarify or add an idea. Accept language support and avoid making explanation length a proxy for writing contribution.

PK4 G4-4: consider the group

Explore alternatives respectfully. Discussed contributions remain evidence even when not adopted. Both small/whole group are source options, not mandatory separate tests.

PK4 G4-5: optional later connection

Return to this draft or another meaningful writing activity later. Record retention/transfer only when actually observed; no unfamiliar-topic gate.

Delivery and evidence boundaries

CO-PLAY and ADULT-LED: adult-child partners discuss and compose with real people. This does not establish peer-group transfer. CLASSROOM: one educator safely supervises a feasible group and notes a few individual contributions, not every child's outcome from the group poster. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: private preparation cannot replace actual human discussion. These are written designs, not verified software or observed mode equivalence.

Observe discussion and ideas for real shared writing, including brainstorming. An adult scribe or unselected proposal does not erase authorship. Teacher-only composition plus decoration does not establish contribution. Editing-only work belongs to IV.B.2; record mixed activities accurately. Missing opportunity, uncertain meaning, refusal and access barriers call for adaptation, not diagnosis. No learner evidence, later revisit, physical print or empirical effects are asserted.
