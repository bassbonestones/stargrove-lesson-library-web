Class Digital Mural · REVIEWED LESSON 01

Our class digital mural: add an idea that is yours

Preschool instructional-design materials · PK4

PK4.X.A.3
Written design reviewedNo classroom outcomes implied

Teach 1-on-1: add your idea to our class mural

Your child uses a real, approved drawing app to contribute an idea to a class picture. This is a preschool-four outcome, not a test every child must pass today. A favorite shape, chosen picture or familiar drawing is welcome. “Own idea” does not mean never seen before.

Get ready

Allow about 5–10 minutes flexibly. You need one approved device and familiar drawing app, a private class mural file, paper/art supplies as an optional alternative, and a private note for yourself. No purchase or printout is necessary. Test drawing, undo, saving and reopening a spare file first. Protect classmates' work with a copy, layer or separate contribution area you can combine later. Check permission to share in class and the child's willingness. No public posting, personal photos, voices or names in this activity.

This can be one child at a time joining a real class project over days. If there is no class project at home, enjoy making a digital picture as practice; don't label it a completed class contribution. The first child can begin the mural; record the class-product part as pending until other real contributions join it.

Check that the child can actually control a choice in this app: large tools, zoom, a switch or child-directed adult pointer may help. Accept their usual language, gesture, sign or communication aid. If the tool is inaccessible or they prefer no screen, offer ordinary art and leave digital observation for another time.

Invite, then follow

Show the class picture: “We are making this together. What would you like to add?” Let the child explore or make a mark without first explaining a plan. If needed, show a brush or undo in the same app on a spare area. “When I move, a line appears.” Then return control. You do not need another app, new theme or elaborate example.

Let the child draw, select a permitted picture, place a shape or direct an accessible edit. One meaningful stroke can be enough. You may help find a control, steady the device or act as their pointer when they direct what and where. Ask before helping. “Would you like help finding the brush?” is different from “Put a blue star here.” Record which kind of help happened.

If a mark lands unexpectedly, offer “Keep it, change it, or undo?” If they choose the same image as a friend, that's fine. Don't demand neat art or a spoken explanation. Choosing, approving, changing or placing an image can communicate meaning. If you cannot tell whether it was intentional, describe it without guessing.

Save, check and stop

You can save or combine contributions yourself. Show where the child's addition appears in the actual mural: “Is that how you want it?” Accept a gesture, correction or refusal. If saving fails, note the app action you saw and that the product still needs confirmation. Don't make the child perform again just for a record.

If a separate less-helped check would be comfortable, ask “Anything you want to keep or change?” Use the same app and mural. A new idea or program is not required. Stop when the child is satisfied, tired or wants to stop. Privately note their choice, app action, where it went, your help and any uncertain or missing part. A finished class picture is not proof that every child contributed. Supported action is worth recording; don't call it independent or mastery.

Come back another day

Reopen this same mural and invite a change or addition if meaningful. Record what really happens and the date. A planned return is not observed retention; a different product is optional transfer. No home device, purchase or additional homework is required.

Paper planning and teacher-only scanning can be valuable art work but do not by themselves show the child's digital app use. Child-directed selection/placement with motor help is different: the child's decisions control the actual app contribution. This is written teaching guidance, not evidence that children have already learned from it.

Teach a class: build a digital mural over several days

Children use an approved creation app to add their own ideas to a shared class picture. A familiar image, shape or favorite drawing is valid; creativity is not a competition for unique pictures. This preschool-four source has no preschool-three equivalent. Younger children's supported play is welcome without inventing a standard.

Prepare one safe station, not a line of waiting children

Use one teacher-adjacent approved device, a familiar drawing app, a private class mural and backup, and your private opportunity/observation note. Have ordinary paper/art materials and established familiar activities for the rest of the group. No new devices, purchases or required printouts. Test mark-making, undo, save and reopen before children arrive. Protect existing work with a layer, copy or separate contribution area to combine. Check actual accessibility, class-sharing permission and each child's willingness. Keep identifiers, photos and voices out of this mural; do not publish it.

Choose a settled art/center period when the whole group stays in your normal sight and hearing. If you cannot supervise while attending to the device, postpone the station. A second adult or peer supervisor is not assumed. Keep the device with you; don't set unattended rotations.

Launch the shared purpose

“Our class is making a picture together. You can add something you want to share.” Use a broad pretend theme or a class-chosen safe idea, never private family disclosures. Show actual classmates' voluntary contributions if present. The first child may start the mural, but the group-product claim stays pending until real others contribute. Don't add pretend peer work to fill the gap.

Children may plan on paper, discuss ideas if they wish, or continue a familiar activity. Paper work is meaningful participation, not evidence of app use. No child must perform publicly or wait in a queue. Briefly show a tool in the familiar app if needed, without choosing anyone's picture.

Invite one willing child at a time

A short focal turn may take about 5–10 minutes, adjusted to comfort and supervision. You might observe two or three children today and others on later days; this is an example, not a quota or tested staffing capacity. Keep a private opportunity list so quiet children and those needing longer access are included. Declines remain respected; an unseen turn is not credited.

Ask, “What would you like to add?” Let exploration lead to a choice. A child can draw, choose a permitted stock picture, arrange a familiar shape or purposefully direct your pointer. Offer large controls, home language, sign, gesture or communication aid; verify that the actual app permits meaningful control. Finding a brush for a child is help, not authoring their idea. Choosing the picture and location for them is different and must not be called their independent idea.

For an accidental mark, ask “Keep, change or undo?” Repeated symbols are fine; neatness and spoken explanations are not required. If they want to alter a peer's mark, ask permission or use a separate space. Don't manufacture a disagreement as an assessment. Peers can suggest only when invited; observe whether the focal child really adopts an idea instead of assuming it.

Keep supervision first

If someone elsewhere needs you, pause, close/lock the device safely and attend to the group. Resume later rather than rush, leave the device unsupervised or assign a child to manage classmates. If input access fails, offer art and defer digital evidence instead of treating the child as unable to create.

Save and observe one child's real contribution

You may save/merge. Show the actual child's part in the mural and invite an accessible confirmation or correction. Save failure means product confirmation is pending, not that their observed idea vanished. A separate less-cued choice can be “Anything you want to keep or change?” Same familiar app/mural is valid. Stop when they are satisfied or need to stop; no novelty test is required.

Privately record the child's decision and app action, where the contribution went, adult/peer help, assent/access and missing or uncertain parts. Record only the children actually observed. A group display, chorus, attendance or teacher-only editing is not evidence for each child. Supported work is valid to describe, not independent mastery.

Revisit without turning it into homework

Offer an optional brief viewing of the real saved class mural and later-day opportunities to add or revise. Celebrate choices without ranking children. Record a later response only when it actually happens. The same project is fine; another app or product is optional transfer, not required retention. No family devices or purchases are needed. This plan has not been field-tested with a class; its safety and access arrangements must fit your actual setting.

Our class digital mural: add an idea that is yours

Purpose, source and fit

PK4.X.A.3: “Child uses digital learning applications to contribute to class-made digital products that express own ideas, as appropriate.” The 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Technology, printed p80, has no PK3 outcome for this domain. Younger learners may enjoy appropriate supported exploration, without assigning a nonexistent PK3 outcome. Familiar app skills are helpful, not admission requirements. The teacher teaches the controls when needed.

Children use an approved drawing/creation application to add something they intend to express to an actual class digital mural. A familiar shape, color arrangement, chosen stock symbol, repeated favorite drawing or imported image of their own work can express their idea. Own does not mean unprecedented, different from peers, artistically recognizable, verbally explained, or made without support. Observe a meaningful choice or expression rather than requiring a story before any mark. A child may discover meaning while exploring. TEA's illustrative behaviors include digital drawing/writing, capturing creations, and co-creating books with adult support. These are examples, not a mandatory checklist. Source is not limited to independent freehand invention or a particular brand of app.

This mural is a chosen route, not all of technology education. Digital interaction and a contribution to a real class product matter here. A paper-only activity is valuable art, not evidence of digital app use. A teacher importing a child's drawing entirely without child app participation cannot evidence the child's application use; a child directing the actual image selection/placement, checking the result and using an accessible motor proxy is a supported contribution, with that help recorded. Teacher may save or assemble contributions without taking authorship of the child's idea. One-child-at-a-time instruction can feed a real multi-child class mural over days. A home-only picture with one child is practice, not automatically a class-made product.

Preparation: one adult, one child

Plan about 5–10 minutes, shorter or longer with the child's interest and comfort; no timed score. Bring one charged, stable, approved device with a functioning accessible creation app; one protected class mural file or class contribution file that will be merged; and an adult-only brief observation note. Use existing approved software rather than requiring purchases, sign-ups or cloud sharing. Have ordinary paper and drawing materials for voluntary planning or an off-screen alternative. Both print packets are optional adult aids, not required worksheets or evidence of app use.

Before inviting a child, test drawing or choosing an image, undo, saving, closing and reopening a spare file. Choose a familiar broad class invitation such as “Things we would like in a pretend garden,” or let children agree another safe theme. No child must reveal their home, feelings, identity or family experiences. Check class-sharing permission and child assent. Use a locally stored, nonidentifying project with teacher-controlled access. Do not photograph children, record their voices, create accounts, buy content or publish. This lesson's conservative privacy choice is not a claim that TEA prohibits all supervised recordings. Use only original or permitted images.

Protect peers' work with a duplicate/layer or a separate contribution area the adult can merge. Existing voluntary classmates' contributions can make the shared purpose visible; starting a real class mural is also legitimate, but mark group-product completion pending until real other contributions are included. Never invent peer marks to make the target appear met. Keep a private attribution note outside public files and repositories. Save a backup before a child's turn, not at the expense of supervising them.

Check the actual input route: large controls, high contrast/zoom, a switch or directed pointer if available, audio or tactile description of the canvas, home language, sign, gesture or AAC. Do not claim an app is accessible just because the plan lists options. If a route does not let this child control a meaningful contribution, change the tool or defer digital evidence and offer art. No extra adult is assumed.

ENTRY: invite and notice

Show the class mural and say, “We are making a picture together. What would you like to add?” Child may explore, choose an image, make a mark or indicate an idea through their usual communication. Ask “Where would you like it?” only if useful; do not turn this into an oral-language test. Record what actually happened and any prior cue. No response yet can mean unfamiliar controls, language access, tiredness or lack of interest, not inability to think creatively. Declining is respected. No compulsory baseline performance before help.

MODEL: teach a tool, not the child's answer

If needed, use the same familiar app, in a spare area or practice file. Say, “I choose this brush; when I move, a line appears. Undo takes my last mark away.” A teacher's different example makes ownership clear, but another application, new theme or unrelated computer is unnecessary. Do not erase or change the child's work to demonstrate. If the child already knows what to do, skip the model. A child adopting a familiar demonstrated technique can still express their own intention; label immediate copying as practice if ownership is uncertain, not as a permanent failure.

DURING: help the child make a real contribution

Let the child choose how much to add: one stroke may be enough. They can draw, select a permitted picture, arrange a shape, or direct a meaningful accessible edit. Ask permission before giving motor help. “Would you like help finding the brush?” preserves choice; “Put a blue star here” supplies the content. When a child says or signals where and what, the adult can act as motor proxy and confirm each meaningful decision in the child's accessible way. Record supported participation, not independent mouse skill.

If a mark goes elsewhere, pause: “Keep it, change it, or undo?” Do not treat accidental contact as intended expression or demand neatness. If the child cannot find a control, briefly show it, then return control. If a child wants the same symbol as a peer, accept it when it is their choice; competition for uniqueness is not the target. Avoid repeated “What is it?” questioning. Meaning can be demonstrated by choosing, returning to, placing or approving a mark, not only naming it. If ownership remains unclear, describe the action and keep the inference uncertain.

Save or merge the actual contribution with the child present when practical, show it in the class mural and invite “Is that how you want it?” A nonverbal indication, correction or refusal is meaningful. If saving fails, retain an honest observation of the child's idea and app use but mark the product outcome pending; do not require the child to redo work just to produce a score. Resolve the technical failure later before claiming completed class contribution. A static screenshot may preserve an actual jointly created mural; it is not disqualified because it is static. A prefilled screenshot children only watched is not their contribution.

EXIT: stop with a bounded observation, not a novelty hurdle

When the child has made a meaningful decision and the adult has verified where it went, stop while interest remains. Invite a further choice or adjustment without telling the answer if a separate less-cued check is appropriate: “Anything you want to keep or change?” The same app, same mural and familiar idea are valid. Distinguish prompted practice from a later child-led decision; do not infer independence from adult help being hidden. A new app or product can be optional later transfer, never compulsory to credit this source. If no separate uncued action occurs, record the supported source opportunity without claiming independent transfer.

Privately record: context/date; child's actual choice and app action; contribution's destination and confirmation; exact adult/peer help; access and assent; and what remains unobserved. A completed class mural alone proves nothing about every child. Candidate source evidence is not mastery or effectiveness. If the child starts the mural before other contributors, record own idea/app use now and collective-product confirmation later, without falsifying the first observation date. Unavailable app, no consent or an unobserved turn means NOT_OBSERVED, not a low achievement score. Unknown help is uncertain, not independent or adult-answered by assumption.

REVISIT: return when meaningful

On another actual day, reopen the same mural if useful and invite a child-chosen change or contribution. Record the actual date, prompt, help and response; a planned return is REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED. Same-day practice is not later retention. A different product is an optional transfer opportunity, separately recorded. Do not make new themes or unfamiliar interfaces a retention requirement.

CLASSROOM: one teacher and multiple children

Use a teacher-adjacent device during an established, safely supervised art/center period. Keep the entire group in view and earshot under the setting's normal staffing rules. Do not start this station if screen attention prevents supervision. Introduce the shared purpose briefly; each child may make an idea on paper, discuss it, or choose an existing familiar activity. Paper planning is real participation but not digital-source evidence. No unattended device rotation or helper child is responsible for supervising peers.

Invite one willing child at a time for a short focal turn. Observe only as many turns as you can safely support; for example, two or three today, then different willing children on later days. Those figures are scheduling examples, not tested capacity or a target. Keep a private opportunity list so quiet children and access-intensive turns are not skipped. Never force a child who declines or demand that everyone wait in a queue. Children may watch with consent, suggest ideas only when invited, or continue their activity; another child's suggestion is not the focal child's decision unless the child meaningfully adopts it. Stop and attend to the group immediately when needed, lock/close the device safely and resume later. No unseen turn receives credit.

Protect existing contributions. If someone wants to change a peer's mark, help them ask permission or put their idea in their own area; do not create conflict to test sharing. Reopen the saved mural for a brief optional class viewing. Do not call every attending child a creator. Celebrate the different choices without comparing ability, speed or originality. Document one child's actual actions, not a class score.

Four routes and boundaries

ADULT-LED: the one-child preparation and ENTRY/MODEL/DURING/EXIT/REVISIT above are teacher-ready; child-led exploration is welcome. CO-PLAY: a caregiver may support a real approved school contribution with the school permission/process established. Without that class connection, make an enjoyable home digital picture and call it practice, not completed class-product evidence. CLASSROOM: use the supervised rolling focal turns above, not simultaneous device tests. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: no autonomous narrated child app is delivered by this lesson. The written plan requires actual approved software and adult safeguarding. No four-mode efficacy equivalence is claimed.

False credit, valid alternatives and larger learning

False credit: the adult draws a mural then credits everyone who watched. Deficient: an attractive product with no child decision or app participation. Adequate: child chooses a meaningful image, directs its placement and teacher saves it into the real class work, accurately recording help. Stronger design: the child can change their mind, revise a real contribution, revisit its place among peers' ideas and retain comfortable access without a language/artistry gate. These are prospective design distinctions, not observed results.

A template is not automatically wrong: a child choosing a personally meaningful picture and placement in a template can meet the construct. Filling teacher-mandated identical stars with no meaningful decision does not demonstrate own ideas. Adult technical save/assembly is legitimate; adult-only expression falsely attributed to a child is not. A real class artifact grows over time; don't penalize the first contributor because they happened to go first.

For related progression and sibling mechanisms see DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. Opening apps (X.A.1), naming tools (X.A.2), information access (X.A.4) and digital safety (X.A.5) are separate outcomes, not automatically earned here. This single lesson is not a balanced technology program. A child's family may decline digital sharing without penalty; use classroom-approved alternatives and never require home devices or purchases.

Evidence limits: written design only. No actual children, classroom effectiveness, independent educator validation, physical print usability, app accessibility trial with learners or later retention observed. The source PDF text is checked against printed p80; do not confuse that bounded concordance with a whole-document audit. Print/render and browser tests establish technical presentation only.

Facilitator: a child-owned contribution to our digital mural

Objective PK4.X.A.3: Child uses digital learning applications to contribute to class-made digital products that express own ideas, as appropriate. No PK3 outcome is printed. See the complete lesson for full source boundaries and the family overview for other mechanisms.

Before the child arrives

Use one approved stable device and familiar creation app, private class mural, backup, ordinary art alternative and private teacher note. Test create/undo/save/reopen in a spare file; verify the actual access route and sharing permission. Keep peer work protected. No account creation, public publishing, names, voices or child photos in this route. A class mural can grow across days; own app use is recorded now, group-product confirmation when genuinely present. A home-only product is practice unless part of a real school class project. Budget roughly 5–10 minutes flexibly, not a timed assessment.

ENTRY: offer an authentic invitation

“We are making a picture together. What would you like to add?” Let the child explore before defining an idea in words. Notice a meaningful choice through gesture, sign, AAC, home language or action. Decline, fatigue or inaccessible controls call for a break/alternative, not failure. A first attempt may be supported; record the help instead of pretending an unprompted baseline occurred.

MODEL: explain a control if needed

In the same app on a spare area, show your own brush mark or undo. Explain the cause: moving makes the mark; undo removes the last action. Return choice to the child. Different software and a novel theme are unnecessary. Do not change the child's idea to fit your demonstration.

DURING: notice, respond and return control

A familiar symbol, stock picture, repeat favorite or directed capture of the child's own artwork may express an idea. Let the child select/position/approve the actual app contribution. For motor assistance ask permission and follow the child's precise meaningful directions; do not invent meaning for random touch. Help find a tool without choosing the content. If control is difficult, try a large target, accessible input or shorter turn; if that still fails, offer art and defer digital evidence. Respond to accidental marks with keep/change/undo choices, not correction toward pretty art.

The adult may save and combine contributions. Show the real saved mural and accept the child's accessible confirmation or correction. Save failure does not erase observed app participation; record pending product confirmation. Teacher-only scanning/uploading without the child's app interaction is not the child's technology action. Same chosen image as a peer is not plagiarism or failure.

EXIT: record a small honest claim and stop

If useful, invite an unsupplied choice: “Anything to keep or change?” Same app and mural are fine. Separate supported teaching from an actually observed less-cued decision, not a demanded new product. Record idea/action, actual digital contribution and destination, support, assent/access, save status and remaining uncertainty. U = child-origin decision; A = access assistance under child control; H = adult supplied the specific content/next decision; X = support or ownership unclear. These are descriptions, not a score. X does not mean adult answered; H still describes meaningful teaching. No observation means NOT_OBSERVED. None of these establishes mastery.

REVISIT: a real later date

Return to the same mural or a different appropriate project on another day if worthwhile. Record date and actual action/help. A new app is optional transfer, not required retention. Until observed: REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED.

CLASSROOM organization

One educator keeps one teacher-adjacent device within normal sight/hearing of the group. Use a settled art/center time; other learners continue meaningful familiar activities or voluntarily plan on paper. Do not run this station if it compromises supervision. Invite one willing focal child; spread turns across days, keep private opportunity records, never promise everyone today. Pause for the wider group, close device safely, resume later. Peer suggestions and finished mural are not individual evidence. Changing peers' work needs permission or a separate area. No forced sharing, public comparison or child supervisor.

Four delivery routes and support

ADULT-LED is the flexible one-child sequence above. CO-PLAY at home requires an actual authorized class contribution to claim this source; otherwise it is related practice. CLASSROOM is the safe rolling-turn organization, not an extra adult or multiple-device requirement. SOLO is NOT IMPLEMENTED; no independently narrated app or observed efficacy is delivered.

Access: short turns/breaks for attention; gesture/sign/home language/AAC for communication; no reading, spelling, quantity or artistic-precision prerequisite; predictable preview and quiet for sensory comfort; one meaningful choice at a time for developmental support; verified actual large-control/switch/directed-motor/visual-description route for physical or sensory access. Preserve child control, not the exact adult interface. Do not infer absence of ideas from a failed tool.

Source checks and limits

TEA 2022 Technology p80 explicitly permits adult-supported co-creation and capture of creations. Novelty, unfamiliar software and independent artistic invention are not printed requirements. Own idea plus actual app contribution to the class product is the boundary. A child's first contribution can await peers without being called failed. Print pages are adult planning/blank recording aids; completed paper is not app evidence. No actual child, independent educator, field accessibility, classroom effect, physical print or retention was validated by authoring this guide.
