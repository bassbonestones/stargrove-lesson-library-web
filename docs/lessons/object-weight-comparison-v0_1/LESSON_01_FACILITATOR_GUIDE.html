Facilitator companion: safe hands-on weight comparison

Exact target and boundaries

PK3.V.D.3: “Child understands that weights of objects can vary and be compared.” Research-cell SHA 931b1d91ef0238fbf7bf842eaa50c26dbb887fee414c051e406220e0167ab247. Practical preparation

Two large intact smooth lightweight objects, a low surface and optional mat suffice. Adult inspects and privately confirms a clear safe contrast; respect the individual child's comfort and existing movement restrictions. No filled bags, sharp/breakable objects, detachable parts or unsafe loads. Ask before placing an object near hands. Compare low and rest between turns. Same pair may support teaching and exploration; record answer cues rather than labeling all reuse invalid.

ENTRY, MODEL, DURING, EXIT, REVISIT

ENTRY: invite safe curiosity and record actual prior contribution. MODEL only when useful: show support, set down and compare, naming your own result as teaching. DURING: accept child choices and meaningful nonspoken/home-language response, clarify neutrally and adapt to comfort. EXIT: stop and summarize observed contribution; it is not a compulsory test. A demonstrated result after answer-bearing help remains supported practice. REVISIT: optional later occasion when useful, not forced new objects. REVISIT NOT_OBSERVED at authoring.

Access and evidence

An adult may name the dimension and proactively arrange neutral access. The child need not request all support independently. Do not attribute a relation supplied by an adult to the learner. Unequal motor support can distort hand-feel; a safe accessible guarded balance is a different legitimate source-level route, not proof of direct heft. If accessible evidence is unavailable, defer and arrange it. Do not fabricate a tactile device or equate verbal telling of the winner with neutral sensation.

A meaningful point after actual comparison may show the relation; a lucky size guess alone does not. PK3 variability/comparability is distinct from PK4 accurate comparison. Record real pair, mechanism, action, actual earlier or immediate cues, neutral help, uncertainty and bounded next step. An unknown cue or method is not evidence that no cue or balance was used. Record who supplied this comparison’s answer if known: adult and peer cues are separate from neutral help, and unrelated talk is not an answer cue. U = child-origin relation; A = neutral access with child-origin relation; H = conceptual help; X = unclear. These are optional codes, not scores. If the child tries both methods, keep a short separate entry for each attempt. Do not count a response from an obstructed or unsettled balance as a hand comparison; keep a genuinely separate safe hand comparison on its own. No observation is NOT_OBSERVED, not failure. A meaningful observed action is not “not observed” just because the child did not speak. Record adult preparation separately from the child’s actual method. A prechecked balance can remain unused; record a verified hand comparison as such without creating a second child attempt. If people disagree about what the child actually used, retain both reports and withhold the comparison claim. Keep conflicting notes without assuming a result. No-stop versus a clearly later stop does not change an otherwise supported, completed earlier comparison; keep both accounts and do not reoffer. If a stop might precede the response, leave its interpretation unresolved. Honor an actual or possible decline or stop immediately without reoffering. If it came before this response, do not count the response; preserve an actually complete earlier comparison if the stop came after it or in a separate attempt. If the only possible stop is confirmed in a different attempt or after a completed comparison, unknown occurrence does not erase the otherwise valid comparison. If it might affect this response, hold interpretation; pause without reoffering either way. A blank is not a documented no.

One-teacher feasibility and safeguarding

Only one attended comparison spot. Other children observe or do familiar safe work within genuine whole-group supervision; no unattended lifting stations. Defer turns when attention is needed elsewhere. Do not infer individual understanding from chorus or conceal children to prevent peer cues. Take turns over ordinary sessions and preserve privacy.

Responsive alternatives

Allow sensory refusal, pauses, familiar materials, emergent play, culturally familiar objects, signed/home-language/AAC response and neutral offered support. Keep the actual physical comparison perceptible. Do not diagnose from strength, speech or sensory discrimination. Explore equality or misleading sizes only if useful, never as a mandatory ladder.

Scope
