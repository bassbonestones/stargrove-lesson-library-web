01 · Practice: people and evidence

Reusable story teaching and observation · original fictional narrators, not real classmate data.

Fictional character 1 · Tali

“I like making pictures from leaves. When I visit my uncle, I sing a little welcome song with him.”

Fictional character 2 · Dev

“I also like making pictures. I use pencils for mine. I usually greet my neighbors with a wave.”

Child-directed evidence questions

What is one thing these two people both do? What is different about what they told us? Which details do their words not tell us? Do all people with the same name or appearance do the same things?

Both · from what they say
Different · from what they say

An adult may model on an unrelated invented story. Do not infer any named culture from portraits, names or preferences. These are fictional characters. A1 real-self and real-classmate comparison must be separately evidenced.

02 · Practice: many kinds of families

No real child is asked to identify their own caregiver, legal history or home life.

Family story · Jo and Ari

Ari and Aunt Jo go to the community library. They choose two new books. Later they read a story together after dinner.

Family story · Bea and grandfathers

Bea lives with her grandfathers. They read a story together each evening. On Saturday they make paper lanterns with Bea.

Family story · Em and Sam

Em and caregiver Sam look after a small garden. They draw what they saw, and Sam helps Em share the pictures with a neighbor.

Compare the stated family characteristics

What family activities are alike? What is different about their *stated* routines or caregivers? Can two families be different in one way and similar in another? What haven't these stories told us?

Familiar accounts are valid. Note whether the child supplied the relation or received conceptual help. Families are not limited to one household; no real-family disclosure is required.

03 · Claim investigator and unknowns

Original fictional community activities. Do not make a person's single experience a universal rule.

Character statement

“When I see my friend at the community center, we like to share drawings and greet each other with a wave,” says Eli.

Different character statement

“I enjoy the same community center, and I often greet my friend with a quiet hello. We like playing with blocks,” says June.

Said by the narrator
Unknown or not said

What can we responsibly conclude?

“Everybody in this community greets people the same way.” Is this statement supported, contradicted or unknown? What do the TWO narrators explicitly tell us? What new question would be respectful?

No physical ritual is required. Never assign culture or identity from a name or portrait. Use familiar or different accounts according to purpose. Record actual child reasoning and help; fictional stories are not real classmate observations.
