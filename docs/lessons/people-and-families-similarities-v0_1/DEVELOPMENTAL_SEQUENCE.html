Shared source and evidence boundaries

Similarity and difference concern actual known details. Safe, voluntary visible characteristics are legitimate, including the publisher's drawing examples about eyes/hair. Do not require body inspection or infer cultural identity, language, disability, beliefs, family or status from appearance or names. Culture-related experience can come from voluntary known accounts or clearly fictional stories; one person's practice is not a rule for a population. A1 self, classmates and other people may be explored across opportunities. Fiction is a valid read-aloud bridge to other people, not a fabricated real classmate. Without genuine classmate evidence, retain learning and mark that component unobserved. A2 compares families separately and requires no personal household disclosure. Family is not limited to one residence or one caregiver pattern.

ENTRY is a first available idea, not a prerequisite uncued test. MODEL illustrates a relation when useful. DURING values supported learning and child-directed inquiry. EXIT notices what is warranted at a natural pause; familiar examples and access supports are valid. REVISIT can occur in the same session or later depending on purpose; record actual elapsed time when making retention claims. No compulsory novel stimuli, withheld materials, new peer, one-turn complete source span, response quota or different-calendar-day gate. A copied relation is not independent; mere familiarity does not make an idea copied. No unobserved future return is credited.

Keep U (child-origin), A (access-only support), H (conceptual help) and X (unclear origin) distinct as descriptions of opportunities, not labels for children. Keep exact relation, source fact, self/classmate/other or family referents and help. Partial, supported, declined, unavailable and unknown are different. No combination of checkboxes establishes mastery or diagnosis.

PK3 enrichment, not a published outcome

E-1: compare two safe toys or fictional characters with full access. E-2: enjoy two family accounts and notice a fact. Play, watching, gesture and home-language responses are valid. Do not make these compulsory prerequisites or assess a PK3 VII outcome that is not published.

PK4 A4-1: a concrete characteristic

Invite a chosen comparison about known people or fictional characters: “What do you notice?” A self-selected innocuous interest may be offered but not demanded. Model with blue/red pens if useful. A child may say two people both have curly hair; accept the factual observation without assigning culture. If the child guesses language from hair, separate visible fact from unknown identity. Retain one genuine relation rather than reject it for not completing every strand.

PK4 A4-2: a real classmate, when available

Use a classmate's voluntarily shared safe activity for the agreed purpose. A recalled or teacher-narrated genuine fact can be valid with honest provenance and current consent; no new live encounter is required. Children may pass privately. Compare a chosen self-interest to that fact, or notice a benign visible fact without inspection/ranking. Fiction may support teaching when real information is absent; record classmate NOT_OBSERVED, do not impersonate a peer or coerce disclosure.

PK4 A4-3: an explicitly described cultural influence

Read the original Tali/Dev accounts from Lesson 01. Tali sings with an uncle as a greeting; Dev waves to a neighbor. Invite a relation grounded in those accounts. Do not infer a named culture or claim these are their only greetings. Children can relate a volunteered familiar practice if they wish, or stay with fiction. Model how to leave an unknown unknown. A narrated family practice broadens thinking; it does not require children to classify themselves culturally.

PK4 A4-4: connect available referents

Let the child revise a portrait including whichever actual self/classmate facts and other-person accounts are available and welcomed. Observe specific characteristics, described influences and alike/different relations across opportunities. Missing evidence is a scope limit, not a reason to press the child. Familiar or different accounts are chosen for teaching purpose, not a fixed novelty threshold. A child-owned comparison can emerge during play. No single all-or-none source score.

PK4 F4-1: notice what a family account says

Read Ari/Aunt Jo or Bea/grandfathers from Lesson 01, one account at a time. Invite one known detail using any accessible response. Do not presume household, family worth or missing relatives from what was not told. No real-family disclosure or family-size arithmetic required.

PK4 F4-2: compare the accounts

Ask what is alike and what differs. Both read together; the described caregivers and other activities differ. Keep a valid partial relation. Model another example if needed, then observe any subsequent idea with help provenance. Drawing neatness, a copied adult answer or saying which family is better is not the source action.

PK4 F4-3: widen without stereotyping

Choose other meaningful fictional or consented accounts when appropriate, including varied caregiving and more than one home. The child may ask, revise or leave details unknown. Distinguish families' reported practices from universal claims about populations. Do not assume all homes are safe or require an upbeat personal story. Protect privacy and respond to distress with care.

PK4 F4-4: revisit a relation

Offer a familiar account or another one when the child is interested. Observe their actual comparison and source facts separately from A1. Record timing only as observed; changed accounts explore generalization but do not by themselves prove transfer or retention. Stop whenever agency, access or supervision requires it.

Human routes and scope

ADULT-LED / CO-PLAY: one adult and one child can explore self/other and family accounts with a blank board and narration. Classmate span stays unobserved when unavailable. CLASSROOM: one teacher keeps familiar quiet activities in one supervised area, listens to focal children as safe, rotates opportunities fairly and never credits a chorus to everyone. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED. Digital accessibility or mode equivalence is not claimed.
