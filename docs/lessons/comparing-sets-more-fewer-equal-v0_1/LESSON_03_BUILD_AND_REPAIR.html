Method 03 — Build a Comparison, Test a Claim, and Repair

Original E2.2 internally reviewed written instruction · 2026-10-30. Read DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md first. Published Texas PK3.V.A.8 compares two sets up to 5 (SHA 779846ea0d4de0ae83ba66c391b769a1083f33ff90d75227c90c87579cdb0dc4) and PK4.V.A.8 compares two sets up to 10 (SHA ab5cf662752a22227509b99cd865d127d47388832931ce4c76867b8aad3b61af). Mechanism: learner constructs pairs of groups to satisfy a meaningful verbal relation, challenges a mistaken fictional character's claim, makes a repair from unequal to equal (or equal to more) by changing one set and explains how comparison changed. This is different from the first method's straightforward matching of already-given sets or second method's rearranging the same counts to rebut visual illusions. Here the amounts themselves may deliberately change to create an independently targeted relation.

First-party original materials and instructional agency

Use broad reusable “GROUP A / GROUP B” empty spaces, at most five large original foam/cardboard shapes per set for PK3 and ten per set for PK4, optional original fictional assistant named “the paper owl” who makes a clearly stated comparison claim that could be correct or wrong. Child must choose independently both group sizes (within grade range), who has more/fewer, and what to adjust to become same. Teacher may issue relation goal “Make A have more than B” without prescribing the numbers. Do not present prepared “five on left, three on right” answer as supposedly child-generated construction. Use separate undisclosed EXIT prompts and different original objects. No food, beans, seeds or small choking pieces.

PK3 developmentally graded ladder

PK3 C3-1 — create first same/different 1–3: ENTRY: two empty broad mat groups with three large blank shapes offered: “Can you build two little groups so one has more than the other? Then tell/show which relative to which.” Observe initial self-authored quantities; model has NOT happened. MODEL on unrelated adult safe paper leaves, adult constructs 1-v-1 first: “Same number because both have one.” DURING learner builds 2-v-1 with child-selected object membership, then can choose to alter one group to make 2-v-2 using a NEW original token (explicitly this changes one group's amount, not just moving existing items). Repair: child piles all tokens in group A and leaves B empty but cannot express relation; adult on different pair demonstrates one matched-versus-unmatched, then novel open-ended construction. Fresh stage EXIT: new two safe paper stamp groups totaling at most 3 each, child independently makes A more than B, indicates relation via speech/AAC.

PK3 C3-2 — inverse more/less up to five: ENTRY adult says, “Make GROUP B have fewer than Group A. You choose how many, but each group can have no more than five.” Child chooses quantities and creates sets; early stages can use 3-v-2 before 5. MODEL different 2-v-4, teacher says “B has fewer; A has more.” DURING child creates 5-v-3, 4-v-5, and deliberately reverses requested relation with child's own counts. If learner always says “A more” when its amount now smaller, use different example with teacher's adult-only roles and offer fresh changed prompt. Fresh EXIT: child makes two original groups within 5 with one chosen to have more, then answers from the reverse viewpoint which has less/fewer.

PK3 C3-3 — actually construct equality and detect non-equality: ENTRY say “Make the two groups have the SAME NUMBER,” with different shapes in each and no prescribed counts. MODEL separate adult 3-v-2: “One lacks a partner. I added one to the smaller group; now 3-v-3, same number.” DURING child creates 4-v-4 and 5-v-5 from large original shapes, checks equality by mapping/count; deliberately add one token to just one group, ask if still equal (no), and invite child to repair by their own choice. False belief: child adds one object to both unequal sets and says gap vanished; teacher models counterexample with different 1-v-3 objects, notes 2-v-4 still unequal, then child tests new task. Fresh EXIT: novel 5-v-4, child independently chooses action to make same-number groups without teacher supplying number, then verbally/signs/AAC labels equality.

PK3 C3-4 — new source comparison both recognition and authorship: ENTRY novel two groups 2-v-5, child compares. MODEL/DURING unrelated three-v-three and three-v-four examples, teacher creates own relations and checks. EXIT concealed two contexts (large original toy ticket station 3-v-5, flat paper shapes 5-v-5) plus open make-B-fewer challenge 4-v-2 selected independently, child expresses correct more/less/same with explicit A/B referents, no cue. Additional constructed equality is instructional evidence, not new Texas mandated substandard. REVISIT real future different date with different quantities and materials, currently NOT_OBSERVED.

PK4 progression: revisit small, extend to ten, develop flexible comparative reasoning

PK4 C4-1 — retrieve and adapt small group relation: ENTRY novice 4-v-3 and 4-v-4 original unmodeled, identify relative comparisons. MODEL unrelated 2-v-3 adult build-and-repair to 3-v-3, emphasize exact group count. DURING child can create 5-v-4 and then 5-v-5, show how one group changed, compare two original sets. Fresh EXIT self-generated two groups of ≤5 with meaningful more/fewer and equality repair.

PK4 C4-2 — six to eight without relying on colors: ENTRY prompt build B with more than A, each 6–8 if ready. Adult supplies RELATION goal but not numbers. MODEL on different original 5-v-7 shapes, teacher adds one to smaller side and says 6-v-7 still fewer, helps notice gap. DURING child independently creates 6-v-8, 8-v-7 and 8-v-8; checks direction, repairs one unequal pair to equality by own chosen add/remove action using safe pieces; avoids physical counting of blank ten-frame. Fresh EXIT new 8-v-7 target and child creates required converse relation with a different count pair.

PK4 C4-3 — nine/ten and accurate one-step change: ENTRY child builds 9-v-10, explains which is one fewer, can show count without saying formal written numeral. MODEL unrelated 6-v-9 original paper designs and demonstrate that adding one to smaller 6 becomes 7, still smaller, not instant equality. DURING 9-v-10→10-v-10, 8-v-10→10-v-10 (requires two new units to smaller) and 9-v-9→9-v-10, child decides to adjust which set and counts exactly; only grade-appropriate quantities within 10. Misconception: child copies teacher's supplied “add two” from different set and says equal without checking, record H; demonstrate matching on unrelated pair and offer new open repair. Fresh EXIT two original group cards 10-v-9 then child independently creates equality within range and labels new relation.

PK4 C4-4 — deliberately wrong claims and direction reversal: ENTRY paper owl claims “Because my seven paper stars are spread out, I have more than a group of nine compact shapes.” Child determines truth on actual two-set count. MODEL different 3-v-5 adult myth: “If I add one to both, they'll be the same” (false; becomes 4-v-6). DURING child challenges false claim, changes one set deliberately to make correct comparison with one group more/fewer, and supplies an example of two equal sets that look different. Teacher asks why a claim was wrong, without demanding full English sentence; pointing to unmatched objects can express meaningful reason. Fresh EXIT new owl incorrectly says two 6-count collections are unequal because one is larger paper; learner makes/correctly compares groups and identifies equal.

PK4 C4-5 — independent open construction and unfamiliar comparison transfer: ENTRY new 7-v-10 and 4-v-4 original large props, child compares without cues. MODEL/DURING on totally separate 5-v-7 teacher story, child independently authors a set contrast with up to ten items, then verifies whether arbitrary claim true and may repair. EXIT withheld physical/pictured 10-v-9 (more and fewer) and 6-v-6 (equal despite different items), plus learner-generated valid 8-v-7 or another chosen values ≤10; independently state comparative relation relative to specific sets, demonstrate inverse when prompted neutrally. Record concept and support. REVISIT actually different date with novel task, NOT_OBSERVED. The construction work enriches comparing, not an invented mandatory standard beyond the published comparative verb.

Complete recurring ENTRY / MODEL / DURING / EXIT / REVISIT cycle in actual teacher language

ENTRY — two distinct, honest opportunities: First offer the prepared, genuinely unfamiliar prebuilt source comparison from the companion: PK3 two safe objects in A versus three in B; PK4 seven in A versus eight in B when stage-ready (or another new smaller-grade-appropriate actual set recorded explicitly). “Here are two separate groups. Which has more, which has fewer, or are they the same number?” Record both actual sets, the child's first independent relative comparison and referent, response language/modality and any support, without requiring construction or prepairing. Then, as optional child-authored exploration, offer new blank A/B spaces and ask “Could you make A have more objects than B? You choose how many safe pieces in each. How do you know?” Record actual child-origin construction amounts and relation separately; choosing this exploration first is legitimate if the scheduled prebuilt source trial stays NOT_OBSERVED until independently attempted. A child who only compares the prepared sets can still satisfy the official comparative learning action; making/repairing sets is a distinctive teaching extension, not a new Texas prerequisite. If boundaries/one-to-one prerequisites are missing, return to simpler two-object matching and home-language vocabulary rather than false “zero skill” mark.

MODEL: on unrelated teacher-only pair, put three in group A and two in B: “A has more than B because its third object has no partner. I can add one to B; now both have three, same number.” Teacher then demonstrates “B had fewer than A” about original pair, not a required English synonym drill. MODEL is never the independent answer.

DURING: child picks their own group amounts, narrates or signals relation, introduces one change (add or remove a new object to one group) and rechecks; can play “detect paper owl's mistake” with first-party separate fictional claim. If child changes both sets by same amount and wrongly declares equal, model different 2-v-4 becoming 3-v-5 and ask “Did the gap disappear?” then use novel task for unprompted trial. If child builds equal but does not express a comparison, invite independent relation response before a conceptual hint; assisted H if instructor gives “equal.” If hand-over-hand takes conceptual agency, don't call independent; child's commands to proxy A acceptable.

EXIT: novel open comparison challenge and new pair near the respective grade limits. Child must convey relative quantities in own meaningful comparative language or AAC and sometimes construct more/fewer/equal, but construction is optional auxiliary evidence: the official objective asks compare two sets, so a child with motor restrictions can meaningfully compare fresh prebuilt sets instead. Novel prompt must not reuse teacher's 3-v-2 repair. Record true values, child expression, whether each original set remains within grade scope, first U/H/A/X and whether explanation contradicts amount.

REVISIT: different actual later-date original context (e.g., two safe classroom paper story ticket groups), fresh comparison/repair. No real date, score or child has been observed.

Four distinct complete teaching routes (written design only)

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: fully child-operated audio-first future tablet prompts child to choose counts up to grade limit, build Group A and B via touch/switch/gaze or accessible AAC, choose relationship from narrated “A has more than B / fewer / same”, receive contingent meaning-specific teaching if gap vs equality confused on different examples, and then repair one group with child-directed new-item addition/removal. It must preserve child ownership and avoid supplying numerical answer, then novel independent EXIT and actually later-date revisit. For PK3 begin 1–3 and progress 5; PK4 revisit 5, later up to ten. Nothing built or proven accessible/effective yet.

CO-PLAY: adult narrates neutral source goal (“Make A have more than B”), child creates groups, then gives an original challenge back to adult and corrects adult's fictional mistake after pair counting. Start 1–3 PK3, then up to 5, PK4 up to 10. Adult can participate but cannot give which set to enlarge in scored exit. A child without mobility uses adult as child-directed hands with clear concept U/A.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: teacher probes before model, provides safe manipulatives and a blank template, models unrelated relationship and how adding to smaller group changes equality, then child builds/compares new pair. Teacher follows child error with purposeful different example and fresh exit, logs source grade and U/H/A/X. No English-only expression, speed ranking or separate arithmetic facts requirement.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: teacher offers neutral relationship goal to small-group stations, each child creates two separate bounded groups and negotiates evidence in own accessible language, teacher demonstrates on separate original group only. For independent assessment circulate new tasks privately to focus learners with fresh amounts; if peer supplies answer or manipulates all shapes, other children are NOT_OBSERVED. PK3 small-bin five max; PK4 extends to ten with high-contrast ten-frame support, near-tie correction. Provide child choice and respectful error repair, not leaderboard.

Source false-positive, safety and non-validated status

false-positive: teacher directs “put five in A, three in B” and child repeats “more”; one scene also has larger A objects, so evidence may be copying or physical size rather than own numerical relationship. Correction: with unrelated teacher-only example demonstrate comparing count and making both groups equal, then child independently chooses counts for new graded set and communicates referent-specific relation; test both inequality and equality, document H for adult-provided counts and concept, A only if access proxy follows child decisions. Safe large materials, audio/tactile/home-language/sign/AAC, motor access, real responsible-team IEP/IFSP/504 planning, no automatic disability inference. No physical educator/child field test, completed SOLO, actual REVISIT or validated four-mode parity; these are written original curricular teaching designs.

Retrospective differentiated comparison audit (2026-11-01)

PK3: teach and fade, new evidence, guard bias

Teach: Child builds two actual sets that express more/fewer/same with adapted safe objects, then repairs by adding ONLY to an identified group; adult may move child specified pieces but not decide quantity.

Unmodeled source evidence: Different new target relation with blank mats; child creates valid up-to-five A/B amounts and communicates directional comparison without copying a prepared amount.

False credit: Teacher announcing 5 and 3 then saying A has more, or adult adding pieces for equality, is adult-owned solution.

PK4: teach and fade, new evidence, guard bias

Teach: Child authors up-to-ten near-tie/equality sets and chooses which set to change when repair is needed; model the misconception that adding one to BOTH unequal sets does not erase the gap.

Unmodeled source evidence: Novel A/B creation and repair task including nine/ten; child must relate original quantities and decide which specific set gets new items for equal or reversal.

False credit: Adding one to both unequal groups and claiming equal, or simply copying teacher prefilled quantities, is not demonstrated source comparison.

Four written modes: SOLO (unbuilt) requires accessible narrated or signed A/B orientation, independent unhighlighted relational choices and actual child-owned paired/set evidence rather than a visually obvious answer button. CO-PLAY may let the parent provide objects and offer learner-chosen motor assistance, but not announce which set is greater. ADULT-LED can teach pairing on separate examples then remove numerical hints for new EXIT. CLASSROOM must record individual first comparative relations, not group chorus or finished arrangements made by peers; later true-date checks must be genuinely observed. Respect real relevant individualized plans, sensory preferences and qualified professional input. Access-only aid, mathematical help and not observed are distinct; learning support is not a disability diagnosis.

Official objective-specific EXIT and genuine calendar REVISIT — lesson 3

PK3.V.A.8 independently compares two new complete real sets each with NO MORE THAN FIVE objects using meaningful directional more/fewer/same comparative LANGUAGE, not just count recital, spatial size or a teacher-completed grouping. An optional rearrangement/build repair is a teaching strategy, not an extra published requirement. PK4.V.A.8 independently compares two fresh real sets no larger than TEN each, including quantities above five to exercise the PK4 progression, with child-authored relation to explicit A/B referents and no demand for formal written inequality symbols. Keep grade-appropriate hidden ENTRY, adult-only MODEL, learner-controlled guided DURING, untouched independent EXIT and a truly later-date novel REVISIT (NOT_OBSERVED until completed); preserve neutral A versus conceptual H and document actual quantities.

Companion Facilitator Guide 03 — Build and Repair Quantitative Relations

Pair: Lesson 03. Source separation: PK3.V.A.8 / PK4.V.A.8. Companion to this specific generative lesson, not a fourth counted plan. Refer to objective guide.

Mechanism and appropriate instructional boundary

The learner CREATES two original sets to make a true more/fewer/same relation and experiments with changing exactly one set to repair a fictional wrong claim. This reveals quantity relationships as actionable, unlike correspondence alignment or illusion testing. Important: Texas V.A.8 is comparing sets using meaningful comparative language; independent construction and numerical repair are valuable teaching opportunities but not mandatory additional source-completion requirements. A learner with limited motor control can still demonstrate the official comparative action on adult-prepared unbiased novel sets.

Preflight: grade-appropriate and safe independent materials

Set two blank clearly bounded A/B mats, oversized safe clean nonchoking tokens (not beans, food or tiny beads), and prepare independent untouched EXIT arrays. ENTRY asks child to compare an unmodeled 2-v-3 (PK3) or 7-v-8 (PK4), with no answer labels. MODEL uses separate teacher-only 3-v-2 then one-item equality repair. DURING: invite child to choose 2 and 4 then add to the smaller group to make equal (PK3 up to five) or 7 and 9 then add two to seven (PK4 ≤10); practice also compare equal unequal randomly arranged groups. EXIT: PK3 unseen 5-v-4 and 3-v-3; PK4 unseen 9-v-10 and 8-v-8. Keep independent EXIT comparisons even when learner cannot build or repair. A separate later calendar date REVISIT uses new safe objects/contexts.

Moment-to-moment adult route

ENTRY: With a genuinely new prebuilt PK3 2-v-3 or PK4 7-v-8 pair (or documented smaller readiness-appropriate pair), ask “These are two groups. Which has MORE, which has FEWER, or same number?” Wait for a child-origin comparative relation with correct A/B referents and no teacher counts/answers. Record actual set numbers and first response. Optional separate construction entry: Provide blank mats with a neutral goal like “Make A have more than B,” and let child choose each group amount and state its actual relation. This is valuable generative exploration but record its own first evidence and help separately; if it comes before the planned prebuilt source trial, mark the latter NOT_OBSERVED until it happens. A child who can independently compare without building must not be falsely marked below the official PK3/PK4.V.A.8 source. MODEL: On unrelated adult-only tokens, show an accurate A-to-B relation, add one to one group, and describe changed relation. Emphasize that adding one to BOTH unequal groups doesn't automatically erase difference. DURING: Let child choose how many safe objects to place in two groups, with neutral instruction (“Can you make one have more?”), verify with actual counting/pairing if useful, and decide how to repair teacher fictional owl's incorrect relation. Provide meaning-specific feedback: if child adds to already greater set when trying to create equality, ask which has unmatched extras and model correction on DIFFERENT pair. If child merely repeats “more,” request which group has more than which on new practice objects. Fade count templates, adult placements, answer words and target numerical counts. EXIT: No assisted construction requirement. Present entirely unfamiliar two groups of grade-range counts: PK3.V.A.8 two-set numerical comparison using own meaningful comparative LANGUAGE within five per set; PK4.V.A.8 meaningful two-set comparison within ten per set and include genuinely above-five pairs. Learner may build an additional own pair, but that does not replace this unbiased source probe. Record the actual two-set relation and U/H/A/X. REVISIT: Genuinely different calendar day, new unequal and equal groups and independent child-origin language, NOT_OBSERVED until witnessed.

Feedback and misconception ladder

Child adds one to both 2 and 4 then asserts equality: model adult-only 1-v-3 becoming 2-v-4, then invite child to investigate an independent new pair. Child merely copies educator-created 5-v-3: don't count generative authorship, and test comparison with new unseen arrays. Child changes which collection is A halfway through: explicitly retain group referents on practice, then remove cues for EXIT. Child says big physical toy means numerically more: return to individual object count or one-to-one pairing without saying the correct side. Child uses accurate alternate home-language “not as many”: preserve meaning, do not demand a single English label or sign.

Seven differentiated profiles and adult mode routes

Attention/executive: predictable short placement cycles with faded turn markers; communication: own referent-specific sign/AAC/home-language relation; literacy: no written inequality symbols; numeracy: child-directed concrete one-at-a-time additions rather than mental arithmetic, not extra official arithmetic prerequisite; autism/sensory: optional no-touch/gaze build and advance notice when owl claim deliberately false; intellectual/developmental: first teach straightforward 1–3 and one addition then later source range, distinguish supported practice from EXIT; physical/motor/hearing/vision: large safe contrasting pieces, neutral child-directed physical placement A, never adult-choosing the counts and words, specialist assistance where relevant. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future engine must accept learner-chosen quantities, source comparison and open feedback without preselecting target response; virtual repair is not real child mastery. CO-PLAY: caregiver can introduce owl mistake but child owns first actual comparison. ADULT-LED: correct misconception on adult-only set then test new two-set source comparison. CLASSROOM: small group builds are collaborative teaching; record individually novel child-origin comparative relation, never group agreement as each child’s proof.
