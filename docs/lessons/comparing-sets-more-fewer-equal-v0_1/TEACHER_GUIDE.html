Objective-level Educator Guide — Mathematics V.A.8 Comparing Two Collections

Guide role: Overarching educator reference; it is NOT one of the three lesson plans. Published 2022 Texas PK outcomes (source research-cell verification, independent PDF layout check pending).

PK3.V.A.8 — source SHA 779846ea0d4de0ae83ba66c391b769a1083f33ff90d75227c90c87579cdb0dc4

“Child compares sets of objects up to 5 using comparative language (e.g., more than, less than, same number of).” The actual concept is a child-owned comparison between two identifiable collections using relational expressive meaning; matching and counting can establish it, but are not substitute evidence by themselves. Each collection must remain at or below FIVE, not total combined five. “More”, “fewer/less”, “same/equal” may be communicated through semantically meaningful home language, sign or learner-composed augmentative and alternative communication (AAC), without compulsory written inequalities. A child may compare by reliable correspondence, counting, or another defensible quantity strategy. Start with two perceptually accessible group boundaries and real 1–3 sets, move to mixed shape/spacing and full ≤5 within PK3 C3-1–C3-4 in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. Fresh source EXIT: Present original untouched two-set comparisons with count ≤5 per set; ask for A relative to B (and varied same/unequal probes as appropriate). Record actual quantities, which group child names, relation, exact spontaneous child language, hints, access assistance and whether apparent success was actually a visual size guess. An adult saying “more” first is H, not U.

PK4.V.A.8 — source SHA ab5cf662752a22227509b99cd865d127d47388832931ce4c76867b8aad3b61af

“Child compares sets of objects up to 10 using comparative language (e.g., greater/more than, less/fewer than, equal to/same number of).” Develop conceptual continuity from PK3 small sets to sets of up to TEN EACH, including near-ties and equality; do not make merely renaming “more” as “greater” the PK4 achievement or require all synonyms. In PK4 C4-1–C4-5, compare concrete objects despite apparent row length, pair up to ten if useful, and name true A-versus-B relation. Ten-frame capacity is not the number of objects. Fresh source EXIT: On untouched mixed-layout unequal and equal comparison sets (some extending above five, no larger than ten), independently express relational meaning with correct referents in the learner’s own communication. E.g., “The set with nine has fewer than the one with ten”; do not demand conventional arithmetic symbols or building exactly the target set. Counting nine alone is not source evidence for comparison.

Why the three complete teaching mechanisms complement one another

  1. One-to-one Partner Laboratory makes actual unmatched/equal correspondence perceptible before language. Its facilitator guide protects the learner's actual pairing and independent directed comparison.
  2. Layout Illusion Investigator challenges the false belief that spread-out or larger physical tokens mean a greater numerical set. Rearrangement with no additions builds invariance; independent genuine comparison is still required. Facilitator 02.
  3. Build and Repair lets the learner author two original amounts and correct an incorrect claim by changing one set, exposing quantity relations causally. Generating target sets is an instructional extension, NOT an additional official source threshold for a child who can accurately compare. Facilitator 03.

Developmental pedagogy and source-bound common errors

Begin with real countable objects of similar accessible size; separate membership from tray, grid slots, painted size and distance. Demonstrate equal pairs and unmatched leftovers on adult-owned props. Support meaningful names “more than which set?” and inverse comparison; fade quantity cues and labels before a new learner-owned EXIT. Diagnose: (a) spatial illusion — spread/compact unchanged pieces then fresh item sets; (b) one-set counting only — invite both true groups and a relation; (c) automatic “left is greater” — swap spatial position; (d) same color means same count — vary shapes/colors; (e) adding one to both unequal sets allegedly makes equal — show preserved numerical gap on separate teacher example. Supported practice after feedback is not a new first-attempt result.

Seven differentiated access pathways and four written delivery modes

Attention/executive: short countable sets and neutral phase markers, faded; language/communication: semantically composed sign, home language, AAC without preselected “more” answer; literacy/phonology: no print word decoding prerequisite; numeracy: tactile or visual one-to-one concrete alignment as a teaching scaffold, later independence; autism/sensory: predictable counts and low-arousal oversized washable materials; intellectual/developmental: work 1–3 first, fade colored bins and modeled matches; motor/hearing/vision: tactile/high contrast/accurately described objects, learner-controlled neutral gaze/switch/proxy and specialist guidance. Avoid choking pieces, food, beans, tiny batteries and forced touch; validate actual accessibility individually. Record conceptual H separately from neutral motor A; do not invent mastery.

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: A future prereader system must let learner compare unprompted genuine set counts with accessible quantities and a directed semantic answer, not animate the conclusion. CO-PLAY: caregiver can handle safe props and serve only child-directed neutral support. ADULT-LED: instructor demonstrates with a separate set and gathers individual uncued evidence. CLASSROOM: private independent comparisons, not a choral response or another learner’s completed correspondence.
