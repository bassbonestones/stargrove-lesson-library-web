Facilitator companion: raincoat without rain

Use with the complete Lesson 01. This is the first-word-removal lesson, not a replacement for the objective-wide reference or the separate last-word/swap lessons.

Get ready

One adult, one willing PK4 child, six large pictures: book/shelf, rain/coat, sun/flower. Draw them on quarter sheets or prepare only the picture areas from ORIGINAL_COMPOUND_MANIPULATION_MATS.html. Keep its printed equations and the adult key away from children. No reading or cutting by children; no tiny pieces. Meaningful gestures can replace cards, with the child's usual communication supports. Check meanings through conversation first. Do not confuse an unfamiliar word or inaccessible response device with weak thinking.

CO-PLAY / ADULT-LED: a usable first session

  1. ENTRY: “Want to play, watch, or come back later?” A child can try raincoat without rain before the demonstration, but an entry answer is not required to receive teaching. Record not observed when watching is chosen.
  2. MODEL: On your own BOOKSHELF pair say the whole word, show book and shelf, remove book and say/sign SHELF. “I took away a whole word.” Your answer is not the child's evidence.
  3. DURING: On RAINCOAT with both supports ask “Say raincoat without rain. What word is left?” Wait. The child may speak, sign, use familiar AAC, point meaningfully or direct a neutral proxy. Moving a card is optional; the linguistic change belongs to the child. Do not point at coat or give its first sound.
  4. If the whole word is repeated, model bookshelf again and invite practice. If the child only moves a card, ask what word remains with the usual communication system. If ambiguity remains, write uncertain. If access is missing, repair access before drawing an inference. If the child declines, stop warmly.
  5. EXIT: Offer raincoat again without a supplied answer, if welcome. Rehearsed does not automatically mean adult-owned. Record rehearsal and possible echo honestly. SUNFLOWER without SUN is an optional different-word opportunity only when familiar and not already answered by an adult/peer. Do not demand novelty as a condition for every source observation.
  6. REVISIT: On another actual day invite a familiar word again. Same-word retention and different-word transfer are distinct. Record only what occurred; planned revisit remains NOT_OBSERVED.

Allow about 5–8 minutes, less when needed. A child chooses response mode/supports, participation, and whether to continue. Preserve first-word removal as this lesson's learning target, not a mandatory correct-answer quota. Optional silly word invention is play, not evidence of manipulating a known compound.

Decide what you actually saw

Record exact whole/removal word, prompt, child response, pictures/gestures, language, access help, any supplied answer, rehearsal and ambiguity privately. Clear child-owned supported word change is SOURCE_ACTION_CANDIDATE_NOT_MASTERY. Adult/peer answer then echo is SUPPORTED_PRACTICE. Picture guessing or unclear intent is UNCERTAIN. Declined/deferred/inaccessible opportunity is NOT_OBSERVED. An understood accessible attempt not showing the change is OBSERVED_NOT_YET_SHOWN. None is a label for the child. Neither chorus, reading an equation, nor moving a named card alone proves the word-level construct. Signs, AAC, accent, familiar words, extra time and child-directed motor proxies can be legitimate. Fade answer cues, not access.

CLASSROOM: one educator with multiple children

Model BOOKSHELF to all, without scoring the chorus. In a small 3–4-child group one six-picture teacher set suffices. Invite each child's own response but do not let a neighbor's answer become an independent score. For a full class, work individual quiet turns into an established supervised routine over several sessions. Keep all children visible and on a safe familiar activity, no isolated station. Stop/defer assessment if supervision is compromised. Unseen turns are NOT_OBSERVED. No second adult, devices or headphones are assumed. Extend invitations over time without compulsory public performance or rankings.

Source and limits

PK4.III.B.4: “Child manipulates compound words with pictorial or gestural support.” First-word deletion is one allowed slice; the official examples also include addition, substitution and combining gestures. No PK3.III.B.4 outcome is published. A fluent speaker must verify true compounding before using another language; translation of English components alone is insufficient. Family suggestions are welcome, not homework obligations. This is written design, not observed learning, educator field approval, physical print validation, delayed retention, or implemented SOLO. Use the plan for complete preparation, safety, cultural/access choices and observation details.
