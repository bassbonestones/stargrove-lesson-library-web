Facilitate real crayon and paper exploration

Companion to LESSON_01_REAL_CRAYON_AND_PAPER_TEXTURES.md. PK4.VIII.A.1 concerns using varied art materials for sensory experience and exploration, not a neat product or two-medium test. TEA 2022 p71 includes different types of paper and says most art experiences should be model/sample free. Page 8 prohibits treating guidelines as an individual assessment checklist.

Set up

For each child put two comfortable broad papers and two intact large washable non-toxic crayons on a stable reachable surface. Keep a spare and handwashing available; inspect age suitability, edges, mouthing/allergy/access plans and loose bits. No abrasive textures, tiny stubs or compelled touching. Paper/crayon substitutions may be familiar and low-cost. Printouts and paint are unnecessary. For a group use only a single visible dry-material area and keep everyone in sight; no simultaneous wet/glue stations. Reduce or defer the activity if one adult cannot safely supervise it.

ENTRY and optional MODEL

Invite: “Here are papers and crayons. What would you like to try?” Accept choice, repeat, looking or decline. Start without a model. If process or access support is needed, briefly show contact on your own spare paper, then give control back. The same material is allowed; do not supply a target picture or require matching. You may help before a first response if needed. Keep help visible in your notes, not as grounds to reject exploration.

DURING

Follow child-origin movement, noticing and experimentation. Say what you actually observe, wait and optionally ask what they want to try. Offer rather than demand a second surface. For no mark, check contrast/stability/reach and intent before explaining; for copying, allow supported play; for frustration pause and co-regulate. Physical help must be wanted and directed, not hand-over-hand coercion. Home language, AAC, sign, gaze and actions are meaningful. A child can keep smooth paper, use visual differences without touch, or use comfortable nonvisual feedback without pretending to see marks. Maintain access supports rather than fading them to prove independence.

EXIT and REVISIT

End when the child chooses or safe supervision ends: offer keep/continue/stop. Note actual materials, action, sensory attention, support and uncertainty. No brand-new materials, comparison sentence, medium quota or complete product is required. Familiar supported crayon/paper exploration is valid bounded evidence, not automatic mastery. A teacher-made picture, offered supplies alone or a peer's performance is not individual use. REVISIT familiar materials when meaningful; record later experience only when it actually happens. NOT_OBSERVED is not a learner failure. Keep private notes outside public files; no photo needed.

Delivery and scope

CO-PLAY is caregiver alongside, not correcting the drawing. ADULT-LED is responsive one-child facilitation. CLASSROOM uses shared visibility, individual choices and brief observation sampling rather than judging a group product. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: no digital or unattended physical lesson has been delivered. This candidate is written design, not field evidence. Broad art opportunities matter across a program, but this one lesson does not certify domain breadth. Follow the detailed plan for safety and extension choices; no extra adult or compulsory wet medium is presumed.
