Music-making and response: objective-level educator reference

Shared source guidance; DOT27 examines Lesson 01 only. The two PK4 source actions are distinct. No published PK3 fine-arts source codes occur in the 2022 TEA guideline; PK3 invitations are enrichment. This page is not an additional counted lesson and does not qualify sibling drafts.

What counts, and what does not

PK4.VIII.B.1: “Child participates in classroom music activities including singing, playing musical instruments, and moving to rhythms.” Source cell SHA bf36f5cb9e73b596e748b6e69def84e71174afb9fb0c550cfa639100b6b13d0c. Printed pp. 72–73 explicitly include familiar singalong and following fingerplay. Supported, familiar and imitated musical joining can therefore be legitimate evidence. Record exactly which component occurs; a tap is not proof of singing and movement too. Voices must be attributable before claiming a named child's singing. Never require public solo, original composition, new context, perfect pitch or rapid timing.

PK4.VIII.B.2: “Child responds to different musical styles through movement and play.” SHA 7fc96eb6bf7ca18616e26ed16086682e6915c9eeb65a847b1b91449a46d793ce. Provide real accessible different styles and observe movement/play responses with support disclosed. Familiar/copy-supported response is legitimate; adult-only demonstration or printed genre labels are not child response. The same meaningful movement may be used twice. No universal dance/emotion stereotype. If a simple improvised contrast cannot substantiate different styles, call it exploration, not whole-source evidence.

Publisher: Texas Education Agency, 2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines, Fine Arts pp. 72–73, original archived PDF under education/planning/source_documents. Page 8 states this is not a curriculum, assessment tool or checklist. Use descriptive observations to improve invitations, never pass/fail placement or diagnoses. Hashes track copies, not source authority or measured outcomes.

Make it usable

Start with a gentle hum or a familiar lawfully usable song, safe padded percussion, clear space and a stop signal. One adult is enough. ENTRY can follow spontaneous music. MODEL quiet sound/rest if helpful. DURING join, echo, repeat and support. EXIT is a natural close with a limited note, not an unfamiliar independent exam. REVISIT occurs later only when real. Retention, transfer and invention are separate claims; don't impose them on participation.

CLASSROOM uses one supervised footprint, not simultaneous stations; short shared voice/movement and optional voluntary pad turns. Others may listen/respond/rest. Observe individuals when safe, note afterward, rotate attention later. Chorus is not individual evidence, but group participation is a legitimate learning context. ADULT-LED one-child work can follow the child's pace privately. CO-PLAY is reciprocal exchange. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED; no packaged audio/app or claimed parity.

No loose fillers, loud surprises, unsafe large movement, forced singing/touch or mouth sharing. Preview sound, stop promptly, preserve access supports and follow relevant plans. Home-language music, AAC/sign choice, seated movement and child-directed proxy are welcome; distinguish child direction from adult execution and never infer unaided hearing from visual/tactile access. Missing equipment permits honest partial music play or postponement, not picture-based instrument credit. Keep private minimal notes without recording children or collecting family disclosures.

Lesson 01 and its facilitator contain complete teaching actions. Developmental opportunities and optional teacher cards support those actions.
