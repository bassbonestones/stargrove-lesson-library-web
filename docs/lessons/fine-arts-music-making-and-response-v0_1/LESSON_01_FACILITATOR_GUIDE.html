Facilitator 01: Rhythm Garden

Pair this companion with the complete lesson. PK4.VIII.B.1 concerns actual musical participation; PK3 is enrichment. PK4.VIII.B.2 is a separate optional styles extension. Neither is an individual readiness checklist.

One adult and one child

Prepare a safe clear seated place, voice and one broad padded percussion surface or inspected large sealed shaker. No loose fillers, loud surprises or compulsory print. Preview a gentle hum and agree a stop signal. ENTRY: “Would you like a hum, tap, little move, or quiet?” Follow the child's existing music. MODEL a soft sound and visible pause; joining/copying is valid participation. DURING echo, repeat or vary at the child's choice. Support remains available; no need to fade essential access. EXIT simply notices an actual chosen action before “one more or finished?” Familiar music, imperfect timing and help do not erase participation. REVISIT later when welcome, with actual dated notes, not an invented retention result.

If the child wants help, model or join; if they withdraw, stop. If the adult alone plays, describe adult demonstration rather than child execution. If a child directs a consenting adult proxy, preserve child direction separately from physical motor action. Record singing, instrument and movement components separately; don't turn a tap into all three or demand all three at once. A spoken response is not automatically sung. No public solo, perfect pitch, mandatory new material or musical talent diagnosis.

One teacher with a class

CLASSROOM uses one visible supervised footprint, not simultaneous stations. Begin with a soft shared hum/song and seated movements. One safe pad can circulate only through supervised, clean, voluntary turns while others make voices/movements, listen, respond or rest. Notice one or two focal children when safe and note afterward. Rotate focal attention later; chorus and attendance are not individual evidence. If staffing/space/sound prevent safe delivery, shorten to a gentle familiar voice routine, defer instruments, or use an already safely supervised smaller group without abandoning the rest.

Source, access and optional expansion

Accept permitted familiar songs, home-language music, child invention and culturally respectful family choices without requiring family contributions. Simple live hum is a no-purchase fallback. Describe visual/tactile/signed/AAC access honestly, never infer hearing or physical execution from a proxy. Keep quiet opt-out within supervision, protect personal notes, and use relevant existing access plans.

For PK4.VIII.B.2 only, provide genuinely different accessible musical styles and observe actual movement/play responses, with help disclosed. Repeated meaningful movement, familiar music and welcomed imitation are allowed; independent creation is a different claim. If an improvised acoustic contrast does not clearly establish different styles, call it exploratory play and defer B.2 inference. Printing the words “different styles” cannot perform music.

CO-PLAY is reciprocal child/adult musical exchange. ADULT-LED is the one-child route above. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED, no functional app or parity. Source guideline pp. 72–73 supplies musical actions and familiar-song/fingerplay examples; p. 8 forbids use as a checklist. All child outcomes, classroom efficacy and external educator approval remain unverified. Optional three-page teacher cards support, but do not govern or replace, these source-faithful decisions.
