01 · My movement, my choice

Original first-party soft-mat teacher model — child may choose another accessible motion

Teacher's OWN MODEL: “I can choose to open my hand, then pause it. Now I gently move it toward my soft mat and stop.” The learner never has to copy adult's exact body part, range, pace or direction.

PK3 source: genuine child-origin bodily control. This lesson teaches start, adjust and stop gradually; not all must appear at once. Record adult assistance separately. Motion of a toy/avatar/caregiver alone is not child's body control. A child may decline. No timed steps, balancing challenge, exercise strain, standing, touching or medical assessment.

02 · My body is here; people choose their space

PK4 teacher-owned adult demonstration; do not impose a fixed distance rule

Own body in space

Noticing where my own hand, supported arm or body is near a safe shared work area. A cartoon position alone isn't actual self-space awareness.

Other person's boundary

A real person may express their boundary now or earlier, or a child may respectfully leave their clearly occupied workspace undisturbed without being asked. Never infer private preferences from silence.

Teacher-owned MODEL (second adult optional): With two willing adults, one asks permission and honors a real answer. With only one adult, narrate a hypothetical exchange on teacher props; an imagined person is never child-source evidence. No forced contact.

PK4 needs both awareness of own body relative to real space and respecting genuine personal boundaries. Reciting “keep distance,” obeying a puppet or correctly pointing to an avatar doesn't establish both. Cultural and sensory distance preferences vary. No touch, proximity or physical movement is forced.
