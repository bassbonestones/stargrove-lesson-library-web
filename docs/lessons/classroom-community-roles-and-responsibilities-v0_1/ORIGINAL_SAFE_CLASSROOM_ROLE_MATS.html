01 · Real useful safe roles for our classroom

Use actual safe classroom information and actual child/teacher consent. A role matters only if real classmates can USE the child's contribution.

Classroom activity picture helper

Choose an accurate big picture so classmates know where a safe teacher-approved story or art activity is happening.

Nonprivate group message helper

Show a broad picture or child-authored safe message about the class's current book/art topic, with teacher factual verification.

Group-choice display helper

Help show real teacher-approved safe optional activity choices. Classmates can decline; adult manages the choices.

Pause, switch or ask for help

A child can choose an accessible role, use sign/AAC/home language, get adult assistance or say no.

PK3: Real child contribution with ADULT ASSISTANCE is developmentally valid. PK4: VARIOUS genuinely different useful roles over natural real experiences, not a costume, leadership title or forced quota. Children are NEVER responsible for peer supervision, discipline, emergency care, medication, private records or dangerous cleaning.

Adult verifies actual class use

The teacher checks safe activities, role accuracy, class access, peer choice and who actually benefits. Teacher assistance may support child's meaningful selection; adult-only finished work cannot be credited to child.

02 · Three different useful class roles

01 · Safe wayfinding and welcome

The real class has two teacher-approved optional learning locations. Learner chooses true broad picture card for one area and directs adult to display it accessibly. Real group children can find their chosen class activity; no child escort duty or peer monitoring.

02 · True nonprivate shared information

Today's actual approved group story/art topic has a big teacher-approved symbol. Learner selects or explains that real class message, and teacher checks it. Do NOT use student names, absences, family details, behavioral incidents or medical information.

03 · Community plan choice help

Two real safe group activities are available. Learner helps show actual big choice cards while teacher receives classmates' preferences and keeps class supervision. A peer may choose differently; helper never enforces classmates' choices.

These are different kinds of community contributions, not merely three attempts at tidying. Actual class members must benefit. A fictional game class is good for practice but NOT evidence of a real classroom responsibility.
