Subitizing — explicit PK3 small-group foundation and PK4 1–6 development

Mandatory developmental teaching companion for all three original subitizing methods · October 30, 2026 · E2.2 draft. The three complete written lessons are pedagogically reusable methods, not a single session in which a three-year-old must immediately perceive three or a four-year-old must immediately perceive six. Explicitly introduce small-group perception, then varied pattern transfer, then the upper size and entire published range. Do not coerce speed, assign a disability label, infer cognition from response latency, or claim functioning SOLO software. Genuine child trials and qualified educator review are open.

Source action is perceiving quantity without serial item-by-item counting; the adult cannot read a hidden child's internal strategy, and correct fast answers alone are not evidence of it. Correct serial counting is valuable counting, not independent proof of subitizing.

Graduated formative instruction and age-specific EXIT boundaries

A short session may last 5–12 minutes, with comfortable unrestricted accessible viewing and a separate actual later revisit. Do not flash cards too fast or use a stopwatch as a “subitizing” diagnostic. Educator records first total, voluntarily described grouping, overt one-by-one tracking, accessibility/visual conditions and uncertainty. Child may use speech, sign/AAC, a child-created pattern explanation, or an accessible choice; don't claim certainty of their internal recognition strategy.

Pathway Explicit model and safe child invitation New mini-check and adaptation
PK3 S3-1: one and two Adult presents teacher-only two large safe shapes in unusual spacing: “I can notice these two together. I didn't need to point to each one.” Then child sees a different unseen one or two object group. “How many do you notice together?” Capture first response, no adult label. On an easy 1/2 group with correct child-origin perceptual explanation/no observed counting, offer new layout; if child counts serially, validate counting and re-model perceptual grouping on a different card.
PK3 S3-2: three Show a new non-die 3 arrangement, e.g. an obtuse triangle rather than familiar row/dice pattern. Adult might describe “I can see two together and another” on a separate model, without implying three-dot grouping requires decomposition source credit. Child notices a different unseen irregular three and explains/points to perceptual grouping if accessible; avoid predictable 1→2→3 sequencing or a speed pass. Unknown private strategy stays UNKNOWN, not invented mastery.
PK3 S3-3: flexible one–three Mix true unfamiliar irregular 1-, 2- and 3-spot arrangements with shifts in spacing/object type, not simply identical dice faces. Full new 1, 2, 3 candidate EXIT and a later actual-date changed-material REVISIT. Accurate overt serial counting is counting evidence, not independent no-count evidence; record strategy ambiguity rather than forcing performance or assigning a false score.
PK4 S4-1: retrieve one–three Begin with new 1-, 2-, 3-object arrays, and explicitly teach unfamiliar shape variation if previous success came only from familiar pip patterns. Uncued fresh 1–3 first answers and uncertainty; a PK4 child recognizing six but not unseen two is not “full 1–6 proven.”
PK4 S4-2: four Adult models four as a new visually structured two-and-two on model-only cards, not four taps in sequence. Invite child to view a new nonstandard four arrangement. New one-off first response + grouping description; if child counts one-by-one, acknowledge valid mathematics and teach seeing clusters using another separate four pattern, then ask an unrelated fresh four.
PK4 S4-3: five and six Gradually introduce five as visible 2+3 or another grouped arrangement, then six as 3+3 or 2+2+2, only when grouping is discernible. No mandatory mental-arithmetic or symbol-writing requirement. Fresh unknown five and six arrays, some irregular and some grouped, interleaved with smaller quantities so size isn't predictable. Record that 6 can be difficult; don't demand instant response in a flash.
PK4 S4-4: flexible one–six Vary spacing, orientation and object type (but not confusing rotations of labels or unknown size), no quantity written on learner-facing arrays. A child may notice the total in groups and describe it their way. Full candidate 1,2,3,4,5,6 fresh EXIT (in original teacher guide G–I and P–R only when truly unseen to that child) and later actual-day independent changed-material REVISIT. Don't claim only 4–6 proves complete PK4 range.

Instructional judgment: A child is ready for a richer teaching example when they can notice a current small unfamiliar group in a couple of meaningfully different layouts, possibly articulating grouping without visible serial count, while retaining honest uncertainty about hidden cognition. Do not define arbitrary reaction-time bands or hard percent thresholds. When the child says they counted, record counting without judgment; provide a fresh pattern later. Split multiple trials to preserve comfort and access. Children ready for six need not be prevented from exploring six merely because a teaching mini-stage is unfinished; grade-specific source evidence is still separately recorded.

Application to each distinct original method

Method 01 — See the Whole Set (TEACHER_GUIDE.md)

  • PK3 S3-1: Model different two-dot arrangement; present unnumbered unseen one and two. S3-2: three as irregular triangle and gentle 2+1 grouping, then new three without adult gesture. S3-3: full unfamiliar 1–3 dot card exit, distinct from all practice; no guaranteed strategy inference from an instant-looking answer.
  • PK4 S4-1: Start with untrained lower-band 1–3 cards, not immediate 4–6 only. S4-2: Four in new 2+2 grouping; S4-3: five 2+3 and six 3+3, then unpracticed alternatives. S4-4: Six total distinct size-check trials from 1–6 in mixed order; original dot kit G–I plus P–R serve as grade-PK4 exit only if G–I have not been used for that child earlier. If they have, make genuinely fresh lower-band arrangements rather than pretending novelty.
  • False credit: Child counts an unknown six correctly while watching each dot: helpful count result, not independently demonstrated subitizing.

Method 02 — Make a Group, Notice a Group (LESSON_02_GROUP_MAKER.md)

  • PK3 S3-1: Child chooses to be MAKER of one or two safe large objects; adult observes creative placement, but that is not subitizing evidence. Exchange roles: adult privately prepares a different, unannounced one/two pattern and child is VIEWER. S3-2: child practices viewer on unseen three-pattern after adult modeled separate 2+1; S3-3: viewer sees independently new 1–3 groups they did not build.
  • PK4 S4-1: Child as viewer of adult-made previously unseen 1–3, with changed material and spatial arrangement. S4-2: new viewer four, maybe grouped 2+2; S4-3: adult creates unseen five and six in meaningful clusters without showing total; S4-4: child as VIEWER only on new independently hidden 1–6, without knowing what they previously made as maker.
  • False credit: Child physically makes five dots and later reports “five” when they themselves knew the intended target: that's a construction activity, not an unknown novel viewer subitizing response.

Method 03 — Same Number, Different Shape? (LESSON_03_QUANTITY_COMPARE.md)

  • PK3 S3-1: Adult demonstrates two distinct one/two groups that can look different, without numbered labels; child views a new pair and notices quantity, then independently recognizes a single fresh group. S3-2: Use unseen three-versus-two and three-versus-three pairs, varying spatial stretch to avoid merely choosing longer row. S3-3: The required fresh single-group 1–3 uncued EXIT follows the comparison; correct “same/different” alone is not enough.
  • PK4 S4-1: Small unseen one–three pairs, followed by a novel single group. S4-2: four-versus-three and four-versus-four non-die comparisons; S4-3: five/six equal and near-one foils, without telling child numerical answers, followed by unknown single-group 5/6. S4-4: after varied comparisons, fresh independent 1–6 single-group recognition check with child-origin grouping if possible.
  • False credit: Child identifies “same” on two matching pictures by symmetry/color without knowing either quantity; must still recognize new single group's numeric amount without serial-count evidence.

Four authentic delivery routes across multiple stages

  • ADULT-LED SINGLE CHILD: Select stage-appropriate only-few unseen material configurations and use exact source action, not a rapid visual quiz. Adult models on separate items, gives specific contingent feedback, notes first results and unknown strategy, and plans real later-date revisit. Allow access adaptations (large high contrast/tactile), home language, sign/AAC.
  • CO-PLAY: Child may design arrangements (maker), adult sees and describes, then adult secretly prepares a different independent viewer array for child's source evidence. Use progression stages so child begins noticing 1–2 before 3, later 4–6. Keep collaboration genuine, but adult label before response = aided.
  • TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Use staged mixed small groups within one classroom; e.g. one group novel one/two, another three, another five/six. A projected class “five!” shout doesn't prove twenty children's hidden strategies. Record only individual first answers and any grouping actually observed; use full-range exit later across small groups and honest missing sizes.
  • SOLO future spec (NOT IMPLEMENTED): Original narrated lesson should allow 1–2, 3, then 1–3 transfer; for PK4 revisit 1–3 before four, five/six and 1–6 transfer. No press-and-count animation in the initial trial, no timed-guess grading, large nonreading access and optional child-originated grouping explanation/shape highlight. No reliable internal mental-state detection from selection speed; if unobservable, store candidate/unknown strategy, never fake mastery. No running app or child independence/parity tests exist.

Evidence and grade progression boundary

Every stage has an ENTRY with new arrays before model, adult explicit MODEL on distinct arrays, child-owned DURING on changed forms, an uncoached mini-EXIT for current small-size band, and a real-date REVISIT plan. Mini-exits are not replacements for full grade-specific 1–3 or 1–6 independent source-range evidence; each unoffered or inaccessible array is explicitly NOT_OBSERVED, and an unknown strategy stays UNKNOWN rather than an invented pass. A correct value after overt serial counting is valid mathematics but not this independent source action; a rapid choice, no visibly observed count and sparse subjective explanation leaves internal strategy uncertain. No child assessment, qualified educator acceptance, solo implementation or four-mode equivalence is claimed by writing this schedule.

Retrospective stage-by-stage learning-differences review — November 1, 2026

A child needing additional viewing time, a quieter setting, accessible AAC, predictable reveal, vision accommodation or more guided teaching must not automatically be judged unable to subitize. Nevertheless, counting aloud/finger-by-finger or being told the sum during EXIT cannot be credited as independent instant small-group perception. The instructional adaptation preserves the official range rather than reducing it.

PK3 S3-1

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: Start with genuinely unfamiliar one/two non-die groups on calm high-contrast mats. Offer child-controlled viewing without stopwatch, model noticing an OTHER array, then fade adult explanatory language.

New accessible evidence: Show new one and two arrangements at differing positions; record first spontaneous quantity or AAC response before teacher label, marking any visible serial pointing separately.

PK3 S3-2

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: Introduce genuinely irregular three objects spaced for perception, not a memorized dice triangle. Allow revisiting one/two without a comparison race; model two-plus-one grouping on a DIFFERENT set, not the probe.

New accessible evidence: Present a new non-dice irregular three with no adult rhythmic tap or counting soundtrack; preserve uncertainty about private strategies.

PK3 S3-3

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: Mix fresh one/two/three layouts, safely changing spacing and object type after preview of procedure. Provide optional quiet/individual presentation to reduce group chatter without changing quantity.

New accessible evidence: On unknown groups for all 1–3 quantities, record accessible first response on fresh forms, observable counting separately and actual later-date transfer as pending.

PK4 S4-1

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: Begin PK4 with novel 1–3 small-group perceptual check; reteach actual gaps without assuming older age means already having this skill or a disability.

New accessible evidence: Use new shuffled unseen 1–3 groups and record actual first perception without prior teachers labelled array.

PK4 S4-2

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: Introduce four in irregular symmetric and asymmetric arrangements; model recognition by noticing subgroups on DIFFERENT examples while avoiding a required verbal decomposition that would add a separate objective.

New accessible evidence: Use new unknown four without finger-by-finger serial counting or adult supplied two-plus-two labels; look for authentic first quantity response.

PK4 S4-3

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: For five/six avoid familiar dice or ten-frame copied patterns. Teach using short optional clustering practice and large distinguishable objects, then remove pattern teaching cues from probes and avoid timed flashes.

New accessible evidence: Show genuinely new five and six groupings with no number labels, stoplight timer or adult counting; record first answer and uncertainty about private count strategy.

PK4 S4-4

Responsive teaching and prompt fade: For mixed 1–6 allow short repeated accessible sessions and stable modality; each child sees distinct genuine sets in neutral surroundings. Do not reduce official range to four because of attention or sensory preferences.

New accessible evidence: Use actual unseen groups 1–6, observe child-directed response and later-date transfer separately; a correct copied dice face or adult-coached grouping does not establish independent subitizing.

This internal review alone is not special-education validation, a finding about individual children, a live SOLO implementation, or demonstrated effectiveness in the four modes.
