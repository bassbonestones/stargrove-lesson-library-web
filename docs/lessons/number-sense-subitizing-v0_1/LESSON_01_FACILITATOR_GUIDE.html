Companion facilitator guide 01 — Whole-pattern noticing

Paired plan: Lesson 01, official PK3.V.A.4 and PK4.V.A.4. Adult rationale/troubleshooting, NOT a fourth plan. The objective guide sets exact bounds.

How the perceptual mechanism helps

The learner notices an entire small group rather than systematically touching/counting each item. The adult models on a separate unnumbered original dot array, never pretends to see inside child's cognition. A quick correct guess or familiar dice image is NOT proof of absence of hidden counting; evidence is a candidate with explicit uncertainty and multiple genuinely unfamiliar arrangements. PK3 up to THREE; PK4 up to SIX, including small 1–3 and upper 4–6.

Preparation and phase script

Use original unnumbered first-party dot arrays or large nonchoking actual discs, adult-only correct count key hidden, accessible distance/lighting/tactile and language adaptations, different unmodeled arrays for ENTRY/MODEL/DURING/EXIT and another real later REVISIT. Actual source kit IDs A–R, not S1–S10: PK3 entry A/B, model C, practice D/E/F, novel exit G/H/I; PK4 entry J/K, model L, practice M/N/O, novel upper-range exit P/Q/R. PK4's smaller exit 1–3 can use G/H/I only if that child has never seen them or a newly prepared unknown 1–3 equivalent; never score only P/Q/R as full PK4 1–6 range. All printed card IDs are organizational labels without numeral answers. Never use a coercive flash or timed scoring. ENTRY: show genuinely new array, “What number do you notice?” Wait and record unprompted answer, any overt item-by-item count, voluntarily offered perceptual group and access. MODEL: on completely different adult-only two-dot array or 2-plus-3 PK4 example, explain seeing a whole group without enumerating; verify by counting only after child had opportunity to notice. DURING: invite new patterns, “What do you notice together?” If learner counts, affirm that valid skill, model grouped noticing on unrelated pattern, then try another novel practice array. Remove arrows, outlines, correct number display and familiar layouts gradually. EXIT: fresh PK3 novel 1–3 set range or PK4 1–6 range including six, no answered cards. Record original first quantity and visible strategy BEFORE later count or hints; not all conditions seen means partial coverage. REVISIT: genuine different calendar day and novel arrangements, NOT_OBSERVED until done.

Mistake-specific feedback

Child counts 1,2,3,4 overtly and gets four: count is useful, but no no-count source inferred; teach a 2+2 grouping on DIFFERENT adult example and offer novel item set. Child recognizes one canonical die face but not unfamiliar pattern: expand novel sparse/dense non-dice layouts. Child guesses quickly incorrectly: invite learner to inspect grouping on practice rather than reward haste. Child cannot see dots: resolve accessible materials and specialist guidance before judging numerical concept, never claim visual no-counting from adult verbal count supplied. Adult prepoints each group or gives “two and three”: classify supported H, not independent source.

Seven profiles and written mode responsibilities

Attention/executive: visually uncluttered new arrays and optional pause, no rushed demand; language: individually authored number signs/AAC/home language; literacy: no numerals or word decoding; numeracy: build early 1–2 perceptual anchors before range; autism/sensory: no flashing or compulsory speed; intellectual/developmental: gradual 1–2, then larger after safe repetition; physical/motor/hearing/vision: large high contrast, reliable accessible alternatives and explicit uncertainty about conceptual equivalence. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: software must record first response before hint/counting animation but cannot see inner cognition. CO-PLAY: caregiver does not reveal key. ADULT-LED: individual nonleading response. CLASSROOM: unseen individual cards, no shared count chorus. Actual child, professional approval, later date retention, physical print, operational SOLO and cross-mode parity NOT VERIFIED.
