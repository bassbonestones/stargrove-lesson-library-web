Companion facilitator guide 03 — Different layouts, same perceived number

Pair: Lesson 03 for PK3.V.A.4 and PK4.V.A.4. Companion teacher guidance, not fourth counted plan. Objective educator guide retains exact official source wording.

Different teaching mechanism and source-bound exit

Contrasting spatially different equal-quantity or near-one unequal arrays helps the child notice that shape/spacing alone does not determine quantity. The extra same/different comparison is an instructional scaffold, not by itself the original subitizing action. Independently scoring the actual quantity of a FRESH individual unnumbered group without observed serial counting is required for a candidate. Do not infer hidden no-count strategy from fast answer or a correct equality choice.

Material setup and five phases

Adult prepares original distinct unnumbered neutral dot sets, accurate hidden quantity key, high-contrast/tactile accessible versions and safe large washable physical pieces; avoid repeating same die arrangement. Keep ENTRY pair(s) separate from adult MODEL equal/different pair, child DURING familiarization, untouched EXIT SINGLE-group novel arrangements, and later calendar REVISIT. PK3 bounds ≤3; PK4 up to 6, including 1–3. ENTRY (match the paired main plan's actual sets): For PK3, show an unknown equal 2-vs-2 pair in two unlike arrangements, then a separate new 3-object group; for PK4, show an unknown unequal 4-vs-5 pair, then a separate new 6-object group. First ask the child to identify each actual quantity separately, recording the raw answers and any observed serial counting before asking whether the paired groups have the same number. Do not invent an unprepared second pair or claim both equality relations were observed for the same child at ENTRY. If another truly unseen pair is instructionally helpful, record it as a separately prepared additional task, not the already-defined E-COMP; the MODEL and DURING phases intentionally teach both equal and unequal patterns. Preserve first-response originality, access, and uncertainty. MODEL: Adult uses separate two-versus-two and two-versus-three (PK3) or four/5–6 (PK4) to show that spreading doesn't add objects and adding a new actual object changes total. This is teaching, not child source. DURING: Let learner compare new shapes and check by physical rearrangement AFTER initial view. If child says identical shapes imply equal, contrast adult-only same-shaped different counts; if child serial-counts, validate and separately model noticing grouped total on a new layout. Fade grouping lines, adult quantity labels, preexisting row color and side hints. EXIT: On completely fresh SINGLE unnumbered groups, PK3 child may recognize 1–3; PK4 1–6 including upper band. Record original quantity and plausible grouping/observed item-by-item behavior, not equality-only response, and maintain uncertainty about internal counting. REVISIT: New real date with independent new layouts, NOT_OBSERVED until actual.

Feedback and access differentiation

Adult says “they look the same” and child echoes: don't score child's perceptual quantity; ask new single group later. Child counts five serially: counting is real math but not evidence of subitizing; model a two-and-three grouping on DIFFERENT practice array and re-probe a novel one. Child names four from four-dot familiar die card: try irregular, unfamiliar positions. Child is slower but clearly notices two-and-two: don't impose stopwatch; interpret expression with qualified team input, preserve uncertainty. Sight, hearing or motor limits require careful accessible non-answer-supplying material; no false equivalence.

Seven profiles and written routes

Attention/executive: uncluttered new material and optional breaks; language/communication: original semantically chosen AAC/sign/home language; literacy: no printed numeral requirement; numeracy: perceptual subgroups before complex pairs; autism/sensory: no flashing or timed display; intellectual/developmental: start one/two and scaffold novel three then upper grade; physical/motor/hearing/vision: accessible safe high-contrast/raised dots and specialist review of whether source inference is valid. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future novel single-group test must not show correct count as auto animation before response. CO-PLAY: adult checks whether child saw pattern before rearranging. ADULT-LED: capture first quantity, not just same/different. CLASSROOM: each child gets fresh unmodeled array, no public countdown or chorus. Child outcomes, qualified educator acceptance, actual later REVISIT, physical print and delivered four-mode parity remain NOT VERIFIED.
