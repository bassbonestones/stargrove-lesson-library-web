Objective-level educator guide — Genuine small-set subitizing

Role: Cross-lesson source guide, NOT a counted teaching method. Original Texas 2022 Math V.A.4 official research cells are SHA pinned; original PDF extraction/concordance and qualified educator assessment remain pending.

PK3.V.A.4 (official SHA 2bcfc37ee04f8c77b148bbc6a7315447a7c65912bb38d358953e678b0fa7fbdf)

“Child instantly recognizes the quantity of up to 3 objects without counting (subitizes).” PK3 developmental progression in DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md first teaches noticing 1 and 2, then an unfamiliar 3 and mixed 1–3 before checking actual full range. “Instantly” must NOT become an abusive stopwatch, forced flash card, or visually inaccessible picture; adequate fair viewing time is necessary. Independent evidence is child’s own quantity recognition across genuinely unfamiliar 1–3 sets with no OBSERVED serial counting, supported by self-explanation when possible. Because silent inner counting cannot be inspected, classify it as candidate / uncertain, never externally verified mastery from quick or accurate response alone.

PK4.V.A.4 (official SHA 708f91809299d7f817915c5c0754ee5761eeba72dbae589df192ec52f25697ea)

“Child instantly recognizes the quantity of up to 6 objects without counting (subitizes).” Revisit 1–3 and progress to new 4, then 5–6 and independently unfamiliar mixed 1–6. Seeing two small meaningful groups as one whole may support recognition of 5–6; DO NOT substitute serial recitation, number-name matching, symbol reading, decomposition calculation, or a correct count of one familiar dice pattern. If the learner has only attempted 4–6, label partial PK4 range. If six unseen trials would overload the learner, spread independent novel probes across real dates, retaining actual boundaries and no fabricated completion.

Three distinct full teaching mechanisms

  1. Lesson 01 — unnumbered whole perceptual pattern recognition with facilitator 01: investigate noticing small groups before item-by-item count, separating spontaneous first response from later verification.
  2. Lesson 02 — builder/viewer role reversal with facilitator 02: constructing one's own counted objects teaches arrangement but independent subitizing is assessed ONLY as viewer of a different unknown grouping.
  3. Lesson 03 — quantity preserved across new spatial forms with facilitator 03: perceptual contrast and near-one distractors lead to a separate novel single-group quantity probe, not merely a “same/different” paired answer.

Teaching and assessment principles

ENTRY: truly previously unseen unnumbered small group, first response before adult numerical cue. MODEL: educator shows a DIFFERENT safe simple group, describes perceiving grouping without a serial count, and validates after the first observation. DURING: child explores distinct new patterns, with separate cognitive feedback if item-by-item count observed (“counting is useful; here's another way to see the whole”), while teacher gradually removes familiar spatial patterns, quantity labels and grouping prompts. EXIT: fresh novel unnumbered group(s) appropriate to exact PK3/PK4 bound, child’s first natural quantity and observation strategy recorded before any counting verification or feedback. REVISIT: another genuinely later calendar day and new novel patterns; NOT_OBSERVED until real.

Misconceptions: a five-dot familiar die arrangement may indicate memorized picture; test unfamiliar geometry. Matching two groups correctly doesn't mean child knows each quantity without counting; ask two independent unseen single groups. Fast response may be guessing, while thoughtful response may be meaningful whole-set recognition; no time thresholds. Overt tapping/dot-by-dot words show counting, which is legitimate mathematical development but not evidence of without counting source. Missing clear access to individual dots must be resolved before inferring a mathematical misconception; never claim an unobservable cognitive strategy is independently proven.

Seven differentiated needs and four written routes

Attention/executive: comfortable focus, brief but not rushed new patterns, predictable breaks; language/communication: meaningful child-composed sign/AAC/home-language number responses; literacy/phonology: numeral word recognition unnecessary; numeracy: structured two/three perceptual grouping before expanded quantities; autism/sensory: no forced flashing or timed demands; intellectual/developmental: child-paced whole group 1–2 then stage-appropriate novel extension; physical/motor/hearing/vision: large high-contrast/raised safe objects, individual accessibility review, no false claim that tactile sequential exploration demonstrates visual instantaneous recognition. Keep valid alternative learning valued without unsupported source-equivalence. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future accessible independent interactions must protect first-answer and avoid clue animation; digital correct clicks are not proof of no counting. CO-PLAY: trusted adult prepares unseen patterns and only separately verifies count. ADULT-LED: educator probes actual first strategy and fades supports. CLASSROOM: individual unseen arrays, not chorally count-spoken answers. External specialist approval, real child outcomes, actual later REVISIT, physical print validation, functional SOLO and parity all NOT VERIFIED.
