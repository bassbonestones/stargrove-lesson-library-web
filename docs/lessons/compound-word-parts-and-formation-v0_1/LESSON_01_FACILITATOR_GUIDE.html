Source objective and purpose

PK3.III.B.3: “Child recognizes the individual words in a compound word.” PK4.III.B.3: “Child uses two familiar base words to form a compound word with pictorial or gestural supports.” TEA 2022 comprehensive guide p.32 was directly text-checked November 13, 2026. PK3 full-cell SHA e5795894f87e3f6cd47927b8051b1b5f999a28e343dd8334efbe88cc6f69d0dc; PK4 6d04061d7a84a1711ce76b1fe07304fa0c14a3888ad13f20b82e2e93b9b83bfd. Published examples include a child adding BOW or DROP after teacher RAIN. Neither novelty nor spoken English is a separate requirement.

Familiarity boundary: For formation, the two base meanings must be familiar; the resulting meaningful conventional compound may be new to the child. Do not reject a first-time SUNFLOWER construction by a child who knows SUN and FLOWER. Recognition begins with an interpretable genuine whole-word prompt; a child hearing STARFISH for the first time may recognize familiar STAR and FISH. Prior familiarity with the completed compound is not a requirement for either target. “Familiar word” checking below is an available comfortable option, never a PK4 prerequisite that the completed compound was previously known.

Prepare and start

One adult, one child; a few comfortable minutes. Use broad sun/flower and rain/coat pictures or gestures; book/shelf is an optional model. Printables are optional, not worksheets; adult cuts only large pieces. Establish familiar meanings, accessible language/sign/AAC/gaze and safe comfortable space. Invite flower or rain words, watching or a break. Do not look at the sun, handle unknown plants, buy supplies or use an app.

ENTRY: PK3 hears SUNFLOWER and identifies its words. PK4 receives SUN and FLOWER with pictures or gestures and forms SUNFLOWER. Repeat input naturally; wait without a timer. Record ambiguous or absent opportunity honestly.

MODEL: “Bookshelf: book and shelf. Book ... shelf ... bookshelf.” Point to meaningful pictures. If unknown, teach the selected word itself. Model to the child; no second adult is needed. Immediate copying is supported practice, not independent formation.

DURING: Child chooses pairs for a pretend garden/rain scene or simply plays with words. Follow valid alternate compounds: rain plus bow/drop is welcome. If child knows one part, acknowledge it and teach the other. If clapping syllables, contrast meaningful words with beats. If a creative nonstandard word appears, welcome the invention while distinguishing a conventional compound; check language context before judging. Offer quiet, sign/AAC, picture choice or child-directed movement. Do not withdraw essential supports.

EXIT: Offer a familiar word without immediately supplying its answer. PK3 whole→parts; PK4 bases→compound, with supports, including child-contributed second base. Record the actual response and assistance. Same taught word can show the source action. Optional RAINCOAT transfer is only useful when rain/coat are known and the child wants it. End for fatigue, disinterest or useful enough practice, not after an arbitrary correct-answer quota.

REVISIT: Invite a familiar word on a real later day for retention, or a different known one for generalization; neither is observed now. Do not make later-day novelty necessary for retention.

What observations mean

Write plain facts rather than U/H/A/X codes: what adult/peer supplied, what the child did, access retained, and whether meaning was interpretable. An immediate echo is not formation evidence; a later purposeful response on the same word may be. A child-controlled AAC symbol is not disqualified simply because it displays the compound. No opportunity/decline/inaccessible language is NOT_OBSERVED; uncertain support is uncertain, not adult-only and not child failure. Picture selection must communicate the lexical relation rather than an adult-directed matching routine. No diagnosis or mastery claim from one trial.

One educator with a class

Use a short familiar book/shelf model in a shared visible space. Children explore broad pictures in optional pairs, gesture or participate in an established safely supervised quiet activity. No extra adult or separate testing room. Take a brief focal response while retaining sight of the class. Stop observing when supervision requires attention. Rotate future opportunities fairly across willing children, including quieter children; record unobserved turns as such. Peer answers and choruses are teaching, not each child's individual result. A later individual conversational response can clarify evidence without punitive silence or compulsory new words.

Access and privacy

Use linguistically valid compounds using familiar base meanings in the child's language, not literal English translations. If unfamiliar meanings or interpreter uncertainty block inference, teach what is accessible and record the limit. Follow individual plans; invite available family language knowledge without requiring homework or family participation. No forced speech, worksheets, diagnosis, public ranking, photos or recordings. Keep any notes confidential and outside repository/public pages.

Human ADULT-LED and CO-PLAY designs are written, CLASSROOM is supervision-aware; SOLO NOT IMPLEMENTED. Child outcomes, educator trial, physical/tactile print access, retention and comparative effectiveness remain unobserved. Sibling lessons are not accepted by this companion's revision.
