Compound-word family: developmental choices, not a checklist

Verified source and scope

TEA 2022 comprehensive guidelines printed p.32: PK3.III.B.3: “Child recognizes the individual words in a compound word.” Full-cell SHA e5795894f87e3f6cd47927b8051b1b5f999a28e343dd8334efbe88cc6f69d0dc. PK4.III.B.3: “Child uses two familiar base words to form a compound word with pictorial or gestural supports.” SHA 6d04061d7a84a1711ce76b1fe07304fa0c14a3888ad13f20b82e2e93b9b83bfd. Original PDF text, including the adjacent child-behavior examples, checked November 13, 2026. A child may contribute BOW or DROP after teacher RAIN, then form RAINBOW or RAINDROP. Two adult-supplied words are one route, not the only valid route.

PK3 whole→meaningful word parts differs from PK4 familiar word parts→compound. Neither is spelling, syllable counting, deleting parts (III.B.4), compulsory English speech or invention of an unheard word. Pictures/gestures are supported explicitly; meaningful child-directed AAC/sign/gaze and culturally valid home-language compounds remain legitimate. Do not literally translate English compounds without checking their linguistic validity.

Familiarity boundary: For formation, the two base meanings must be familiar; the resulting meaningful conventional compound may be new to the child. Do not reject a first-time SUNFLOWER construction by a child who knows SUN and FLOWER. Recognition begins with an interpretable genuine whole-word prompt; a child hearing STARFISH for the first time may recognize familiar STAR and FISH. Prior familiarity with the completed compound is not a requirement for either target. “Familiar word” checking below is an available comfortable option, never a PK4 prerequisite that the completed compound was previously known.

Local optional progression

These example labels preserve family references but are not publisher stages, grade-placement thresholds or required sequences. Select familiar meanings and the child's interest; older children can use prerequisites too. - PK3 C3-1: hear SUNFLOWER and recognize SUN/FLOWER. - PK3 C3-2: explore RAINCOAT recognition when RAIN/COAT are known. - PK3 C3-3: explore BOOKSHELF or a child-selected familiar compound. - PK3 C3-4: optional changed-example recognition; lack of transfer does not erase observed familiar-word recognition. - PK4 C4-1: SUN/FLOWER formation with pictures/gestures. - PK4 C4-2: RAIN/COAT formation with retained support. - PK4 C4-3: BOOK/SHELF or another familiar combination. - PK4 C4-4: child supplies a meaningful second base after an adult first base. - PK4 C4-5: optional formation across changed known examples, not a requirement for all source-action evidence.

Teaching cycle

ENTRY: invite a choice, observe a meaningful age-appropriate first response if the child is ready. MODEL: demonstrate BOOKSHELF to the child, or teach the selected compound directly when needed. A different example can help separate checking from immediate copying; no ban on teaching the target. DURING: supported playful word exploration with contingent feedback, meaning clarification and access retained. EXIT: an interpretable child response on a familiar taught or different known compound; record prompt and support, never automatic credit from a chorus or immediate echo. A different example tests generalization separately. REVISIT: record an actually later occasion; repeat a known word for retention or change it for generalization. Future learning remains NOT_OBSERVED.

Practical routes and safeguards

ADULT-LED one child and CO-PLAY use optional broad first-party pictures or drawings/gestures, a few comfortable minutes and no device. CLASSROOM keeps one educator in sight of all children, uses familiar safe activities while brief focal responses occur and defers observations whenever supervision needs attention. A group response is practice, not evidence for each child. SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED; no app, observed effectiveness or digital parity claim.

Teach word meanings before interpreting unfamiliar vocabulary as a compound-skill gap. Respond to one-part recognition by acknowledging it and teaching the missing part; contrast syllable beats with meaningful words without shaming. Welcome playful invented words while separately checking conventional compounds and the child's language context. No required worksheets, reading, small pieces, plants, photographs or recordings. Keep notes private and outside shared repositories.

Record adult/peer input, child action, access and uncertainty in plain words. Immediate answer copying differs from a later self-directed response on the same familiar word. Unknown provenance is uncertain, not proof of failure. A purposeful whole-compound AAC symbol is not automatically a supplied answer; inspect the actual child's use of both bases. Decline or no accessible opportunity is NOT_OBSERVED, not inability. Physical/tactile print usability, child outcomes, educator trial and delayed retention have not been verified.
