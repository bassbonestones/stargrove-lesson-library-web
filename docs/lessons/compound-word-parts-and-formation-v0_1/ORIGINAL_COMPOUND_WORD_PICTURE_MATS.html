1 · Two individual complete meaningful base words

Use truly familiar oral English compound bases, or linguistically appropriate genuine home-language compounds. Large original pictures/gestures show meaning, not phonics or syllables.

SUN

One real familiar word meaning the sun.

FLOWER

One different complete real word meaning flower.

RAIN

A distinct meaningful word about water from a cloud.

COAT

A separate complete word about clothing.

PK3: Say the genuine whole word SUNFLOWER or RAINCOAT clearly and ask learner to show BOTH real individual component WORD meanings. PK4: Show TWO separate familiar base words with their pictures/gestures and allow learner to FORM the real compound. A child may supply a second base after your first; note any immediate answer copying separately. Neither is splitting syllables. Familiar base meanings matter; the whole compound may be new to the child.

2 · Real meaning connections: BOOK + SHELF

BOOK

One original familiar meaningful base word.

SHELF

Another separate genuine complete word.

PK3 whole word → constituent meaningful words

Adult clearly supplies genuine BOOKSHELF whole FIRST. Child finds or indicates BOTH BOOK and SHELF meanings. Adult must not select both for child.

PK4 separate bases → compound word

Adult provides BOOK and SHELF as genuinely separate familiar words with these supports FIRST. Child creates BOOKSHELF with these familiar meanings. A purposeful picture/AAC choice can express formation; support is allowed and expected.

Model as needed, including the same word. An immediate echo alone is not formation evidence; later child-owned use of familiar bases can be. Pictures/AAC are access, not automatic disqualification.

C4-1 SUN + FLOWER → SUNFLOWER. C4-2 RAIN + COAT → RAINCOAT. C4-3 BOOK + SHELF → BOOKSHELF. C4-4 child contributes a second familiar base with support. C4-5 optional changed combination of familiar bases such as BED + ROOM → BEDROOM.
