Lesson 04 — Build the Collection: plan, count, verify, explain

Required developmental teaching sequence: PK3 foundation → PK4 extension. PK3: 1–2 objects, then 3, then mixed 1–5; PK4: recheck 1–5, introduce 6–8, then 9–10, then mixed 1–10. Assess one-to-one count (C) and last-number cardinality (K) separately. The existing full-grade EXIT is a later source-range check, not the first teaching session. Apply this ordered sequence in SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes; SOLO remains a written specification only.

ID: MATH-PK-COUNT-04 • v0.1 • 2026-10-30 • UNIVERSE-NEUTRAL, ORIGINAL INTERNAL DRAFT. The pedagogy is learner-controlled construction and design, rather than counting a given set (01), physical traversal (02), or investigating someone else's flawed method (03). The learner invents the arrangement and checks whether the set they made really matches a requested amount. A correctly constructed amount by itself is not enough to credit either official action without observed count-word/object correspondence and an independent explanation that the last count word describes the whole creation.

A. Teacher-facing lesson card

Published source actions (2022 Texas Prekindergarten Guidelines): PK3.V.A.2 count up to five objects with one-to-one correspondence; PK3.V.A.3 communicate that the final number counted represents total of up to five; PK4.V.A.2 count up to ten objects with one-to-one correspondence; PK4.V.A.3 final-number whole-set total up to ten. Exact source text and per-cell hashes are retained in education/planning/EDUCATION_TX_MATH_SOURCE_OUTCOME_INVENTORY_V0_1.csv and EDUCATION_E2_PK3_PK4_TEN_DOMAIN_SOURCE_FOUNDATION_V0_1.csv. See the official 2022 PDF: https://tea.texas.gov/academics/early-childhood-education/2022-texas-pkg-comprehensive-guide.pdf . Its locally inventoried extraction still requires final source-review acceptance.

Essential learning question: “If you design a collection yourself, how can you be sure exactly how many pieces you used—and explain it to someone else?”

Grade-level design: PK3 requested target quantities 2–5; target-announced construction can reach 5. PK4 target quantities 4–10; target-announced construction can reach 10. At X4A the independent object→count-word trace may provide bounded C evidence at the five/ten-item target, but a repeated adult-announced number is not unprompted K evidence. X4B adds an unannounced different four-/nine-object count; that may provide stronger independent K evidence only at its actual size, not a blanket ten-object PK4 K claim. The later unannounced R4 at five/ten is a planned stronger full-range probe, never already observed. This staged source-scope distinction is essential, not an age ceiling or a predicted pass/fail result. A learner starting at one to three pieces may progress through smaller guided checks without being judged to have verified PK4 ten-object competence.

Timing: ~20–25 minutes for a possible adult-led session, not a deadline. A complete X4A construction plus fresh X4B count may need additional time, particularly with AAC, thoughtful design, access assistance, or a child's request for a break. If the two-part exit does not fit, stop after recording X4A and reserve a fully unfamiliar, unannounced X4B for another real encounter—even on another date—without pre-naming its four/nine objects or total. Mark X4B PENDING_NOT_OBSERVED until it occurs; never fill its C/K from X4A or treat a deferred X4B as automatically completed R4. R4 is a separate later-date five/ten unfamiliar collection with its own actual first response and remains NOT_OBSERVED until held. A learner who willingly completes both independently in one feasible session may do so. Genuine learner choices replace timer/efficiency scoring. This is not a numeral-recognition activity: all required requests can be narrated in spoken/home-language number words or selected from accessible original audio/sign/AAC; no reading or recognizing digits is prerequisite.

Materials: Up to ten large, washable, identical safe building pieces plus at least four additional large choices; one blank flat Design Space mat, two safe large optional containers, adult-original paper METHOD_04_DESIGN_CARDS.html (blank design mat and two-sided adult prompt/observation card), loose shapes with no numeral printed on manipulatives. If printing pieces, adult cuts them. No choking-sized loose parts, small magnetic toys, food incentive, inaccessible color-only distinction or mandatory fine-motor construction.

Observable C/K distinction: (a) After choosing/placing each piece the child independently gives that unique piece an appropriate successive number word. (b) At completion, child answers how many pieces in the entire designed set and communicates that their last count word denotes the total. The child's decision to place five pieces when requested five is not in itself evidence of C, and a correct label alone can be a guess or direct copy of the request. Record both independently, with U unprompted, H concept hint/model, A physical/communication access-only proxy, X unknown. Do not treat success on source-adjacent PK3.V.A.6/PK4.V.A.6 quantity representation as automatic—building a collection may be a candidate for those distinct published actions but this lesson's assessed scope must be separately proven.

B. Four distinct task phases and one actually later revisit

ENTRY E4 — choosing and recounting, before any model. Give PK3 three large pieces already placed as a small loose shape, and PK4 six different-shape pieces already on one mat. Ask: “A builder left this collection. How many pieces are there? Can you show how you know?” Record child-originated object/count-word trace and whether their communicated total clearly refers to all. This is not yet a creation prompt, so existing prior knowledge is observable and does not leak the desired outcome.

MODEL M4 — adult intentionally designs a different two-piece collection. Put E4 away. Say “I want a collection with two objects.” Select one large piece and say “one,” then a second and say “two.” Point to both and say “The last count word two means there are two in my whole design.” Change the arrangement without adding pieces: “I moved a piece, but I didn't create another one.” This is instructor instruction; it does not grant independent child C/K status.

DURING D4 — learner invents a shape and checks the number. Child chooses a design (tower, row, curve, free-form pattern) and a safe narrated target: PK3 four, PK4 eight. Do not show a target array of exactly that quantity or a printed numeral as an answer template. Teacher says: “Build something with the amount I said. You decide where each piece belongs. How will you make sure each gets one counting word?” Child selects each object and communicates next number word (or directs adult to move one at a time). After the child stops, teacher asks: “How many altogether did you use? What does that final number tell us?” If child moves a piece and calls it an extra object, model adding a genuinely different object with a different mini-set, then reset D4; the lesson is about accounting for actual items, not an automatic joining/addition objective. No model count should supply the child's exit answer.

EXIT X4 — transfer into a fresh unfamiliar real-world task. Put away the design toys entirely. Provide a new mixed pile of safe large items in a separate tray and an empty collection mat. Tell a practical but universe-neutral scenario: “We need a display with exactly [PK3 five / PK4 ten] pieces. Build it however you like. As you choose each piece, show which number word belongs to it. Then, without hearing me count, tell how many are on your display altogether and how you know.” The adult speaks the target amount only once; no picture shows target quantity and the count word for each placed object comes from the child. The adult cannot assume independent cardinality because the child may simply echo the requested target. After recording the first creation and total, offer an unannounced, different independent verification collection made from safe unfamiliar items with no target spoken: PK3 four pieces, PK4 nine. Ask only “This display has no label. Can you show how many are here and how you know?” Record a second C/K trace separately; do not combine it with creation evidence or supply the answer. The unannounced verification is the stronger independent K candidate; a correct count on the requested display alone has an obvious answer leak. Optional follow-up on the child-built display (“Could you show how you checked?”) is teaching/triangulation, not a substitute for the new probe. Do not require numerals, decorative perfection, writing or architectural stability. Each challenge is one collection—not an addition word problem.

REVISIT R4 — genuinely different later date, different task form. At a later actual meeting, give an already built, unfamiliar single collection of [PK3 five / PK4 ten] objects that child did not design. “Someone made this. Can you check how many are here and show me your way?” New date/task ID. This reverses construction versus interpretation and probes whether child can count the whole set without anchoring on a spoken target. Until actually observed, REVISIT=NOT_OBSERVED; no inferred retention.

C. Complete adult-led instruction with exact response contingencies

0–4 minutes — baseline: E4 on a clean unnumbered mat. Invite learner to show/communicate count. Record first object-to-count-word correspondence, first whole-set total and unprompted explanation. If learner gives correct number immediately, ask after noting the first response to show method; do not automatically mark correspondence.

4–7 minutes — explain design accounting: Replace with M4; select two pieces, one successive word per item, then explain last word describes how many all pieces. Move one existing piece to demonstrate that relocation isn't a newly counted piece. If helpful, show a visually different example, but keep all teacher-given number sequences in the modeled phase.

7–15 minutes — child-designed D4: Invite child-chosen shape and adult-narrated four/eight target. Let child choose objects from a tray and control placement. Adult prompts if necessary: “You've moved that same piece. Did a new piece join your design?” or “Which pieces already have a count word?” If a sequence mistake is observed, model on a new two-object set, then let child try another fresh practice arrangement. The adult may support physical placement without supplying count words or totals (access-only A). If adult counts or chooses quantity, H.

15–21 minutes — clean X4 independent creation: Remove all model/practice objects and teacher demonstration. Give entirely new materials. Narrate the target once and do not count aloud, show five/ten dots or repeat “last word” before the child's first whole-set answer. Record ordered pairs of child-selected object and number-word, then first final answer plus any explanation. When the child remains ready and the allotted time permits, present the unannounced new four-/nine-piece verification (without stating its size) and separately record its object-word pairs and total/meaning: this check avoids simply echoing the spoken creation target. Otherwise defer X4B as a truly new, unannounced first task at another actual encounter; record X4B as pending, not failed or completed, and preserve the distinction from any later R4 full-range revisit. If child needs a conceptual hint, give it after preserving the first attempts, then offer a different follow-on construction to learn; do not rewrite X4 as successful independent evidence.

21–25 minutes — reflect and optionally exchange roles: “You selected these pieces and worked out the total.” Only say “counted each once” if observed. Optionally let child request a collection from adult as a separate nonassessed co-creation; choose to stop without penalty.

Later real day: Give R4 unfamiliar assembled set without announcing the amount before the child counts, in contrast to X4. Record individual first C/K responses and actual support.

D. Four authentic mode plans

SOLO — child-operated interactive design lab; SPECIFICATION ONLY

Entry: A narrated child-facing digital workbench (no written number requirement) displays E4 pieces. Child chooses each object and the next number word via replayable audio/sign/AAC alternatives. App stores first pairs of (object ID, selected successive number-word), repeated IDs, omissions, word-order errors and first total as different fields. No score inferred from tapping order alone.

Model: Child sees an adult-neutral two-piece construction with synchronized movement/count words and a spoken demonstration of final word meaning. Narration must have replay, break, caption and nonaudio equivalent. The model stays separate from the scored task.

Practice: Child chooses freely how to design D4. A spoken nonprint numeral target is provided once. The system may give child-selected “help me plan” cards, but if a card supplies number words or number of pieces, mark H for that attempt. The game gives specific feedback when the same object is moved and counted twice versus when a genuinely new object is placed. No color-coded right-answer template.

Exit: The workshop switches to a new functional context X4 with different unnumbered objects. The spoken target is given once. Child independently chooses and arranges objects, selects count-word/object matches and indicates the first total. Then show a separately unannounced four-/nine-piece verification collection with fresh materials and a first-trial count-word/object trace and whole-set total, without revealing its count. If (where accessible) the learner is later asked whether the last word describes the last object or the whole collection, a forced binary choice may cue the concept: keep that only as a limited, coached consistency check, not proof of spontaneous understanding. Preserve both first totals and traces separately. Record answer history/hints and no-response honestly, without real child identity.

Revisit: No synthetic passage of time, durable record or claim of retention. Separate-day access only after lawful/authorized storage or caregiver-managed return, and genuine pre-reader usability QA. No solo software implemented; neither number-audio usability nor actual educational parity is verified.

CO-PLAY — collaborative maker and independent ownership

At entry child counts a caregiver-created E4 collection first without prompts; adult models two items (M4) on a different mat. For practice, adult plays the builder requesting help: “I'd like four/eight pieces, but I want you to decide how to make them.” Child authors the design and verifies total while adult follows their instructions, not their numbers. Midway swap roles: child asks adult for a different smaller count and corrects a move-without-adding misunderstanding during teaching. Before X4, clear all adult designs. Child builds their own independent new display, counts every new piece with chosen count words and explains total before any caregiver recounting or applause cue; adult then gives the unannounced fresh four-/nine-piece verification set and keeps its amount secret until child counts and explains it. Adult records where a hint or physical-only support occurred. Activity remains genuinely instructive even with no tablet.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD — tangible purposeful design

Use script C with real safe manipulatives, an original blank design mat and X4's different real-life context. Adult can adapt the child's construction theme without changing source-bound set size and count requirements. Verify full collection's count separate from manipulating shape and optional creativity. Do not incorrectly credit numeral recognition or conservation of number based on the artifact.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM — individual creations within a shared challenge

For 20 learners, prepare 200 large safe pieces for group construction (ten per child at the highest PK4 bound), enough distinct adult-only M4 pieces, and separate protected unfamiliar X4B sets for only the children actually being individually observed in the current rotation (for example, 3 distinct sets × 9 objects = 27 extra large safe objects at PK4, or 3 × 4 at PK3). Prepare 20 blank individual design mats and 20 optional count strips; keep the alternate X4B sets out of children's sight until each actual independent attempt. One teacher cannot responsibly distribute, supervise and observe 20 separate new X4B object-count trials in the same six-minute block. Stage the remaining protected X4B opportunities during actual later small-group center rotations of four or five with real adult supervision; reserve and replenish different unfamiliar sets or rotate previously unseen object types appropriately without revealing a target. A child whose X4B has not happened is NOT_YET_OBSERVED on that task, not passed or failed. Original print METHOD_04_DESIGN_CARDS.html provides a blank mat and adult observation script, not physical packets of child-specific pre-counted sets. One shared model is instruction, not 20 observed performances.

  • 0–4 min: Each learner gets E4 already-made collections and independently counts them. Teacher observes 2–3 focus learners' complete counts and totals. Others not individually witnessed are ? NOT_YET_OBSERVED.
  • 4–7 min: Teacher models M4 with different two-piece object types; projects empty/finished zones, shows moving isn't new-object counting.
  • 7–15 min: Individuals create D4 collections from a shared adult-prepared supply; teacher or trained adult oversees distribution; each child controls their own choices and count words. In pairs, one child may check after the creator gives first total. A shared finished object is not individual reasoning evidence.
  • 15–21 min (flexible; two different tasks, not one simultaneous test): Give every participating child a fresh X4A set to build after the adult says five/ten exactly once; this is participation and source-relevant candidate C evidence only where the teacher actually observes the child's unique object-word trail, not 20 automatic passes or unprompted K from repeating the target. Only for the 2–3 current focus learners, if the teacher can safely supervise the other children, separately offer reserved protected unfamiliar X4B four/nine sets without announcing counts; observe their actual first individual object-word and whole-total responses while others continue a familiar supervised safe task or choose to pause. If that supervision cannot be done safely, move all X4B checks to scheduled later small groups instead. Do not announce X4B's target, borrow an already seen set, or count a partner's answer as independent. Missing X4B opportunities remain unknown, not failed.
  • 21–25 min: Brief voluntary reflection and safe clean-up; arrange genuine subsequent educator-observed X4B mini-group rotations for all other children with appropriate safe reserved new object sets, varying stage and access as needed. Preserve each child’s actual first task/date, size, help and total. This may require extra sessions rather than claiming every independent count completed in the first 25 minutes; no additional staffing is assumed. Store any real response records only under actual institutional policy.
  • Later actual day: For R4, give learner a display built by someone else, no announced target, observe independent count and total. This is a different transfer.

Retrospective differentiation correction — 2026-11-01

PK3: access, scaffold, independent source evidence

Teach and fade: Child selects/places 1–5 objects using accessible nonstacking arrangement or precisely directed adult hands; a requested target is practice, not independent knowledge of unknown total.

Uncued source probe: After announced five-piece creation, record unknown separate four-piece verification and later unfamiliar five-piece collection with first C and K.

False credit or bias risk: Repeating adult request five or watching adult assemble the collection does not demonstrate a child-originated last-word total.

PK4: access, scaffold, independent source evidence

Teach and fade: Build in stages from 1–5 through 6–8 to 9/10; use accessible design choices and safe larger pieces; no automatic placement or supplied count word.

Uncued source probe: After narrated ten-piece request, record truly hidden unknown nine-piece verification and later ten-piece full-range transfer.

False credit or bias risk: A correct construction target can be echoed from the adult; even perfect building is not the independent two-part C/K assessment.

Mode adaptation: SOLO (specified, unbuilt) must permit narrated untimed child-controlled object and number-word selection with separate uncued final total; never award C from taps alone or autoplay correct sequence. CO-PLAY caregivers and ADULT-LED teachers may prepare objects, model on different sets and handle child-directed physical movement, but may not choose item, word or total for independent evidence. CLASSROOM rotations must observe each individual pair sequence and whole-set answer; participation or choral response alone is not a pass. Use real individualized family/IEP/IFSP/504 guidance and qualified specialists where relevant; tag access-only, helped, modified objective and not observed separately. No automatic diagnosis from delay, silence or preference. Published size bounds remain unchanged.

Lesson 04 companion teacher/facilitator guide — Independent construction and unannounced verification

Paired standalone plan: Independent construction and unannounced verification. This adult instructional companion is NOT a counted lesson. Refer to separate objective educator guide. The four official source objectives and exact source cell hashes remain in their original authoring audit.

Specific teaching mechanism

Creating one's own object set teaches quantity control, yet a learner can build exactly five by imitating a displayed target or counting aloud while still lacking a general independent counting/total response. This lesson therefore separates a child-designed collection from a truly unannounced OTHER collection for official A.2 correspondence and A.3 cardinality evidence. Optional building is not an added published attainment condition.

Adult preparation, material safety and phase boundaries

Prepare safe broad unnumbered blank group mats and adult-inspected, non-swallowable large washable nonfood pieces; no written numeral target or printed model answer is necessary. The main lesson and the actual two-page METHOD_04_DESIGN_CARDS.html prescribe E4 fresh unannounced PK3 three / PK4 six, M4 a distinct adult two-object build with correctly explained total two, D4 learner-authored construction after a spoken PK3 four / PK4 eight request, and a deliberately TWO-PART EXIT: X4A a new child-built display after the adult announces PK3 five / PK4 ten, preserving the first object-by-word sequence but NOT treating a repeated announced target as independent cardinality meaning; then X4B a separate untouched unannounced new PK3 four / PK4 nine object set, with its own first count trace and whole-group meaning, to challenge echoing. Model materials and prior constructed sets must not be recycled at exit. R4 on a genuinely different date offers unannounced fresh PK3 five / PK4 ten, and stays NOT_OBSERVED until it actually happens. Earlier smaller stage-specific mini-checks are valid practice, never silently counted as the full-range source exit. A clear 9-object unprompted K at X4B cannot itself establish full PK4 ten-object K, and an announced ten-object X4A alone cannot overcome answer leakage; retain the precise source-bound uncertainty, not an inflated overall mastery claim. These are separate learner responses, not one blurred scored event. Do not force both independent exit tasks into the nominal six-minute slot: when a learner needs extra time, access support, rest or has finished only X4A, schedule X4B as a wholly fresh, unannounced task in an actual later encounter. Mark X4B=PENDING_NOT_OBSERVED until the first real attempt; keep X4A evidence unchanged and do not call a deferred X4B the separate later-day full-bound R4. Both tasks can be completed together when genuinely feasible; pacing is a flexible teaching judgment, not a speed-based assessment.

MODEL: on other safe objects make an adult two-piece collection, assigning one/two and explaining whole-set total two (not three). Contingent misconception feedback and remediation

If child builds “five” but repeats one token during counting, help on a different adult example and later use novel real exit set; builder success is not C. If child reads a displayed “7” on adult target card without counting, do not credit A.2 or A.3. If child can count eight but says “eight” after teacher asks “is that your last number and total?” the adult planted K meaning, so supported H. A child with mobility difficulty may direct neutral proxy; unassisted construction not compulsory official standard.

Four separate official outcome interpretations (apply to this lesson)

PK3.V.A.2: independent one-to-one C count on an actually fresh real ≤5 set; one count word per actual item, no double count/omission, regardless of total statement. PK3.V.A.3: child counts an actually fresh ≤5 set and independently communicates the last count word as the total of the WHOLE group, not a teacher-fed “last” echo. K requires count and cardinality meaning. PK4.V.A.2: independent one-to-one C count on genuinely new real 6–10 items as well as supported small foundations; do not infer upper range from ≤5. PK4.V.A.3: child counts a real 6–10 group and independently states that final count word tells how many there are altogether; distinguish K from merely repeating the end of C. Use separate first COUNT sequence and first TOTAL response fields and U child-origin, H conceptual help, A neutral access and X unknown provenance. If one authentic trial independently demonstrates C and K, it can support both separately; no automatic cross-credit.

Seven differentiated learner/access pathways

Attention/executive: limited items visible at first and predictable phase cues, fade hints for EXIT. Language/communication: self-authored semantically meaningful home-language, sign, or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) counts and total; never adult-selected messages. Literacy/phonology: don't require numeral decoding as a counting prerequisite. Numeracy: 1–3 real safe items first, later PK3 4–5 and PK4 6–10 with authentic correspondence. Autism/sensory: predictable voluntary safe activity with no speed pressure. Intellectual/developmental: model errors/total on distinct adult material and fade. Motor/hearing/vision: oversized contrast/tactile safe objects and individually reviewed unbiased child-directed proxy; a proxy physically moving child-selected items is access A, not permission to speak the count or total.
