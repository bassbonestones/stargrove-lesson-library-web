Counting and Cardinality — progressive PK3 foundations and PK4 extension

Mandatory teacher-facing sequence for all four original methods · October 30, 2026 · E2.2 draft. The four lesson methods are reusable, not a one-day prescription to count to five or ten. Grade-based source expectations and actual student readiness determine the set sizes used in instruction; children first encounter a small manageable range, revisit, then extend. This corrects the earlier design's relatively strong assessment boundaries but insufficiently explicit learning progression. SOLO remains NOT IMPLEMENTED, no proven delivery-mode equivalence or child learning.

Research source: 2022 Texas PK Guidelines Mathematics p.51 and locally hash-pinned cell inventory. C = correct next number word, each actual object exactly once; K = after count, child communicates the final count word is how many are in the whole group. These are separately observable: a perfect count sequence without meaning of last word is C evidence, not K evidence. Printed numeral reading, subitizing and representation of requested amount are distinct outcomes. Zero can be introduced meaningfully as an empty-set contrast, but do not substitute empty-tray success for correct one-to-one counting of actual objects.

Teach across actual sessions: small groups then whole source range

Use short practice blocks with child-controlled movement and multiple real sessions/days; “week” or stage is an instructional choice, not an age threshold or official promotion rule. At each stage give an ENTRY fresh object set before any model, MODEL a separate quantity, DURING coached practice, a new small mini-check, and an actual future REVISIT. These mini-checks inform the next lesson, but final full-range EXIT and later revisit of the original four methods are additional, not replaced by a successful easy small set.

Path Instructional sequence and example script Child-originated evidence, critical response
PK3 C3-1: objects 1–2 On real safe large pieces, adult separately models moving one piece at a time from “not counted” to “counted”: “This one gets the first word, one; the next one gets two.” Count only adult sample. Then show child a different 1- or 2-piece set. Child says/indicates one appropriate word per unique object (C) and then on a new query “How many objects altogether?” conveys whole set without an adult saying the answer (K). If child points twice at same object, give an object-separation teaching model on another sample, then retry fresh.
PK3 C3-2: objects 3 Introduce 3 in a loose irregular arrangement, not only a neat line. Adult model on different three, “Touch each once so none are missed.” Teacher should not model the exit's precise layout. Child tracks three distinct objects once and separately communicates that three is the amount of all of them. Child who only chants “one two three” into air needs direct real-object tracking; child who counts accurately but cannot respond to whole-set question has C candidate, K not verified.
PK3 C3-3: objects 4–5, mixed 1–5 Build up four and five with more widely spaced and clustered real objects; teach self-monitoring by moving each piece to “counted” zone if useful. Mix 1–3 too. Fresh uncoached mixed 1–5 EXIT must separately capture first C and first K evidence on actual previously unseen group layouts, including 5; later real-date revisit. A five-item-only success does not prove every specific number/size context, but the family can document all intended values without coercive overtesting.
PK4 C4-1: re-establish 1–5 Check real fresh sets 1–5 in varied orientations and explicitly ask the whole-set question after counting, not as a cue before response. Reteach object tracking or cardinality separately as needed. Document separate C/K observed across earlier-range material. A four-year-old need not instantly jump to ten; conversely a child ready for nine may explore while any missing foundations receive support.
PK4 C4-2: extend 6–8 Add six, seven and eight pieces gradually, mixing known 3–5; show on teacher-only sample how to organize larger sets, e.g. counted/noncounted areas. “Where could you put each one so you don't count it twice?” Child-originated first C sequence on new 6/7/8 sets; separately child reports how many are in whole set after final count. If item tracked twice, return to spatial organization; if final answer is an echo of teacher's total, K is helped, not independent.
PK4 C4-3: extend 9–10 With large real objects teach nine and ten, including scattered layouts, not only a fixed neat 10-row. Model adult self-check on different larger set and separately ask how the last number names the group. Unprompted one-to-one 9 and 10 counts plus distinct whole-set response, with 0–8 mixed practice. A child who accurately counts eight does not automatically get ten credit.
PK4 C4-4: flexible 1–10 Mix 1–10 sets, different safe object types, reversed spatial directions and loose groups, with new material and no response cue. Don't require printed numeral 10 or a specific counting-pointing speed. Full source-range up to ten novel EXIT for C and K separately and an actual later-date recheck. Grade expectation is not satisfied by only new six-through-ten sets if earlier one-through-five have never been observed under relevant conditions.

Optional number zero: Introduce empty containers as number conversations elsewhere, but one-to-one counting requires actual objects. Avoid a false transition from zero to “zero objects were counted” as proof of C. For PK3 one-to-one and cardinality go to 5, PK4 to 10; rote number-word order without objects belongs to separate V.A.1 learning outcome. Teacher can use an accessible communication mode to elicit total without demanding an English-only spoken phrase.

Formative decision: If child can coordinate one word to one actual object and independently communicate the final whole-set number across at least two different arrangements at the current size, vary size/space next. If one component lags, respond to that specific misconception; no universal number of days or invented percent cutoff. Children can keep practicing at a smaller meaningful group while experiencing richer guided work.

Each of the four methods uses the progression differently

Method 01 — Counting Collections Studio (TEACHER_GUIDE.md)

  • PK3 C3-1: Adult uses separate counted/un-counted areas and models safe one and two; child moves new objects one-by-one and names count word, then independently gives total. C3-2: New three objects with a different spacing; track and ask total after sequence. C3-3: Four and five objects then fresh mixed 1–5 exit, no model visible, first C and K independently.
  • PK4 C4-1: Revisit mixed 1–5, including irregular layouts; C4-2: use 6/7/8 objects with child-chosen tracking strategy, adult models a distinct 7 only; C4-3: new nine/ten whole-set counts and independent totals; C4-4: full changed-object set 1–10 transfer.
  • Pitfall: Child says “five” after hearing adult say it; don't label K independent. A perfect sequence but skipped object means C not yet shown.

Method 02 — Walking the Count Trail (LESSON_02_MOVEMENT_COUNT_TRAIL.md)

  • PK3 C3-1: Use one/two real object stations on a seated hand-path or optional walking path, never an arbitrary number-word-only stepping game. Child visits each actual object once, assigns next number word, then communicates whole-set total. C3-2: 3-object spatial path with one out-of-line item; C3-3: a four/five object nonlinear trail plus unannounced exit, no forced gross motor movement.
  • PK4 C4-1: New path with 1–5 actual objects, different order to expose missed stations. C4-2: 6–8 nonordered real object stations, with child choice of path and no double visits; C4-3: 9/10 sparse stations, organized tracking strategy; C4-4: new layouts to ten, observe C and K separate from physical route choice.
  • Pitfall: Walking ten steps on empty squares is not counting ten actual objects. Mobility supports/hand selection count as access, not evidence that motor coordination = math competence.

Method 03 — Counting Detective (LESSON_03_COUNTING_DETECTIVE.md)

  • PK3 C3-1: A puppet/teacher counts two pieces but touches one twice; child notices with safe correction, then independently counts a different fresh one/two-piece collection, reports total. C3-2: Faulty three-object demonstration skips one; child independently counts new three. C3-3: Four/five objects with skip/double-counting trap; independent uncued actual child count and separate total, not just saying “puppet wrong.”
  • PK4 C4-1: Re-establish 1–5 actual counts and totals after discussing diverse puppet errors; C4-2: hidden 6–8 item double-count/skip demonstrations (model distinct from exit) then child independent new 6–8; C4-3: 9/10 object tracking errors, child develops correction; C4-4: independent new 1–10 checks, no adult “remember last number” cue during assessment.
  • Pitfall: Successfully diagnosing a mistaken puppet algorithm is valuable reasoning but it is not by itself independent C/K evidence; a fresh self-performed count is required.

Method 04 — Build the Collection (LESSON_04_BUILD_COLLECTION.md)

  • PK3 C3-1: Child builds requested one/two group using safe pieces (representation practice), then adult unexpectedly supplies a different unannounced real 1/2 object group to count without being told its total, asks whole-set after. C3-2: child creates three, but independently counts unrelated unknown three (new layout/material). C3-3: build four/five, then uncued independent 1–5 verification sets, C/K separately.
  • PK4 C4-1: New 1–5 build/independent-verify tasks (prior familiarity isn't proof of last-number meaning); C4-2: 6–8 child-build but new adult hidden group for counting; C4-3: 9/10 independent unknown group counting with larger safe objects; C4-4: new full range 1–10 verification, separately C/K.
  • Pitfall: Child repeats the total the adult announced when commissioning a collection: not independent cardinality about a new collection. This method's existing unannounced separate verification rule is mandatory, not optional.

Differentiated stage access and fresh evidence — retrospective revision

Access supports do not supply the source answer: preserve child-chosen object identities and successive number words (C), then child independent last-word meaning for ALL objects (K). Child-directed physical proxy and accessible communication count as access; adult number-word cue, proxy selecting next object or hint to last-word concept is HELPED, not unprompted achievement. If materials cannot be perceived or response is inaccessible, record NOT_OBSERVED, never a disability diagnosis. A separate individualized modified goal is not silently credited as the published standard.

Stage Distinct scaffold and fade Uncued new-set evidence
PK3 C3-1 Model two DIFFERENT pieces, let child control one/two accessible pieces with a counted zone, then fade adult next-item and next-word cues. New one/two object set: first object-to-word sequence and separate whole-set total with no supplied count or answer.
PK3 C3-2 Teach three in changing non-row layouts with optional tactile counted marker; remove object tracking prompts after a different modeled example. New scattered three: child chooses each unique object and next word then supplies independent last-word total.
PK3 C3-3 Offer four/five large safe objects, variable spacing and child-directed proxy; fade hints and mix earlier one–three in practice. Novel mixed one–five through actual five, first C and K separately, then a genuine later-date transfer.
PK4 C4-1 Recheck one–five in accessible unknown layouts without assuming four-year-old readiness; separately reteach whole-set meaning if needed. Unmodeled new smaller sets up to five, recording first C and first K independently before larger-range work.
PK4 C4-2 Gradually add six/seven/eight objects and child-selected spatial organizer, then fade route/count hints before probe. Unfamiliar seven/eight actual objects, each paired to child count word, followed by independent total.
PK4 C4-3 Build nine/ten with large sensory-accessible objects and pauses; remove quantity clues, not necessary physical access. Unknown nine and ten layouts, uncued correspondence and distinct whole-set meaning—not repeated adult ten.
PK4 C4-4 Mix one–ten, vary path and materials, fade familiar station order while retaining child-chosen accessibility assistance. New mixed one–ten including ten and later date, preserve first C/K response and unknown evidence, no extrapolation from one easy layout.

Apply to all four methods: Studio counted-zone supports may be faded while a motor proxy remains; movement trails count real physical object identities, not footsteps; puppet-error recognition must be followed by a separate child-performed count; building a requested number leaks a candidate total, so the separate unannounced verification is essential. In SOLO written plans, audio word choices must not highlight the correct next value or auto-advance. In CO-PLAY and ADULT-LED, teachers model on separate pieces and fade conceptual hints. In CLASSROOM, child-specific observable C/K is necessary; an unobserved partner or chorus response stays unknown. Qualified specialist review for nonvisual/tactile correspondence and adapted selection is outstanding.

Carry across the four delivery modes

  • SOLO (NOT IMPLEMENTED): Future prereader audio can introduce 1–2 real digital objects, then three, then 4–5; PK4 rechecks 1–5 before 6–8 and 9–10. It must capture first individual object-ID→count-word sequence, then separately elicit the child's total without announcing the last word/answer in advance. Accessible alternative input/switch supported. No software, voice, privacy consent or solo independence has been demonstrated.
  • CO-PLAY: Child chooses object style/path/detective mistake/build strategy while adult models on other examples and observes independent first actual count/whole-set answer. Peers may help during practice but not silently during a scored trial; stages guide gradual introduction.
  • ADULT-LED ONE CHILD: Prepare only needed stage's real safe objects, not a pre-correct ten-piece array on the first PK3 session; use precise modeling and answer-contingent help. Record separately C and K, including teacher cues and actual later-date revisit.
  • TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM: Whole class can share one short lesson theme while focused groups work at distinct C3/C4 stages. Separate group chants and partner counting from individually attributable 1-to-1 and total evidence. Teacher can plan multi-session independent small-group checks for full up-to-five/up-to-ten source ranges; record gaps as NOT OBSERVED.

End-state honesty: This is a stage-specific authored instructional correction, not educator accepted, child-tested or “PK4 full mastery.” Each original method's current novel EXIT and REVISIT remain required for source-action evidence; stage mini-checks select next instruction and do not automatically satisfy published outcomes.
