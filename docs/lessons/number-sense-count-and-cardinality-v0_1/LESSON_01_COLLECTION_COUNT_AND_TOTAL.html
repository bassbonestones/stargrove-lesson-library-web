Teach Counting Collections Studio

Teacher-friendly reading guide. The complete internally reviewed source plan and facilitator guide are in the other tabs; this page is a summary, not a separately classroom-tested lesson.

What the child is learning

Two separate skills: Count each object once, matching one number word to each real object; and understand the total, knowing the last count word says how many are in the whole group. A child can learn the first before the second.

Gather and choose the level

Use a few large, safe blocks/counters, a clear space with “not counted yet” and “counted” areas, and an optional tray. Avoid small swallowable pieces, magnets and sharp items. Allow speech, sign, home language, an accessible communication device, or a child-directed adult to move pieces. Never require writing numbers or touching objects.

Do not start every child with the age group's maximum. Progress over appropriate sessions:

Child's starting point Begin with Work toward, gradually
Child in the 3-year-old preschool group still learning to count 1–2 objects 3, then 4–5
Child in the 3-year-old preschool group comfortable with 1–2 3 objects 4–5, mixed 1–5
Child in the 4-year-old preschool group still building foundations Revisit 1–5 6–8, then 9–10
Child in the 4-year-old preschool group already comfortable counting A suitable familiar range Mixed groups up to 10

The published targets cover up to five objects for the 3-year-old preschool group and ten for the 4-year-old preschool group. These are later learning goals, not demands that the first session reach five or ten.

Five steps to teach

1. First look: What does the child already do?

Arrange a fresh small set at their actual current level.

Say: “Can you show me how many pieces are here? You can count your own way.”

Watch or listen without pointing to each piece, choosing their next number, or revealing the total. If they do not want to try, that's okay—start with supportive teaching instead.

2. Show the idea with different objects

Put the first set aside and use two different blocks.

Say and demonstrate: “I'll count each block once. One … two. My last word was two. So there are two blocks altogether.”

Move each block to a separate “counted” place as you say its number. The key idea is that the last number names the whole collection, not only the last block.

3. Let the child practice

Offer a different small group suitable for their current level.

Say: “Which piece do you want to count first? How can we be sure each piece gets counted just once?”

The child chooses the order and moves, points to, or directs an adult to move each piece. Then ask: “How many altogether?” If an object gets missed or counted twice, demonstrate with separate practice pieces and let the child try a fresh group. If they count correctly but cannot tell the total, model the last number = whole amount idea using different objects. Keep the interaction inviting.

4. Give a new independent opportunity

Put the practice pieces away. Present another new arrangement at the child's learned range.

Say: “Show me how you would count these. How many are there altogether?”

Watch the actual count of each object and the whole-group total separately. Let the child use needed neutral access support, but don't supply the next number or tell them “your last word is the answer” before their own first response. If they need help, help them learn; simply record that the response was supported.

Important distinction: This small check helps you decide what to teach next. The full technical plan also describes a later independent check with five objects for the 3-year-old preschool group or ten for the 4-year-old preschool group. That larger check is for an appropriate later stage, not automatically the first lesson.

5. Revisit on another genuine day

Choose new objects/arrangement another day and see what the child can do. The later revisit hasn't happened just because it appears on a written plan. Stop or adapt when the child needs a break.

How to respond to what you see

Observation Practical response
Counts every piece once and explains the total Celebrate the actual strategy; offer a different appropriate set another time
Counts accurately but doesn't know the total Model that the last count word tells how many in the whole group, using a different set
Skips one or counts one twice Practice moving each counted object to another section

Teach Counting Collections Studio with a Class

Classroom teaching pathway for preschool educators working with multiple children. The companion Teach 1-on-1 path serves one adult and one learner. These are educational written designs, not evidence of observed classroom effectiveness. Classroom structure and group size may be adapted to real staffing and children's needs.

What children are learning

Two separate skills: Count each object once, matching one number word to each real object; and understand the total, knowing the last count word says how many are in the whole group. A child can learn the first before the second.

Prepare the classroom

For a class of about twenty, plan an individual mat and up to ten large, washable counters per child, plus a visibly different adult demonstration set. Use fewer counters for early practice; younger learners start with one or two, then three, and build toward five. Older learners revisit those amounts before six to eight and later ten. Lay out a separate, private place for answers and enough space for children who direct an adult's movement.

Introduce the idea together

Show two large model objects and ask, “How could we find out how many there are without counting anything twice?” Invite children to think or show a method on their own mat before hearing classmates. Demonstrate moving each model piece once while speaking one number word, and then name the total.

Give children meaningful practice

Give children their own small collections to investigate; pairs may check one another's moves during supported practice, but they must not say the next number or total for a partner's independent attempt. One teacher can circulate among a few focal learners while the others count at established safe tables. Later small-group rotations provide additional individual opportunities; no need to assess all twenty in one sitting.

Observe individual learning fairly

For each learner actually observed, watch the object-to-number-word correspondence and ask what the last number tells about the entire collection. Record these as two different observations: a correct final card alone does not prove a correctly executed count. Offer each focal child a new, previously unseen set for an independent check at the level taught; reserve full five- or ten-object checks for appropriate later progression.

Include, respond, stop and revisit

If a child skips or repeats an item, demonstrate moving a different set into a finished area, then try a new small collection. Accept child-selected counting order, nonspoken number communication and child-directed adult movement. A child not observed during the rotation is not yet observed, never automatically proficient or deficient. Return with fresh objects on another actual day.

Teacher's recording rule: Whole-group talk and collaborative practice count as learning opportunities, not automatic proof of every child's independent understanding. Record only the action, context, support and developmental stage actually observed. For children whose independent opportunity did not occur, use not yet observed and schedule a suitable later chance; a planned later-day revisit is not already completed. No child should be deprived of meaningful participation just because the educator cannot assess everyone at once.

For the exact technical learning targets, grade-specific later-range checks, safety conditions and original materials, open Full technical plan or Facilitator guide above.

The Counting Collections Studio — teacher/parent lesson guide

Required developmental teaching sequence: PK3 foundation → PK4 extension. PK3: 1–2 objects, then 3, then mixed 1–5; PK4: recheck 1–5, introduce 6–8, then 9–10, then mixed 1–10. Assess one-to-one count (C) and last-number cardinality (K) separately. The existing full-grade EXIT is a later source-range check, not the first teaching session. Apply this ordered sequence in SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes; SOLO remains a written specification only.

A reason for the child to care (choose, never compel): Before the child encounters the actual set, invite an honest purpose for knowing its total: perhaps finding out how many blocks are available for a planned build, how many pretend dishes are ready, or how many large toy animals joined a play scene. A child may suggest their own safe familiar collection or simply enjoy a quiet pattern-of-counting investigation with no story. The educator selects the safe quantity and protected fresh set appropriate to the current stage; never announce the total, pre-match every object to a numbered place, let a playful context cue the answer, or change the independent X1 task into a copy of practice. The intended pleasure is the child discovering and communicating a real total, not a performance, prize, or forced fantasy. Counting itself is a legitimate interest when no invented context is wanted.

Core pedagogical distinction: C = each object is assigned exactly one successive count word, with no skip/recount; K = after counting, child indicates that the last word is the total for the whole set, rather than merely naming the last object. A learner may demonstrate C without K, K for a very small known set without reliable C at the full bound, or neither. Do not award either from a numeral button, adult's count, or a memorized chant. Rearrangement invariance and making an equivalent set are optional deeper probes, not silently added published success requirements.

Preparation and sequence of set identities

Use a new set arrangement for each assessment phase; draw or arrange with genuine empty space and no printed number labels that disclose the total.

  • ENTRY E1, before instruction: PK3 use three large identical squares in an irregular triangle; PK4 use six large pieces, in an irregular cluster, not a familiar die face. Do not ask a counting question while pointing or recounting for the child.
  • MODEL M1 (different objects): Adult works with two upright toy blocks and then, if useful, four flat safe counters. Adult may touch/move each once and say “one… two…; the last number is two, so there are two altogether.”
  • DURING D1: PK3 four safe objects in a spread; PK4 eight. Have child select an order and move one object at a time to a “counted” section; do not require the child to use the teacher's order.
  • EXIT X1 (fresh and unmodeled): PK3 five objects scattered irregularly within one outlined collection area, not a recognizable two-plus-three grouping; PK4 ten objects with widely varied spacing and no regular rows. Whole collection remains stable while the child counts. Do not accidentally create visually separate groups that invite addition.
  • REVISIT R1 (on another actual date): New safe objects chosen by educator; PK3 a five-piece set in a different arrangement, PK4 a ten-piece set in a different arrangement. Record actual date and a new set ID; without an actual later encounter, status = NOT_OBSERVED. Never pretend five minutes later was long-term retention.

Important calibration: Entry and Exit have different set sizes and are not a controlled pre/post comparison. Use them to locate observable strategies, not to calculate a gain score or infer a global grade-level mastery claim. PK4 eight/ten-item assessments are more demanding than PK3 four/five; where developmentally appropriate, smaller starting sets are supported practice and cannot be promoted to the full published maximum.

Ask “Do we still have the same number?” If child predicts a different total, invite recounting and reflection. C. Four genuinely different delivery routes (same C/K targets)

1. SOLO — child-only tablet authored interaction specification, NOT IMPLEMENTED

Requires original narrated/audio-described screens with replay, no reading requirement, large countable pieces and motor/access alternatives. A functional program must embody these steps, not simply show this text to the child:

  1. ENTRY, child-operated: Show E1 as movable large objects in an irregular arrangement. Child chooses to touch/drag/direct an accessible scanning selector to mark each object once, with optional number-word audio off by default for evidence. For each object the child chooses an independently selected next spoken-number option from a replayable audio/accessible number bank, then marks that specific object as counted. The app must record the object-ID → selected count-number sequence, including repeated objects, skipped objects, incorrect word order, unanswered choices and retries—not just a bare sequence of taps. Only after this separate correspondence trace does the child choose the whole-set total from an independently accessible spoken-number bank or “not sure.” Number options must not be preselected, color-cued, placed in the correct answer location consistently or highlighted based on the expected value. Even a perfect trace is candidate in-app correspondence evidence, not validated actual oral counting, reliable language comprehension or learner mastery.
  2. Explicit teaching MODEL: Separate original two-block screen demonstrates one tap per object with spoken “one…two; last word two means two in the whole collection.” Replayable, pauseable, no speed penalty, no demand to speak into a microphone.
  3. GUIDED practice: D1 set allows a learner-selected movement path, feedback for revisiting the same object or leaving one behind, explanation of the whole-set total, and a different small example if needed. A tap that marks the last object is not automatically evidence of understanding cardinality; solicit total separately.
  4. Fresh EXIT: X1 has a different layout and object design with no recent model or answer cue. Save the first object-by-object count-word selections, first chosen total, help clicks and any changes as separate anonymous in-session evidence. If a child chooses a correct total after counting two objects twice and omitting two, C fails despite correct numeric guess. The app cannot know whether the child whispered a count or received out-of-app adult hints: mark provenance limit EXTERNAL_SUPPORT_UNKNOWN rather than claiming independent competence.
  5. Later REVISIT: Offered on a distinct real date only if an appropriately authorized storage/scheduling system exists. Not implemented, no fake durability or reminder. Tablet route also requires recorded natural voice, communication-accessibility review, offline testing, device testing and playtesting before claiming child-only parity.

Status: AUTHORED_SPEC_NOT_RUNNING_SOLO. A narrated concept script alone is not a working app, and pointing/dragging correctness is not a fully validated child understanding of number words.

2. CO-PLAY — parent and child collaborate rather than adult leads the answer

Two separate collections are prepared: parent privately counts a three-object model set and child receives an accessible E1 set (three for PK3 or six for PK4). Each independently decides a counting route, but the adult does not supply the child's numbers. Child is the counting coach: ask adult to count a new four-piece practice set and purposely begin moving the same token twice only during teaching, inviting child to notice and repair; mark this as supported reasoning, not an exit. Parent then explicitly explains last-number-total with a different modeled set. At EXIT, the adult presents the prepared new X1 collection, while keeping the adult's own unfamiliar set hidden. The child first counts X1 and reports a total/reason without guidance; the parent reveals their own set afterwards as a playful comparison. Record the child's first independent response before it becomes a coached social conversation. Children may opt out or direct adult handling. Usable physical script, untested with families.

3. ADULT-LED ONE CHILD — hands-on counting collections

The detailed B sequence is the complete route: teacher explicitly models distinct correspondence and last-number meaning, child physically orders counters, adult diagnoses skips/recounts versus missing total concept, with new independent X1 and later R1. The child may use tactile trays, sign, AAC or direct a motor proxy. A motor proxy may move exactly the piece that the child indicates; no count or total may be chosen by proxy if recording child-originated evidence. Written script and material path supplied; no real learner trial.

4. TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM — genuine whole-class opportunities and defensible observation

For 20 children, prepare 20 bags of ten large washable reusable counters (200 pieces total) and 20 individual mats; an adult supplies them without small, swallowable components. If available, have one projected teacher mat for explicit model. A child can point/direct rather than grasp. Timing is an estimate needing classroom feasibility review:

  • Minutes 0–3: Each learner independently explores E1 pieces; teacher poses baseline prompt once, then stops. No choral number answer is accepted as individual C/K evidence. Teacher observes 2–3 focus learners while others manipulate independently.
  • Minutes 3–6: Teacher models M1 with a separate large set; all children watch, rehearse with their own model set, then cleanly reset.
  • Minutes 6–12: Children work D1 independently or with an intentional partner checking that each object moved once. Teacher announces “I saw one piece moved twice. How would we fix that?” without revealing totals. Each child places a private total card or expresses via AAC as available; a partner's answer never becomes independent evidence.
  • Minutes 12–16: All children receive X1 anew. Individually count; choose or communicate a total without teacher pointing or telling which answer; children communicate a total privately by speech, signing, AAC or placing an optional non-cueing number card face down until counting is complete; the adult records only responses actually observed. To assess correspondence, teacher must actually witness touch/move sequence or obtain a trace (e.g., numbered movement path made independently) rather than infer it from the final card.
  • Minutes 16–20: Share a couple of strategies, not public child rankings. Collect materials safely and store each person's paper exit slip privately if authorized by school policy. Because one teacher cannot verify twenty movement sequences at once, mark unobserved C as ?, not a pass.
  • Follow-up: In 4–5 small groups of four or five across later center rotations, observe individual full C/K paths, including new R1 sets on another day, while remaining children use already-established supervised activities. Avoid a fabricated same-day complete 20-child mastery record.

Classroom realities: A number card or privately communicated total can show a K candidate, not proof of last-number meaning or count-by-count C; oral chorus is neither. Need 20 safely prepared sets, a distribution/cleanup routine, nonreading pictorial cards and additional adult support according to actual setting. Written feasibility only; no actual classroom deployment.

E. Formal observation and reporting contract

Record separate C and K for EACH source-specific size/range, task ID and date.

  • C2 — Demonstrated in this task: child intentionally tracks each item exactly once and accurately pairs successive number words/communicated count units with items. C1: partial correct tracking, omissions/duplicates or help limits. C0: attempted but demonstrably not showing correspondence. C?: not observed (including purely numerical answer).
  • K2 — Demonstrated in this task: child independently identifies the last count number as total of entire set when asked separately, in speech, sign, AAC, or another meaning-preserving method. K1: correct total but ambiguous meaning or reason. K0: attempted total explanation demonstrably incorrect. K?: not observed. A correct guessed number with no evidence of last-word meaning does not get K2.
  • Assistance/provenance: U child-originated without conceptual hint; H number/strategy or concept hint supplied; A access-only proxy or device allowing child's own cognitive decisions; X unknown external support. A child can be C2/H but this is not C2/U. When access aid plus a concept hint coexist, record both, do not pretend A cancels H.
  • Other fields: PK band, grade-bound intended max, actual set size, layout, exact observed count trail/first total, first vs retry, language/access method, supports/uncertainty, entry/during/exit/revisit phase, source ID assessed, adult observation source, next action. No actual child information is included in the shareable printed template.

Honest interpretation: One successful five-piece task can offer a PK3 source-relevant candidate for C/K; it does not establish skill across contexts or qualify PK4 ten-piece evidence. PK4 source range is up to ten: an eight-piece success is not a blanket claim for full-range performance. A classroom math app and teacher observation can collect useful authentic evidence, but no scoring model here is calibrated or educator accepted.

F. Accessibility, inclusion and safety (mandatory)

Built in: opt-in child interest, no mandatory speed or public ranking; visual high contrast plus safe tactile representation; home-language number sequences and careful translation of how the child communicates total; chosen sign/AAC/eye-gaze or spoken communication; large graspable pieces; seated/mat option; flexible rest/return; predictable transition; adult handles any cutting/printing. When a child cannot manipulate counters, let them direct the placement and keep count choices child-authored. Do not confound articulation, sensory access, language variety or fine-motor ability with mathematical understanding.

Specific supports: start with two or three pieces for practice; use an empty-to-counted tray and visually distinct zones; model on M1 only and then reset with a different set for independent data; optional deeper set reconfiguration, self-generated counting puzzle, or child explanation of unchanged quantity. Avoid color-only coding or assuming ability based on age. The original source PK-level is the intended teaching/curriculum scope, not a ceiling on children's abilities.

Retrospective differentiation correction — 2026-11-01

PK3: access, scaffold, independent source evidence

Teach and fade: Studio: counted/not-counted area first on one/two objects; fade visual path at 3 and 4–5. A proxy can physically move only child-chosen items, with word and total supplied by child.

Uncued source probe: Unmodeled new five-piece collection: log every object plus successive child word, then total and whether final word refers to all.

False credit or bias risk: An adult silently counting while moving pieces or a child echoing five can falsely turn adult math into C or K.

PK4: access, scaffold, independent source evidence

Teach and fade: Studio: confirm new 1–5 then model organization of 6–8 and 9/10; accessible tangible spread, no speed requirement, fade all instructor choice of next object or count word.

Uncued source probe: Unknown scattered ten-piece collection and separate observed earlier sizes; independently map each item to word and report whole-set total.

False credit or bias risk: Perfect eight-piece practice cannot automatically earn ten; a crowded visual array with inaccessible items should not be scored as mathematical failure.

Lesson 01 companion teacher/facilitator guide — Collection counting studio

Paired standalone plan: Collection counting studio. This adult instructional companion is NOT a counted lesson. Refer to separate objective educator guide. The four official source objectives and exact source cell hashes remain in their original authoring audit.

Specific teaching mechanism

The real-object counted-zone laboratory teaches a learner to move or direct one genuine object from uncounted to counted after associating a single count word. A separate whole-set question checks meaning beyond count accuracy. Avoid treating an independently correct “five” guess as correspondence or a last-word echo as cardinality.

Adult preparation, material safety and phase boundaries

Use the exact phase identities in the paired Lesson 01; do not silently substitute developmental mini-checks for its named assessments. In the paired main lesson's later source-range example, ENTRY E1 uses PK3 three unfamiliar objects or PK4 six; MODEL M1 is a separate adult-owned two-block illustration (optionally another four-counter illustration); DURING D1 uses PK3 four or PK4 eight; and the protected, independently attempted EXIT X1 uses PK3 five or PK4 ten unfamiliar objects. Keep model objects, exit layout, and answer cues separate from the learner's independent task. Every selected set has a distinguishable uncounted/counted area without a preprinted numeral answer.

Earlier-stage instruction is deliberately different, not a conflicting definition of E1/M1/D1/X1. Begin at 1–2 objects for developing PK3 learners, or recheck 1–5 before 6–8 and 9–10 for developing PK4 learners, following DEVELOPMENTAL_SEQUENCE.md. Provide a fresh stage mini-check appropriate to each current stage; record its actual size and scope. These checks can inform next-step teaching but do not become X1 source-range evidence by renaming, and no child is expected to traverse the entire range in a first 15–20-minute sitting. Prepare as many genuinely distinct arrangements and later sessions as needed for the chosen stage and eventual novel full-range exit. No small parts, beans, coins, choking pieces or forced touch.

ENTRY → MODEL → DURING → independent EXIT → real later-date REVISIT

ENTRY: “Would you count these for me?” after child chooses an available accessible way of tracking actual pieces. Do NOT cue “what is the total?” before first natural counting/whole-set expression. MODEL: on the distinct two-block M1, pair each real object with one number word; afterward explicitly distinguish “two was the last word; that is how many are in ALL the pieces.” The optional four-counter model also uses its actual count, never an answer for X1. DURING: let learner own each visit and first count-word mapping, correct repeats by practicing moving each counted piece once, then fade counted-zone arrows and adult pointing. If child counts perfectly but doesn't say whole total independently, teach the meaning on another modeled group without scripting the target EXIT. EXIT: new source-range actual objects, record first individual C and K evidence separately before feedback. REVISIT: real later date on genuinely new objects; currently NOT_OBSERVED.

Contingent misconception feedback and remediation

If a learner double-counts one token, first use different teacher-only pieces to show how each object is visited once and fade any tracking scaffold; don't label a corrected same-set count independent. If learner says “four” after adult says “your last number tells the whole set,” record K as assisted H. If learner counts 9 pieces but cannot map each count word to its real object, a correct spoken 9 cannot retroactively establish C. For learner-controlled motor proxy require child to select each object and count meaning; adult movement may be neutral A, adult selecting order/words is H.

Four separate official outcome interpretations (apply to this lesson)

PK3.V.A.2: independent one-to-one C count on an actually fresh real ≤5 set; one count word per actual item, no double count/omission, regardless of total statement. PK3.V.A.3: child counts an actually fresh ≤5 set and independently communicates the last count word as the total of the WHOLE group, not a teacher-fed “last” echo. K requires count and cardinality meaning. PK4.V.A.2: independent one-to-one C count on genuinely new real 6–10 items as well as supported small foundations; do not infer upper range from ≤5. PK4.V.A.3: child counts a real 6–10 group and independently states that final count word tells how many there are altogether; distinguish K from merely repeating the end of C. Use separate first COUNT sequence and first TOTAL response fields and U child-origin, H conceptual help, A neutral access and X unknown provenance. If one authentic trial independently demonstrates C and K, it can support both separately; no automatic cross-credit.

Seven differentiated learner/access pathways

Attention/executive: limited items visible at first and predictable phase cues, fade hints for EXIT. Language/communication: self-authored semantically meaningful home-language, sign, or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) counts and total; never adult-selected messages. Literacy/phonology: don't require numeral decoding as a counting prerequisite. Numeracy: 1–3 real safe items first, later PK3 4–5 and PK4 6–10 with authentic correspondence. Autism/sensory: predictable voluntary safe activity with no speed pressure. Intellectual/developmental: model errors/total on distinct adult material and fade. Motor/hearing/vision: oversized contrast/tactile safe objects and individually reviewed unbiased child-directed proxy; a proxy physically moving child-selected items is access A, not permission to speak the count or total.
