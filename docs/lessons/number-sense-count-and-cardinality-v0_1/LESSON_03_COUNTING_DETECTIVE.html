Lesson 03 — The Counting Detective: find the mistake, prove the total

Required developmental teaching sequence: PK3 foundation → PK4 extension. PK3: 1–2 objects, then 3, then mixed 1–5; PK4: recheck 1–5, introduce 6–8, then 9–10, then mixed 1–10. Assess one-to-one count (C) and last-number cardinality (K) separately. The existing full-grade EXIT is a later source-range check, not the first teaching session. Apply this ordered sequence in SOLO, CO-PLAY, ADULT-LED and CLASSROOM routes; SOLO remains a written specification only.

ID: MATH-PK-COUNT-03 • v0.1, 2026-10-30 • Original theme-neutral teaching plan, internally authored only. This lesson teaches by contrasting worked examples and eliciting child-built counterexamples: the learner is a detective investigating whether a proposed counting procedure can be trusted. Unlike Lesson 01's collection counting or Lesson 02's movement trail, the central cognitive work is diagnosis and justification. Crucially, spotting someone else's error alone is not equivalent to independently counting a collection or understanding the final number.

A. Teacher planning card and precise source boundary

Field Actual specification
Published source target 2022 Texas Pre-K PK3.V.A.2, one-to-one correspondence to 5; PK3.V.A.3, final word means whole set total to 5; PK4.V.A.2, correspondence to 10; PK4.V.A.3, final-word whole-set cardinality to 10. Official inventory: education/planning/EDUCATION_TX_MATH_SOURCE_OUTCOME_INVENTORY_V0_1.csv; hashes in canonical source foundation. Research inventory still identifies extracted text as pending formal final source review. This family does not automatically assess rote sequence up to 10/30, numeral recognition, subitizing, geometry, or art.
Learning question “How can you tell whether somebody really counted every object once? How can you know what the last number tells you?”
Intended child learning action Observe two competing original counting demonstrations, explain with visible evidence why an object was skipped/count-repeated or why total was misconstrued; independently count an unfamiliar new real set and communicate whole-set total without the faulty demonstration's answer cues.
Duration and pacing Roughly 18–23 minutes with breaks or split session; later new-case revisit 5 minutes on a separate date.
Original materials Up to ten large, safe, non-swallowable blocks or laminated unnumbered object pictures, two simple blank unnumbered learner surfaces (plain large paper, flat washable mats, or clean stable tabletops; draw no answer numerals, grids of correct count, or preassigned visit slots), optional broad safe reusable “visited” markers or an empty/finished section; adult-only model diagrams Model A and Model B and a private teacher observation form supplied in METHOD_03_DETECTIVE_CARDS.html. That HTML does not provide two blank learner Detective Windows mats. An educator prepares the blank surfaces directly from the recipe here without needing a printer or sibling resource. Keep Model A/B answer labels face-down/out of learner view outside explicit teaching; never display the teacher-answer page during independent X3. Adult prepares materials; no child scissors or small loose parts.
Access / safety No compulsory spoken correction, public embarrassment or forced peer criticism. Child may communicate using sign, home language, augmented and alternative communication (AAC), gaze, pointing or an adult-directed motor proxy. The “mistake” belongs to a fictional grown-up puppet or intentional adult demo, never a named classmate. No small parts, unsafe magnets or consumable counters.
What can count C: independently pairs each real object with one correct successive number word, no omissions/duplicates. K: independently expresses that the final word is the quantity of the whole collection. Evaluate separately at proper PK3 (≤5) / PK4 (≤10) bounds, with first response and help provenance U/H/A/X. Error spotting is useful formative content but not sufficient official source evidence.

B. Four distinct material scenes and one true later revisit

ENTRY E3 — no error prompt yet. PK3: adult quietly places three plain large circles in a loose arch; PK4: six squares scattered across one mat. Ask: “Someone wants to know how many there are. Can you find out in your own way? How many altogether?” Observe first physical/count-word trail and last-word/total answer before describing any detective trick. Never display numeral labels. Assess C and K separately or mark not observed.

MODEL M3 — compare two original demonstrations on different objects. Three large washable star shapes on a different mat. Adult controls a first puppet-hand and counts aloud “one” on first, “two” on second, “three” again on second, leaving third untouched. Adult says, “That last word was three. Does it mean I counted all three objects?” Then adult explicitly explains: “No. I called the second star two different numbers. The third star got none. To know how many in the whole collection, I need one number word for each star and no double visits.” Adult then demonstrates correct one-to-one on the same model star set and connects “three” to the whole set. The model answer is not independent evidence for child.

DURING D3 — compare and repair an invented faulty method, without answer leaks. PK3 four large tree silhouettes; PK4 eight different bottle-shaped pictures, on a new unnumbered mat. Teacher places a removable visited marker after each demonstrated tap: intentionally skips the last object or counts a marked object twice (choose one per attempt). Ask “Where would you investigate first? What could our pretend detective change to make the count trustworthy?” Learner points to suspect object and independently demonstrates a corrected count with their own successive number words. If child only answers “you counted twice,” respond “How could you show a new count that doesn't do that?” and let them lead. Later, adult misstates “the last word is the number of the last object, not all the objects.” Ask which meaning is supported by what they counted and model correct whole-set interpretation on a different set only if necessary.

EXIT X3 — independent proof, genuinely new context. Before the child sees it, create a new, mixed-material collection: PK3 exactly five assorted large safe nonfood blocks in a single cluster; PK4 exactly ten diverse safe large objects on one plain mat (no recognizable rows, repeated puppet or special suspect object). No deliberately faulty demonstration is played immediately before this exit. Say: “We haven't counted this collection. Can you show a count that would let someone know how many objects are here? How many altogether, and what tells you?” Observe child-selected pairing of objects and successive number words, first total and evidence. Do not say “last word” or “count each once” in the exit prompt, since they disclose the criteria under assessment. If the child asks “Did I count twice?” allow child-originated self-check; preserve first attempt and classify adult prompts after it as coached.

REVISIT R3 — actual different date. New combination of safe items (PK3 five / PK4 ten) placed in a totally different space/layout. Ask “If someone is not sure of the number, can you show how to find out?” Without any modeled mistake. Observe whether the child still gives one word per object and communicates whole-set total. If session never occurs, NOT_OBSERVED, not passed retention.

C. Fully specified adult-led one-child instruction

0–4 min, baseline: Place E3; say exact entry prompt. Hold off coaching until the first count and separate whole-set response have been observed. If child passes, ask whether they'd like to investigate a puppet story or stop. No shame.

4–9 min, explicit contrast: Put away E3. Put three new stars before child. Adult performs obviously incorrect count on the second star twice and omitted third, while the learner watches. Adult explicitly compares with a correct complete count using a visible count trail. Ask: “What was unfair about how the puppet counted? Which object never received a number? Is saying ‘three’ enough to know there are three altogether?” If child merely picks the happy puppet, point back to object actions; teach the causal procedure rather than emotion cues. Clarify a correct final number must describe the entire actual set.

9–15 min, guided detective and child repair: Arrange D3 on a fresh mat. Let child choose between two different flawed traces prepared by adult: skip-one or double-count-one. Show exactly one flawed trace at a time with adult-produced mark strip. Child identifies evidence, not a “bad” person. After discussion, clear marks and invite child to perform a true independent count on D3. If the adult supplies the correct next number, mark H for that action; don't upgrade it to independent later. For K confusion, model last-word-to-total with a separate two-block example and then let child try another unmodeled set later.

15–20 min, new proof exit: Remove all stars and puppets. Display X3 new mixed-material collection, no trace already drawn. Say only: “We haven't counted this collection. Can you show a count that would let someone know how many objects are here? How many altogether, and what tells you?” Wait. Write count-word/object trail and first total before feedback. If child arrives at correct total by subitizing, appreciate reasoning but do not claim C if one-to-one action was not observed. Keep a child's valid unusual order; only the exact-once relationship matters.

20–23 min, synthesis: “A count is trustworthy when every object gets one number word. The last word tells the number of all those objects.” Explicit teacher synthesis is after independent exit and is not retroactive evidence. Let child invent a fictional silly counting mistake for the adult to repair, or finish.

D. Four full-mode routes (same math, different pedagogy)

SOLO, independently narrated tablet — specified, NOT IMPLEMENTED

  1. Entry: A child-controlled picture board shows E3 objects with unnumbered larger touch targets, replayable spoken instructions, reading-free help/not-sure and accessible switch/scan. For each object, child selects its next independently chosen spoken count word via a non-cued audio-number selection, recording (object ID, count-word ID, first attempt, assistance) tuples. A separate afterward question asks whole-set total without displaying the correct quantity. Pure tap order without child count-word input is not evidence of correct correspondence.
  2. Model: Two animated generic unnumbered hands count three stars, one mistakenly counts second twice while third is untouched; narration demonstrates the reason and a correct alternative. No shaming or identity analogy.
  3. Guided inquiry: In D3, child taps a specific skipped or double-counted object in a marked suspect trace; after selecting, animation shows which object had zero or two number-word assignments. Child then counts a different new group by themselves with feedback explaining remaining omitted/duplicated object, not only red/green correctness.
  4. Novel exit: X3 has completely different shape/layout, and no error animation or count-number cue immediately before it. Child enters count-word/object trace and selects whole-set total separately; the app records the first untouched response and help/no-answer paths. A lucky correct total with inaccurate object-word matching cannot satisfy C. A copied count sequence and taps still cannot prove real speech understanding; observed child usability remains outstanding.
  5. Later revisit: Do not pretend same-session re-entry is another day or durable memory. Requires authorized scheduling/retention architecture; not built.

Solo delivery gap: This is a substantive production spec, not a functioning independent child lesson, not proven pre-reader communication access or equal validated parity.

CO-PLAY — cooperative error detectives

Adult deliberately plays a fictional counting-robot who is allowed to make harmless counting mistakes only during model/practice. Child chooses which suspect trace to inspect; adult must follow child-authored repair instructions, not supply right number words. Child then offers an original different mistake and has adult explain why it affects object count. At exit, robot exits the scene, and the child counts X3 privately in their own way before adult compares strategies. The parent records only actually observed C/K and distinguishes a parent hint from child source knowledge. Parent may choose home-language counting or handle objects at child's direction; no pressured speech.

ADULT-LED ONE CHILD — actual detective inquiry

The exact timetable in C is the usable route: uncoached baseline, one real mistaken count and one correct adult demonstration with concrete models, child-diagnosed D3 plus their own repaired count, and independent X3 fresh object set. Avoid telling child “You're wrong”; always frame fallacy as a puppet's or adult's method, and let learner change it. Use METHOD_03_DETECTIVE_CARDS.html original reference diagrams and plain large physical counters, not copied publisher pages.

TEACHER-LED CLASSROOM — independent counting, not just collaborative chorus

For a class example of 20, prepare 200 large reusable objects (ten per learner maximum), 20 unique small trays/mats and one teacher large puppet/trace mat. Use real large manipulatives, not small beads. One teacher cannot actually certify 20 correspondence sequences simultaneously.

  • 0–4 min: Each child receives E3 unnumbered objects and individually attempts whole set. Teacher observes 2–3 focus children's count-word/object traces; all others opportunity to explore, NOT_YET_OBSERVED academically.
  • 4–9 min: Project/perform the mistaken second-star count; everyone receives an identical simple blank trace strip and circles which object the adult skipped. Group discussion of reason is teaching, not 20 independently verified C/K responses.
  • 9–15 min: In groups of four, rotate detective roles: investigator identifies a demonstrated puppet flaw, counter shows a new valid count, witness checks distinct object visits, recorder draws marks. Teacher changes role after a few minutes so every child counts their own physical set. Peer feedback is helpful instruction, not unprompted exit.
  • 15–20 min: Give each learner X3 independently; they count actual individual sets and privately show total/strategy. Teacher observes 2–3 actual full sequences; a correct card or point to the omitted object is not enough to infer individual C and K. Collect permitted anonymous/practice artifacts only per actual school policy.
  • 20–23 min: Ask a few voluntary pupils to describe different safe counting routes; no identity-based or ranking critique. Teach later rotations of groups of four/five for teacher-observed counts; provide normal teacher-supervised centers to peers.
  • On separate day: R3 new item/location for observed individuals, not imaginary retention.

E. Evaluation criteria and responsive supports

C2 = observed child-owned one count word per real object exactly once in appropriate ascending count sequence; C1 = attempted partly correct or not enough for certainty; C0 = observed unsuccessful count strategy; C? = not observed. K2 = observed child-owned meaning of last count word for the whole collection; K1 = right final number with ambiguous source/meaning; K0 = observed wrong whole-set meaning; K? = not observed. U unprompted, H conceptual help, A access-only support, X provenance unknown; support labels may coexist and never overwrite first responses. A child who only spots a counting error has not thereby shown C2 or K2 on their own set.

Misconception-specific response:

  • “The puppet said three so that must be right” → Show second star received two separate words and third none; ask child to contrast with a different accurate set; clarify numerical label alone is not proof.
  • Child catches skip yet self-counts incorrectly → Acknowledge detective insight, then scaffold with an empty/finished mat using another set; independently reassess later. Do not award the source action for spotting adult flaw.
  • Child knows count words but repeats an object → offer removable visited markers as a teaching aid, explicitly tag H if adults suggest it.
  • Child counts all once but says total is the last star only → model two-block whole-collection principle, use a different practice set, revisit K separately.
  • Child says total right instantly but doesn't count visibly → possible subitizing or memorized pattern; celebrate idea without false C credit; invite another task where method can be observed.
  • Child declines detective framing or cannot interpret cartoon/error graphics → switch to same mathematical inquiry using real objects and simple truthful explanation; no penalty for missing the fictional error.

Accommodations: Child-chosen pretend error, never publicly criticized peer; accessible large objects, table- or floor-safe use, tactile number-word prompts, home languages, sign/AAC, child-directed physical adult proxy. Make all choices perceivable without color alone, do not force speaking, movement, social performance or identifying emotionally “wrong” people. Individualized accommodations status REVIEW_NOT_COMPLETED; appropriate educator must consult actual relevant IEP/IFSP/504 provisions and the child/family as applicable. No inference about disability from score, no learner data in repository. Printed resources adult-prepared and tested for print/safety before classroom use.

Retrospective differentiation correction — 2026-11-01

PK3: access, scaffold, independent source evidence

Teach and fade: Prepare predictable accessible puppet demonstration: double-tap a 2-piece model, then skip a different third piece; provide calm/nonverbal chance to notice, not mandatory public criticism.

Uncued source probe: Hide the puppet for a separate unfamiliar 1–5 real-object count and first whole-set total; no error clue before exit.

False credit or bias risk: Catching the puppet error or repeating its correction does not establish the learner can actually count an unseen set.

PK4: access, scaffold, independent source evidence

Teach and fade: After rechecking 1–5, use new 6–8 then 9/10 wrong demonstrations with captioned/described actions, quiet sensory option and no speed scoring.

Uncued source probe: Independently count fresh ten unannounced objects without puppet, teacher indication of which item was missed or feedback before first answer.

False credit or bias risk: Correctly choosing the preprinted detective answer is reasoning practice, not ten-object independent counting or cardinality.

Mode adaptation: SOLO (specified, unbuilt) must permit narrated untimed child-controlled object and number-word selection with separate uncued final total; never award C from taps alone or autoplay correct sequence. CO-PLAY caregivers and ADULT-LED teachers may prepare objects, model on different sets and handle child-directed physical movement, but may not choose item, word or total for independent evidence. CLASSROOM rotations must observe each individual pair sequence and whole-set answer; participation or choral response alone is not a pass. Use real individualized family/IEP/IFSP/504 guidance and qualified specialists where relevant; tag access-only, helped, modified objective and not observed separately. No automatic diagnosis from delay, silence or preference. Published size bounds remain unchanged.

Architecture-specific source EXIT and later-date REVISIT

PK3.V.A.2: Independently count every previously unmodeled real object one-to-one within ≤5 with no skip or double count; this C action does not require the separate last-number K communication. PK3.V.A.3: Actually count a novel ≤5 real set, then personally express how the final count word means the number in the whole collection, without adult cue; this K action includes counting. PK4.V.A.2: Independently count novel six-to-ten actual physical items with one count word per item and no phantom slots; PK3-like five-only example cannot prove this grade extension. PK4.V.A.3: Actually count a novel 6–10 real set and independently express last-number-as-total meaning for all objects, not a memorized adult answer. In Lesson 3, the specific movement, puppet error or constructed grouping is DURING instruction only. Independent EXIT uses truly fresh untouched items and the original source actions (C and K) for each relevant outcome; REVISIT on a genuinely later calendar date with different real items remains NOT_OBSERVED. Lesson 03 companion teacher/facilitator guide — Counting error detective

Paired standalone plan: Counting error detective. This adult instructional companion is NOT a counted lesson. Refer to separate objective educator guide. The four official source objectives and exact source cell hashes remain in their original authoring audit.

Specific teaching mechanism

Deliberately faulty adult/puppet counting can expose common one-to-one errors: skipping a real object, counting one twice, counting empty space, or saying a last word unrelated to the whole set. Critically identifying a puppet's flaw is pedagogical DURING work, NOT independently counting a different real set. A learner must perform the original source verbs later.

Adult preparation, material safety and phase boundaries

Prepare two blank unnumbered child-safe surfaces from ordinary plain paper, washable mat or stable clean tabletop, without any printed total, answer-coded grid or forced path. The included METHOD_03_DETECTIVE_CARDS.html supplies adult-only Model A/B diagrams with visible answers plus a private observation form, NOT two blank learner Detective Windows mats. Keep those teacher-answer panels covered outside explicit M3/D3 teaching, especially for X3; no printer is needed for the blank mats. Provide distinct safe non-swallowable adult-only puppet model objects for controlled mistakes. MAIN stage identities: E3 PK3=3, PK4=6; M3=3 different star objects in correct-versus-incorrect model; D3 PK3=4, PK4=8 unnumbered new objects; X3 PK3=5, PK4=10 genuinely fresh mixed real objects; R3 different actual day and novel set. Earlier small readiness mini-checks do not become X3 by renaming. Never intentionally trick a child into feeling incompetent; mistakes belong to a fictional adult demonstration, not peers. Privately identify exact missing/double-counted object in teacher key, and keep all printed answers away from child independent source assessment.

ENTRY → MODEL → DURING → independent EXIT → real later-date REVISIT

ENTRY: before detective role, offer a neutral new real set and record child's unprompted item-to-word count and whole-set communication. MODEL: adult puppet on different pieces makes one visible intentional duplicate; another consenting adult gently corrects by recounting each genuine item exactly once and stating total. DURING: learner notices a skip in puppet's new setup, proposes repair; educator models on yet another example only if needed and fades error flags. EXIT: puppet fully removed, child independently counts entirely new actual objects with no flaw indicated; assess C and afterward independent K separately. REVISIT: fresh genuine later calendar date with independent set, not a repeated solved puppet scenario.

Contingent misconception feedback and remediation

If learner says puppet wrong because “red looks bigger,” teach object-visit evidence rather than size. If child repairs puppet count correctly but then serially counts a new real set with duplicates, do not credit A.2. If a teacher tells learner “the last number tells how many,” a subsequent copied “five in all” is assisted, not new independent A.3. For language access allow learner-origin sign, AAC and home language; a proxy must not supply which puppet step was wrong or child’s EXIT total.

Four separate official outcome interpretations (apply to this lesson)

PK3.V.A.2: independent one-to-one C count on an actually fresh real ≤5 set; one count word per actual item, no double count/omission, regardless of total statement. PK3.V.A.3: child counts an actually fresh ≤5 set and independently communicates the last count word as the total of the WHOLE group, not a teacher-fed “last” echo. K requires count and cardinality meaning. PK4.V.A.2: independent one-to-one C count on genuinely new real 6–10 items as well as supported small foundations; do not infer upper range from ≤5. PK4.V.A.3: child counts a real 6–10 group and independently states that final count word tells how many there are altogether; distinguish K from merely repeating the end of C. Use separate first COUNT sequence and first TOTAL response fields and U child-origin, H conceptual help, A neutral access and X unknown provenance. If one authentic trial independently demonstrates C and K, it can support both separately; no automatic cross-credit.

Seven differentiated learner/access pathways

Attention/executive: limited items visible at first and predictable phase cues, fade hints for EXIT. Language/communication: self-authored semantically meaningful home-language, sign, or augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) counts and total; never adult-selected messages. Literacy/phonology: don't require numeral decoding as a counting prerequisite. Numeracy: 1–3 real safe items first, later PK3 4–5 and PK4 6–10 with authentic correspondence. Autism/sensory: predictable voluntary safe activity with no speed pressure. Intellectual/developmental: model errors/total on distinct adult material and fade. Motor/hearing/vision: oversized contrast/tactile safe objects and individually reviewed unbiased child-directed proxy; a proxy physically moving child-selected items is access A, not permission to speak the count or total.

Truthful four written delivery modes

SOLO — NOT IMPLEMENTED: future prereader app would have to preserve each learner-chosen item→count-word association and independent last-number whole-set meaning, with unmodeled EXIT and separate H/A provenance; a correct onscreen numeral button is not proof. CO-PLAY: parent arranges the safe set but cannot supply next item or total at assessment. ADULT-LED: educator models on unrelated objects, then records independent C/K separately. CLASSROOM: individual fresh real-object first responses, not choral count or another child's completed set.
